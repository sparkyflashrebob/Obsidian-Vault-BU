### ETFs - Session 1: Market Outlook & Investment Strategies
📅 June 25, 2026
👥 Don Raymond (Regional Brokerage Consultant, Fidelity), Christy Akulian (Head of iShares Investment Strategy, Americas, BlackRock)
### Outline
### Webinar Overview and Format

- Presented as a three-hour broadcast across three sessions, the event is a collaboration between Fidelity and iShares by BlackRock, focused on understanding ETFs in today's market.

- Topics covered across the sessions include market outlook, strategies for navigating volatility with ETFs, and ways investors can evaluate ETFs based on common financial goals.

- Each session consists of a presentation from an iShares team member followed by a live platform demonstration on fidelity.com.

- Live Q&A is available throughout, with the option to contact a local Fidelity branch for any questions that cannot be addressed during the broadcast.

### Mid-Year Market Performance Review

- Nearly all major asset classes have beaten cash returns year to date, reinforcing the case for investing over saving.

- Large cap stocks have performed well in the first half, though they have been outpaced by small caps and international equities.

- Emerging market equities have returned approximately three times as much as developed markets, reflecting significant diversification and dispersion within international stocks.

- A rotation away from mega-cap technology stocks, often referred to as the MAG7, has been observed, with broader diversification emerging within equity markets.

- Despite strong small cap performance, a preference is maintained for large caps due to their higher quality profile.

### U.S. Economic Resilience and Outlook

- A temporary economic slump at the end of 2025 was largely attributed to a government shutdown, after which GDP and GDP expectations rebounded quickly.

- Contrary to negative headlines, the totality of economic data suggests that recession odds have decreased rather than increased in 2026.

- The labor market shows some underlying weakness, with approximately 80 to 90 percent of jobs added over the past three years concentrated in healthcare, government, and education sectors.

- Overall, the rewards of investing are viewed as outweighing the risks, with a preference for allocating to areas of the market showing the strongest growth.

### Role of AI in Driving Economic Growth

- Structural growth driven by AI and AI-related investment is identified as a key reason for the U.S. economy's resilience in the face of headwinds such as tariffs, trade conflicts, and Middle East tensions.

- Since the launch of ChatGPT in 2023, AI and AI-related expenditures have made up a growing share of total GDP growth year over year.

- AI investment is described not merely as a stock market story but as a real spending and investment phenomenon percolating across the entire economy.

- This macroeconomic context is presented to establish that the AI theme is fundamentally driven rather than based purely on future expectations.

### Inflation Trends and Federal Reserve Outlook

- Inflation has remained above target for five years, driven in part by higher fuel prices resulting from conflict in the Middle East feeding into broader price increases.

- Analysis of CPI components does not show re-acceleration of inflation, and future inflation expectations remain relatively contained.

- Goods inflation sparked by prior tariffs is beginning to roll off, partially offsetting oil-driven price increases in other parts of the inflation basket.

- New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh confirmed his commitment to fighting inflation at his first Fed meeting, leading markets to price in expectations of a rate hike later in the year.

- BlackRock's view diverges from consensus, as the expectation is that the Fed will hold rates steady for most of the year rather than hiking, given the continued downward trajectory of inflation and falling fuel prices.

### Investment Implications of the Macro Outlook

- Historical analysis shows that periods of positive growth combined with a Fed on pause have still produced strong returns from stocks, particularly growth stocks, and reasonable returns from bonds.

- The current scenario of strong growth and a paused Fed is considered a favorable backdrop for both equities and fixed income, even if less ideal than a rate-cutting environment.

- A focus on following earnings is presented as the central principle guiding allocation decisions, given the strength of fundamental earnings growth across markets.

### Election Risk and Market Behavior

- Markets historically underperform in the lead-up to midterm elections due to policy uncertainty, but tend to rebound strongly once election outcomes are known.

- Data shows that U.S. stock market performance typically bottoms approximately 22 days before elections, as polling becomes more accurate and uncertainty diminishes.

- Staying invested through election-related uncertainty is recommended, as exiting the market risks missing the rally that typically follows once results are clear.

- Keeping politics out of portfolio decisions is emphasized as a key principle supported by historical market data.

### AI as an Investment Theme and Earnings Driver

- Comparing current market conditions to 1999, earnings growth has actually exceeded stock market performance in recent years, whereas in 1999 stock returns far outpaced underlying earnings.

- Price-to-earnings ratios for the broad index and for technology are lower at the end of June than at the start of the year, indicating that performance is fundamentally rather than speculatively driven.

- AI companies are generating the strongest earnings growth at the index level, and the definition of what constitutes an AI company is broadening across sectors.

- Targeted ETF exposures are highlighted as a way to access the full AI investment stack, including hyperscalers, semiconductors, chips, memory, data centers, and power infrastructure.

- Specific funds mentioned include BAI, an active ETF capturing the AI trade, and ARTY, an index-based ETF providing AI-specific thematic exposure.

### Dispersion Within the Technology Sector

- The largest dispersion on record has been observed within the technology sector in 2026, with semiconductors rallying strongly while software experienced a sharp sell-off before partially recovering.

- This level of internal divergence means that simply allocating to the broad technology sector is insufficient to capture the AI opportunity effectively.

- Modular building blocks in the form of targeted ETFs allow investors to select specific sub-sectors such as semiconductors or software rather than taking undifferentiated technology exposure.

### International and Emerging Market Opportunities

- Earnings revisions are currently stronger in emerging markets than in the United States, with analysts upgrading company earnings forecasts at a higher rate internationally.

- The composition of emerging market indexes has shifted dramatically, with technology now representing over 40 percent of the emerging market index compared to approximately 16 percent in the pre-pandemic era.

- Countries such as Korea and Taiwan are central to the physical build-out of AI, producing critical components including semiconductors and memory chips.

- Developed market international equities, while less exposed to the AI trade, provide diversification through different drivers of risk and return.

- Rather than diversifying down into small caps to reduce U.S. large cap concentration, looking abroad to international equities is recommended as a higher-quality diversification approach.

- Investors are cautioned that similarly named emerging market funds can have materially different holdings and performance outcomes depending on whether they track MSCI or FTSE indexes, as MSCI includes Korea as an emerging market while FTSE does not.

### Diversification Within Equity Portfolios

- Over the past three years, despite a strong bull market at the index level, 42 percent of individual stocks lost money, illustrating the risk of concentrated single-stock exposure.

- Less than one tenth of one percent of U.S. stocks, mutual funds, and ETFs lost money over the same period, demonstrating the protective power of diversification.

- ETFs are presented as an efficient tool for accessing hundreds or thousands of underlying stocks within a single trade, building in diversification without requiring individual stock selection.

### Fixed Income Opportunities and Portfolio Diversification

- Rising yields, while creating short-term headwinds for bond performance, have produced a significantly improved entry point for fixed income investors.

- Approximately 80 percent of outstanding bonds now offer yields above 4 percent, compared to less than 20 percent in 2020 and 2021, representing a dramatically expanded opportunity set.

- AGG is described as a diversified starting point for bond exposure, while BINC is an active fund targeting higher yields with controlled duration risk.

- Fixed income remains an important component of a balanced portfolio even in a positive equity outlook, providing risk management and income generation alongside stock holdings.

### Long-Term Investing Principles

- Even investors with the worst possible market timing have generated positive returns over the long term if they remained invested, as illustrated by analysis of returns following historically poor entry points.

- Stock market returns exist precisely because risk exists, and attempting to avoid all risk by staying out of markets leads to missed gains.

- ETFs are positioned as tools that simplify the investing journey by offering diversified baskets at varying levels of granularity, from single-sector exposures to whole-market access in one trade.

### ETFs Versus Mutual Funds

- Both ETFs and mutual funds offer diversification, but ETFs are often more tax-efficient, making them particularly advantageous for investments held outside of tax-advantaged accounts such as 401ks or IRAs.

- ETFs frequently offer lower fees than mutual funds, though this is not universally the case.

- Active management within an ETF can take the form of fundamental stock-picking by portfolio managers with deep sector knowledge, or systematic quantitative investing using data signals to identify mispriced or under-owned opportunities.

- Index-based ETFs provide broad market or sector exposure without active decision-making, suited to investors comfortable with owning the full market or a defined segment of it.

### Dividend Treatment in ETFs

- ETFs function as pass-through vehicles, meaning all dividends collected from underlying holdings must be distributed to ETF shareholders.

- Dividends are aggregated across all dividend-paying stocks within the fund over a given period and paid out as a single distribution on either a quarterly or semi-annual schedule.

- Investors can view the dividend payment schedule in advance, providing transparency and predictability around income distributions.

### AI Spending Versus Earnings Dynamics

- A widening distinction exists between AI companies that are writing the checks, meaning those spending heavily on infrastructure and compute, and those that are cashing the checks by generating earnings from AI demand.

- Questions persist around whether the large capital expenditures being made by hyperscalers and infrastructure builders will translate into commensurate returns, creating performance divergence within the AI universe.

- Memory chip companies have seen particularly strong performance in 2026 as agentic AI has grown in importance, driven by scarcity of materials required for the build-out.

- Active AI funds are positioned as better able to identify winners and losers within the AI theme, given that not all AI-labeled companies have performed well due to the spending-versus-earning dynamic.
