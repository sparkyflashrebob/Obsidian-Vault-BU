# Voice, Tone & Style Guidelines for Talks, Blogs, and Meeting Scripts (Andy / Living Mindfully)

1. **Non-Prescriptive, Gentle Tone (No Commands)**:
   - Never give orders or imperative commands to the audience or reader (avoid "Stop gaslighting yourself", "Catch yourself", "Do this", "Don't do that").
   - Use gentle, inviting, observational phrasing instead: "There is no need to gaslight yourself", "We can notice...", "We can remind ourselves...", "Holding...".
   - Use the inclusive human "we" rather than lecturing "you" ("we catch ourselves", "we experience", "we tend to add on").

2. **Authentic Natural Vocabulary & Phrasing**:
   - For physical tension in the stomach, say "our stomach is tight or knotted up" (do not use dramatic or cliché phrases like "clenched like a fist").
   - Use simple, direct everyday phrasing (say "have been feeling lately", not "feeling in our bones"; say "hit me personally", not dramatic idioms like "struck me right between the eyes").
   - Frame difficulty as things "entering our senses" (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind) rather than "landing on physical resources".
   - Ground teachings in everyday life (the checkout counter, utility bills, rude interactions, difficult news, bodily aches) without limiting difficulty solely to finances.

3. **Core Thematic Pillars & Canonical Integrity**:
   - **Canonical & Contextual Authenticity (No Forced Modern Templates)**: Never force modern contemporary teachers (e.g., Tara Brach) or modern pedagogical models (e.g., "The Two Wings of Practice") onto classical suttas or Dhamma talks where they do not belong. Honor the canonical integrity and specific lineage/context of the text. Modern teachers or external frameworks should ONLY be referenced when analyzing their specific talks or when explicitly requested.
   - **The Two Arrows (*Sallatha Sutta*)**: Associate the raw sensory impact of life as the First Arrow, and the added mental reaction, aversion, storytelling, and proliferation directly as **dukkha** (the Second Arrow).
   - **Compassion (*Karuṇā*)**: Only raise compassion as a theme when it is directly called out or doctrinally integral to the sutta/talk at hand (e.g., the Brahmavihāras, bodhicitta, or explicit contextual prompts). Do not artificially insert compassion themes into technical meditative or analytical discourses.
   - **Conditionality (*Idappaccayatā*)**: Somatic contractions (tightness in the belly) are natural biological survival reactions to perceived threat, not personal or moral failures.
   - **Animals vs. Humans in Threat Response**: In nature, a deer runs from danger, shakes off the shock when safe, and goes back to grazing. Humans keep the threat alive in thought: *"We think about the tiger when there is no tiger."*
   - **The Habit of Stress & Skillful Energy**: Stress easily becomes a chronic habit of the mind. Practice offers immediate real-time relief, but the deeper long-term fruit is rewiring the habit into peace—freeing up our vital energy so we can deal with life in a skillful, grounded manner.

4. **Formatting & Document Workflow**:
   - **Markdown Tables over ASCII Art**: Whenever presenting multi-step sequences, protocols, comparisons, or summaries—especially for Markdown (.md) files—always use clean, responsive standard Markdown tables instead of fixed-width ASCII box-drawing art, ensuring they paste cleanly into blog posts, Obsidian, and publishing platforms.
   - **Preserve User Direct Edits**: When updating or revising existing documents (blog posts, Dhamma talks, essays), always inspect the document first (in the Obsidian vault and workspace) to identify and preserve any direct edits, examples, and phrasing adjustments made by the author.
   - **Transcript Paragraphing & Timestamping Standard (No Mid-Sentence Breaks, Natural Pauses Only)**:
     * **Natural Paragraphs over Fragmented Lines**: Do NOT stamp every single subtitle line or 3–5 second Whisper segment. Group speech into coherent, natural paragraphs representing complete thoughts and sentences (~60–120 words or 45–90 seconds of speech).
     * **No Mid-Sentence Timestamps**: Never break mid-sentence with a timestamp or speaker label. Complete thoughts before any timestamp marker. Paragraph boundaries must occur ONLY after complete sentences or natural breath/clause pauses—never after prepositions, articles, conjunctions, linking verbs, or incomplete clauses.
     * **Speaker Label Economy (Only Once or on Speaker Change)**: Indicate the speaker only ONCE at the start of a discourse or section (e.g. `**Ajahn Pasanno:**`), and only re-introduce the speaker when the speaker actually changes (e.g. Inquirer vs. Ajahn, or Luang Por Jumnien vs. Joseph Kappel). Do NOT repeat the speaker tag on every paragraph.
     * **Timestamp Placement**: Place exactly one timestamp `[mm:ss]` (or `[hh:mm:ss]`) at the very beginning of each grouped paragraph (e.g., `[00:00] Paragraph text begins here...`).
     * **Remove Duplicate & Unnecessary Data**: Clean up stuttered fragments, repetitive false starts, and fragmented line breaks to yield clean, publication-grade reading prose.
   - **Mandatory Authentic Faster-Whisper Pipeline for All Audio**:
     * **No Synthetic Stubs or Fabricated Transcripts**: Never synthesize, summarize, or place placeholder stubs under full transcript headers.
     * **100% Verbatim Audio Ingestion**: Every single audio talk incorporated into the vault MUST be transcribed directly from source audio using the Faster-Whisper transcription pipeline, ensuring authentic, faithful, publication-grade data.
     * **Audit Trail & Verification**: Transcripts must strictly follow the natural paragraph and timestamping standard so every paragraph maps to real audio timestamps.

   ### A. Spoken & Written Dhamma Talks / Sutta Expositions (`type: dharma-talk-report`)
   *Applies to spoken retreat discourses, evening talks, morning reflections, and written Dhamma essays/books.*
   - **YAML Frontmatter**: Title, speaker/author, date, venue/location setting, sutta reference (if specified/studied), collection/Nikāya, type (`dharma-talk-report`), status, transcription_engine (e.g. `faster-whisper (base.en)`), transcription_date (e.g. `2026-11-06`), transcription_quality (`verbatim-complete`), and comprehensive doctrinal and concept tags.

     - **Location**: Specific modern Dhamma talk recording venue/platform setting (e.g. Angela Center, Abhayagiri Temple, Spirit Rock, IMS, etc.).

     - **People Involved**: The speaker/Buddha, interlocutors (e.g. Ānanda, Sāriputta), senior monastics, and lay assembly/audience.
     - **Theme of the Dharma Talk**: The central contemplative, psychological, and meditative focus of the teaching.
     - **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: A distilled, clear executive summary of foundational teachings.
     - **Expanded Version of the Teachings**: Detailed exploration, doctrinal depth, somatic resonance, and practical application.
   - **Topic Breakdown with Supporting Tables**: Methodical thematic exploration with clean Markdown comparison tables for sequential protocols, practices, or lists.
   - **Stories & Anecdotes Offered**: Dedicated section capturing every narrative, historical sutta story, personal teacher anecdote, and practical illustration shared.
   - **Notable Quotes**: Verbatim, impactful quotes by the speaker, accompanied by exact audio timestamps (`[mm:ss]`) or page numbers.
