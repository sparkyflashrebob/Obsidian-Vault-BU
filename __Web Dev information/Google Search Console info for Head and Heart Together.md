Since we just recently set up your **Sitemap** and **robots.txt**, your foundation for search engine discovery is solid! However, Google Search Console (GSC) isn't just a "set it and forget it" tool.

To actively improve your outreach and visibility using Google Search Console, here is what you should do next:

### 1. Actively Submit Your Sitemap

Even though we added your sitemap to 

robots.txt, you should still officially submit it inside GSC.

- **How:** Go to GSC -> Click **Sitemaps** (on the left menu) -> Enter 
    
    sitemap.xml in the "Add a new sitemap" bar -> Click **Submit**.
- **Why:** This tells Google exactly where to find your map and GSC will give you a specific report on how many pages it successfully discovered and indexed from it.

### 2. Use the "URL Inspection" Tool for New Blogs

Whenever you upload a new blog post (or update a page like your Calendar), don't wait for Google to find it.

- **How:** Copy the URL of your new blog post, paste it into the top search bar in GSC ("Inspect any URL"), and click **"Request Indexing"**.
- **Why:** This essentially "pings" Google and forces their crawlers to look at your fresh content, often getting it onto search results within hours rather than days or weeks.

### 3. Mine the "Performance" Report for Content Ideas (The Goldmine)

Once GSC gathers a few weeks of data, the **Search Results** (under the "Performance" tab) becomes your best friend.

- **Look at your "Queries":** This shows you exactly what words people typed into Google to find Head & Heart Together.
- **Find the "Low Hanging Fruit":** Look for search queries where you are getting high **Impressions** (people are seeing you in search results) but a low **CTR** (Click-Through Rate).
- **Action:** To improve your CTR for those queries, slightly tweak your page's 
    
    ```
    <title>
    ```
    
     or 
    
    ```
    <meta name="description">
    ```
    
     to make it more enticing for someone to click. Or, write a brand new blog post centered entirely around that popular search query.

### 4. Monitor Page Experience & Core Web Vitals

Google heavily favors websites that load fast and look good on mobile.

- **How:** Check the **Experience** tabs on the left side of GSC (Core Web Vitals & Mobile Usability).
- **Why:** If Google detects that a button is too small to tap on a phone, or an image takes too long to load, they will flag it here. Fixing these errors directly boosts your search ranking.

### 5. Consider "Structured Data" (Advanced)

Since you host weekly gatherings (Events/Google Meets) and write Articles, you can eventually add "Schema Markup" (Structured Data) to your website code. GSC tracks this.

- **Why:** This turns your standard link on Google into a "Rich Snippet", meaning Google might display the dates and times of your upcoming gatherings directly on the search results page before someone even clicks on your website!
