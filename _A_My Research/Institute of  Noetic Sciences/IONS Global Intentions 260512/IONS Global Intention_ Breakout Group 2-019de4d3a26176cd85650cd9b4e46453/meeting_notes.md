# IONS Global Intention: Breakout Group 2 - May, 01

### Global Intention: The Power of Alignment Breakout Group 2
📅 May 1, 2026
👥 Andy McGuire, Speaker 2 (Tim), Speaker 3 (Jackson)
### Outline
### The Power of Collective Intention
- A group discussion was opened around the question of what becomes possible when many people focus their attention and intention together toward a shared vision of the world.
- A historical example was raised involving transcendental meditators who collaborated with the Washington D.C. police department approximately 40 years ago, with the aim of reducing crime through collective intention.
- Calls for police assistance reportedly dropped to around 78% of normal during that period, offering a small but meaningful indicator of what focused collective intention might achieve at a larger scale.

### Collective Intention in Crisis Response
- The September 11 attacks were cited as an example of a moment when a globally shared emotional response — grief and care — could be felt and even measured as an energy field.
- Community workers deployed after the attacks were noted to have learned, through experience rather than formal training, to release their own assumptions about their role.
- Rather than providing therapy, these workers focused on pointing people toward their own internal strengths, which was described as a particularly meaningful and effective approach to trauma support.

### Challenges to Achieving a Shared Global Vision
- Andy expressed measured pessimism about humanity's current capacity to sustain a shared collective vision, noting that while group experiences such as music and communal gatherings demonstrate the uplifting power of shared focus, humanity's attention appears more fragmented than unified.
- Collective moments of shared intention, such as those following major tragedies, were described as fleeting, with people quickly returning to personal concerns and individual priorities.
- The central challenge identified was not whether collective intention is possible, but how to sustain it and make it a broader cultural priority.

### The Role of Fear, Bias and Self-Interest in Blocking Collective Progress
- Avoidance of painful topics — such as death, loss, or systemic injustice — was described as a natural human tendency rooted in the desire to avoid discomfort rather than in deliberate conspiracy.
- Confirmation bias and cognitive filters were highlighted as mechanisms through which individuals and groups selectively interpret facts and truths according to their own preferences.
- Greed was reframed as an expression of deep-seated fear and instinct around survival, operating at both individual and collective levels.
- People's desire to be accepted and seen as right within their social groups was identified as a driver of entrenched thinking, preventing honest engagement with larger shared problems.
- Insecurity was raised as a related force, with examples including the pharmaceutical industry and the military-industrial complex as systems that profit from and reinforce manufactured rather than tangible security needs.

### Indigenous Worldviews as an Alternative Model
- Western capitalism was contrasted with indigenous models of economy and community, in which resources and responsibilities are understood as shared rather than individually accumulated.
- Indigenous thought was described as more grounded, healthier, and more literally connected to the earth, offering a different and potentially more sustainable framework for collective living.

### The Concept of Hitting a Collective Bottom as a Catalyst for Change
- A parallel was drawn to recovery culture, where meaningful change typically does not occur until a person or group reaches a breaking point or "bottom."
- Global warming was noted as an issue that has not yet produced sufficient collective urgency, suggesting that the connection between human behavior and planetary consequences has not been deeply felt by enough people.
- American affluence and arrogance were identified as factors that may delay the country's reckoning, as material comfort insulates people from the consequences of systemic failures.
- The diminishing global standing of the United States was framed as a potentially constructive development, one that might encourage greater humility and openness to collective planetary responsibility.

### Emerging Signs of Community Resistance and Collective Action
- Grassroots community efforts in cities such as Minneapolis and in Oregon were cited as examples of people organizing effectively around shared values, particularly in resistance to immigration enforcement infrastructure.
- These local mobilizations were described as generating a tangible collective energy, and were seen as early indicators of a broader wave of community-driven action.
- Growing public resistance to policies perceived as harmful to families and communities was identified as a sign that people are beginning to reassess the consequences of their political and social choices.

### Strengthening Global Governance and International Cooperation
- Andy expressed a wish for a stronger United Nations, particularly reform of the Security Council, where a single veto can block action on unjust situations and paralyze the organization.
- International economic realignment, with countries forming new agreements and coalitions in response to current global disruptions, was noted as a positive development that may gradually shift the balance of global influence.
- The question of how long generational change might take before these shifts meaningfully affect the United States and its population was left open.

### Next Steps
- The group affirmed the value of continued dialogue in spaces like this one as a means of strengthening collective intention and shared vision.
- Attention was directed toward supporting and amplifying grassroots community efforts as a practical expression of collective action.
- Ongoing reflection on how to move beyond fear, bias, and self-interest toward a more unified and planet-centered set of priorities was identified as a continuing focus.


### Key Points
- Purpose of the meeting: exploring what becomes possible when many people collectively focus their attention and intention toward a shared vision for the world. 
- Transcendental meditators collaborating with Washington D.C. police reportedly reduced calls for assistance to 78% of normal, offered as a small but hopeful indicator of collective intention's measurable impact. 
- Post-9/11 community outreach workers found success by releasing their own agendas and redirecting people toward their own internal strengths as a foundation for trauma recovery. 
- Andy expressed cautious pessimism, noting that collective focus exists but tends toward division rather than shared vision, citing music and clubbing as examples of positive but fleeting group uplift. 
- 9/11 was cited as evidence that global collective emotion is real and even measurable as an energy field, but such unity is fleeting as people quickly return to personal concerns. 
- Fear and avoidance—not necessarily conspiracy—were identified as key reasons people resist confronting uncomfortable collective truths, with the brain filling gaps through confirmation bias. 
- Self-interest and greed, rooted in instinctual survival fear, were seen as core drivers that override collective priorities, reinforced by individual and group-level confirmation biases. 
- Seeking truth fearlessly was raised as a personal practice that expands perspective, contrasted with the more common tendency to defend existing beliefs in order to feel accepted and avoid being wrong. 
- Indigenous economic models—based on sharing and mutual support rather than accumulation—were highlighted as a healthier, more grounded alternative to Western capitalism. 
- Insecurity was linked to systemic misdirection of resources, with the pharmaceutical industry and military-industrial complex cited as examples of prioritizing imagined over tangible security. 
- Drawing on recovery circles, the group agreed that collective change rarely happens until a "bottom" is hit, with global warming noted as a crisis that has not yet triggered that threshold. 
- American arrogance and affluence were identified as barriers to reaching that collective bottom, with the U.S.'s declining superpower status seen as a potential catalyst for broader global alignment. 
- Grassroots community resistance—such as successful opposition to ICE detention centers in Oregon and Minneapolis—was cited as encouraging evidence of collective intention producing real outcomes. 
- Visible consequences of divisive policies are forcing people to reconsider their positions, with hope expressed that this mirrors how social norms around racism shifted when public acceptance was withdrawn.
