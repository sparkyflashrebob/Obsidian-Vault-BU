The "one company" referenced in the video, DuPont, is described as having poisoned the planet through the widespread use and disposal of chemicals known as PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances), particularly PFOA (also known as C8) and later GenX [1-5].

Here's a breakdown of how this unfolded:

* **The Origin of Teflon and C8:**

  + In 1936, DuPont sought a safer alternative to toxic and flammable gases used in refrigerators [6, 7]. Their chemist, Roy J. Plunkett, accidentally discovered polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), later trademarked as Teflon, a seemingly indestructible and highly inert substance due to its strong carbon-fluorine bonds [8-12].
  + Teflon was crucial for the Manhattan Project because it resisted corrosive uranium hexafluoride [13-15].
  + To produce Teflon efficiently and safely after an explosion at their plant, DuPont needed a way to dissipate heat during the polymerization process [16, 17]. In 1951, they purchased a special acid from 3M called PFOA, or C8, which acted as a dispersant, allowing the reaction to happen evenly in water [2, 18-20].
  + After the war, Teflon became widely used in everyday products like non-stick pans, stain-resistant carpets (like 3M's Scotchgard), waterproof clothing (Gore-Tex), and medical implants [21-24]. C8 and similar chemicals were used in many of these products [23].
* **Discovery of Harm and Concealment:**

  + As early as 1961, DuPont's in-house scientists tested C8 on rats and found that even small amounts caused abnormal liver growth and that higher doses were lethal, harming organs like the stomach, intestine, brain, lungs, and pancreas [25, 26]. Similar toxic effects were seen in dogs in 1965 and monkeys, some of whom died [27]. These studies were not shared with the scientific community or the public [27].
  + C8's chemical structure, with stable carbon-fluorine bonds and a resemblance to fatty acids, meant it was incredibly persistent in the environment for decades and could bioaccumulate in the body, potentially disrupting biological systems [26, 28]. It was described as a "ticking time bomb" due to its toxic, persistent, and bioaccumulative nature [26].
  + In 1975, independent researchers found organic fluorine, which was not naturally occurring, in the blood of people across the US [29, 30]. 3M, approached by these researchers, initially denied knowledge, but their own comparisons confirmed their chemicals, like PFOA, were in people's blood [30, 31].
  + Both 3M and DuPont checked their workers' blood and found C8 contamination levels 1,000 times higher than in the general population, with many workers showing signs of liver disease [31].
  + Despite this knowledge, DuPont continued to dump almost 10 tons of C8 into the Ohio River annually and piled up thousands of tons of C8 sludge in landfills, like the one next to Earl Tennant's farm in Parkersburg, West Virginia [31, 32].
* **Environmental Contamination and Health Impacts:**

  + Earl Tennant's cows were mysteriously dying, suffering from tumors and black teeth, with white foam coming from a discharge pipe from DuPont's Washington Works factory landfill [33, 34]. The chemical causing this, C8, was not listed on DuPont's permits [35].
  + A cancer study in rats in the early 1980s confirmed PFOA caused testicular tumors [32].
  + DuPont's own tests revealed C8 in the public water supply around Washington Works [36]. In 1984, they concluded that economically attractive alternatives to C8 for their business were unavailable [36].
  + DuPont scientists set their own "safe" drinking water level for C8 at one part per billion (ppb) [37]. However, the wastewater leaching from their landfill into Earl's creek was found to be 1,600 ppb [38].
  + A medical study initiated in 2005 involving 70,000 people around Washington Works confirmed a probable link between C8 exposure and six human diseases: thyroid disease, testicular cancer, kidney cancer, high cholesterol, decreased immune system response to vaccines and infections, and decreased growth in infants [39-41]. The risk for conditions like kidney cancer could double with higher C8 blood levels [42].
  + By 2000, 100% of blood samples from thousands of Americans across the country tested positive for C8, with an average of five parts per billion [39].
  + PFAS are now found globally, including in untouched wilderness and even in rainwater on the Tibetan Plateau, due to their persistence and entry into the water cycle [43, 44]. They are also in food packaging and firefighting foams, which can seep into soil and groundwater [44-46].
* **Continued Problem and Regulatory Challenges:**

  + Although DuPont was pressured to phase out C8 by 2017 and paid over $600 million to victims, they spun off their Teflon business into Chemours, which began using GenX [3, 47]. GenX, a shorter-chained PFAS, was later found to cause the exact same tumors in rats as PFOA, and its higher mobility means it could contaminate larger areas [3, 48].
  + This is part of a larger issue with over 14,000 different manmade chemicals, generically termed PFAS, which are persistent "forever chemicals" [4, 5, 43].
  + Companies like DuPont and 3M knew how dangerous these chemicals were decades ago but did not inform the public or regulators, leading to widespread global contamination that is only now being fully understood [49].

Here's how it happened:

* **The Creation of Teflon and the Need for PFOA (C8)**: In 1936, DuPont sought a non-toxic and non-flammable chemical for refrigerators [7]. Their chemist, Roy J. Plunkett, accidentally discovered polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), which they trademarked as Teflon in 1944 [8, 9, 12]. Teflon was highly valued for its non-reactive and indestructible qualities, particularly for its use in the Manhattan Project to resist corrosive uranium hexafluoride [10, 11, 13-15]. However, the process of making Teflon involved highly exothermic reactions that could cause explosions [16, 17]. To control these reactions and dissipate heat, DuPont purchased PFOA (C8) from 3M in 1951. C8 acted as a dispersant, allowing the polymerization of TFE into Teflon to happen safely in water, and it also facilitated spraying Teflon as a coating [2, 18-21].
* **Widespread Use and Environmental Release**: After World War II, Teflon became immensely popular and was used to coat a vast array of everyday products, including non-stick pans, stain-resistant carpets and sprays (like 3M's Scotchgard), waterproof clothing (Gore-Tex), medical implants, and even food packaging [21-24, 45]. Many of these products contained C8 and similar chemicals [23]. As DuPont ramped up production at their Washington Works plant in West Virginia, they began dumping almost 10 tons of C8 into the Ohio River each year and piling thousands of tons of C8 sludge in landfills, which then leached into nearby creeks [31, 32, 34]. These chemicals were also used in firefighting foams, particularly at military bases and airports, where fire drills led to their seepage into surrounding soil and groundwater [44, 46].
* **Known Toxicity and Concealment**:
  + As early as 1961, DuPont's own in-house scientists conducted studies on rats, finding that C8 caused abnormal liver growth and was lethal at higher doses, also causing injury to the stomach, intestine, brain, lungs, and pancreas [25, 26]. Similar toxic effects were confirmed in dogs in 1965 and in monkeys, some of whom died [27].
  + DuPont knew that C8 was incredibly stable due to its carbon-fluorine bonds, meaning it wouldn't break down in the environment for decades and could bioaccumulate in the body [28].
  + Despite evidence mounting and internal concerns, these findings were not shared with the scientific community or the public [27, 31]. In the early 1980s, rat studies confirmed PFOA caused testicular tumors [32].
  + When C8 was found in public water supplies near their plant, DuPont officials met in 1984 but concluded that safer, economically attractive alternatives were not available [36]. They then established their own "safe dose" for drinking water, which was at the lowest level they could detect [37].
* **Harm to Living Beings and Global Contamination**:
  + The chemicals poisoned Earl Tennant's cows, causing them to waste away, develop tumors, and have black teeth [33].
  + In 1975, researchers found organic fluorine, which is man-made, in the blood of people across the US [29, 30]. 3M, after initially denying knowledge, confirmed that their chemicals were indeed in the blood of people across the United States [30, 31]. Both DuPont and 3M found their own workers had C8 levels 1,000 times higher than the general population, with many showing signs of liver disease [31].
  + By 2000, 100% of blood samples from thousands of Americans tested positive for C8 [39].
  + A major medical study completed in 2013 confirmed a probable link between C8 exposure and six human diseases: thyroid disease, testicular cancer, kidney cancer, high cholesterol, decreased immune system response to vaccines and infections, and decreased growth in infants [40, 41]. The true risk, especially considering people who died from exposure were not always included, could be even higher [42].
  + PFAS, including C8, are now found in virtually every living creature, from polar bears to fish, and have reached "planetary saturation levels," meaning they are present in rainwater even in remote areas like the Tibetan Plateau [43, 44].

In essence, DuPont created and widely used chemicals for their profitable Teflon business, despite knowing for decades about the severe health risks and environmental persistence of these chemicals, leading to widespread contamination of the planet and its inhabitants [1, 24, 27, 43, 49, 51].
