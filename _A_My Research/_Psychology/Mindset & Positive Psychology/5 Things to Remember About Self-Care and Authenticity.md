# 5 Things to Remember About Self-Care and Authenticity

All of us are born into families who expect—or need—us to be something. We might be expected to be a carer, we might be expected to put up with abuse, or we might find ourselves singled out as the scapegoat in order to allow our dysfunctional family to operate in the way that certain people need it to operate.

Part of the way in which we meet other people’s expectations and fulfil the roles into which we might be pushed is to ignore our own needs. Becoming more authentic requires that you begin to become aware of the layers of expectations that have been placed on you and to strip back those which conflict with what you feel you really need and want in order to become happier and more fulfilled. Integral to this process is a commitment to taking care of yourself.

Taking care of yourself involves stating—to yourself and to those around you—that you place enough value on yourself as a person to identify what it is you need to do to look after yourself and to prioritise those needs above the needs and expectations of other people. Taking care of yourself is the fundamental building block from which you can become more authentic, flowing from that point of communicating that you are a precious human being who needs to be looked after. And, importantly, thatyouare the person who knows how to look after you best.

If you fail to take care of yourself in the most fundamental ways, your attempts to be more authentic might just feel as if you’re putting another mask on—the mask ofauthenticity. Let’s imagine, for example, that as part of your journey towards authenticity, you are making an effort to say no to people’s demands and speak your mind about issues that are important to you. These things are great, but if you go home and continue to manage thestressinvolved in putting these changes into place by opening a bottle of wine or binge eating, what you’re essentially doing is engaging in self-destructive, damaging behaviours in order to support your moves towards authenticity. You're acting in a contradictory way—on the one hand communicating to others that you matter as a person, whilst on the other hand, communicating to yourself that you don’t matter enough to look after yourself in a healthy, nurturing manner.

Without working out, and working towards, what your body and mind need to feel safe and cared for, you run the risk of “moving towards authenticity” becoming an approval-seeking exercise that is judged more in terms of other people’s responses to you than how you feel deep within.

Establishing what it is that nurtures and cares for you also provides the opportunity for you to step back and look at yourself as an individual, accepting your needs and wants in all their uniqueness. In this process, you’ll learn that there is no one-size-fits-all approach to looking after yourself. What feels right and true to you will be very different from what feels right and true for someone else.

If looking after and prioritising yourself is new to you, here are some things to consider:

1. It takes effort.

It’s very easy for all of us to fall back into old ways of being, especially if we’ve had the message hammered into us over the years that we need to put other people first or that we should be spending time focusing on achievement. It takes a while to rewrite old scripts we tell ourselves. Find ways of committing to putting in self-care, such as timetabling a session with yourself and writing down why it is so important that you do this.

2. Self-care versus self-destruction.

Self-care can be anything that you make it. Sometimes that might mean having a duvet day and eating ice cream or having a boxset binge. There’s nothing wrong with either of those choices, if you keep them in moderation.

But when “self-care” consistently means drinkingalcohol, eating junk food, spending money unwisely online, or any other choices which are damaging to your health and wellbeing, you need to be aware that you could be slipping into self-destruction. When you’re putting self-care measures into place, ask yourself the question: “How will I feel as a result of this?”

3. Balance.

We all tend to have behaviours which are unhealthy and are driven by beliefs about ourselves and the world around us. Self-care is an opportunity to balance out some of our more unhealthy or destructive behaviours. For instance, if you work long hours and have a tendency to become exhausted, self-care for you might be forcing yourself to quit work and have an afternoon lie down. If you’ve got a tendency to be unproductive and are feelingdepressedabout a lack of direction, self-care might involve setting a target for a creative project. How can you care for yourself in a way which will restore some balance to your life?

4. Relationships.

Taking care of ourselves means looking after ourselves in the context of our relationships with others. Self-care involves making time to be around people who strengthen you and make you feel good about yourself. It also means making time to spend with yourself.

At the same time, caring for you requires settingboundarieswith regard to spending time with people who sap your energy or make you feel bad about yourself. Do you feel obliged to spend time with someone just because they’re your parent,sibling, or child even though you know you’ll feel drained and upset afterward? How can you reset the balance so that this time is spent on meeting your own needs instead?

5. Engaging in healthy activities.

Self-care isn’t all about eating fruit and vegetables, exercising, andmeditation—for instance, reading a book might be as valid an activity. But it is important to bring in behaviours which are going to actively contribute to a healthier mind and body. What lifestyle changes can you make to nurture your physical and mental health? Do you need more sleep? Could you cut down on alcohol? How can you fit more exercise into your life?

Take a while to imagine what self-care would look and feel like for you and decide what small steps you can take towards implementing it. This will lead to bigger changes in how you feel about yourself.
