The method works for general learning because of how the brain naturally operates:

The brain is a pattern machine, not a hard drive:

The brain functions as a "pattern recognition and pattern generation system" rather than a storage device [1]. You learn any subject best by finding regularities, building mental models to predict what will happen, and adjusting those models when your predictions fail [1].

Active engagement beats passive reception:

When you passively listen to a lecture or read a textbook, your brain stays comfortable with its current model and can easily fall into an "illusion of understanding" [1, 2]. Active engagement—where you are forced to make informed guesses and test them—forces the brain to actively update its models [1].

Explaining reveals knowledge gaps:

The practice of explaining a concept out loud works universally because if you can't actually use a pattern or explain an idea in your own words, you don't truly understand it [2, 3]. Attempting to use the information reveals the gaps in your knowledge, which is "the first step toward filling them" [3].
