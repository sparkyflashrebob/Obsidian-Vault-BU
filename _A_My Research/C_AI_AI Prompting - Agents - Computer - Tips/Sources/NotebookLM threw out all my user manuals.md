NotebookLM is now heralded as the ultimate study buddy, a research assistant capable of digesting dense academic papers and synthesizing them into succinct summaries. It’s the tool of choice for students drowning in coursework or researchers handling large amounts of statistical data in mixed media formats. However, pigeonholing this tool as strictly an academic aid sells it short. I’ve found that its ability to parse complex documentation makes it the perfect solution for a much more mundane, yet universally frustrating problem involving textual matter: managing user manuals for gadgets and appliances I own.
I recently purged my drawers of those thick, multi-language booklets and replaced them with a single, digital notebook. I've found that NotebookLM excels at handling product troubleshooting and answering those simple how-to questions that would typically send us down a rabbit hole of SEO-optimized spam. By supplying the correct source material — like user manuals and support forum links—you can turn NotebookLM into a personalized support genius that remembers your washing machine model from the get-go.

#### Feeding the devourer of documents

##### No restriction on content types

At its core, NotebookLM excels at digesting documents. It takes the heavy lifting out of reading, allowing you to query specific information without wading through hundreds of pages of text. It helped me understand complex sources, making it a valuable research tool for anyone dealing with large volumes of information. While marketing focuses on research, the mechanism is indifferent to the subject matter. It processes a user manual for a blender with the same rigor applied to a treatise on quantum mechanics.
Therein lies the beauty that I'm not bound to use it as an academic tool. So long as the file type is supported, my notebook can be about practically anything I fancy. I can feed it the dry, technical user manual or often poorly translated literature that comes in the box with electronics, and that becomes the AI's sole source of truthful answers.

#### The conundrum of finding answers on the fly

##### Winging it just doesn't work

I spend a lot of time with my gadgets, and like most people, I prefer familiarizing myself with their controls on the fly. It's a risky way to live, but I get by with many frantic Google searches about how to lock my camera’s autofocus on a moving subject in the middle of a jungle safari, missing the shot while I scroll through forums. Even more mundanely, I once spent an embarrassing amount of time figuring out how my fridge goes into defrost mode the same day I had to clean it. Relying on memory or intuition lets me down when the stakes (or the mess) are highest.
One day, on a whim, I added user manuals for most of my gadgets to a new NotebookLM notebook. Now, instead of hunting for a physical booklet I threw away or navigating a brand's slow website on my phone, I just query the notebook. I can ask specific advice like, "How do I set up back button focus?" or "What does error code E4 mean?" and get an immediate answer based solely on the documentation I uploaded. It saves a lot of time otherwise spent reading multiple articles from random people who might not even own the same version of the product I have.

I didn't know what I was missing out on until I started using NotebookLM
[3](https://www.xda-developers.com/author/parth-shah/) By [Sumukh Rao](https://www.xda-developers.com/author/sumukh-rao/)Aug 8, 2025

#### Google AI Summaries in Search aren't all that helpful

##### Finding sources is easy, too

Sure, AI summaries in Google Search are great, but they are vastly detail-deficient and generic. If I search for "how to fix the dishwasher draining issue," I get general advice that applies to most dishwashers but perhaps not mine. To get specific advice, I must add the product model number in the search query. This number can run into tens of characters, and I may not even have it on hand immediately. As such, I end up reading several unhelpful articles, watching videos that bury the lead, and reading summaries before stumbling upon something even remotely helpful.
NotebookLM notebooks, in comparison, only need to be populated with the model number once. When I upload the manual for a Sony A7M3, the AI knows that's the context for every question I ask in that notebook. I don't need to type out the serial number every time I have a question. Getting the sources is usually the easy part. I found the source using a simple Google search followed by " filetype:pdf " to filter results directly to the documentation. If that doesn't work, I submit a comprehensive video about the product as a source, usually from a prominent and reliable YouTuber. If all else fails, when I have an obscure device on hand, I manually take photos of the physical user manual, compile it into a PDF on my phone, and upload that. It’s worth noting that NotebookLM's own source discovery tool isn't the most effective at finding these specific technical documents. I often landed on files for other locales that would lack region-specific product features and detail.
Since I can stuff in 50 sources per notebook, that's at least 25 different home appliances and gadgets, assuming there's more than one document I'll need per model. I usually prefer uploading the PDF directly because it feels more permanent, but I've found linking to the parent domain where support material is hosted usually works just as well. For a single household, one or two notebooks are usually enough to cover everything from the thermostat to the toaster oven.

#### NotebookLM manuals still have limitations

##### A few quirks

The only limitation to this method I see is being constrained to the manufacturer's own wisdom about the product. I might not get to see the community-curated hacksmith engineering solutions to everyday problems, like percussive maintenance for fixing rattly parts. In its current state, I'm confident NotebookLM won't hallucinate a fix that isn't in the manual. Thankfully, I've not lost my ability to tell when a user manual is needlessly indirect, and for 99% of error codes and maintenance, the official book is exactly what I needed.

##### No more "smash that Like button"

Ultimately, making a user manual so digestible and easy to access is valuable in the field where you need reliable answers immediately. I don't have the time to watch a YouTuber segue to a VPN sponsor for two minutes just to find out which button resets the filter light, nor do I want a recipe blog to pepper me with video adverts while I'm trying to fix my oven. NotebookLM cuts through the noise. It gives me the answer, purely the answer, and lets me get back to my life.
