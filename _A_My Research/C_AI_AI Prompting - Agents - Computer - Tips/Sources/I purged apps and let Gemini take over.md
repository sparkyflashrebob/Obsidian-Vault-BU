Every day, even before I brew my coffee, I start my morning by jumping on my phone to catch up on messages, emails, and notifications, along with my to-do list for the day and what I need to finish.

This kicks off my day, and I have grown used to the routine. Over time, however, I started feeling inundated with all these tasks.

It feels exhausting to juggle multiple apps just to get my schedule together and plan ahead.

That is no longer the case after discovering what Gemini and its extensions can do.

Gemini alone has replaced most of these planning tasks, saving me a huge chunk of time, and I even stopped paying for some app subscriptions.

I get reminders and to-do checklists directly in Gemini

Stop scouring through separate calendar and note apps

Google Pixel 9 Pro XL home screen with to do microsoft and todoist apps showing app info

While I love setting reminders for everything from a simple grocery run to paying bills, I hate the tedious chore of opening a note app and jotting everything down, especially when I am tied up with work.

Since Gemini arrived, I have started assigning most of these everyday or lightweight tasks to Google's chatbot.

With extension support for Calendar, Keep, and Tasks, I only have to chat with Gemini to manage these reminders.

I can pull up upcoming calendar entries and to-dos, or filter out only the most urgent items using brief commands or voice input.

Plus, I can manage events and lists inside Gemini without opening the standalone apps.

Gemini pulling out Google Keep Notes on Samsung Galaxy Z Flip 7

Gemini pulling out Google Calendar events on Samsung Galaxy Z Flip 7

I even started unloading old to-do and tracker apps I used to rely on, including Microsoft To Do and Todoist. The latter locks its advanced features behind a paywall.

Gemini fixes the biggest bottleneck that frustrated me with these apps: fragmentation and the inefficiency of managing them separately.

The only drawback with Gemini is that I need to be online at the time. Given that I mostly work from home, it remains a solid all-in-one task tracker.

I turned Gemini Spark into an inbox assistant

I stopped checking email apps manually

Google Pixel 9 Pro XL smartphone on Gemini Spark splash screen

I have also started adding Gemini Spark to the mix and have been testing it for a week now.

So far, I have learned how to adapt it to my personal life and work, and I have been thoroughly impressed.

It acts like the proactive counterpart to Gemini Live, working in the background as long as it is given a clear set of instructions.

When I set it up, it quickly learned my general background, including my job and what I do for a living. It then suggested routines and tasks to help me use it better.

Right now, I have limited it to serving as an agent for checking my emails and nudging me about important events in my inbox.

What I like best is that it can run multistep actions. For example, I have assigned it to check for messages that need a quick response, estimate a budget for a task I need to complete, or pull an agenda based on an upcoming meeting attached to an email.

On my Samsung Galaxy phone, this reduced the need to open Gmail or the Samsung Email app unless I need to send a message.

It also works around the clock in the background, so I do not need to set a strict schedule or build a custom routine for it.

I am still learning the ropes, but so far, I am amazed at how useful it is as an agentic assistant, reducing how often I need to check my inbox.

Use Gemini scheduled actions as a news aggregator

Skip the clutter of standalone news feeds

Google Pixel 9 Pro XL smartphone with Gemini widget on its home screen

Ever since Google introduced scheduled actions to Gemini, it has quickly become my favorite feature.

It has partly replaced many of the automation routines I used to run on my Android device.

More than that, it is remarkably handy as an alternative to dedicated news aggregators and RSS readers like Google News and Feedly.

Every day, I check for the most interesting tech news from the previous day, along with breaking stories that dropped overnight while I was asleep.

Instead of browsing through a news app, I offloaded this task to Gemini via scheduled actions. It filters relevant, top-tier news for me and delivers it at a preset time.

This also works as a recurring routine, offering a much faster alternative to visiting every website in my feed.

Gemini Spark can handle the same tasks with even better nuance and command filtering.

However, it is locked behind the paid Pro and Ultra plans, whereas standard scheduled actions are available without a premium.

Control my smart home with Gemini

Google Home integration makes managing devices effortless

Google Pixel 9 Pro XL phone with Gemini Google Home and utility apps

At home, I have multiple smart home devices, each tied to its app platform. Managing them is painful, especially with a handful of gadgets across different brands.

That is why it was a relief when Google Home expanded support for these connected platforms.

I can now manage most of my smart home gear inside the Google Home app without opening individual apps.

This also means I can use Gemini's smart home integration to control everything directly.

For example, I stopped opening the Xiaomi Home app to adjust the air purifier or the smart bulb in my room. All I need to do is trigger Gemini via voice or text and give my command.

The same applies to other supported brands, including TCL for our smart air conditioner and TVs, and LG for our other appliances.

I stopped relying on those companion apps entirely, since I can control all my devices from Gemini.

Use Gemini as a personal wellness coach

I let Gemini build my meal plans and generate health insights

Google Pixel 9 Pro XL smartphone with Fitbit Air tracker

For years, I relied on paid health and fitness trackers like MyFitnessPal and Huawei Health. While the free tier handles the basics, essential tools like detailed dietary guides remain locked behind a paywall.

The same goes for the updated Google Health platform, which locks its AI-powered wellness insights behind a premium subscription tier.

Instead of paying recurring fees for health and fitness insights, I customized Gemini to serve as my personal health coach, specifically as my diet planner.

I created a dedicated chat for food and liquid intake, letting Gemini log the data over time and get macros.

I also ask for targeted advice and workout plans to improve muscle gain or cut fat based on the nutrition data I provide.

Its generative capabilities let it create structured charts tailored to my fitness goals.

Using Gemini this way has stopped me from relying on MyFitnessPal and Huawei Health, and even Google Health.

I purged apps and let Gemini take over

Gemini features and app extensions on a Samsung Galaxy Z Flip 7 phone

I have uninstalled or completely abandoned about a dozen or more single-purpose planning apps by offloading their entire workloads onto Gemini and its extensions.

It saves me a ton of time, organizes my chaotic daily routines, and declutters my Android home screen from single-task apps and bloatware. More importantly, this makes completing tasks on my device feel faster and more efficient.
