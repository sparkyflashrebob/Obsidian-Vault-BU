The sources portray the Black radical tradition not merely as a chapter or footnote, but as the **foundational "spine" of American socialism** [1-3]. This tradition insists that racism and capitalism are inextricably linked through "racial capitalism," arguing that the social construct of whiteness was deliberately invented to justify African enslavement and maximize capitalist profits [2, 4-6].

**Deep Roots and Movement Models**
The roots of these socialist and collectivist values do not begin with 19th-century European thinkers, but rather trace back to ancient West African traditions [1, 2, 5, 7]. Within the American context, **Harriet Tubman is highlighted as a defining model for the Black radical and socialist imagination.** Described as a "one-person movement," Tubman built structures of collective liberation under extreme personal danger [1, 2, 8, 9]. Her power was not just individual bravery, but relied on collective knowledge built over generations—understanding the stars, nature, and secret communication systems—demonstrating how oppressed people engineer their own freedom without waiting for the political establishment [8, 10].

**Suppression of the Socialist Civil Rights Movement**
Mainstream political narratives and corporate media have deliberately suppressed, watered down, and appropriated the history of Black radicalism to fit a comfortable, capitalist framework [11-13]. The sources emphasize that **many of the most influential Civil Rights leaders were open socialists and leftists:**

* **Martin Luther King Jr.:** Frequently whitewashed in modern retellings, King actively engaged in class struggle, openly opposed capitalism, and suggested that America move toward democratic socialism [14-16].
* **A. Philip Randolph and Bayard Rustin:** The famous 1963 March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom was masterminded by these two prominent socialists, who deeply understood the need to link racial justice with organized labor [17-19].
* **The Freedom Budget for All Americans:** King, Randolph, and Rustin championed a platform that reads like a modern democratic socialist agenda, demanding full employment, guaranteed living wages, universal medical care, and housing [20-22].
* **Black Women:** Figures like Ella Baker, Fannie Lou Hamer, and Angela Davis functioned as the backbone of the movement. Hamer notably cofounded the Freedom Farm Cooperative, putting socialist principles of worker-owned production into direct practice [23-25].

**The Black Panthers and State Repression**
As the movement progressed into the late 1960s, organizations like the Black Panther Party emerged with explicitly Marxist platforms. Their Ten-Point Program demanded an end to capitalist robbery of Black communities, and their Free Breakfast for Children program served as a practical application of their leftist convictions [26-28]. Because organized Black socialists are viewed as the most terrifying threat to the capitalist ruling class, **the FBI ruthlessly targeted them through COINTELPRO.** Fred Hampton, who was murdered by the state, was specifically targeted because he successfully organized a working-class "Rainbow Coalition" that crossed racial lines to fight capitalism with socialism [29, 30].

**A Framework for Modern Organizing**
Historian Robin D. G. Kelley's work stresses that uncovering this radical imagination is crucial for the modern American left to bridge the struggles against racism and class oppression [31, 32]. For organizations like the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA), fully integrating this tradition means:

* **Serving as the "Textbook":** Socialists must educate the public and correct the historical record—from the self-liberation of Black people during Reconstruction to the anti-capitalist roots of the Civil Rights Movement [33-35].
* **Centering Reparations:** Reparations cannot be treated as a side issue; they are inseparable from self-determination and collective liberation [1, 2, 4, 36].
* **Electing "Battle-Tested" Leaders:** The movement must elevate politicians who have "skin in the game" and a proven track record of standing firm against capitalist coercion, right-wing smears, and corporate lobbying (such as AIPAC) when there is nothing to gain and everything to lose [1, 2, 37-40].
* **Building a Modern Coalition:** Just as earlier radicals proposed the Freedom Budget and the Rainbow Coalition, modern socialists must unite the working class behind universal demands—like Medicare for All and universal housing—to fulfill the ultimate promise of the Black radical tradition [41, 42].
