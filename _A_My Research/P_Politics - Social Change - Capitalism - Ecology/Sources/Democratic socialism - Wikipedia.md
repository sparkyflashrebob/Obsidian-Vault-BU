Communist opposition to Democratic Socialism

https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Democratic\_socialism&action=edit&section=15

On the other end of the spectrum, several

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism

thinkers and groups have strongly criticized democratic socialism and many of its aspects. The roots of this can be found in the writings of Marx and Engels. In

The Principles of Communism

, they describe democratic socialists as either "proletarians who are not yet sufficiently clear about the conditions of the liberation of their class" or "representatives of the

petty bourgeoisie

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty\_bourgeoisie

Their criticisms of "bourgeois socialists" can also be interpreted as a critique of the same tendencies democratic socialists represent today. They described them as those who fear the inherent evils of capitalist society but seek to improve it, rather than overthrowing its foundations. Marx and Engels assert that they propose "welfare measures" or "grandiose systems of reform," which, while seemingly meant to re-organize society,

[...] are in fact intended to preserve the foundations, and hence the life, of existing society. Communists must unremittingly struggle against these bourgeois socialists because they work for the enemies of communists and protect the society which communists aim to overthrow.

Vladimir Lenin

expanded on this critique, specifically targeting those who believe socialism can be achieved through democracy:

[...] the democratic republic, the Constituent Assembly, general elections, etc., are, in practice, the dictatorship of the bourgeoisie, and for the emancipation of labor from the yoke of capital there is no other way but to replace this dictatorship with the dictatorship of the proletariat.

Thus, he opposed attempts to achieve socialism by electoral or democratic means, viewing democratic institutions under capitalism as instruments of

capitalist class rule

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship\_of\_the\_bourgeoisie

Certain contemporary communist organizations have also echoed this view, such as the

International Communist Party

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International\_Communist\_Party

in their article "Democratic Socialism: False Friend of the Working Class".

They argue that electoral democracy "will not be enough" for the overthrow of capitalism. They call for a

proletarian revolution

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian\_revolution

which strips the

bourgeoisie

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie

of political rights and participation, and uses its "dictatorial grip on power to abolish by force the basis for

capitalist exploitation.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation\_of\_labour

According to these communists, the problems caused by capitalism cannot be resolved through

incremental democratic reforms

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformist\_socialism

or electoral victories as proposed by many democratic socialists.

Instead, they claim, only a

dictatorship of the proletariat

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship\_of\_the\_proletariat

global proletarian revolution

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian\_revolution

can dismantle the capitalist system and eliminate its "exploitative foundations."
