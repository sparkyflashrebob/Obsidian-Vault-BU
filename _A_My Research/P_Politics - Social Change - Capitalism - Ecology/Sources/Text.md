The Powell Memo (1971) was a confidential strategy document by lawyer Lewis F. Powell Jr. for the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, urging businesses to launch a coordinated, long-term campaign to counter what he saw as attacks on American free enterprise from academia, media, and social movements, advocating for funding think tanks, monitoring media, and influencing courts and politics to shift public opinion and policy. It's considered a blueprint for modern corporate political engagement, inspiring conservative movements to organize and fund efforts to advance free-market agendas. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]

Key Points:

• Origin: Written by Lewis Powell in August 1971, shortly before he became a Supreme Court Justice, to the head of the U.S. Chamber of Commerce.

• Title: "Attack on American Free Enterprise System".

• Core Concern: Powell identified a broad "assault" on capitalism from environmentalists, consumer advocates, civil rights activists, and critics in academia and media.

• Proposed Strategy: A coordinated, well-funded effort by businesses to:

• Influence higher education and textbooks.

• Monitor and critique media coverage.

• Build legal foundations to fight in the courts.

• Increase lobbying and public affairs presence in Washington.

• Develop a strong, organized political voice for business.

• Impact: The memo is credited with inspiring a significant increase in corporate political spending and lobbying, helping to shape conservative policy and influence the direction of American politics and law for decades. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]
