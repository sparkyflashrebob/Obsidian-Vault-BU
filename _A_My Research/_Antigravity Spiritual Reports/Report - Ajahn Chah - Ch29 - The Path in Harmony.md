## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Location:**
  * *Original Spoken Context:* Delivered to assemblies of monastics and lay disciples at Wat Nong Pah Pong, Wat Pah Nanachat (Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand), and during tours abroad.
  * *Modern Preservation:* Recorded and published as the standard audio compendium of Ajahn Chah's life teachings by the Amaravati Sangha.
* **Theme of the Discourse:**
  * Piercing through theoretical study to encounter the truth of the mind directly. Moving beyond worldly extremes of indulgence and self-mortification to discover the peaceful Middle Way within.
* **Condensed Version of the Teachings:**
  * Today I would like to ask you all: ‘are you sure yet, are you certain in your meditation practice?’ I ask because these days there are many people teaching meditation, both monks and laypeople, and I’m afraid you may be subject to wavering and doubt. If we understand clearly, we will be able to make the mind peaceful and firm.
* **Expanded Version of the Teachings:**
  * You should understand the eightfold path as morality, concentration and wisdom. The path comes together as simply this. Our practice is to make this path arise within us.

## 💬 Notable Quotes from the Discourse

> *"You should understand the eightfold path as morality, concentration and wisdom. The path comes together as simply this. Our practice is to make this path arise within us."*
>
> *"If the mind is confused and won’t concentrate on the breath, take a full, deep breath, as deep as you can, and then let it all out till there is none left. Do this three times and then re-establish your attention. The mind will become calm."*
>
> *"In the end the path becomes one and functions at all times. We should look after the strength which arises from the path, because it is the strength which leads to insight and wisdom."*
>
> *"Samādhi is capable of bringing much harm or much benefit to the meditator. You can’t say it brings only one or the other. For one who has no wisdom it is harmful, but for one who has wisdom it can bring real benefit, it can lead to insight."*
>
