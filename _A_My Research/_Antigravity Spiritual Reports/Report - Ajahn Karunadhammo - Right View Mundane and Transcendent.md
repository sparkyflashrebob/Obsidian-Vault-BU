## 🏛️ Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Location:** Spirit Rock Meditation Center, Woodacre, California.
* **Circumstances:** Afternoon discourse during a Sunday daylong co-led by senior monks from Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery.
* **Theme:** Demystifying Right View (*sammā-diṭṭhi*), examining why moral action, generosity (*dāna*), and karmic accountability (*kamma-vipāka*) form the necessary foundation for transcendent insight.
* **Summary:** In MN 117 (*Mahācattārīsaka Sutta*), the Buddha draws a fundamental distinction between two levels of Right View:
  1. **Mundane Right View (*Lokiya Sammā-diṭṭhi*):** That which is accompanied by taints, aligns with merit, and ripens in positive rebirth. It affirms that actions have consequences (*kammassakā sattā*), generosity bears fruit, and ethical integrity (*sīla*) protects the heart.
  2. **Transcendent / Noble Right View (*Lokuttara Sammā-diṭṭhi*):** That which is noble, taintless, transcendent, and a factor of the path. It sees directly into the Four Noble Truths: Dukkha, its Origin, its Cessation, and the Path.
  Ajahn Karunadhammo warns against spiritual bypassing: trying to leap into transcendent "emptiness" while ignoring the mundane integrity of conduct and generosity.
