# Living Mindfully Standard Open — Foundational Quotes, Context, & Protocols

> *"Well, the intention is good, the time is now, and the place is here."*  
> — **Ajahn Sumedho**, *Amaravati Buddhist Monastery*

> *"Awakening is not a rare privilege reserved for a few saints or ascetics; it is the natural birthright of every human being."*  
> — **Jack Kornfield**, *The Wise Heart*

> *"We are not human beings having a spiritual experience; we are spiritual beings having a human experience."*  
> — **Pierre Teilhard de Chardin** *(frequently contextualized by Jack Kornfield in A Path with Heart)*

---

## Dharma Talk & Contemplative Metadata

### 1. Location
* **The Living Mindfully Sangha / Meeting Circle**: The direct contemplative threshold where practitioners gather—whether in person or online—transitioning from the momentum of daily duties into formal practice.
* **Lineage & Geographical Roots**:
  * **Hertfordshire, England (Amaravati Buddhist Monastery)**: The monastic seat where **Ajahn Sumedho** (senior Western disciple of Ajahn Chah) grounded Theravada Thai Forest monasticism in Western culture through radical simplicity and present-moment awareness.
  * **Woodacre, California (Spirit Rock Meditation Center)**: The insight community where **Jack Kornfield** established an accessible, heart-centered integration of classical Dhamma with deep psychological understanding.

### 2. Circumstances
Every meditation gathering or Dhamma talk begins with a transition. People arrive carrying the accumulated sensory residue of daily life: navigating heavy traffic, checking unresolved utility bills, holding recent difficult news, or experiencing bodily aches and tension. Frequently, practitioners also carry an internal subtle judgment: a feeling that they are "unprepared," "too agitated," or that they must first fix themselves before they are worthy to sit.

The opening protocol addresses this very threshold. Rather than commanding attention or forcing an abrupt silence, the teacher or facilitator offers a gentle verbal bridge. By anchoring the gathering in the direct wisdom of **Ajahn Sumedho** (eradicating the delay of seeking peace elsewhere) and **Jack Kornfield** (affirming that presence is an inherent birthright rather than an earned status), the opening relieves performance pressure and invites practitioners to settle immediately into what is already here.

### 3. People Involved
* **Ajahn Sumedho (Luang Por Sumedho)**: Foremost Western disciple of the venerable Ajahn Chah. Known for teaching "the sound of silence," intuitive awareness (*sampajañña*), and cutting through spiritual ambition with practical reflections on *the way it is*.
* **Jack Kornfield**: Author, psychologist, and co-founder of the Insight Meditation Society (IMS) and Spirit Rock. Celebrated for bringing tenderness, innate goodness, and universal accessibility to mindfulness practice.
* **Pierre Teilhard de Chardin**: French philosopher and paleontologist whose famous reflection on our underlying spiritual nature was adopted by Kornfield to remind practitioners of their deeper dignity.
* **The Living Mindfully Facilitator / Speaker**: Holding a non-prescriptive, welcoming, and grounded space for the community.
* **The Practicing Circle / Assembly**: Seekers and householders cultivating clarity and emotional resilience amid the demands of modern life.

### 4. Theme of the Dharma Talk & Openings
The central themes of this opening framework are:
1. **The Immediacy of the Dhamma (*Sandiṭṭhiko* & *Akāliko*)**: The realization that truth is visible right here and now, and is timeless. Awareness cannot happen yesterday, nor can it awaken tomorrow.
2. **The Inherent Human Birthright**: Freedom, compassion, and awareness are not external prizes to be purchased through spiritual exertion; they are the innate ground of consciousness.
3. **The Sincerity of Wholesome Intention (*Sammā Saṅkappa*)**: Validating that showing up with an open, honest heart is entirely sufficient.
4. **Disarming the Second Arrow (*Sallatha Sutta*)**: Stepping out of mental proliferation and self-criticism the moment we sit, allowing somatic contractions to soften under wise observation.

## 6. Expanded Version of the Teachings

### Part I: The Architecture of Ajahn Sumedho’s Teaching
When Ajahn Sumedho established the monastic Sangha in Britain, he walked alms round (*piṇḍapāta*) through rural towns and city streets. People would sometimes react with suspicion, amusement, or confusion at the sight of a monk in ochre robes. In an interview reflecting on these encounters and the perceived strangeness of spiritual practice in modern society, he offered his timeless formula:

> **"Well, the intention is good, the time is now, and the place is here."**

Later, during deep monastic retreats (such as his June 2008 retreat at Spirit Rock), he titled his primary address **"The Place Is Here, The Time Is Now."** 

In the Thai Forest tradition, this is not a casual motivational slogan. It is a direct pointing to two foundational attributes of the Dhamma (*Dhammaguṇa*):
1. ***Sandiṭṭhiko*** — Direct, immediately visible, to be seen here and now. The mind is perpetually plotting to attain enlightenment somewhere down the road—when the kids grow up, when retirement comes, or when life is less chaotic. Ajahn Sumedho cuts through this delusion: the only place awareness can wake up is right where the body sits.
2. ***Akāliko*** — Timeless, immediate, not bound by chronological sequence. You do not wait for time to heal or liberate you; liberation is stepping out of the mental projection of time altogether.
3. **The Sincerity of the Heart**: When he adds *"the intention is good,"* he liberates the practitioner from feelings of unworthiness. The mind may be scattered, tired, or troubled by difficult events entering our senses during the day, but the wholesome volitional impulse to sit down and be present (*kusala chanda*) provides a complete, steadfast foundation.

### Part II: The Architecture of Jack Kornfield’s Teaching on "Birthright"
In his seminal works—*A Path with Heart* and *The Wise Heart*—Jack Kornfield emphasizes that meditation is not a self-improvement project designed to manufacture a manufactured state of holiness. Instead, it is an act of **remembering who we already are**.

Kornfield frequently reflects:
> *"Awakening is not a rare privilege reserved for a few saints or ascetics; it is the natural birthright of every human being."*

He pairs this canonical truth with Pierre Teilhard de Chardin’s famous cosmological perspective:
> *"We are not human beings having a spiritual experience; we are spiritual beings having a human experience."*

In modern life, people frequently suffer from a subtle "spiritual impostor syndrome"—a feeling that peace, deep stillness, or unconditional acceptance belongs to monks, mystics, or enlightened teachers, while their own lives are too compromised by family stress, financial pressures, or emotional turbulence. 

Kornfield’s use of the word **birthright** directly dismantles this spiritual hierarchy:
* **Inherent Wholeness**: Just as a newborn does not need to earn the right to breathe, awareness does not need to pass an exam to experience presence.
* **Beneath Conditioning**: Our habitual worry, impatience, and anxiety are learned social habits and survival defenses. Beneath them lies our natural capacity for clarity, tenderness, and peace (*pakati-citta* / original nature).
* **Honoring the Human Condition**: Remembering that we are "spiritual beings having a human experience" allows us to treat our clumsy, tender, and vulnerable human feelings with warmth rather than impatience.

## Practical Spoken Scripts for the Living Mindfully Open

Here are four adaptable options designed to open your meetings, classes, or Dharma talks. Each script strictly observes our non-prescriptive, observational guidelines (using the inclusive "we," honoring conditionality, avoiding rigid commands, and providing spacious pauses).

---

### Option A: The Foundational Grounding Open (Recommended Standard)
*Pacing: Warm, steady, unhurried.*

> *"Welcome, everyone. As we take our seats and gather together, we can allow ourselves a quiet moment simply to transition.*
>
> *Ajahn Sumedho often shared a simple, grounding reminder that cuts through so much of our mental striving: he would say, **'The intention is good, the time is now, and the place is here.'** We don't have to look anywhere else, and we don't have to wait for a better moment. This moment, exactly as it is, is the doorway.*
>
> *And as Jack Kornfield reminds us so gently, waking up to peace and living with a compassionate heart isn't a rare prize reserved for a select few. It is our natural human birthright. We don't have to earn it, and we don't have to prove anything to be here.*
>
> *Whatever has entered our senses today—demands at work, conversations, bodily fatigue, or if our stomach is tight or knotted up—we can let it all be held in awareness. Our intention is good, the time is right now, and together, we can simply arrive."*

---

### Option B: The Contemplative & Spacious Open (Reflective)
*Pacing: Slower, rhythmic, leaving 3–5 seconds between thoughts.*

> *"A warm welcome to our practice circle today.*
>
> *So often in modern life, we carry the subconscious feeling that we are trying to become someone else—someone calmer, someone wiser, someone more spiritual.*
>
> *(Pause)*
>
> *Yet the great forest teachers remind us that we cannot awaken in tomorrow, and we cannot practice in another room. **The place is here, and the time is now.** When we bring an honest, sincere heart into the room, our intention is already whole.*
>
> *(Pause)*
>
> *Jack Kornfield often reflects that we are not fragile beings struggling to attain an artificial spiritual state; we are spiritual beings navigating a messy, tender human life. Freedom and peace are our birthright—already woven into the fabric of who we are beneath all our stress and conditioning.*
>
> *(Pause)*
>
> *So as we begin, there is nothing we need to force. We can simply let the body settle, let the breath move naturally, and rest in the awareness that is already present."*

---

### Option C: The Somatic & Everyday Grounding Open (Stress Relief)
*Pacing: Down-to-earth, physical, restorative.*

> *"Good evening / Good morning, everyone. Welcome.*
>
> *Before we do anything formal, we might take a moment to notice what is actually happening in the body right now. In a typical day, life enters our senses quickly—traffic, screens, errands, news, and daily chores. Our nervous system naturally contracts. We might notice that our shoulders are raised, our breath is shallow, or our stomach is tight and knotted up.*
>
> *In nature, an animal flees danger, shakes off the shock once it reaches safety, and quietly returns to grazing. For us humans, we tend to keep the threat alive in thought—we think about the tiger when there is no tiger in the room.*
>
> *Right now, in this room, we can remind ourselves: **the place is here, the time is now, and our intention is good.** As Jack Kornfield has taught for decades, a peaceful, open mind isn’t an exclusive privilege—it is your natural birthright as a human being.*
>
> *We have arrived. There are no demands on this hour. Let’s allow the body to breathe, letting our vital energy settle back into ease."*

---

### Option D: The 30-Second Concise Invocation
*Pacing: Crisp, clear, centering.*

> *"Welcome, everyone. We can begin today with a profound reminder from the Thai Forest elders: **the intention is good, the time is now, and the place is here.** Awakening and an open heart are not distant achievements we have to struggle to earn; they are our innate human birthright. Let’s take our seats, release the day, and arrive right here."*

| **Timeless / Immediate** | *Akāliko* | Fourth quality of the Dhamma; immediate in fruition, unbound by temporal delay. | Validates that peace is an instantaneous quality of awareness, not a chronological goal. |
