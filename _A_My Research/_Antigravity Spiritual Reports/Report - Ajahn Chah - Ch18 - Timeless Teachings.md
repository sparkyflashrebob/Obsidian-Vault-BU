## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Location:**
  * *Original Spoken Context:* Delivered to assemblies of monastics and lay disciples at Wat Nong Pah Pong, Wat Pah Nanachat (Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand), and during tours abroad.
  * *Modern Preservation:* Recorded and published as the standard audio compendium of Ajahn Chah's life teachings by the Amaravati Sangha.
* **Theme of the Discourse:**
  * Piercing through theoretical study to encounter the truth of the mind directly. Moving beyond worldly extremes of indulgence and self-mortification to discover the peaceful Middle Way within.
* **Condensed Version of the Teachings:**
  * Everyone knows suffering - but they don’t really understand suffering. If we really understood suffering, then that would be the end of our suffering.
* **Expanded Version of the Teachings:**
  * Westerners are generally in a hurry, so they have greater extremes of happiness and suffering. The fact that they have much kilesā, can be a source of wisdom later on.

## 💬 Notable Quotes from the Discourse

> *"Westerners are generally in a hurry, so they have greater extremes of happiness and suffering. The fact that they have much kilesā, can be a source of wisdom later on."*
>
> *"From this base, samādhi will come, but not yet wisdom. One must watch the mind at work - see like and dislike arising from sense contact, and not attach to them."*
>
> *"‘Our worldly work is never finished,’ he explained. ‘You should use such occasions as this as exercises in mindfulness, and then when you have worked long enough just stop. Put it aside and continue your sitting and walking practice.’"*
>
> *"Meditation is giving rise to wisdom in the mind. This we can do anywhere, any time and in any posture."*
>
