## 🏛️ Structured Contextual Metadata & Core Teachings

* **Opening Literary Motif:** Ajahn Amaro opens with T.S. Eliot’s famous lines from the 1934 pageant play *The Rock*:
  > *"Where is the Life we have lost in living?*  
  > *Where is the wisdom we have lost in knowledge?*  
  > *Where is the knowledge we have lost in information?"*
* **The Triad of Cognition:**
  1. **Information:** Raw, fragmented facts and digital data points. Easily accumulates, creates mental clutter, and drives anxiety.
  2. **Knowledge:** Organized conceptual frameworks, theories, and intellectual competencies.
  3. **Wisdom (*Paññā*):** Intuitive, direct experiential knowing of things as they are (*yathābhūtañāṇadassana*). Free from clinging, ego-defense, and fear.
* **Stories Offered:**
  * **The Sabbatical in Corsica and Gloucestershire:** Ajahn Amaro describes taking a full sabbatical year completely disconnected from digital media, emails, news, and phone calls. He observed how the nervous system settles into profound peace when freed from the barrage of trivial information.
  * **Childhood Encyclopedia Reading:** Remembering his childhood obsession with reading the *Encyclopedia Britannica* from A to Z, realizing decades later that collecting facts never touches the heart's thirst for peace.
  * **T.S. Eliot at Burnt Norton:** Recounting the historical context of Eliot’s spiritual awakening at the Gloucestershire manor house (*"At the still point of the turning world"*).
* **The *Loka Sutta* (SN 35.82):** When asked where the world is, the Buddha answered: *"In this very fathom-long body with its perceptions and thoughts, I declare the world, its origin, its cessation, and the path."* True wisdom is knowing this immediate six-foot organism, not accumulating facts about the cosmos.
