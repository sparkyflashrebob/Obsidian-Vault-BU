## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Location:**
  * *Original Spoken Context:* Delivered to assemblies of monastics and lay disciples at Wat Nong Pah Pong, Wat Pah Nanachat (Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand), and during tours abroad.
  * *Modern Preservation:* Recorded and published as the standard audio compendium of Ajahn Chah's life teachings by the Amaravati Sangha.
* **Theme of the Discourse:**
  * Piercing through theoretical study to encounter the truth of the mind directly. Moving beyond worldly extremes of indulgence and self-mortification to discover the peaceful Middle Way within.
* **Condensed Version of the Teachings:**
  * Wat Wana Potiyahn1 here is certainly very peaceful, but this is meaningless if our minds are not calm. All places are peaceful. That some may seem distracting is because of our minds. However, a quiet place can help us to become calm, by giving us the opportunity to train and thus harmonize with its calm.
* **Expanded Version of the Teachings:**
  * You should all bear in mind that this practice is difficult. To train in other things is not so difficult, it’s easy, but the human mind is hard to train. The Lord Buddha trained his mind. The mind is the important thing. Everything within this body-mind system comes together at the mind. The eyes, ears, nose, tongue and body all receive sensations and send them into the mind, which is the supervisor of all the other sense organs. Therefore, it is important to train the mind. If the mind is well trained, all problems come to an end. If there are still problems, it’s because the mind still doubts, it doesn’t know in accordance with the truth. That is why there are problems.

## 💬 Notable Quotes from the Discourse

> *"Wisdom is something each of us finds for oneself. To see it we must go neither fast nor slow. What should we do? Go to where there is neither fast nor slow. Going fast or going slow is not the way."*
>
> *"All of you ask yourselves now, is this right? To practise when you feel like it, not when you don’t: is that in accordance with the Dhamma? Is it straight? Is it in line with the teaching? This is what makes practice inconsistent."*
>
> *"3: These are the two extremes pointed out as wrong paths by the Buddha in his First Discourse. They are normally rendered as ‘indulgence in sense pleasures’ and ‘self-mortification’."*
