## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Location:**
  * *Canonical Sutta Geography:* The root of a tree or an empty hut (*araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā*), as described in the *Ānāpānasati Sutta* (MN 118).
  * *Modern Venue:* Insight Meditation Society (IMS), Main Meditation Hall, Barre, Massachusetts.
* **Circumstances:**
  * Delivered at the start of a sitting period to establish right attitude and prevent meditators from creating tension by "trying too hard" to concentrate. Ajahn Chah directly deconstructs the common error of forcing the breath or striving to create special visions.
* **Theme of the Instructions:**
  * Meditation is not a struggle to pin down an object by force; it is the art of maintaining alert awareness while stepping out of interference. Breath awareness (*ānāpānasati*) must be guided by wisdom (*paññā*), allowing bodily sensations and mental phenomena to arise and cease naturally.
* **Condensed Version:**
  * When sitting, there is no need to think about many things. Fix attention first at the head, sweep down through the body to the feet, and return to the crown. This provides an immediate, grounded inventory of the physical form as it actually is. 
  * Then, make the in-and-out breath your sole duty. Do not force the breath to be long, short, deep, or shallow; allow it to flow naturally and evenly. Let go with each inhalation and exhalation. When doubts, memories, visions, or wandering thoughts arise, do not fight them or indulge them—recognize that you have no other business right now and let them dissolve by themselves.
* **Expanded Version:**
  * Ajahn Chah stresses the synthesis of *samatha* (calming) and *vipassanā* (discerning). If we attempt to create tranquility through brute force, the mind pushes back with restlessness and headache. True stillness arises through surrender: letting go of past, future, and expectations. By watching the natural breath enter and leave without meddling, the mind becomes an unmoving observer at the doorway of sensation. Sights, sounds, and thoughts are like passersby on the street; as long as the host does not invite them into the house, they pass on without leaving a trace.

## 📊 Practice Protocol & Step-by-Step Architecture

| Step | Meditative Phase | Contemplative Action | Sutta Anchor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | **Initial Body Scan** | Sweep awareness from the crown of the head down to the tips of the feet, and back up. | *Kāyagatāsati* (MN 119) |
| **Step 2** | **Boundary Setting** | Resolve that you have no responsibilities, no tasks, and no need to evaluate results. | *Nekkhamma-saṅkappa* (Renunciation of concern) |
| **Step 3** | **Natural Breath Awareness** | Observe in-breath and out-breath without altering rhythm, volume, or depth. | *Ānāpānasati* Steps 1–2 (MN 118) |
| **Step 4** | **Letting Go (*Vossagga*)** | Release mental tightening on each out-breath while preserving lucid presence. | *Paṭinissaggānupassī* (MN 118, Step 16) |
| **Step 5** | **Non-Engagement with Arising States** | Allow thoughts, feelings, and sensations to arise and cease without reaction. | *Vayadhammānupassī* (MN 10) |

## 💬 Verbatim Quotes & Timestamps

> **[00:17 - 00:27]**  
> *"Sitting meditation and making the mind peaceful, you don't have to think about too much. Right now, just focus on the mind, and nothing else."*

> **[00:41 - 00:56]**  
> *"First, fix your attention at the head and move it down through the body to the tips of the feet, and then back up to the crown of the head. Pass your awareness down through the body observing with wisdom. We do this to gain an initial understanding of the way the body is right now."*

> **[01:03 - 01:25]**  
> *"Don't force the breath to be any longer or shorter than normal. Just allow it to continue easily. Don't put any pressure on the breath; rather, let it flow evenly, letting go with each in-breath and out-breath."*

> **[01:45 - 02:05]**  
> *"Maintain the resolve that at this time, you have no other duties or responsibilities... During the meditation, there is no need to pay attention to anything that arises in the mind. Once they arise, just let them cease by themselves."*
