## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Location:**
  * *Original Spoken Context:* Delivered to assemblies of monastics and lay disciples at Wat Nong Pah Pong, Wat Pah Nanachat (Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand), and during tours abroad.
  * *Modern Preservation:* Recorded and published as the standard audio compendium of Ajahn Chah's life teachings by the Amaravati Sangha.
* **Theme of the Discourse:**
  * Piercing through theoretical study to encounter the truth of the mind directly. Moving beyond worldly extremes of indulgence and self-mortification to discover the peaceful Middle Way within.
* **Condensed Version of the Teachings:**
  * Some of us start to practise, and even after a year or two, still don’t know what’s what. We are still unsure of the practice. When we’re still unsure, we don’t see that everything around us is purely Dhamma, and so we turn to teachings from the Ajahns. But actually, when we know our own mind, when there is sati to look closely at the mind, there is wisdom. All times and all places become occasions for us to hear the Dhamma.
* **Expanded Version of the Teachings:**
  * We can learn Dhamma from nature, from trees for example. A tree is born due to causes and it grows following the course of nature. Right here the tree is teaching us Dhamma, but we don’t understand this. In due course, it grows and grows until it buds, flowers and fruit appear. All we see is the appearance of the flowers and fruit; we’re unable to bring this within and contemplate it. Thus we don’t know that the tree is teaching us Dhamma. The fruit appears and we merely eat it without investigating: sweet, sour or salty, it’s the nature of the fruit. And this is Dhamma, the teaching of the fruit. The leaves then grow old. They wither, die and then fall from the tree. All we see is that the leaves have fallen down. We step on them, we sweep them up, that’s all. We don’t investigate thoroughly, so we don’t know that nature is teaching us. Later on the new leaves sprout, and we merely see that, without taking it further. We don’t bring these things into our minds to contemplate.

## 💬 Notable Quotes from the Discourse

> *"This feeling was different from anything he’d ever known before. He truly realized his mind, and so ‘Buddha’ arose within him. At that time the Buddha declared that Aññā Kondañña had received the ‘Eye of Dhamma’."*
>
> *"There! Aññā Kondañña had received the Eye of Dhamma. He saw nature, which we call sankhārā, according to truth. Wisdom is that which knows the truth of sankhārā. This is the mind which knows and sees Dhamma, the mind which has surrendered."*
>
> *"1: Nature here refers to all things, mental and physical, not just trees, animals etc."*
>
