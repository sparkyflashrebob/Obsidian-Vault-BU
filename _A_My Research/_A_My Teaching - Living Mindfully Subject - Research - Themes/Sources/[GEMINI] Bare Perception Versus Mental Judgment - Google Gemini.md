Compile a full report on this, including the earlier processes of sensing data at the raw input (somatic method), at the perceiving and feeling tone stages, all the way up to the judgment part.
Does this fall in line with the Buddha's teachings on dependent origination?

---

Model: Summary
This process maps directly onto the core teachings of early Buddhism, specifically Dependent Origination (Paṭiccasamuppāda) and the cognitive breakdown given in the Madhupiṇḍika Sutta (MN 18) and Bāhiya Sutta (Ud 1.10).
The movement from raw physical contact to mental judgment is not a single instantaneous event, but a micro-chain of conditioned stages. Tracing this sequence somatically reveals exactly where unconscious reactivity hooks in—and where freedom (nibbāna / unbinding) is accessible in real time.

1. The Step-by-Step Cognitive & Somatic Chain The Five Aggregates of experience. Source: Buddhism.net
   Stage 1: Raw Sense Base & Object (Rūpa / Āyatana)
   The Mechanism: An external vibration, wavelength of light, or physical pressure impinges upon an intact sense organ (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, or mind-organ).
   Somatic Experience: Pure pre-conceptual physics. In the body, this is raw vibration, pulsation, heat, cold, or mechanical pressure with zero meaning or owner attached.
   Stage 2: Bare Consciousness (Viññāṇa) & Contact (Phassa)
   The Mechanism: The meeting of sense base, sense object, and respective consciousness (e.g., eye + visual form + eye-consciousness = visual contact).
   Somatic Experience: The raw "knowing" that contact has occurred. It is simple registration without categorization.
   Stage 3: Feeling Tone (Vedanā)
   The Mechanism: The immediate, involuntary affective charge born of contact: sukha (pleasant), dukkha (unpleasant), or adukkhamasukha (neutral/neither-pleasant-nor-unpleasant).
   Somatic Experience: A micro-shift in the nervous system. Pleasant feeling manifests as an energetic opening/ease; unpleasant manifests as a subtle tightening, contraction, or recoil; neutral feels flat or easily ignored.
   Stage 4: Perception & Recognition (Saññā)
   The Mechanism: The mind scans its memory bank (saṅkhāra impressions) to identify features, match patterns, and apply a label (e.g., "siren," "knee joint," "critical tone of voice").
   Somatic Experience: The perceptual "overlay." The raw sound pattern becomes a recognizable object in awareness.
   Stage 5: Mental Formations & Volition (Saṅkhāra / Cetanā)
   The Mechanism: Craving (taṇhā) or aversion reacts to the feeling tone. The mind decides what to do with the experience (fight, flee, hold on, ignore).
   Somatic Experience: Muscular tension, bracing, shallow breath, or an internal reach/pull.
   Stage 6: Proliferation & Narrative Judgment (Papañca)
   The Mechanism: As stated in MN 18: "What one perceives, one thinks about; what one thinks about, one mentally proliferates." The mind spins stories: "Why does this always happen to me?", "They don't respect me," "I must fix this immediately."
   Somatic Experience: Full-body stress response, clenched jaw, tight chest, mental rumination, and a solid sense of an "I" who is suffering.
2. Alignment with Dependent Origination (Paṭiccasamuppāda)
   The breakdown is the exact middle arc of the 12 Links of Dependent Origination:
   Six Sense Bases→Contact→Feeling Tone→Craving→Clinging→Becoming / Suffering (Saḷ a ˉ yatana→Phassa→Vedan a ˉ →Taṇh a ˉ →Up a ˉ d a ˉ na→Bhava / Dukkha)
   The Inevitable Links: Sense bases, Contact, and Feeling Tone (Vedanā) are karmic results (vipāka). As long as you have an alive body and nervous system, they will arise.
   The Breakable Link: The pivot occurs between Feeling Tone (Vedanā) and Craving (Taṇhā).
   Unmindful processing: Unpleasant → Aversion/Judgment → Clinging to identity → Suffering.
   Mindful processing: Unpleasant → Pure observation ("in the felt, only the felt") → Craving does not ignite → Proliferation ceases.
3. A Somatic Method for Real-Time Deconstruction
   When physical or emotional tension arises, use this sequential inquiry to trace backward through the chain:
   [ Story / Judgment ] --> "This pain is ruining my day; I can't stand it." │ ▼ [ Label / Perception ] --> "Pain in the lower back." │ ▼ [ Feeling Tone ] --> Unpleasant (instinctive micro-contraction). │ ▼ [ Raw Data ] --> Heat, throbbing, pressure, shifting tingling.
   Step 1: Notice the Story: Recognize the narrative commentary and gently name it: "Story arising, judging arising."
   Step 2: Drop into the Body (Somatic Grounding): Bypass the mental story and locate the actual physical sensations in the body.
   Step 3: Isolate the Feeling Tone (Vedanā): Feel the raw unpleasantness without calling it an enemy. Notice it simply as a biological reflex of protection.
   Step 4: Deconstruct into Raw Sensation: Look closely at the "pain" or "tension." Is it a solid block, or is it made of dynamic micro-sensations—pulsing, warmth, density, movement?
   Step 5: Rest in Bare Knowing: Allow the raw sensation to rise, shift, and pass within open awareness, leaving no "owner" behind to be burdened by it.
4. Cross-Tradition Parallels
   Advaita Vedanta: The 5 Sheaths (Pañcakośa) describe the same layering: from the physical sheath (Annamaya) through energy (Prāṇamaya) and lower mind/perception (Manomaya), up to intellect/ego (Vijñānamaya). The error is Adhyāsa (superimposition)—the Witness (Sākkṣī) falsely identifies with the contents of the mental sheath.
   Classical Stoicism: Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius distinguish between the raw initial impression (phantasia) and the subsequent value judgment (dogma / assent). Physical discomfort is indifferent (adiaphoron); distress only begins when the mind assents to the belief that the sensation is an intrinsic evil.
   Indigenous & Earth-Based Perspectives: Relational listening treats raw sensory contact with the elements (weather, terrain, animal life) as a direct conversation before the conceptual intellect sections nature into objects for human use. Discomfort is not judged as a flaw in reality, but respected as an instructive physical signpost of the living landscape.

The descending and ascending leg circuits of the Macrocosmic Orbit (Da Zhou Tian) map almost one-to-one onto Thomas Myers' myofascial meridians in Anatomy Trains.

1. Descending Yang Flow & the Superficial Back Line (SBL)
   The SBL connects the entire posterior surface of the body like a continuous sheet of laminated armor, running from the bottom of the foot up to the brow ridge of the skull.
   The Common Trajectory:
   In Qigong, the Du Mai (spine) connects directly to the Bladder channel at the sacrum, traveling down the back of the thigh, popliteal fossa (behind the knee), calf, and wrapping under the lateral heel to the sole.
   In myofascial anatomy, the erector spinae fascial envelope links via the sacrum and sacrotuberous ligament into the hamstrings, then through the gastrocnemius/soleus into the Achilles tendon and plantar fascia.
   Clinical / Somatic Implication:
   When a person sits for long periods or braces defensively, the entire SBL shortens and locks.
   Circulating the descending Yang breath along this line somatically softens the myofascial tension that binds the sciatic nerve, allowing the sacrum to drop and the lower back (Mingmen) to open.
2. Ascending Yin Flow & the Deep Front Line (DFL)
   The Deep Front Line is the myofascial "core" of the body. It is the three-dimensional structural scaffolding that runs deep to all superficial movement muscles.
   The Common Trajectory:
   In Qigong, the Yin current begins at Yongquan (Kidney 1) in the hollow of the foot, ascends the medial arch, travels up the inner seam of the tibia (spleen/liver), deep through the adductors into the groin (kua), and anchors into the perineum (Huiyin) and lower Dantian.
   In anatomy, the DFL begins under the medial tarsal bones and ascends through the tibialis posterior and deep flexors, passes behind the medial epicondyle of the knee, moves up the adductor compartment, and integrates into the pelvic floor, iliopsoas, and respiratory diaphragm.
   Clinical / Somatic Implication:
   A collapsed medial arch or tight hip adductors deadens proprioceptive feedback from the legs (numbness, coldness, or instability).
   Drawing the breath up this inner Yin/DFL track activates the deep stabilizing tone of the inner thighs and pelvic floor without generating superficial muscular grip, restoring a buoyant structural lift up into the core.
3. The Functional Synergy: The Fascial Loop
   In Myers' model, the SBL and DFL act as a continuous antagonist-synergist pair that maintains upright posture and transfers kinetic load between the earth and the spine.
   Postural Harmony: When the SBL is excessively tight (posterior tilt, tight lower back) or the DFL is inactive (collapsed arches, anterior pelvic drift), energy pools as pain in the lumbar spine or knees.
   The Orbit as Fascial Flossing: By synchronizing the descending exhalation through the SBL (releasing the back surface) with the ascending inhalation through the DFL (lifting the core seam), the Macrocosmic Orbit systematically unwinds fascial adhesions and balances reciprocal tension across the lower body.

In Daoist internal arts, the Lower Dantian (Xia Dantian) is described as the body’s primary center of gravity, energetic reservoir, and kinetic pump. Anatomically, this energetic space maps directly to the Deep Front Line (DFL) core cylinder—a dynamic myofascial pressure chamber formed by the continuous connection between the Respiratory Diaphragm (top), the Pelvic Floor (bottom), and the Iliopsoas complex and Transversus Abdominis (walls).
The Anatomical Cylinder: Fascial and Muscular Architecture
The Roof (Thoracic Diaphragm): The primary muscle of respiration. Its muscular crura reach downward and anchor directly into the anterior surfaces of the upper lumbar vertebrae (L 1 ​

The Floor (Pelvic Diaphragm / Huiyin): The levator ani and coccygeus muscles form the pelvic sling, maintaining upward structural tone and supporting internal organs.
The Psoas Bridge: The psoas major originates along the lumbar spine and intervertebral discs, interweaving directly with the connective tissue of the diaphragmatic crura and the arcuate ligaments. It sweeps down through the pelvis (kua) to attach to the lesser trochanter of the femur.
The Fascial Continuity: The fascia of the psoas blends seamlessly with the transversalis fascia, the quadratus lumborum, and the deep fascial lining of the pelvic floor, creating a sealed, pressurized fascial pouch in the lower abdomen.
Functional Mechanisms of the Lower Dantian
The Hydrostatic Kinetic Pump (Diaphragmatic Synergy):
In Dantian / abdominal breathing (Dan Tian Hu Xi), the respiratory diaphragm descends on inhalation while the pelvic floor gently broadens and yields. On exhalation, the pelvic floor gently rebounds upward as the diaphragm ascends.
This piston-like movement creates continuous, rhythmic intra-abdominal pressure changes. It massages the abdominal viscera, promotes venous and lymphatic return from the lower body, and creates the internal hydraulic power known in martial arts as Fa Jin.
The Psoas as the "Muscle of the Soul" & Emotional Reservoir:
Because the psoas is wired directly to the sympathetic nervous system, acute or chronic stress triggers a primal fight-or-flight contraction, pulling the lumbar spine forward and locking the breath into the upper chest.
Sinking awareness and breath into the Lower Dantian signals the psoas to release its chronic contraction. This allows the lumbar spine (Mingmen) to decompress, the sacrum to drop, and the nervous system to shift from sympathetic alarm to parasympathetic grounding.
Center of Mass and Structural Buoyancy:
Biomechanically, the body's center of gravity lies just anterior to the sacrum, roughly 2 inches below the navel—the exact traditional location of the Lower Dantian.
When the psoas, diaphragm, and pelvic floor work in balanced tension (tensegrity), mechanical load from the torso transfers effortlessly down the legs to the earth (Yongquan) without collapsing into the lower back or knees.
Practical Integration: Cultivating Dantian Tensegrity
