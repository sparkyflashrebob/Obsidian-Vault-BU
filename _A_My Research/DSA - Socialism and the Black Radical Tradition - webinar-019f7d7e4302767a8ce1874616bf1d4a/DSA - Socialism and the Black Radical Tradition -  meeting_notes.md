# DSA - Socialism and the Black Radical Tradition - webinar - Jul, 20

### DSA Webinar: Socialism and the Black Radical Tradition
📅 July 20, 2026
👥 Sid Carlson White, Cori Bush, Chris Rabb, DSA Representative
### Outline
### Introduction of Panelists and DSA's Electoral Mission

- DSA's National Political Committee member Sid Carlson White opened the webinar, welcoming attendees to a discussion on socialism and the Black Radical Tradition.

- Serving as liaison to the DSA Afro-Socialists and Socialists of Color Caucus, Sid emphasized the importance of people of color building a socialist movement together within DSA.

- Work with the National Electoral Commission was highlighted as DSA's nationwide fundraising and electoral arm, focused on building a socialist bloc in Congress and state legislatures.

- Cori Bush introduced herself as the former congresswoman for Missouri's first congressional district, a registered nurse, pastor, activist, and the first Black woman to represent Missouri in the U.S. House of Representatives.

- Chris Rabb introduced himself as a current Pennsylvania state representative, soon to become a U.S. Representative for the bluest congressional district in the nation, the left side of Philadelphia.

- Rabb noted that he won his Democratic nomination on May 19th, the 101st birthday of Malcolm X, and described himself as new to DSA but not to the values it represents.

### Personal Journeys to Socialism and the Black Radical Tradition

- Sid framed the question by noting that over 400 years of American history, the class struggle in the United States has been waged by Black Americans using different modes of struggle and tools to fight for equality and liberation.

- Rabb described himself as first and foremost a Black father, deeply influenced by his ancestors, tracing his lineage to Black abolitionists, freedom fighters, and civil rights activists.

- Descending from a wide array of activists shaped his understanding that the values of socialism are rooted in the Black resistance movement and long predate 19th-century socialist theory.

- Collectivist traditions, described as being as much West African as anything else, were identified as the true ancient roots of socialism, with the fingerprints of West Africans brought to the U.S. in bondage visible in practices such as mutual aid.

- Rabb argued that the Black radical tradition is not a chapter or footnote but the spine of American socialism, and that collective liberation requires centering those who have been doing the work for centuries.

- Racial capitalism was identified as the framework through which the origins of capitalism in the United States must be understood, with whiteness described as a social construct created to justify African enslavement and support economic exploitation.

- Bush connected her activism to her family history, noting that a cousin was among the young Black teenagers photographed being sprayed with water hoses in Birmingham, illustrating how the fight for justice was present before her.

- Raised in a household where her father ensured she understood Black excellence and history from an early age, Bush described watching Roots and Eyes on the Prize archives instead of regular television and reading books on Black liberation theology.

- Witnessing her father's direct community service, including helping young people find employment and providing for those experiencing homelessness, instilled in her the values of the Poor People's Campaign around fair wages, housing, and employment.

- Personal experiences of wage inequality, housing insecurity, and food insecurity, including sleeping in a car with her children, drove Bush to ask who in positions of power would speak up for people in those circumstances.

### The Role of Race and Blackness in American Capitalism and Socialist Organizing

- Sid argued that Blackness represents the core factor in the American system, as the state's tactics for repressing any group rising up have historically been learned from how Black people were specifically repressed.

- The design of the American system was described as one built to crush and enslave Black people, with that design visible across all of American history.

- Rabb stated that he does not approach organizing from a purely class-based perspective, nor from a purely race-based one, but insists that the existence of whiteness is what has supported racial capitalism and led to the current state of fascism.

- White people as a racial category were described as not having existed until that classification was created to justify the transatlantic slave trade and maximize profit through African enslavement.

- Whiteness was characterized as a social construct that does not benefit most white people, and white leftists were challenged to acknowledge this construct rather than reducing all analysis to class.

- Holding multiple truths simultaneously was described as a form of collective genius, particularly among Black people who have navigated systems of extraordinary harm.

- White leftists were encouraged to use their understanding of these dynamics as messengers to white communities who do not yet identify with socialism, making internal conversations about race central rather than peripheral.

- Rabb disclosed that he had initially been unwilling to seek a DSA endorsement because the application did not reference reparations, and only reconsidered when that was updated, citing reparations as inseparable from collective liberation.

### Harriet Tubman as a Model for Movement Building and Personal Resilience

- Bush identified Harriet Tubman's journey as the historical example she keeps before her most consistently, describing Tubman as a one-person movement who saved over 70 enslaved people across multiple trips between the South and freedom.

- The physical and logistical dangers of Tubman's journeys were emphasized, including traveling on foot, managing the varying physical abilities and fears of those she guided, and facing danger both in retrieving people and in returning to do so again.

- Tubman's example was invoked as a source of personal strength during moments of exhaustion, including fatigue from political attacks, demonization, and the ongoing effort to make people understand that humanity is the baseline standard for rights and self-determination.

- The Ferguson uprising, in which Bush participated for more than 400 days through rain, sleet, and snow, was connected to Tubman's instruction to keep going when you hear the dogs, with Bush describing having literally seen and heard dogs used against protesters in her own community.

- Rabb described taking his sons to see the film Harriet and using the moment to connect their family's direct ancestral ties to abolitionists who worked alongside Tubman in Philadelphia, including William Still who documented those stories.

- All people, regardless of bloodline, were described as inheritors of the legacy of Harriet Tubman, Sojourner Truth, and Frederick Douglass, with the distinction being not ancestry but whether one embodies that legacy and uses it as fuel in the struggle.

- Tubman was highlighted as a tiny, disabled, illiterate Black woman whose power came from knowledge built in community over generations, including understanding of stars, nature, spirit, and communication systems that those who enslaved people could not access.

- The question of what can be accomplished now, with Wi-Fi and greater organizing tools than Tubman had, was posed as a challenge to the present movement to recognize its own indomitable potential.

### DSA's Role in Preserving and Advancing the Black Radical Tradition

- Sid argued that both the Black radical tradition and American socialist traditions have been deliberately obscured because organized Black people and organized socialists represent the most threatening forces to the existing capitalist order.

- The story of Reconstruction was cited as an example of deliberate historical distortion, with the standard narrative falsely centering white politicians in Washington rather than the Black people who liberated themselves and defined their own futures in the American South.

- DSA was described as one of the only functional democracies in American society, making it a space where the vision of Black liberation can be built and fought for through collective will.

- Rabb argued that DSA must treat the Black radical tradition not as a monthly focus or a DEI initiative but as central to the very mission of the organization, with Blackness as the spine rather than a chapter.

- The organization's ability to evolve was acknowledged, with Rabb noting that DSA's Philly chapter had come a long way and urging grace for organizations that demonstrate genuine capacity for growth and self-reflection.

- Bush emphasized the education function of DSA, arguing that 100,000 members with access to high-level information represent a powerful counter to the misinformation spread through right-wing and capitalist-aligned media channels.

- Delivering clear, accessible messaging on issues such as Medicare for All, abolishing ICE, anti-imperialism, and reparations was identified as a core way DSA can shift public understanding and counter the framing that socialist positions are dangerous or extreme.

- The connection between foreign military spending and the defunding of domestic communities was cited as an example of the kind of information that, when delivered clearly, changes people's political orientation.

- Bush stressed that DSA must serve as a textbook for communities whose history is being watered down or rewritten in school curricula, particularly given that descendants of enslaved people have had their languages, cultural identities, and origins stripped from them.

- Sid connected the historical banning of literacy for enslaved people to the present-day obscuring of socialist and Black radical knowledge, arguing that the same fear of collective knowledge building drives both forms of suppression.

### Electoral Strategy and the Goals of DSA-Endorsed Candidates in Congress

- Rabb described his congressional approach as saying the quiet parts out loud unapologetically, particularly by naming and defining capitalism in spaces where it is assumed as the default but never explicitly examined.

- Distinguishing commerce from capitalism was identified as a key rhetorical tool, with worker cooperatives cited as proof that commerce can exist without worker exploitation.

- Issues such as a living wage, abolishing ICE, ending endless wars, and opposing the genocide in Gaza were described as increasingly popular positions that connect to the felt needs of people's lives and create a virtuous cycle of electoral support.

- The incoming DSA-aligned congressional cohort was described as significantly larger than previous socialist blocs, creating new capacity to hold up narratives and values rarely embraced in mainstream governance.

- Bush expressed her intention to convene DSA members and DSA-aligned members of Congress in a structured way so that legislative positions are informed by what DSA and community members are saying, particularly on controversial votes.

- The eviction moratorium campaign during COVID, in which Bush camped on the Capitol steps for four nights and five days as a freshman congresswoman and won an extension despite being dismissed by media, colleagues, and the president, was cited as a model for movement-driven legislative impact.

- Founding the Equal Rights Amendment Caucus with Representative Ayanna Pressley was described as a template for the kind of convening Bush wants to replicate, noting that the caucus became the fifth largest in Congress within three months by providing members with accessible, well-researched information.

- DSA's Federal Socialist and Office Committee infrastructure was identified as a vehicle for producing and sharing knowledge about legislation in ways that the Democratic Party and progressive nonprofits will not do on their own.

### Qualities Required for Battle-Tested Socialist Leadership

- Bush argued that candidates for socialist office must have already been tested, meaning they must have faced a moment where the easier, more financially rewarding path was available and chose the harder path aligned with their values instead.

- Pressure tactics encountered within 48 hours of taking office were described, including unsolicited wire transfers with strings attached and threats that bills would not advance out of committee if certain votes were not cast, all of which Bush refused.

- Standing firm on a no vote despite repeated calls from colleagues and their networks was described as the moment that established her reputation as someone whose principles could not be bought or pressured.

- Candidates were advised to know what they are willing to risk and lose before running, so that when lies are planted, stories are fabricated, and even allies turn against them, they have the internal foundation to remain standing.

- Rabb affirmed that battle-tested character is more important than polish, formal education, or eloquence, and that the people who can be trusted in the trenches are those who spoke up for what was right when there was nothing to gain and everything to lose.

- A warning was issued against performative candidates who join the movement when it becomes popular, with Rabb citing specific examples of politicians he did not trust despite enthusiasm from white progressive circles, including concerns about racial politics and track records that did not match rhetoric.

- Harriet Tubman was invoked again as the ultimate example of looking beyond the package, noting that by conventional measures she would have been dismissed as incapable, yet she became one of the most extraordinary figures in the movement's history.

- The principle that candidates must have skin in the game was described as the only reliable safeguard against the pattern of elected officials liquidating into right-wing elements of the Congressional Black Caucus or accepting APAC money after claiming radical values.

### Next Steps

- DSA members were urged to sign up for phone banking shifts for Cori Bush's August 4th election, with the goal of reaching voters across Missouri's first congressional district including areas that voted for her opponent in the previous race.

- Donations to the Socialist Cash federal slate were solicited, with the call having raised $6,400 and the campaign approaching $100,000 raised for the year, with contributions directed to both Bush's campaign and Oliver Larkin's campaign.

- DSA members interested in electoral organizing were encouraged to join the National Electoral Commission to vote on endorsements, interview candidates, host phone banks, and build skills as part of a coast-to-coast socialist electoral infrastructure.

- The broader plan to grow DSA's socialist bloc in Congress was affirmed, with the incoming cohort on January 3rd described as significantly larger and positioned to work collectively with DSA on legislative priorities under the Workers Deserve More program.


### Themes
### Key Points
- Purpose of the webinar: DSA's National Electoral Commission hosts a panel with congressional candidates Cori Bush and Chris Rabb to connect the Black radical tradition with socialist organizing, and to fundraise for the Socialist Cash federal slate. 
- Both panelists trace their political foundations to family histories of Black resistance — Rabb through genealogy and abolitionist ancestors, Bush through her father's direct community activism and civil rights family ties. 
- Rabb argued that the roots of socialism predate the 19th century, are collectivist and West African in origin, and that racial capitalism — built on the construct of whiteness — must be centered in any honest socialist analysis. 
- Bush grounded her socialist values in lived experience: wage inequality, housing instability, and hunger, framing personal hardship as the source of her commitment to fighting for universal housing, healthcare, and fair wages. 
- Harriet Tubman was highlighted as a defining model for the movement — a single person who built structures of liberation under extreme risk, used as a touchstone for sustaining commitment when the work becomes exhausting. 
- Rabb emphasized that all DSA members, regardless of bloodline, are inheritors of the Black radical tradition, and that embodying — not just referencing — that legacy is what drives meaningful political action. 
- DSA's role in preserving and spreading these traditions was identified as critical: with 100,000 members surpassing the peak of the Socialist Party of America, the organization has unique capacity to produce and democratically distribute political knowledge. 
- Rabb stressed that the Black radical tradition must be the spine — not a chapter — of DSA's mission, and noted he only pursued DSA endorsement after reparations was added to the application, citing it as inseparable from collective liberation. 
- Bush called on DSA to serve as a counter-narrative infrastructure — educating the public on Medicare for All, abolition, anti-imperialism, and the true cost of military spending, particularly connecting AIPAC funding to diverted community resources. 
- On electoral strategy, Rabb outlined a method of naming capitalism explicitly in corporate media spaces where it goes undefined, arguing that connecting socialist policy to people's felt needs — living wages, ending endless wars, Gaza — is both principled and electorally effective. 
- Bush proposed creating a formal DSA-congressional caucus structure modeled on her Equal Rights Amendment Caucus, which became the fifth largest in Congress within three months by providing members with ready research and briefings. 
- Battle-testing was identified as the essential quality for trustworthy socialist candidates — both speakers warned against performative alignment, citing examples of politicians who quote radical figures but capitulate under pressure or accept AIPAC money. 
- Bush described specific pressure tactics used against her within 48 hours of taking office — unsolicited wire transfers, vote-trading threats, and intimidation calls — framing resistance to these as the proof of genuine political integrity. 
- Rabb reinforced that organized people beat organized money, pointing to his $1.2 million grassroots fundraise built from $1–$5 donations as evidence that enthusiasm and people power outperform capital in movement elections. 
- DSA's Socialist Cash campaign raised $6,400 on this call alone, its most successful to date, with phone banking for Bush's August 4th primary identified as the immediate priority — 15 days remaining, open to DSA members nationwide.
