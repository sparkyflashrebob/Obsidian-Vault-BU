## Document Overview & Study Notes
- **Comprehensive Report**: [[Concentration_In_Buddhist_Lists|Detailed Canonical Report: Concentration Across the Buddhist Lists]]
- **Focus**: Right Concentration (*Sammā Samādhi*) as the culminating limb of the Noble Eightfold Path—explicitly designated under the Four Noble Truths as something to be systematically developed and cultivated (*bhāvetabbaṃ*)—and its structural conditionality across five foundational Buddhist matrices.
- **Key Lists & Concepts**: 
  - **Jhānic Factors (*Jhānaṅga*)**: Aiming (*Vitakka*), Sustaining (*Vicāra*), Rapture (*Pīti*), Happiness (*Sukha*), One-pointedness (*Ekaggatā*).
  - **Noble Eightfold Path (*Ariya Aṭṭhaṅgika Magga*)**: Right View through Right Mindfulness as the essential supports and requisites (*saparikkhāra*) for Right Concentration (*Sammā Samādhi*).
  - **Five Spiritual Faculties & Powers (*Indriya & Bala*)**: Faith (*Saddhā*), Energy (*Viriya*), Mindfulness (*Sati*), Concentration (*Samādhi*), Wisdom (*Paññā*)—with mindfulness balancing energy and concentration.
  - **Seven Factors of Awakening (*Satta Bojjhaṅga*)**: Mindfulness, Investigation, Energy, Rapture, Tranquility, Concentration, Equanimity—balancing mental sluggishness with active factors and mental agitation with calming factors.
  - **Transcendent Dependent Origination (*Lokuttara Paṭiccasamuppāda*)**: Suffering -> Faith -> Joy -> Rapture -> Tranquility -> Happiness -> **Concentration** -> Knowledge & Vision of things as they are -> Disenchantment -> Dispassion -> Emancipation -> Knowledge of destruction of the cankers.

## Notable Quotes & Citations
- *"For one who is happy, the body is tranquil; for one whose body is tranquil, there is happiness; for one who is happy, the mind concentrates; for one whose mind is concentrated, there is the knowledge and vision of things as they truly are."* — *Upanisa Sutta* (SN 12.23)
- *"One-pointedness of mind equipped with these seven factors is called noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites."* — *Mahācattārīsaka Sutta* (MN 117)
- *"When struck by an arrow, the untrained person sorrows, grieves, and laments... Thus they feel two feelings—a bodily one and a mental one. It is as if a man were pierced by an arrow, and following that, immediately pierced by a second arrow."* — *Sallatha Sutta* (SN 36.6)
