# Tab 1

The core exercise of the *Drig-Drishya Viveka* aligns directly with the **Bāhiya Sutta** ( *Udāna 1.10* ), one of the most revered and minimalist teachings on direct liberation in early Buddhism.

While Advaita Vedanta and Theravada Buddhism hold different ultimate metaphysical positions, they converge perfectly on a practical level when it comes to the immediate deconstruction of experience. Both use radical discrimination to dismantle the ego's habit of claiming ownership over reality.

##### 1. The Bāhiya Formula: Direct Sensory Isolation

In the sutta, the Buddha delivers a swift, unelaborated instruction to the ascetic Bāhiya of the Bark-garment:

*"In the seen, there will be only the seen. In the heard, there will be only the heard. In the sensed, there will be only the sensed. In the cognized, there will be only the cognized."*

This formula functions exactly like the step-by-step logic of the *Drig-Drishya Viveka* :

**Stripping the Overlays:** Both teachings strip away mental commentary. When you isolate the "seen to the seen" or the "object to the object," you strip away the secondary conceptual projections ( *vrittis* or *papañca* ).

**Breaking the Habit of Grasping:** The moment a visual form arises, it is just visual form. When a thought arises in the mind, it is just an object of cognition.

##### 2. Eliminating the "Seer" vs. Locating the "Seer"

This is where the two traditions share an identical *method* but use different language to describe the result:

**The Advaita Approach:** The *Drig-Drishya Viveka* tells you to move inward, realizing that you are not the objects, the eyes, or the mind. It isolates a final, immutable Subject (the *Drig* , or *Atman* ) that witnesses it all.

**The Buddhist Approach:** The Buddha tells Bāhiya that when the seen is *just* the seen, *"then, Bāhiya, there is no 'you' in connection with that. When there is no 'you' there, you are neither here nor there nor in between. This, just this, is the end of suffering."*

**The Convergence:** By seeing everything as a passing object, the false sense of a solid, localized ego is dissolved. Whether you describe this state as discovering **Pure Subjectivity** (Advaita) or realizing **Anattā/Non-Self** (Theravada), the experiential reality is the same: the illusion of a separate "me" operating inside the body-mind vanishes.

##### 3. Evading the Ego Trap

Both teachings place an identical warning flag right at the edge of insight.

As noted in the video, the moment the mind claims an insight—saying, *"I am pure awareness"* —the ego ( *ahankara* ) has hijacked the teaching and made awareness into a concept.

The Bāhiya Sutta cuts this trap off at the root. By ensuring that in the cognized there is *only* the cognized, there is no room for the mind to construct an "I" who is doing the cognizing. The thought "I am observing this" is recognized simply as another passing phenomenon—just a *cognized object* floating through space.

##### Comparative Summary

| **Metric / Aspect** | **Drig-Drishya Viveka (Advaita)** | **Bāhiya Sutta (Theravada)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Method** | Discriminate the Seer from the Seen. | Rest strictly in the raw data of the senses. |
| **Handling the Mind** | Treat thoughts and insights as objects. | In the cognized, let there be only the cognized. |
| **The Ego Trap** | *Ahankara* claiming "I am the witness." | Creating a "you" in connection with experience. |
| **The Ultimate View** | You are the ultimate, unchanging Subject. | There is no solid, inherent Subject to be found ( *Anattā* ). |
