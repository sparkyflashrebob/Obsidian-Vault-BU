If my thoughts create my destiny, how does colonial trauma affect this?

Model: TL;DR: The popular concept that "thoughts create destiny" is an individualistic oversimplification when viewed alongside colonial trauma. While New Age paradigms often imply absolute personal autonomy over reality, ancient traditions (Theravada, Advaita Vedanta, and Indigenous philosophies) recognize that our minds do not exist in a vacuum. Instead, individual thoughts are deeply conditioned by collective, intergenerational, and historical currents. Colonial trauma shapes the foundational sankharas (mental formations), ancestral fields, and neurological baselines from which thoughts arise, meaning destiny is a co-creative process between historical conditioning and present-moment awareness.
The Clash of Frameworks: Individualistic vs. Collective Reality
The idea that "your thoughts create your destiny" often originates from a hyper-individualistic framework. It assumes a level playing field where every mind is a blank slate, free from history.
When colonial trauma is introduced—which operates as a systemic, multigenerational injury to a collective's culture, language, land connection, and psyche—the individualistic model falls short. It risks "spiritual bypassing" or victim-blaming by implying that those carrying historical trauma are simply choosing the wrong thoughts.
Theravada & Advaita Perspectives: The Conditioned Mind-Stream
Looking through the lens of Eastern psychology, the relationship between thought, karma, and historical trauma becomes much clearer.
Theravada Buddhism (Sankharas): In early Buddhist psychology, thoughts do not appear out of nowhere; they arise based on sankharas (mental formations or habitual tendencies). Colonial trauma can be understood as a massive, collective sankhara passed down through generations. It shapes the deep-seated conditioning—such as hyper-vigilance, unworthiness, or a sense of scarcity—that dictates which thoughts naturally arise in the first place. Destiny is created not just by random thoughts, but by how consciously you relate to this inherited conditioning.
Advaita Vedanta (Prarabdha Karma & Maya): Advaita teaches that the individual mind (jiva) operates within Maya (the cosmic illusion), bound by Prarabdha Karma—the specific cosmic and ancestral trajectory assigned for this lifetime. Colonial trauma is part of this collective karmic fabric. While your ultimate identity is pure, unconditioned consciousness (Atman), your temporary psychological vehicle is deeply shaped by the historical currents of the world it inhabits.
Indigenous & Earth-Based Perspectives: The Ancestral Web
Indigenous paradigms offer perhaps the most direct challenge to the solitary "manifestation" model, replacing it with an interconnected, relational view of time and mind.
Epigenetics meets Intergenerational Memory: Indigenous philosophies have long taught what modern science is only recently verifying through epigenetics: we carry the experiences, grief, and resilience of our ancestors in our literal bodies.
The Seven Generations Principle: In many Native American traditions, decisions and thoughts are evaluated by how they impact seven generations into the future, acknowledging that we are simultaneously living out the choices of the seven generations behind us.
Web of Relation: From an earth-based perspective, "destiny" is not an individual trophy won by pristine thinking. It is an ongoing relationship with land, lineage, and community. Colonial trauma broke these vital relationships, fracturing the mirror through which the mind understands itself. Therefore, healing the thoughts requires healing the relations.
Stoic Realism: The Dichotomy of Control
Stoicism provides a highly practical framework for reconciling structural trauma with personal agency.
The Given Landscape (Adiaphora): Stoics categorize historical events, systemic structures, and inherited genetic or psychological baselines as adiaphora (indifferent things outside our direct control). You did not choose the colonial history that shaped your lineage or your nervous system.
The Power of Assent (Sunkatothesis): Where your destiny actually shifts is in your assent to the thoughts generated by that trauma. The trauma may automate a thought of fear or powerlessness, but Stoicism argues that your true character—and ultimate destiny—is determined by whether you choose to align with that automated thought or step back and examine it with reason.
Integrating the Two: Healing the Conduit
If colonial trauma conditions the mind, how do you actually reclaim your destiny?
Shift from Content to Context: Instead of aggressively trying to force "positive thoughts" over a landscape of historical grief, focus on becoming the spacious, compassionate awareness witnessing the trauma responses.
Somatic Reclamation: Because colonial trauma lives in the nervous system as survival physiology, changing your destiny requires moving beyond cognitive thought. Grounding practices, breath, and reconnecting with the earth help discharge the historical survival energy that feeds anxious or disempowered thought loops.
Co-Creative Agency: Realize that you are not failing at manifestation if heavy thoughts arise. You are simply processing a larger share of the collective human cross. Every time you meet an inherited trauma-thought with mindfulness and presence, you alter the karmic trajectory for yourself and the ancestors walking with you.
