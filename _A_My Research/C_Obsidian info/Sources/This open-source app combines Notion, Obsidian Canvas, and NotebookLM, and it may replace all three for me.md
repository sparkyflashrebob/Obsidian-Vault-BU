  There are so many productivity apps out there that many of them can start to blur together. And every couple of months, I discover new productivity tools that all promise to change everything. Many just end up giving me more work because there are so many features and functions to navigate. But a few have actually stuck and become part of my daily workflow. One of them is AFFiNE.
  This open-source, local-first project and knowledge management app has pretty much replaced Notion for me. It [does everything Notion does](https://www.xda-developers.com/open-source-app-like-notion-but-better/) - databases, documents, tasks - and then some. It doubles as a creative canvas, kind of like Obsidian Canvas but better, and its context-aware AI feels a bit like a [NotebookLM alternative](https://www.xda-developers.com/notebooklm-self-hosted-alternative-keep-data-control/).

#### What is AFFiNE?

##### A unified space for notes, projects, and tasks

AFFiNE is an [open-source knowledge management and productivity platform](https://www.xda-developers.com/self-hosted-alternative-notion-best-pkm-tool/) built around a hybrid workspace of documents, whiteboards, lists, and databases, all in one place. It’s still in the early development stage, but it already feels more mature than half of the commercial apps out there. You can write anything from short notes to structured drafts (which you can export as Markdown files), create task lists and Kanban boards, brainstorm on an infinitely-scrollable whiteboard with comprehensive design features, and you can connect everything seamlessly. It uses the slash command for all block inserts like lists, headers, tables, and so on.
Everything runs locally by default so your data is yours, but you can also sync to the cloud. There are paid tiers that give you more cloud storage, larger file uploads, and enhanced AI capabilities. It’s suitable for anyone; from creatives to researchers and heavy knowledge workers. Personally, I mainly use it for my writing work and design projects, as well as managing some research. Here’s why I think it could be a replacement for Notion, Obsidian Canvas, and NotebookLM…

#### It’s structured like Notion

##### But less suffocating

Notion’s biggest strength is structure - especially when it comes to databases. But that’s also its downfall, in my opinion, because its databases [can be complicated to navigate](https://www.xda-developers.com/tips-to-make-notion-less-overwhelming/). AFFiNE takes that same idea but makes it feel lighter. You can still build project trackers and content calendars, but it’s without the nested friction that can make Notion feel like a chore.
You can turn existing text into a database by highlighting it and selecting **Change to Table** . Or use the slash command to create a table or Kanban board from there. Each database functions like a flexible unit where you can sort, filter, and view items any way you like. It doesn’t have Notion’s complex rollups or multi-level relations, but it does have linking and filtering tools that cover most use cases. For example, **Add Filter > Contains One Of** surfaces specific items in your tables. In the Kanban view, you can drag and rearrange the cards wherever you want. And you can create linked documents by highlighting the text and selecting **Create Linked Doc** .
This pretty much replaces Notion’s database functions for me. Sure, I have fewer options, but that’s exactly why I prefer it - it's much quicker to navigate.

#### A whiteboard like Obsidian Canvas

##### But it’s more well-rounded

AFFiNE has a whiteboard tool called Edgeless. It’s exactly what it sounds like - an edgeless, infinitely scrollable canvas. I’ve written before about how this is a [viable replacement for Obsidian’s Canvas](https://www.xda-developers.com/open-source-tool-replaced-obsidian-canvas/) tool. Obsidian Canvas is great for laying out your documents in a visual format and connecting them, but Edgeless just gives you much better features to work with.
First of all, you can seamlessly switch between Document view and Edgeless view within any document. Switching to Edgeless nests your document as an editable element on the canvas - Obsidian doesn’t have this function. Then there are the tools; you’ve got frames, connecting arrows, sticky notes, a pen and eraser, illustrations, and even premade templates. And everything is editable by size and color. I’ve said before that it reminds me a bit of Canva, because you can go all out with the visual demonstrations here.

#### It’s smart like NotebookLM

##### But keeps everything local

AFFiNE’s AI assistant is one of my favorite parts about it. Like NotebookLM, it’s built on the RAG framework (Retrieval-augmented Generation), which means it searches and references your own workspace before generating responses. If I were to ask it to “summarize my last three design notes”, it will pull directly from those documents - not hallucinate an answer. It’s also context-aware and capable of giving you different perspectives. So if I want to retrieve and change the tone of something I've written, it will give me suggestions. And of course, it’s also great at summarizing longer documents so you can get a quick overview of your notes.
The cool thing is that it includes the sources it referenced its answers from, just like NotebookLM. Furthermore, it lets you add more reference materials, including plain text files, PDF files, and images. It also has some real-time web search capabilities, which [NotebookLM also started incorporating this year](https://www.xda-developers.com/notebooklm-search-web-research-made-easy/). AFFiNE’s AI isn’t perfect yet, and not quite as polished as NotebookLM, but it’s more grounded than any other assistant I’ve used in a productivity app. Plus, all your chats are stored locally, not on Google's servers.

##### AFFiNE can do it all

This year, AFFiNE became my go-to productivity tool. It’s more like an all-in-one creative hub for how I use it. It’s not flawless yet; some of the features are still evolving, and they have only just started experimenting with plugins. But it’s one of the few open-source tools that feels modern and approachable. It can do what Notion, Canvas, and NotebookLM do in one workspace, and it might replace all three for me.
