# Fidelity seminar bonds yields and markets update - Jul, 15

### Fidelity Seminar: Bond Yields and Markets Update
📅 July 15, 2026
👥 Karen (Presenter), Kate (Host/Moderator)
### Outline
### Fundamentals of Bond Investing

- A bond represents a lending relationship where an investor provides capital, typically $1,000, to a borrower with the expectation of receiving the principal back at the end of the term along with periodic coupon payments.

- Coupon payments are determined based on the prevailing interest rate environment and the credit quality of the borrower, reflecting their willingness and likelihood to repay.

- Analysis of the bond issuer, whether a government, corporation, or institution, forms the backbone of day-to-day fixed income investment activity.

- Individual bonds are aggregated into baskets of securities such as ETFs, separately managed accounts, and mutual funds, which are the primary vehicles through which investors access fixed income markets.

### Bond Pricing and Yield Dynamics

- Yield serves as the primary metric for evaluating a bond, with price adjusting over time to reflect the prevailing interest rate environment.

- An inverse relationship exists between yield and price, meaning that as yields fall, bond prices rise, and as yields rise, bond prices decline.

- A bond market rally is defined as a period in which yields fall and bond prices or the value of a basket of bonds increases accordingly.

- Reinvestment risk refers to the uncertainty of whether returned capital can be reinvested at a higher or lower interest rate, which directly affects total portfolio returns.

- Inflation erodes the purchasing power of fixed income returns, as illustrated by a scenario where a 4.5% Treasury yield is largely consumed by inflation running at 3 to 3.5% annually.

- Additional risks in fixed income include the possibility of bonds being called or prepaid, which introduces similar challenges to reinvestment risk.

### Credit Quality and Risk Assessment

- Credit risk encompasses the spectrum between full repayment and default, and analysts continuously assess the financial health and willingness of issuers to meet their obligations.

- Liquidity risk relates to the ability to sell a bond quickly at the expected price in order to facilitate portfolio reallocation.

- Currency risk may be present in certain portfolios that hold bonds denominated in foreign currencies, and this must be clearly disclosed in the investment's prospectus.

- Credit ratings are assigned by nationally recognized statistical rating organizations including Moody's, Standard and Poor's, and Fitch, with AAA representing the highest quality.

- The distinction between BBB-rated and BB-rated bonds marks the boundary between investment grade and non-investment grade, or high yield, categories, reflecting a significant difference in debt levels relative to cash flow.

- Active managers derive their own internal credit ratings and assess valuations relative to their perceived risk, which may differ from the ratings assigned by external agencies.

### The Role of Fixed Income in a Portfolio

- Fixed income provides liquidity primarily through money market instruments, where principal is not placed at risk and investors expect to receive back the full amount invested while earning income.

- Diversification benefits arise from fixed income's tendency to behave differently from equities and private instruments across varying market environments.

- Tax advantages can be achieved through the use of specific types of fixed income securities, particularly municipal bonds, depending on the investor's tax bracket and state of residence.

- Income generation, or coupon clipping, is the primary driver of total return in fixed income, as demonstrated by the dominance of income return over price return in long-term index performance.

- Price return in fixed income was significantly eroded during the rapid rate increase following the pandemic period through mid-2023, underscoring the importance of income as the stabilizing component of total return.

- Adding even a modest allocation of Treasuries to a portfolio of stocks and cash improves total return while reducing overall volatility, illustrating the risk-management value of fixed income.

### Fixed Income Instruments and Duration

- High yield corporate bonds offer a yield to worst of approximately 6.7% as of June 2024, while emerging market debt yields approximately 6%, placing both in the more aggressive income category.

- Core fixed income instruments include investment grade corporates at 4.8%, mortgage-backed securities at 4.7%, Treasuries at 3.9%, and municipal bonds at 3.7%.

- Duration measures the sensitivity of a bond or portfolio to changes in interest rates, with high yield corporates carrying a lower duration of 2.7 years due to higher income and shorter issuance terms.

- Investment grade corporate bonds typically carry a 10-year issuance term, resulting in approximately six years of duration, reflecting the longer-term nature of higher-quality borrowers.

- Multi-sector portfolio managers utilize the full range of fixed income instruments in a balanced manner to achieve strong risk-adjusted returns.

### Tax Advantages of Municipal Bonds

- Municipal bonds offer tax-exempt income, and their taxable equivalent yield can exceed 7% for investors in the highest tax bracket holding 30-year maturities.

- The attractiveness of municipal bonds depends on the investor's state of residence, income level, and overall tax situation, making them a highly individualized tool in portfolio construction.

- Current conditions, including elevated income levels and the likelihood of future tax increases, make the consideration of municipal bonds particularly relevant for taxable investors.

### Macroeconomic Environment and Key Shocks

- The U.S. economy has demonstrated resilience despite a series of significant shocks beginning in 2025, including changes in immigration policy, federal government efficiency initiatives, tariffs, and a government shutdown.

- Legislative changes introduced through the One Big Beautiful Bill in 2025 brought important shifts in tax policy that continue to influence the economic environment.

- Current year developments include the Iran conflict and other geopolitical tensions, the declaration of certain tariffs as illegal, changes in Federal Reserve leadership, and the acceleration of AI infrastructure investment.

- Private credit has gained increased prominence in financial markets, and the current year also represents a midterm election cycle, adding further complexity to the macroeconomic backdrop.

- The overall structural characterization of the current environment is one of persistent inflation and an unusual labor market dynamic.

### Oil Prices and the Strait of Hormuz

- Brent crude oil was trading at approximately $84 per barrel as of the meeting date, up from a low of $72 at the end of June, while West Texas Intermediate was near $80, up from $69.

- Oil price movements are more acutely felt in Asia and the Middle East than in the United States, as most of the supply flowing through the Strait of Hormuz serves those regions.

- Beyond oil, the Strait of Hormuz is a critical passage for goods including urea, sulfur, and fertilizer feedstock, meaning any disruption would affect global food prices.

- Helium, which flows through the strait, is essential to semiconductor manufacturing and therefore directly tied to the AI infrastructure buildout and the continued growth of technology companies.

- Chips are increasingly viewed as the next oil in terms of their centrality to the global economy, with Taiwan playing a particularly important role in their production.

### Fiscal Policy, Debt, and the One Big Beautiful Bill

- The United States carries a significant budget deficit, and various think tanks project differing estimates of the national debt trajectory going forward.

- The White House Council of Economic Advisers targets 3.5% annual GDP growth as a means of making the debt and deficit more manageable over time.

- Tariff revenue was intended to serve as a fiscal offset following the extension of prior tax rates under the One Big Beautiful Bill, replacing revenue that would have been generated by allowing those rates to sunset.

- Tariff collections, shifts in tariff policy, and the pass-through of tariff costs to consumer prices are being closely monitored as key variables in the inflation and fiscal outlook.

### Federal Reserve Leadership and Policy Direction

- Kevin Warsh returned to the Federal Reserve as chairman effective May of the current year, replacing Chair Powell, who remains as a member of the Board of Governors.

- Warsh is regarded as an inflation hawk, meaning he prioritizes price stability and is more willing to use policy tools to combat inflation than to stimulate growth or employment.

- Five task forces were established by Warsh upon assuming the chairmanship, focused on Fed communications, balance sheet management, data utilization, productivity and AI, and inflation.

- Despite expectations that Warsh would immediately vote to lower rates given his selection by the administration, his early actions and statements have demonstrated a more cautious and independent approach.

- The Federal Reserve operates as a committee of voters rather than a single decision-maker, and Warsh's early communications appear aimed at aligning the committee around his priorities.

- Warsh has emphasized that inflation has been too high for the past five years and signaled that the Fed intends to stay ahead of inflationary pressures rather than fall behind them.

### Inflation Trends and Consumer Impact

- Inflation peaked and has been influenced by energy prices, though no wage-price spiral has emerged, which would be a more entrenched and difficult condition to reverse.

- Energy prices are particularly concerning because they affect the cost of transportation and serve as an input across a wide range of goods and services, amplifying inflationary pressure throughout the economy.

- CPI data as of May showed energy as the primary driver of inflation, though the most recent report indicated a softening of that pressure.

- A steady downward path toward the Fed's 2% inflation target has not been achieved since the pandemic, as different sectors of the economy have continued to generate inflationary impulses.

- The misery index has climbed to a three-year high, reflecting the cumulative burden of elevated inflation on consumers over the past five years rather than just recent monthly changes.

- Consumer spending has remained resilient despite dissatisfaction with prices, though the strain on household budgets is becoming increasingly real and visible in purchasing behavior.

### Labor Market Conditions

- The Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey shows a sharp decline from a post-pandemic peak of two job openings per available worker to approximately one-to-one at present.

- During the great financial crisis, the ratio was inverted, with fewer than half a job available per worker, representing a dramatically weaker labor market than today.

- Demographic factors, particularly the aging of the baby boomer cohort, have contributed to structural changes in labor force participation and the worker-to-population ratio.

- Pandemic-era workforce exits have not been fully reversed, resulting in lower labor force participation than pre-pandemic levels and a tighter overall employment environment.

- Companies are expected to be more reluctant to use layoffs as a cost management tool given the difficulty of replacing workers in a tight labor market, representing a shift from prior business cycle behavior.

### AI Investment and Fixed Income Markets

- Hyperscaler technology companies have been issuing investment grade corporate bonds in large volumes this year as part of their AI infrastructure financing strategy.

- Initial expectations that this surge in supply would widen corporate bond spreads have not materialized, as demand for fixed income has kept pace with the increased issuance.

- These companies are financing AI capital expenditure through a combination of cash, equity, and debt in order to minimize their weighted average cost of capital, consistent with standard corporate finance practice.

- Concerns in equity markets that debt issuance signals financial stress or a potential bubble are not shared from a fixed income perspective, where this activity is viewed as a natural lengthening of the AI investment cycle.

### Yield Curve Analysis and Historical Context

- During the tech bubble, the Federal Reserve lowered its policy rate to support the economy, with the primary impact felt at the front end of the yield curve.

- The great financial crisis prompted the Fed to cut rates from over 5% to between 1 and 2%, with the impact felt across the entire curve due to the severity of the financial disruption.

- During the pandemic, the Fed cut rates by 200 basis points to near zero, resulting in a parallel downward shift across the entire yield curve, an historically unusual outcome.

- The current yield curve as of late June shows a normalized upward slope, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.56% and the 2-year Treasury at approximately 4.14 to 4.15%, representing a steepening curve.

- Market expectations for Fed policy have shifted dramatically from pricing in two to two and a half rate cuts at the start of the year to now pricing in a potential rate hike by year end.

- The longer end of the yield curve is being priced by the bond market based on inflation expectations, geopolitical risks, and concerns about debt and deficits rather than Fed policy alone.

### Fixed Income Investment Strategy and Positioning

- The relationship between money market yields and the Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Bond Index yield, which previously favored extending duration, has become far less clear in the current environment.

- A risk-reward analysis across fixed income instruments favors the middle to shorter end of the duration spectrum, where income can be captured without taking on excessive interest rate risk.

- Using the 2-year Treasury as an example, the range of total return outcomes spans from approximately 2.3% on the downside to 6.1% on the upside, representing an attractive profile for fixed income investors.

- Corporate bond spreads for both investment grade and high yield are currently tight relative to historical medians, warranting selectivity and careful valuation discipline in credit allocation.

- Municipal bond ratios relative to Treasuries are historically tight, but the value of the tax exemption remains high given elevated income levels and the expectation that tax rates are more likely to rise than fall.

- A preference for shorter duration bonds in the one to three or one to five year maturity range is recommended in the current environment, as the yield curve has steepened meaningfully at the very front end in recent weeks.

- Multi-sector active management is favored as it provides portfolio managers with the full toolkit of fixed income instruments needed to generate optimal total returns across varying market conditions.

### Sensitivity of Bond Valuations to Rate Changes

- Bond valuations are highly sensitive to future rate decisions by the Federal Reserve, particularly for longer-maturity instruments where duration amplifies the price impact of rate movements.

- Inflation remains the most confounding variable in forecasting Fed policy, making it difficult to predict the direction and timing of rate changes with confidence.

- Labor market trends are viewed as more predictable and generally positive, while inflation continues to introduce significant uncertainty into the rate outlook.

- The recommendation to favor shorter maturities reflects the principle of leaning into what is known rather than unknown, reducing exposure to the unpredictable longer end of the rate environment.

### Next Steps

- Investors are encouraged to reassess their fixed income allocations with a preference for shorter duration instruments in the one to three year maturity range and higher credit quality.

- Consideration of municipal bonds as part of taxable portfolios is recommended given the current value of the tax exemption and the likelihood of future tax increases.

- Ongoing monitoring of tariff collections, Federal Reserve communications, oil prices, and inflation data is identified as essential to navigating the fixed income market in the near term.

- Attendees are directed to download the presentation materials and to explore additional webinars and coaching sessions available through Fidelity's online platform.
