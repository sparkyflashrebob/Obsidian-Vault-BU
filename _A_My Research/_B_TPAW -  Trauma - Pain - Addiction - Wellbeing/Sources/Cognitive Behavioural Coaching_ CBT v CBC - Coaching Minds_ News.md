##### **💫 Edition 35 of Coachology in Practice**

Written by Paul Smith, Managing Director
**So… how much psychology does a coach really need?**
And where does coaching end and therapy begin?
Today we will explore the concept of Cognitive Behavioural Coaching and how it differs from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy. Over the next 15 editions we will build your coaching toolkit with psychological concepts, theories, and models we can apply to our coaching.
At the heart of Cognitive Coaching sits a simple but powerful idea: **it's not what happens to us that causes suffering, but how we interpret it** . Take flying, for example. The plane isn't the problem; it's the story we tell ourselves about the plane. Fear and excitement sit uncomfortably close together; our thoughts decide which one turns up. In the words of the infamous Captain Jack Sparrow:
**“The problem is not the problem. The problem is your attitude about the problem.”**
Cognitive Coaching also reminds us that while **pain is inevitable, suffering is optional** . A large proportion of human suffering is created in the mind, replaying the past, catastrophising the future, or clinging tightly to unhelpful thoughts. As coaches, our role isn't to erase pain but to help clients see their thinking more clearly and choose a different response. Change the thought, and behaviour often follows.

##### **Cognitive Behavioural Coaching (CBC)**

Cognitive Behavioural Coaching borrows heavily from **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)** , the tools, the structure, and the focus on thinking patterns, but the purpose is very different.
CBT is a form of psychotherapy used to treat diagnosable mental health conditions such as depression and anxiety. It requires clinical training, diagnosis, and a clear duty of care. **Coaching does not.**
CBC works with psychologically well-functioning individuals who want to improve performance, well-being, or perspective. We are not treating illness; we are supporting growth. Same toolbox, different job description.
This distinction becomes especially important given the lingering stigma around therapy. Some clients may choose coaching because it feels safer or less threatening. That doesn't mean coaching replaces therapy, but it can offer structured, ethical support when used appropriately and within scope.

##### **Our Role as Coaches Using CBC**

Human behaviour is influenced by environment, physiology, thoughts, and emotions. Yet many clients believe events cause their reactions. Coaching gently challenges this assumption.
Clients often focus on the **effect** (“This is awful”) rather than the **cause of their suffering** (“What am I telling myself about this?”). Our work is not to analyse why something happened, but to explore how the client is interpreting it.
Take redundancy. Losing a job is difficult, but the suffering often comes from the internal narrative:
*“I've failed. I'll lose everything. My family will leave me.”*
The job loss isn't the coaching focus, the thinking is. When the perspective shifts, new options emerge:
*“This is hard, but it's also a turning point. I can regroup and move forward.”*
From there, action becomes possible.
As Cognitive Behavioural Coaches, we explore the relationship between **thoughts, emotions, and behaviour** , helping clients recognise patterns, question assumptions, and experiment with new perspectives.

##### **Using CBT Tools in Coaching (Without Becoming a Therapist)**

Using CBT tools in coaching requires discipline. We are not reducing symptoms or diagnosing, we are facilitating insight.
A simple framework:
1. Identify an unhelpful thought or perspective
1. Offer a relevant tool
1. Use it **only if the client agrees**
No forcing. No convincing. If a client declines a tool, we continue coaching. Models are invitations, not prescriptions.
And finally, a reminder we all need from time to time: **we are facilitators, not fixers** . The awareness must come from the client, not our clever interpretations. Stay within scope, stay curious, and let the tools serve the conversation, not dominate it.
We will be exploring a lot of tools over the next 15 weeks, so remember to subscribe and follow to build your psychological coaching toolkit.
