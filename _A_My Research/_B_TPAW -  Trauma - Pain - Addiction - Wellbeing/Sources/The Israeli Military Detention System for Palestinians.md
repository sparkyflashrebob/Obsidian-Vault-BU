Palestinians are politically detained by Israeli authorities primarily through sweeping military orders in the occupied territories. This system allows for indefinite imprisonment without trial, systematic nighttime arrests, military trials with minimal due process, and harsh conditions that human rights organizations identify as widespread abuse. [1, 2, 3, 4]

Administrative Detention (Imprisonment Without Charge)

Indefinite Terms: Israel uses "administrative detention" to hold thousands of Palestinians—both adults and children—for months or years without formal charges or trial.

Secret Evidence: Detention orders are based on "secret evidence" that is withheld from both the detainees and their defense lawyers, making it impossible to mount a proper defense.

Widespread Usage: Out of roughly 9,300 to 9,600 overall Palestinian political prisoners, over 3,300 are held on these indefinite administrative orders. [9, 10]

Arrest and Interrogation

Night Raids: Detainees are frequently arrested by heavily armed forces during violent night raids on their homes.

No Parent Present: Unlike standard civil juvenile laws, Israeli military law permits the interrogation of children as young as 12 without a lawyer or guardian present.

Coerced Confessions: Human rights groups document that detainees are routinely subjected to physical violence, blindfolding, and threats designed to coerce confessions, which are then used in military courts. [2, 6, 11]

Military Courts and Charges

Separate Legal Systems: Palestinians living in the West Bank are tried in Israeli military courts, while Israeli settlers in the same territory are subject to civil law.

High Conviction Rates: The military court system features an extraordinarily high conviction rate. The most common charge for children is stone-throwing, which under military law can carry sentences of up to 20 years in prison.

Post-Sentence Extension: In some documented cases, after children complete their sentences, authorities issue new administrative detention orders, effectively punishing them twice for the same alleged offense. [6]

Conditions and Abuse

Harsh Confinement: Palestinian prisoners face severe restrictions on family visits and lack proper access to legal counsel.

Torture Allegations: Released detainees, as well as United Nations bodies, have reported systemic abuse including starvation, medical neglect, solitary confinement, and sexual and physical violence inside Israeli detention facilities. [7, 14, 15, 16, 17]

For detailed documentation and up-to-date tracking of cases, you can consult reports from the Addameer Prisoner Support and Human Rights Association, the B'Tselem Information Center, and Defence for Children International Palestine.
