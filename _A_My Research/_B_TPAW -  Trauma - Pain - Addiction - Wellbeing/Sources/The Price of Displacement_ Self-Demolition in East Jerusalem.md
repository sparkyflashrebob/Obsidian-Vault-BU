Palestinians are frequently forced to demolish their own homes or pay exorbitant municipal fees for Israeli authorities to do so. This practice heavily targets Palestinians in occupied East Jerusalem and Area C of the West Bank under the premise of building without permits. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]

The "Self-Demolition" Policy

Palestinian residents routinely struggle to obtain building permits from the Jerusalem Municipality and Israeli civil administration, as permits are heavily restricted or virtually impossible to secure. When residents build without permits to accommodate their families, they receive demolition orders. [2, 4, 7, 8, 9]

To avoid the immense financial burden of the municipality bringing in bulldozers, police, and security forces, families often choose to demolish their own properties, a phenomenon known as "self-destruction". [2, 10, 11]

The Financial Burden

If a Palestinian resident refuses or fails to demolish their own property, Israeli authorities carry out the demolition and send the homeowner a massive bill.

Municipal Fees: Demolition fines and contractor bills can range from 40,000 to 100,000+ shekels (roughly $11,000 to $30,000 USD).

Added Expenses: If Israeli crews perform the destruction, families are often charged for the deployment of bulldozers, security forces, ambulances, and even guard dogs.

Self-Demolition Costs: Even hiring private equipment to self-demolish is highly expensive, but families do it to avoid the even steeper municipal fees. [12]

Broader Implications

Human rights organizations, including Human Rights Watch and the Israeli NGO B'Tselem, extensively document these practices. They cite them as part of a coercive environment aimed at displacing Palestinian populations and altering the demographic makeup of East Jerusalem. International bodies also monitor and provide cash and legal aid to mitigate this crisis. [5, 13, 14, 15]
