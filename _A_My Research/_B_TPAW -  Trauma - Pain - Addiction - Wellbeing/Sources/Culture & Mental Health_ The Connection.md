Culture & mental health: The connection
Mental health is a complex issue that can be affected by many different factors. While science has made progress in understanding the role of genes and biology in mental health, culture still plays a significant role in shaping how people think and feel about their mental health. It is important to understand culture’s role in mental health to create an inclusive environment that supports people of all backgrounds.
Defining culture
Culture is the set of beliefs, values, practices, and norms that characterize a group or society. It shapes our perceptions, behaviors, and ways of interacting with others. Culture influences how we see ourselves and the world around us. It can either be a source of strength or a source of stress. When it comes to mental health, culture can play a significant role. Mental health is shaped by our cultural beliefs, values, and norms. Our culture can impact the way we understand and cope with mental illness. It can also affect how we seek help and support.
Culture and mental health: What’s the connection?
Culture can either be a source of strength or a source of stress. It can provide us with a sense of belonging and support. It can also be a source of conflict and misunderstanding. When it comes to mental health, culture can have both positive and negative impacts.
Our cultural beliefs and values can affect how we see ourselves and the world around us.
Our worldview is essentially the lens through which we view the world. It is shaped by our cultural beliefs and values, and it can majorly impact our mental health. If we have a negative or distorted view of the world, it can lead to feelings of anxiety, depression, and isolation. On the other hand, having a positive or realistic worldview can help us cope with life’s challenges and appreciate the beauty around us.
Our cultural background can affect how we cope with stress and adversity.
In some cultures, it is more acceptable to express emotions openly, while in others, emotional restraint is the norm. This can affect how we cope with stressful situations and how well we bounce back from setbacks. Some cultures may also emphasize individualism, while others may emphasize the importance of community and interdependence. This can affect how we view ourselves about others, and how much support we seek when facing challenges.
Cultural factors can affect the prevalence and presentation of mental disorders.
Studies have shown that depression is more common in cultures that value individualism and achievement than in collectivist cultures. And while anxiety disorders are found across all cultures, they tend to be expressed differently in different cultures. In Western cultures, anxiety is often experienced as fear or dread. In contrast, in Eastern cultures, it may be more likely to be experienced as physical symptoms such as heart palpitations or dizziness.
Culture can influence how mental health problems are diagnosed and treated.
In the West, mental disorders are often seen as medical conditions that need to be treated with medication or other medical interventions. But in many traditional cultures, mental health problems are seen as spiritual issues that need to be addressed through religious or shamanic rituals.
Culture can affect our willingness to seek help for mental health problems.
In some cultures, mental health problems are seen as a sign of weakness, and people may be

Cultural factors impacting mental health
Mental health is shaped by cultural factors such as family background, religion, social norms, and traditions. Each of these factors can impact an individual’s mental health differently.
Family background
Studies have shown that people from families with a history of mental illness are more likely to develop mental health problems themselves. This is partly due to genetic factors, but it also reflects that families provide an important context for our early development. Our family members are the first people we learn to interact with, and they play a crucial role in shaping our social and emotional skills. If our family members are struggling with mental health issues, we may not learn how to manage our emotions effectively or cope with stress. As a result, we may be more likely to develop mental health problems later in life.
For many people, religion is an important source of comfort and support. It can provide a sense of community and belonging, as well as a framework for understanding the world. However, religion can also be a source of conflict and stress. People who belong to minority religions may feel alienated or persecuted. Those who have been raised in religious households may find it difficult to break away from the beliefs of their childhood. And even people who are comfortable with their religion can find that its beliefs conflict with their mental health. For example, some religious teachings suggest that mental illness is a sign of moral weakness or possession by evil spirits. This can make it difficult for people with mental illness to seek help because they may feel ashamed or believe their condition is not treatable.
Social norms
The way we are expected to behave in our culture can impact our mental health. In collectivist cultures, people are often expected to put the needs of their community or family above their own. This can lead to a sense of self-sacrifice that can be detrimental to mental health. People who always put others first may find it difficult to take care of themselves, and this can lead to feelings of resentment or burnout. In individualistic cultures, people are often expected to be self-reliant and independent. This can be a source of stress for people who feel they cannot meet these expectations.
Cultural traditions can also impact mental health. Some traditions, such as those around gender roles or marriage, may be harmful to mental health. For example, women who are expected to be submissive to their husbands may be at risk for domestic violence. People who are not allowed to marry the person they love may experience intense feelings of sadness and isolation. Other traditions, such as those around grief and loss, can be helpful to mental health. In many cultures, it is tradition to take time off from work or school to mourn the death of a loved one. This can give people the space they need to grieve in a healthy way.
Cultural competence in mental health care
Cultural competence is understanding, appreciating, and working with different cultures’ beliefs, values, and practices. Mental health care providers need to be culturally competent to provide quality care to all patients. This includes understanding how culture can impact mental health and being aware of the different cultural traditions and practices around mental health. It also means respecting patients’ beliefs and values, even if they differ from ours. When providers are not culturally competent, they may make assumptions about their patients that are not accurate. This can lead to misdiagnoses, inappropriate treatments, and a lack of trust between patients and providers.
