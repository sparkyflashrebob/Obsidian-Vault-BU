TL;DR: Dr. Samah Jabr completely reformulates trauma by rejecting the traditional Western diagnostic model of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) in favor of Continuous Traumatic Stress Disorder (CTSD). Her core critique is that Western psychiatry pathologizes the individual for having a normal reaction to an abnormal, ongoing systemic reality. Instead of treating trauma as a past event causing an internal psychological injury, she reframes it as an unceasing, external, and political environment of oppression.
Dr. Jabr’s reformulation shifts the entire paradigm of mental health in occupied territories along three distinct dimensions:

1. The Myth of the "Post" (Past vs. Present) In standard Western clinical frameworks, PTSD assumes a linear timeline: an individual experiences a discrete traumatic event, the event ends, the individual returns to a safe environment, and their psyche fails to process the memory, leading to dysfunction.
   Dr. Jabr exposes this as an impossibility under prolonged occupation. There is no "post" because the trauma never stopped. The threat of house demolitions, military checkpoints, arrests, and violence is a permanent fixture of daily life. Therefore, symptoms like hypervigilance or anxiety are not a psychological malfunction; they are an accurate, highly adaptive tracking of a dangerous environment.
2. From Individual Pathology to Political Context Western diagnostic manuals (like the DSM-5) view trauma as an internal chemical imbalance or individual cognitive distortion to be cured with medication or therapy. Dr. Jabr argues this depoliticizes human suffering and implicitly places the blame on the victim's inability to cope.
   Her formulation insists that:
   The distress is a normal, healthy emotional response to systemic cruelty.
   Treating the individual in isolation, without acknowledging the political source of the injury, acts as a form of clinical gaslighting.
   The "cure" for this level of psychological distress is ultimately social justice, sovereignty, and human rights, not just clinical intervention.
3. Replacing Passive "Fragility" with Sumud (Agency) Traditional trauma frameworks often reduce the patient to a passive victim needing to develop Western-defined "resilience"—which often means quietly enduring adversity. Dr. Jabr counterbalances this by elevating Sumud (steadfastness). Under her reformulation, psychological endurance is intentionally tied to political awareness and community solidarity. Survival is reframed as an active, collective form of resistance rather than a private psychiatric struggle.
