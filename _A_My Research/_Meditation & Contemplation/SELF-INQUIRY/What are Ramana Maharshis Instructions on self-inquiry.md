## What are Ramanarshi's instructions on self-inquiry?

Gemini:

Ramana Maharshi's teachings on self-inquiry (Atma Vichara) focus on investigating the nature of the Self (Atman). Here are some core aspects of his instructions:

- **Who am I?** - This is the fundamental question that forms the basis of self-inquiry. Ramana Maharshi instructs introverts to persistently inquire within themselves as to the source of their thoughts.
    
- **Attention on the source of thought:** Self-inquiry is not intellectual contemplation but rather a sustained attention directed inwards towards the source of the "I" thought.
    
- **Abide as the Self:** The ultimate goal is to abide as the Self, which is described as a state of pure awareness beyond thought.
    
- **Surrender to the Self:** Ramana emphasizes the importance of self-surrender, letting go of the ego and surrendering to the process of self-inquiry.

**[[SELF-INQUIRY/SELF-INQUIRY]]** is a powerful spiritual practice that encourages self-exploration and self-realization. It has been developed and promoted by the Indian sage Ramana Maharshi as a direct method to truth-realization, abiding non-dual awareness, and enlightenment. Let’s delve into the principles of self-inquiry:

1. **The Question: “Who Am I?”**: The core principle of self-inquiry involves repeatedly asking yourself the question, “Who am I?” This seemingly simple inquiry serves as a powerful tool to cut through the layers of [[ego]], identity, and illusion. By introspectively exploring this question, you gradually move beyond surface-level identifications and discover your true essence.
    
2. **Simplicity and Directness**: Self-inquiry is remarkably straightforward. You don’t need elaborate rituals, complex techniques, or external aids. Instead, you focus on the fundamental question without [[intellectualizing]] or analyzing it excessively. The simplicity of this approach can be deceptive, but its power lies precisely in its directness.
    
3. **[[Beyond Words and Concepts]]**: Ramana Maharshi emphasized that words can never fully express the truth beyond words. While some theory exists around self-inquiry, it ultimately transcends intellectual understanding. The silent teaching—the unspoken transmission of wisdom—was considered his highest form of teaching. So, don’t get caught up in theories; instead, dive into the experience of self-inquiry.
    
4. **Fervent Desire to Know**: The effectiveness of self-inquiry depends on your earnest desire to know the answer to “Who am I?” Approach the question with genuine curiosity and a longing for self-realization. When you ask with fervor, the inquiry becomes an ego-dissolving force that gradually dismantles illusions.
    
5. **Avoiding Misconceptions**:
    
    - **Not a Mantra**: Self-inquiry is not a mantra to repeat mechanically. It’s not about chanting a phrase but about diving into the essence of your being.
    - **Not Intellectual Exercise**: Avoid turning self-inquiry into an intellectual exercise. It’s not about analyzing concepts or constructing elaborate theories. Instead, it’s about direct experience.
    - **Beyond Prescriptions**: Unlike many prescriptive methods, self-inquiry doesn’t prescribe steps or techniques. It invites you to explore the fundamental question without rigid guidelines.

Remember, self-inquiry isn’t about accumulating more knowledge; it’s about direct realization.
