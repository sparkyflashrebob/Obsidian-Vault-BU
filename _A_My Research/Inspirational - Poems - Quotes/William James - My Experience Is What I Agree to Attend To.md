# William James on Perception: What We Agree to Notice

> *"My experience is what I agree to attend to. Only those items which I notice shape my mind — without selective interest, experience is an utter chaos."*  
> — **William James**, *The Principles of Psychology* (1890), Vol. 1, Ch. XI

> *"Free will is the sustaining of a thought because I choose to when I might have other thoughts... My first act of free will shall be to believe in free will."*  
> — **William James**, Personal Journal (April 30, 1870)

---

### Contemplative & Teaching Notes

* **The Sense Doors & Modern Life**: Sights, sounds, digital pings, aches, and thoughts constantly enter through the six senses. Left unexamined, the mind grabs onto anxiety, catastrophizing, or grievances. 
* **The First and Second Arrows**: The sensory input arrives on its own (First Arrow). What we agree to dwell upon and nourish with our attention (Second Arrow) decides whether an afternoon becomes a spiral of inner tension or an experience of clarity and peace.
* **Empirical Validation (*Harvard 2010*)**: In Killingsworth & Gilbert’s study (*"A Wandering Mind Is an Unhappy Mind"*), researchers found people’s minds wander 47% of the time, and what people attend to predicts their emotional well-being far more powerfully than their external activity.
