Can I cook hulled barley in a rice cooker and how would I do that?
Yes, you can absolutely cook hulled barley in a rice cooker. Because hulled barley retains its bran and germ, it behaves more like brown rice than white rice, requiring more water and a longer cooking time.
As an expert in culinary techniques and kitchen efficiency, here is the optimal method for achieving a chewy, nutty, and perfectly cooked grain.
The Golden Ratio
For hulled barley, use a ratio of
hulled barley
liquid (water, vegetable broth, or chicken stock)
A pinch of salt and a teaspoon of butter or olive oil to prevent foaming.

---

Step-by-Step Instructions
Rinse Thoroughly:
Place the barley in a fine-mesh strainer and rinse under cold water until the water runs clear. This removes excess starch and prevents the barley from becoming overly gummy.
Optional Soak (Highly Recommended):
Hulled barley is quite tough. If you have the time, soak the grains in water for
30–60 minutes
before cooking. This ensures a more even texture and reduces the strain on your rice cooker’s heating element. Drain the soaking water before adding to the cooker.
Combine Ingredients:
Place the rinsed (and soaked) barley, liquid, and salt/fat into the rice cooker pot.
Select the Setting:
If you have a "Brown Rice" setting:
Use this. It includes a pre-soak cycle and a lower, longer simmer that is perfect for the density of hulled barley.
If you only have a "Standard/White Rice" switch:
Simply press start. Note that you may need to run the cycle a second time or let it "rest" on the Warm setting for 15 minutes if the grain is still too firm when the switch flips.
The Rest Period:
Once the cooker switches to "Warm," do not open the lid. Let the barley steam for at least
10–15 minutes
. This allows the remaining moisture to distribute evenly.
Fluff and Serve:
Open the lid, fluff with a fork or rice paddle, and drain any tiny amount of excess liquid if necessary (though the 1:2.5 ratio usually results in full absorption).
Quick Tips for Success
Watch for Boilovers:
Barley produces a lot of natural foam. Adding a small amount of oil or butter breaks the surface tension and keeps the foam from clogging the steam vent.
Flavor Boost:
Since you are interested in mindfulness and well-being, consider cooking the barley in a light kombu broth (seaweed) to add trace minerals and a savory umami base without extra sodium.
