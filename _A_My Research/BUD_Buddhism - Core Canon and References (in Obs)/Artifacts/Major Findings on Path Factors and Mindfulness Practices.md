| Saccavibhanga Sutta: The Exposition of the Truths | The Buddha and Sariputta | Provides a detailed factorial analysis of the Four Noble Truths and defines each factor of the Noble Eightfold Path. | [1] |
| Satipatthana Sutta: The Foundations of Mindfulness | The Buddha | Expounds the four foundations of mindfulness (body, feelings, mind, and mind-objects) as the direct path for the purification of beings and the realization of Nibbana. | Anapanasati Sutta: Mindfulness of Breathing | The Buddha | Details sixteen steps of breath meditation that fulfill the four foundations of mindfulness, which in turn fulfill the seven enlightenment factors, leading to true knowledge and deliverance. | [1], [2], [6], [7] |
| Mulapariyaya Sutta: The Root of All Things | The Buddha | Analyzes cognitive processes and identifies delight as the root of suffering. Rejects the "supposing" or "conceiving" of things as "mine" or "self," emphasizing that an Arahant knows things directly without identification. | [1], [2], [3], [4] |
| Sabbasava Sutta: All the Taints | The Buddha | Identifies seven methods for the destruction of the taints ( $asavas$  or effluents): seeing (wise attention), restraining, using (requisites), enduring, avoiding, removing (unskillful thoughts), and developing enlightenment factors. | [1], [2], [3], [4], [5] |
| Chachakka Sutta: The Six Sets of Six | The Buddha | Provides a recursive analysis of the six internal bases, six external bases, and the corresponding consciousness, contact, feeling, and craving as not-self ( $anatta$ ) because they are all subject to arising and falling away. | [1], [4], [8] |
| Dhātu-vibhaṅga Sutta: The Exposition of the Elements | The Buddha | Presents the human being as a composition of six elements: earth, water, fire, air, space, and consciousness. Identifies Nibbāna as the only undeceptive truth and describes the "sage at peace" as one who has overcome the notion of "I am." | [2], [3], [4], [9] |
| Mahāvedalla Sutta: The Greater Series of Questions and Answers | Venerable Sāriputta | Explains that wisdom and consciousness are conjoined; wisdom is to be developed while consciousness is to be understood. | Cūḷavedalla Sutta: The Shorter Series of Questions and Answers | Bhikkhunī Dhammadinnā | Defines identity as the five aggregates affected by clinging and its origin as craving. Explains the Noble Eightfold Path as a conditioned state included within the three aggregates of training (virtue, concentration, and wisdom). | [2] |
| Vitakkasaṇṭhāna Sutta: The Removal of Distracting Thoughts | The Buddha | Teaches five methods for managing unwholesome thoughts: shifting focus to a wholesome object, examining the danger of the thought, ignoring the thought, stilling thought-formation, and using forceful mental suppression. | [2], [3], [4] |
| Mahācattārīsaka Sutta: The Great Forty | The Buddha | Explains noble right concentration as being equipped with the other seven path factors and distinguishes between mundane right view (affected by taints) and supramundane right view. | [2] |
| Indriyabhavana Sutta: The Development of the Faculties | The Buddha | Defines the supreme development of the faculties as the ability to maintain equanimity, mindfulness, and awareness ( $sati-sampajanna$ ) in the face of both agreeable and disagreeable sense objects. | [1], [10] |
| The Path of Purification (Visuddhimagga) | Bhadantācariya Buddhaghosa | A comprehensive manual structured around virtue ( $sila$ ), concentration ( $samadhi$ ), and understanding ( $panna$ ). | The Numerical Discourses of the Buddha (Aṅguttara Nikāya) | The Buddha | Organizes suttas by numerical patterns. Key insights include the mind's intrinsic luminosity, the mechanics of  $kamma$ , and the factors for abandoning the five hindrances. | [12], [13] |
| Questions of King Milinda (Milindapañha) | Venerable Nāgasena | Explores  $anatta$  through the chariot simile and defines the characteristic marks of faith (purifying), mindfulness (not losing/keeping up), concentration (leadership), and wisdom (cutting off). | [14] |
| The Middle Way Within | Ajahn Chah | Teaches that the Middle Way transcends dual extremes of pleasure and pain. True peace is found by separating "the one who knows" from mental states and recognizing all conditioned things ( $sankhara$ ) as uncertain, unsatisfactory, and not-self. | [15], [16], [17] |
| Breath Meditation: Seven Steps | Phra Ajaan Lee Dhammadharo | Details a method of breath meditation ( $anapanasati$ ) that involves adjusting breath energy for comfort and spreading it through the body's nerves and organs to reach  $jhana$  and develop discernment. | [18], [19], [20], [21] |
| The Heart of Understanding | Thich Nhat Hanh | Presents the concept of "Interbeing," where everything is interdependent. Explains that emptiness ( $shunyata$ ) is not non-existence but the lack of a separate self-entity in the five  $skandhas$ . | [22], [23], [24] |
| Buddhist Monastic Code (I & II) | Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu | Analyzes the  $Patimokkha$  and  $Khandhaka$  rules as tools for restraint and mindfulness. Highlights the principle of seniority and the "Great Standards" for applying rules to modern contexts. | [25] |
| The 52 Mental Factors (Cetasika) | Not in source | Categorizes mental factors into Universal, Occasional, Unwholesome, and Beautiful groups to analyze the components of mentality and how they color consciousness ( $vinnana$ ). | [26], [27], [28], [29], [30] |
| Manual of Insight | Mahāsi Sayadaw | Presents a system of  $Satipatthana$  meditation focusing on "momentary concentration" ( $khanikasamadhi$ ) through "noting" physical and mental phenomena to realize the three characteristics. | [31], [32], [33] |
| The Diamond That Cuts Through Illusion | Thich Nhat Hanh | Explores  $prajnaparamita$  and "signlessness." Meditative practice involves being free from the four notions of self, person, living being, and life span to achieve the wisdom of nondiscrimination. | [34] |
| A Heart Released | Phra Ajaan Mun Bhūridatta Thera | Emphasizes the heart ( $mano$ ) as the primal foundation for meditation. Focuses on investigating the body and analyzing the "primal mind" ( $thitibhutam$ ) to end the cycle of rebirth. | [35], [36] |
| The 31 Planes of Existence | Ven. Suvanno Mahathera | Maps Buddhist cosmology into Sensuous, Form, and Formless worlds, emphasizing that rebirth is a causal result of "death-proximate kamma" and that Nibbana is the only escape. | [37], [38] |
