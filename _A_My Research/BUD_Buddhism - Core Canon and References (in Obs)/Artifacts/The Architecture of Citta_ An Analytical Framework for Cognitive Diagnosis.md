### The Architecture of Citta: An Analytical Framework for Cognitive Diagnosis
In the practice of professional ethics and transpersonal psychology, focusing on symptomatic behavior often obscures the causal mechanics of the psyche. A superior diagnostic approach, derived from the Abhidhamma, prioritizes the "roots" ( *Mūla* ) of consciousness. These roots are the primary drivers of human conduct, determining whether a cognitive event is  *Kusala* —a term denoting "skillful," "healthy," or that which "shakes off" ( *ku*  = bad;  *sal*  = to shake) and "cuts off" vice. By moving beyond what an individual does to the strategic core of why they do it, practitioners can evaluate behavioral intent with clinical precision, distinguishing between conditioned reaction and ethical volition.
#### 1. Foundational Axioms: The Hexagonal Root System
The ethical quality of any cognitive state is determined by its underlying root system. Consciousness is not a monolithic entity but a dynamic process polarized by six fundamental forces.
##### The Ethical Polarization of Consciousness
| The Three Roots of Evil (Akusala Mūla) | The Three Roots of Good (Kusala Mūla) |
| ------ | ------ |
| **Lobha (Attachment):**  Etymologically "clinging" or "sticking." It is the drive to possess or hold, acting as the psychological "pull" toward sensory objects. | **Alobha (Generosity):**  More than the absence of greed; it is a positive force of detachment, active altruism, and the capacity for renunciation. |
| **Dosa (Illwill):**  Defined as "striking against." It is a reactive state of "bad-mindedness" that pushes away, representing the psychological "push" of aversion. | **Adosa (Loving-kindness):**  A positive force of benevolence, goodwill, and "middleness." It is the presence of an agreeable disposition toward others. |
| **Moha (Delusion):**  Characterized as "clouding" or "blinding." It is the state of bewilderment that prevents one from seeing the true nature of reality. | **Amoha (Wisdom):**  The positive force of  *Ñāṇa*  (knowledge) and penetrative understanding of things as they truly are ( *Yathāsabhāva-pativedho* ). |

##### The Strategic Impact of Moha (Delusion)
In this diagnostic system,  **Moha**  (Delusion) acts as the universal common denominator in all immoral states. Diagnostically, it is critical to note that  **Lobha**  (pulling toward) and  **Dosa**  (striking against) are mutually exclusive—they cannot inhabit the same thought-moment. However, Moha is present in both, clouding the mind’s executive clarity. Interventions in behavioral ethics must therefore target Delusion as the fundamental obstruction to clarity, for where Moha is absent, immoral states cannot manifest. These internal roots project into the broader landscape of the four spheres of existence.
#### 2. The Four Spheres of Cognitive Frequency
A "Sphere" ( *Avacara* ) functions as a diagnostic boundary for cognitive movement. It describes the frequency at which consciousness operates and the limits of its environmental frequency.
##### The Four Classes of Consciousness
1.  **Sensuous-Sphere (Kāmāvacara):**  Frequenters of the sentient realms. This consciousness is primarily concerned with sense-objects (forms, sounds, contacts) and sensory gratification.
2.  **Form-Sphere (Rūpāvacara):**  Consciousness based on material objects of concentration. This represents a professional shift toward disciplined "Ecstatic Concentration" ( *Appanā* ).
3.  **Formless-Sphere (Arūpāvacara):**  Consciousness transcending all material residuum. In this frequency, the mind is so refined that by sheer will-power, it can be separated from the body, focusing on concepts like infinite space or nothingness.
4.  **Supramundane (Lokuttara):**  Consciousness that transcends the world of the five aggregates (mind and body). It is exclusively focused on the ultimate realization:  *Nibbāna* .
##### From Reaction to Discipline
The transition from the Sensuous to the Form and Formless spheres marks a professional shift from spontaneous, reactive thought to disciplined concentration. While sensuous consciousness is dominated by fluctuating desires, the "Sublime" spheres require the systematic inhibition of mental hindrances, moving from habitual response to high-frequency, stable focus.
#### 3. Diagnostic Tool: Immoral and Rootless States
To mitigate dysfunctional behavior in professional environments, one must identify  *Akusala*  (immoral) states. These are classified by their dominant root and emotional accompaniment.
##### The 12 Immoral Types of Consciousness
*   **Group 1: Attachment-Rooted (8 types):**  Driven by  *Lobha* . These are diagnosed by:
    *   **Feeling:**  Joy ( *Somanassa* ) vs. Indifference ( *Upekkhā* ).
    *   **Perspective:**  Connected with Wrong View ( *Diṭṭhi* ) vs. Disconnected. Note that  *Diṭṭhi*  and  **Conceit**  ( *Māna* ) are like "two lions in one den"—they cannot coexist in the same moment.
    *   **Inducement:**  Unprompted ( *Asaṅkhārika* ) vs. Prompted ( *Sasaṅkhārika* ).
*   **Group 2: Illwill-Rooted (2 types):**  Driven by  *Dosa* . These always involve Displeasure ( *Domanassa* ) and Aversion ( *Paṭigha* ), whether expressed as gross rage or subtle regret.
*   **Group 3: Delusion-Rooted (2 types):**  Driven by sheer ignorance. These involve either  **Doubt**  ( *Vicikicchā* —skepticism/indecision) or  **Restlessness**  ( *Uddhacca* —mental distraction).
##### The 18 Rootless ( *Ahetuka* ) States
These states lack the six ethical roots ( *Hetu* ), yet it is a diagnostic error to assume they lack an efficient cause ( *Nibbattaka-hetu* ). They represent "Resultants" ( *Vipāka* )—the passive processing of sensory data (seeing, hearing)—or "Functionals" ( *Kiriya* ). They are baseline moments of perception that do not generate new ethical consequences but are conditioned by past patterns.
##### Assessment of Intent
A key differentiator is whether an act is  **unprompted**  (instant excellence or habitual vice) or  **prompted**  (induced by external pressure or internal deliberation). This distinction is vital for assessing the strength of  *Kamma*  (intentional action) and the depth of behavioral conditioning.
#### 4. The "Beautiful" Framework for Pro-Social Development
*Sobhana*  (Beautiful) consciousness is the foundation of ethical leadership. These states are "Beautiful" because they yield high-quality outcomes and are anchored in blameless roots.
##### The 24 Sensuous-Sphere Beautiful States
These are classified by the triad of  **Feeling**  (Pleasure/Indifference),  **Knowledge**  (Associated/Dissociated), and  **Inducement**  (Unprompted/Prompted).
For a thought to be classified as  **Beautiful** , it must possess two layers of mental factors ( *Cetasikas* ):
1.  **The 7 Universals (**  ***Sabbacittasādhāranā***  **):**  The "hardware" found in  *every*  thought—Contact ( *Phassa* ), Feeling ( *Vedanā* ), Perception ( *Saññā* ), Volition ( *Cetanā* ), One-pointedness ( *Ekaggatā* ), Psychic Life ( *Jīvitindriya* ), and Attention ( *Manasikāra* ).
2.  **The 19 Beautiful Universals (**  ***Sobhanasādhāranā***  **):**  The "software" of excellence—including Confidence ( *Saddhā* ), Mindfulness ( *Sati* ), Moral Shame ( *Hiri* ), Moral Dread ( *Ottappa* ), and Equanimity ( *Tatramajjhattatā* ).
##### The Role of Wisdom
Diagnostically, we distinguish between "Knowledge-Dissociated" good acts (a child saluting a mentor without understanding the principle) and "Knowledge-Associated" acts. Only the latter facilitates  *Yathāsabhāva-pativedho* —a penetrative understanding that leads to irreversible wisdom-growth.
#### 5. Distinction of Realities: Mundane vs. Supramundane
Effective diagnosis requires a distinction between  **Apparent Reality**  ( *Sammuti-Sacca* )—conventional labels—and  **Ultimate Reality**  ( *Paramattha-Sacca* )—the irreducible abstract truths of mind and matter.
##### Mundane vs. Supramundane Realities
| Mundane (Lokiya) | Supramundane (Lokuttara) |
| ------ | ------ |
| Composed of the three spheres; subject to the stream of Kamma. | Transcends the five aggregates; focused exclusively on Nibbāna. |
| Characterized by attachment to fleeting mental and material states. | Categorized by the four Paths ( *Magga* ) and four Fruits ( *Phala* ). |

##### The Path of Purification
Transitioning from mundane to supramundane involves nine specific insights ( *Ñāṇas* ), following a sequence from the Arising and Passing Away of things to the  **Knowledge of Dissolution**  ( *Bhanga Ñāṇa* ), where the practitioner perceives the constant flux of mind and matter, eventually reaching the Purity of Knowledge and Vision.
#### 6. Functional Mechanics: Resultants vs. Functional Actions
To understand behavior, one must distinguish between the cause, the effect, and the neutral function.
*   **Kamma (Effective):**  Intentional moral or immoral actions that accumulate reproductive energy.
*   **Vipāka (Resultant):**  The mental fruits of past Kamma; passive experiences (e.g., pain/pleasure) that do not generate new ethical debt.
*   **Kiriya (Functional):**  Actions that are "karmically ineffective," found in Arahants. For instance, laughter/smiling is a form of bodily expression ( *Kāyaviññatti* ). There are 13 classes of consciousness through which one may smile, but for the enlightened, these are purely functional.
##### The Javana (Impulsion) Stage
The most critical diagnostic stage in any thought-process is the  **Javana** . For an ordinary person, this is the exercise of free will where Kamma is performed. In an Arahant, however, the Javana is merely functional ( *Kiriya* ). Because they have eradicated the roots of evil, their actions no longer possess "accumulative power," effectively ending the cycle of ethical reactivity.
#### 7. Summary and Diagnostic Synthesis
The architecture of Citta comprises  **89 types of consciousness** . This number expands to  **121**  when the 8 Supramundane states are multiplied by the 5  *Jhāna*  factors (levels of concentration), representing the full spectrum of cognitive possibility.
##### The Diagnostic Checklist
1.  **The Root Test:**  Is the behavior driven by  **Attachment**  (Lobha),  **Illwill**  (Dosa), or  **Delusion**  (Moha)? Or is it rooted in Generosity, Loving-kindness, and Wisdom?
2.  **The Plane Test:**  Is the consciousness restricted to the reactive  **Sensuous Sphere** , or has it been elevated to a  **Sublime**  or  **Supramundane**  frequency?
3.  **The Functional Test:**  Is the current mental moment a  **Resultant**  of past conditioning (a passive fruit) or a new  **Kamma-generating**  action (an exercise of free will)?
The goal of this framework is to transform the technicalities of Abhidhamma into an "intellectual treat" for the wise seeker. By applying these diagnostic markers, one moves toward a life of informed, ethical excellence and the realization of ultimate truth.
