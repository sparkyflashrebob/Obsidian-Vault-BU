## 🚶 Sequential Somatic Walking Stages

| Stage | Focus of Awareness | Somatic / Mental Modality | Canonical Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1: Standing Stillness** | Upright alignment at start of path | Sensing vertical balance & gravitational ground | Establishes spatial orientation (*ṭhāna*) |
| **Stage 2: Foot Contact & Stepping** | Sole contact, lifting, moving, placing | Tactile sensation (*phassa*) & weight shift | Grounds consciousness in direct physical touch |
| **Stage 3: Bodily Demeanor & Breath** | Whole-body carriage, breath rhythm | Relaxed shoulders, soft belly, rhythmic breathing | Integrates physical momentum with prāṇa |
| **Stage 4: Turning at the Threshold** | Stopping at end of path, mindful turning | Pausing, turning 180 degrees, re-grounding | Prevents momentum from becoming mindless |
| **Stage 5: Observing Inner Drive** | Tracking urge to rush or internal resistance | Observing mental volition (*cetanā*) & mood | Direct insight into conditionality (*anattā*) |

## 📜 Meditation Instruction Transcript

*(Teaching delivered with deliberate spacing and practical guidance)*

**Ajahn Pasanno:**

Now we transition to walking meditation. 

When the Buddha spoke of meditation practice, it was never confined merely to seated meditation. He spoke of all four physical postures that we utilize in our lives as human beings: sitting, walking, standing, and lying down. 

We deliberately give ourselves the opportunity to develop familiarity with applying mindfulness and clear attention across all of these different postures.

---

### Step 1: Establishing the Path and Posture

Choose a level, defined path—approximately twenty to thirty paces in length. 

Stand at the beginning of the path for a moment before moving. 

Bring attention to the upright posture of the body: 
* Feel the soles of the feet resting firmly against the earth. 
* Allow the weight of the torso to settle down through the hips and legs into the ground. 
* Keep the head balanced comfortably over the spine, and let the arms clasp gently in front or behind the back.

---

### Step 2: The Rhythm of Stepping

Begin walking at an easy, natural pace. 

Experience the walking. 

Experience each step as it unfolds:
* Feel the foot lifting;
* Feel the foot swinging forward through space;
* Feel the contact as the heel and sole touch the ground again.

Experience the breath as you walk. 

Experience how you hold your physical body: 
* Notice the balance or the lack of balance. 
* Notice the unconscious compulsion to lean forward, rushing toward the end of the path. 
* Or notice whatever resistance or heaviness might be present. 

Whatever arises, simply experience it directly without judgment.

---

### Step 3: Turning and Continuity

When you reach the end of your walking path, do not turn automatically or abruptly. 

Bring the feet together and pause for a breath. 

Experience standing still. 

Then mindfully turn the body around, re-establish your gaze gently toward the floor about six to eight feet ahead, and begin walking back along the path.

We can be just as peaceful, clear, and settled in walking meditation as we are in sitting meditation. It is entirely a matter of how we hold the mind and facilitate our clarity. 

Take this period for walking meditation until our next seated session.
