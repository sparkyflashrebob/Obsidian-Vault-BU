## 📜 Guided Meditation Transcript

*(Teaching delivered with rhythmic spacing and deliberate pauses for somatic absorption)*

**Ajahn Karunadhammo:**

Let’s find a comfortable, upright sitting position for contemplation and listening.

I am going to go through the thirty-two parts of the body as we have done before. A helpful approach—one that I find particularly useful—is to combine visualization with direct tactile sensations. Tuning into the subtle physical sensations that accompany the contemplation of the body grounds the mind and anchors the attention.

For right now, we use this as a way to cultivate genuine bodily presence. It cuts through sensual craving, but it is also an inherently cooling, settling, and stabilizing contemplation in its own right. 

Follow along, and see where it takes the mind.

---

### Step 1: Establishing Whole-Body Presence

First, cultivate a clear feeling—a direct sense of the presence of the entire body sitting here.

Notice this posture. 

Notice the disposition of the body, whether sitting on a mat and cushion, or sitting on a chair. 

Notice the sensation of pressure at the points where the body makes contact with the floor or the chair.

Allow a visual outline of the body in this position to arise in coordination with the direct tactile feelings. Actually feel the sensations throughout the physical frame as a whole.

This body, from the soles of the feet up and from the crown of the head down, is a partially sealed sack of skin filled with diverse constituents.

---

### Step 2: The Five External Envelopes (*Taca-Pañcaka*)

The first five are the external aspects of the body:

Visualize and physically feel the hair of the head. Notice the sensation across the scalp.

Notice the fine hair of the body: the hairs on the arms, the torso, the legs, down to the tiny hairs on the tops of the feet and toes.

Bring attention to the nails: the fingernails and the toenails.

Notice the teeth. You can visualize what your teeth look like—straight or crooked, white or stained. Actually feel them touching where the upper and lower jaws meet.

Notice the skin covering the entire surface of the body. What is its texture? Its temperature? Its sensitivity?

---

### Step 3: The Internal Solid Earth Element (*Pathavī-Dhātu*)

Now, we move beneath the skin to the solid, structural elements of the body:

Contemplate the flesh—the muscle tissue of the arms, the chest, the abdomen, the back, and the thighs. What lies just beneath that layer of skin? Pink, red, fibrous muscle tissue. Feel the solidity of that tissue. Notice if some muscles are sore from sitting, or if there are obstructions. Just bare flesh.

Bring awareness to the sinews, the tendons, and the ligaments—everything connecting muscle to bone. You can feel them directly: the prominent tendons across the back of the hands, along the top of the feet, the thick Achilles tendon behind the heel, and the cords behind the knees.

Now contemplate the skeleton—the hard bones. Feel the structural weight and heaviness of this skeletal architecture.

Feel the small bones in the toes, the arches of the feet, and the ankles. 

Move up to the two bones of the lower legs, and the kneecaps. 

Feel the large femur bones of the thighs plugging into the pelvis. 

Notice the lower spine: the sacrum, the lumbar spine in the small of the back, rising vertebra upon vertebra into the thoracic spine where the rib cage attaches. 

Feel the ribs wrapping around from the back to meet at the breastbone, the sternum. 

Feel the flat shoulder blades across the upper back, connecting to the collarbones. 

Feel the bones of the fingers, hands, wrists, forearms, elbows, and upper arm bones socketing into the shoulders. 

Follow the cervical spine up through the neck, supporting the base of the skull. Feel the hard bones of the skull: the jaw, the eye sockets, the forehead, and the crown. 

Feel the immense solidity of the entire skeleton supporting this posture.

Sense the bone marrow—the soft, porous core inside the center of the bones.

Now turn attention to the internal organs:
* The kidneys, nestled against the mid-back;
* The heart—if you are very still and quiet, you can feel it rhythmically pumping;
* The liver, a large organ occupying space beneath the right side of the rib cage;
* The membranes and diaphragm (*kilomaka*), that muscular sheet attached beneath the ribs that rhythmically rises and falls with every breath;
* The spleen, on the left side;
* The spongy tissue of the lungs, filling the chest cavity, continuously expanding and contracting;
* The bowels: the long coiled small intestine and the large intestine;
* The stomach, with its digesting meal churning away;
* The excrement contained within the lower tract;
* And the brain, resting inside the enclosure of the skull.

---

### Step 4: The Fluid Water Element (*Āpo-Dhātu*)

Now, broaden awareness to contemplate the fluid, liquid element of the body:

* Attune to the bile in the gallbladder;
* The phlegm in the respiratory pathways;
* Pus where tissues inflame;
* The blood coursing through miles of vessels, pumped by the heart;
* The sweat on the surface of the skin;
* The layer of fat beneath the skin;
* Tears in the eyes;
* Skin grease and oils;
* Saliva in the mouth;
* Mucus in the nasal passages;
* The lubricating synovial oil inside the joints, allowing smooth movement without friction;
* And urine collecting in the bladder.

---

### Step 5: Whole-Body Elemental Presence

This body: from the soles of the feet up, and from the crown of the head down, is a container filled with these varied parts.

Now, pull the awareness back together. Feel the body as an integrated whole right now. 

Maintain this clear sense of the body, just as it is. 

There is no need to attach personal meaning, definitions, or vanity to it. It is simply this organic physical form with its biological functions, fluids, and structures. 

Suspend all opinions and judgments. Feel the direct tactile reality:
* Feel the solidity, weight, and hardness of the Earth element.
* Feel the moisture, fluidity, and cohesion of the Water element.
* Notice temperature: where is the body warm, and where is it cool? Feel the Fire element.
* Feel the movement of air—not only breath moving through the nostrils, chest, and belly, but the energetic currents coursing through the limbs. The Wind element.

Allow the attention to rest gently in the natural flow of breathing. Feel the breath energy suffusing the entire physical container. It is a deeply cooling, calming, and stabilizing contemplation.

---

### Step 6: The Anattā Investigation — Who Is in Charge?

At this point, we can turn this grounded stillness into an inquiry into non-self (*anattā*).

Make a firm, deliberate resolution to be completely, perfectly still right now. 

Hold the body as motionless as you possibly can. 

Internally issue a command:  
*"I am the owner and master of this body. I command this body to be absolutely and perfectly still."*

Notice what happens. 

Does this body obey your command?

Can you command your heart to stop beating?

Can you command your lungs to stop breathing for very long?

Resolve to hold all the muscles of your face completely relaxed without the slightest movement—and then watch. 

Watch the subtle twitches around the eyelids, the mouth, the jaw. Notice the involuntary adjustments the nervous system makes to relieve tiny pressures and discomforts. 

Observe these involuntary movements and ask yourself:  
*Who is in charge?*

Rest in this clear, dispassionate seeing of bodily nature as we sit together.
