# Meditation Instructions: Embodied Awareness & Breath Rhythm
### *Abhayagiri Thanksgiving Monastic Retreat 2009 — Day 2 Morning Guided Meditation*
#### *Teacher: Ajahn Metta | Venue: Angela Center, Santa Rosa, CA*

---

## 🧘 Practice Orientation & Somatic Staging

* **Retreat Container**: Abhayagiri Thanksgiving Monastic Retreat 2009.
* **Teacher**: **Ajahn Metta** (Abhayagiri Monastic Sangha).
* **Setting & Focus**: Morning sitting meditation establishing somatic stability (*kāyagatāsati*), releasing postural rigidity, connecting with physical support, and attuning to the unmanipulated rhythm of breathing.

### 📋 Sequential Contemplative Stages

| Stage | Somatic Anchor | Contemplative Guidance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1: Postural Alignment** | Spine & Head Alignment | Establishing an upright spine without rigid tension. Relaxing the shoulders and face while seated on cushion or chair. |
| **Stage 2: Earth Element & Contact** | Points of Physical Pressure | Feeling where the body makes contact with cushion, chair, and floor. Letting the ground completely carry the weight. |
| **Stage 3: Receiving the Breath** | Respiratory Expansion & Contraction | Attuning to the natural breath without manipulation or control; noting the gap between in-breath and out-breath. |
| **Stage 4: Subtle Somatic Waves** | Palms, Shoulders & Back | Expanding wakeful, gentle alertness to subtle muscular shifts in the back, warmth/coolness in the palms, and ease. |

## 📜 Guided Meditation Transcript

*(Teaching pauses and somatic transitions are formatted with paragraph breaks to reflect the contemplative pacing of the guided session.)*

**Ajahn Metta:**

Beginning again, starting from where we are right now. 

Being present here at this moment. 

Make sure the spine is upright, with the head in line with it. Just as a reminder: if you are sitting on a chair, just observe how straight the spine is. It doesn't need to be rigid, but upright. The rest of the body remains relaxed, completely at ease.

Now, making a connection with the ground. 

Noticing where the body touches the ground. Which parts of the body are touching the floor or cushion, and noticing the direct physical sensation of that contact. Allowing the ground to carry the entire weight of the body. 

Noticing the pressure on those parts of the body that are touching the ground... feeling the pressure, and at the same time feeling the supportive connection with the ground in this room right now.

And noticing the rhythm of the breath. 

Being with the body breathing, however that is unfolding—whether as a pleasant experience, or maybe not so pleasant if you have a cough coming up. It is all part of this moment. There is no need to fear or resist; you don't need to push anything away.

Noticing if the breath is long, or if it is short... or perhaps something in the middle. 

Noticing the inhalation—where it begins, and where it ends. 

Noticing that small, natural gap in between... and then the exhalation begins, and it comes to an end. 

Just really being aware of this natural process, taking it in, allowing it to flow in its own way without manipulating it, without directing it, simply being present with it.

Being aware of the physical movements of the body as it is moved by the breath. The chest expanding and contracting. The rise and fall of the abdomen. Noticing those physical sensations directly, rather than thinking about them. Really experiencing the sensations as they unfold.

How do you experience this breath right now?

Or noticing the more subtle movements of the shoulders, moving slightly up and down in this rhythm. There is no need to do anything with it; it is simply being aware. 

Being with the body breathing.

The quality of our attention is a gentle alertness—wakefulness, and yet completely relaxed. Nothing being forceful. 

It is this sense of being with, allowing what is happening to unfold without wanting it to be otherwise. Just looking at it. Being with it mindfully.

Noticing the sensations in the palms of our hands... the light tingling or movement that arises, or perhaps just the direct experience of warmth, or the coolness of the air on the skin.

Noticing the very gentle, subtle movement of the muscles in the back—very lightly expanding with the inhalation, and relaxing with the exhalation. 

It is like listening to the body. Receiving it, and resting with it as it unfolds in its natural rhythms and functions. Seeing it just as it is, and being present with it.

Noticing how this process works on its own, and that no willful intervention or force is needed. It happens by itself. 

The only thing we have to do is to be with it... to be aware of it. 

Being present with the body. This body breathing.
