## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Anandabodhi.

* **Contemplative Domain**: Working with Hindrances & Four Noble Truths (*Cattāri Ariyasaccāni*) — The Three Turnings and Twelve Aspects applied experientially to sitting meditation.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered during the final days (Track 15) of the Angela Center October Retreat as an integrating contemplative instruction.

* **Condensed Version of the Practice**: Ajahn Anandabodhi illuminates how the breath serves as an intimate mirror for the Four Noble Truths. In traditional Buddhism, the Four Noble Truths are often approached as theoretical doctrine; here, they are presented as a direct, somatic methodology. The First Noble Truth is not that life is miserable, but that *dukkha* exists and is to be fully understood (*pariññeyya*). Our chronic habit when discomfort, restlessness, or pain arises is to avert attention, suppress the feeling, or weave elaborate stories around it. Ajahn Anandabodhi invites practitioners to touch *dukkha* directly with awareness. When we stop running and let the running mental commentary fall away, we see the grasping that fuels suffering, allowing craving to be naturally abandoned (*pahātabba*). Cessation (*nirodha*) is tasted right within the quiet heart that no longer clings.

## 🧘 Experiential Guided Meditation Script

### 1. The Breath as Teacher [00:00–04:30]

We establish our posture and bring gentle attention to the breathing. When we are truly present with the breath, it becomes a very deep teacher. We notice the rising, the falling, the natural rhythm of life unfolding without our management.


We allow ourselves to rest right here. Noticing if our stomach is tight or knotted up, inviting that tension to soften, giving the breath full permission to circulate.


`[Pause 45s]`


### 2. The Three Turnings of Truth [04:30–12:15]

We reflect on the Three Turnings of the Four Noble Truths: recognition of the truth, understanding the duty associated with it, and realizing its completion.


There is suffering (*dukkha*); it should be understood; it has been understood.

There is the origin of suffering (craving / *taṇhā*); it should be abandoned; it has been abandoned.

There is the cessation of suffering (*nirodha*); it should be realized; it has been realized.

There is the path leading to cessation (*magga*); it should be cultivated; it has been cultivated.


`[Pause 60s]`


### 3. Touching Dukkha with Awareness [12:15–21:05]

Now, in our own direct experience, notice whatever discomfort, physical ache, restlessness, or hesitation is present. There is no need to run away. There is no need to create a drama.


`[Pause 45s]`


You don't have to listen to the running commentary that accompanies the feelings. Notice the difference between the raw sensation itself and the storyline woven around it. Touch *dukkha* directly with awareness. In that loving, unentangled holding, healing and letting go naturally unfold.


`[Silence 2 min]`


`[Long Bell]`

## 📋 Doctrinal Architecture of the Three Turnings

| Noble Truth (*Ariyasacca*) | 1st Turning: Sacred Truth (*Sacca-ñāṇa*) | 2nd Turning: Contemplative Task (*Kicca-ñāṇa*) | 3rd Turning: Liberating Fulfillment (*Kata-ñāṇa*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Dukkha (Suffering / Stress)** | *"This is dukkha"* | To be thoroughly understood (*pariññeyya*) | Fully understood (*pariññāta*) |
| **2. Samudaya (Origin / Craving)** | *"This is the origin of dukkha"* | To be completely abandoned (*pahātabba*) | Abandoned (*pahīna*) |
| **3. Nirodha (Cessation / Peace)** | *"This is the cessation of dukkha"* | To be directly realized (*sacchikātabba*) | Realized (*sacchikata*) |
| **4. Magga (The Noble Path)** | *"This is the path to cessation"* | To be steadily cultivated (*bhāvetabba*) | Cultivated (*bhāvita*) |
