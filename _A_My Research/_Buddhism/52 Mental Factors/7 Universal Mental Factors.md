# The 7 Universal Mental Factors (*Sabbacittasādhāraṇa*)

The **Seven Universal Mental Factors** (*Sabbacittasādhāraṇa Cetasikas*) are the most fundamental building blocks of cognition in Buddhist psychology. They are called **"universal"** (*sabbacitta*) because they are present in **literally every single moment of consciousness without exception**—whether wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate.

> *"These factors perform the most rudimentary and essential cognitive functions, without which consciousness of an object would be utterly impossible."*  
> — **Bhikkhu Bodhi**, *A Comprehensive Manual of Abhidhamma*

| 2 | **Vedanā** | **Feeling-Tone** | Experiencing the affective flavor of the object (pleasant, painful, or neutral). | A king enjoying a royal feast prepared by the cook. |
| 3 | **Saññā** | **Perception / Recognition** | Marking, recognizing, and identifying the object by its features. | A carpenter recognizing pieces of wood by marks; a scarecrow recognizing form. |
| 4 | **Cetanā** | **Volition / Intention** | Will, intention; directing and organizing accompanying factors toward action. | A senior farmhand organizing laborers in the field to harvest the crops. |
| 5 | **Ekaggatā** | **One-Pointedness** | Centering the mind steadily upon a single object; the seed of *Samādhi*. | An unmoving candle flame in a windless room; water binding mortar. |
| 6 | **Jīvitindriya** | **Psychic Life Faculty** | Sustaining, vitalizing, and infusing life into co-arising mental factors. | Water sustaining lotus buds; a nurse nurturing an infant. |
| 7 | **Manasikāra** | **Attention** | Steering and driving the mind directly onto the object; orientation. | The helmsman of a ship; a charioteer steering horses to the target. |

## 2. In-Depth Psychological Breakdown

### 1. Phassa (Contact)
* **Definition**: The spark of contact where the internal sense organ, external sense object, and consciousness collide (*"The meeting of the three is contact" — MN 18*).
* **Role**: It is not physical touch, but the mental act of contact. Without *phassa*, the mind and external world could never interface.

### 2. Vedanā (Feeling Tone)
* **Definition**: The raw affective experience of the object: pleasant (*sukha*), painful (*dukkha*), or neither-pleasant-nor-painful (*adukkham-asukha*).
* **Role**: In Dependent Origination (*Paṭiccasamuppāda*), *phassa-paccayā vedanā* (contact conditions feeling), and *vedanā-paccayā taṇhā* (feeling conditions craving). Awareness of *vedanā* is the crucial intervention point for freedom.

### 3. Saññā (Perception / Labeling)
* **Definition**: The mental capacity to recognize, categorize, and conceptualize based on past memory.
* **Role**: It notes: *"This is blue; this is a bell; this is a threat."* When distorted (*vipallāsa*), perception mistakes the impermanent for permanent and not-self for a solid self.

### 4. Cetanā (Volition / Kamma)
* **Definition**: The coordinating impulse of the will. The Buddha famously declared: *"Cetanāhaṃ bhikkhave kammaṃ vadāmi"* — *"Monks, volition is what I call kamma"* (AN 6.63).
* **Role**: Volition generates intentional momentum, setting the ethical consequence (*vipāka*) of every conscious act.

### 5. Ekaggatā (One-Pointedness)
* **Definition**: The capacity of consciousness to hold an object without scattering.
* **Role**: In ordinary consciousness, it is merely rudimentary focus. When developed and purified through meditation, it matures into absorption (**Samādhi** and the **Jhānas**).

### 6. Jīvitindriya (Mental Life Faculty)
* **Definition**: The non-physical vitality that keeps mental phenomena energized and coherent throughout their brief duration.
* **Role**: Just as physical life faculty (*rūpa-jīvitindriya*) sustains the biological body, mental life faculty maintains the psychic continuity of mind moments.

### 7. Manasikāra (Attention / Engagement)
* **Definition**: The mental rudder that turns consciousness toward an object.
* **Role**: 
  * **Wise Attention (*Yoniso Manasikāra*)**: Directing the mind to see impermanence, suffering, and not-self $ightarrow$ leads to Awakening.
  * **Unwise Attention (*Ayoniso Manasikāra*)**: Directing the mind to see permanence, attractiveness, and self $ightarrow$ fuels the Hindrances and Defilements.
