## 1. Introduction to the Lexicon

This reference document serves as the doctrinal commentary and analytical glossary to the visual [[Wheel of Dharma]]. It systematically outlines the major canonical groupings, numerical lists (*saṅgaha*), and psychological categories of the Early Buddhist teachings preserved in the Pāli Canon.

## 2. Core Doctrinal Groupings

### I. The Three Characteristics of Existence (*Tilakkhaṇa*)
All conditioned phenomena (*saṅkhāra*) share three universal hallmarks:
* **Anicca (Impermanence)**: Everything that arises is subject to cessation. Conditioned reality is in perpetual flux and instability.
* **Dukkha (Unsatisfactoriness / Stress)**: Because conditioned things are impermanent, they cannot provide permanent refuge or lasting contentment. Grasping after them generates tension and distress.
* **Anattā (Non-Self / Insubstantiality)**: No phenomenon contains an unchanging, autonomous essence or separate "self". All experiences arise dependently without ownership.

### II. The Five Aggregates of Clinging (*Pañcupādānakkhandhā*)
The experiential constituents that the uninstructed mind misidentifies as "I", "me", or "mine":
1. **Rūpa (Form / Matter)**: The physical body, the four great elements (*mahābhūta*: earth, water, fire, wind), sense organs, and tangible physical objects.
2. **Vedanā (Feeling Tone)**: The affective valence of experience arising from sensory contact: pleasant (*sukha*), unpleasant (*dukkha*), or neutral (*adukkhamasukha*).
3. **Saññā (Perception / Memory)**: The mental faculty of recognition, conceptual labeling, and memory retrieval.
4. **Saṅkhāra (Mental Formations / Volition)**: The volitional, intentional, and emotional conditioning of the mind (habits, intentions, emotional states).
5. **Viññāṇa (Consciousness)**: The pure cognizing or receptive knowing of an object through the six sense doors (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind).

### III. The Four Noble Truths (*Cattāri Ariyasaccāni*)
The framework uniting diagnostic analysis with pragmatic liberation:
1. **The Truth of Dukkha**: Suffering, unsatisfactoriness, impermanence. *Duty: To be fully comprehended (pariññeyya).*
2. **The Truth of Origin (*Samudaya*)**: Craving (*Taṇhā*) driven by ignorance. *Duty: To be abandoned (pahātabba).*
3. **The Truth of Cessation (*Nirodha*)**: The complete extinguishing of craving (*Nibbāna*). *Duty: To be directly realized (sacchikātabba).*
4. **The Truth of the Path (*Magga*)**: The Noble Eightfold Path. *Duty: To be cultivated and developed (bhāvetabba).*

### IV. The Three Unwholesome Roots (*Akusalamūla*)
The three psychological defilements from which all unskillful action arises:
* **Lobha (Greed / Lust / Attachment)**: Craving, pulling experiences inward, clinging to pleasant feeling tones.
* **Dosa (Aversion / Hatred / Ill-Will)**: Pushing experiences away, anger, irritability, resistance.
* **Moha (Delusion / Ignorance / Confusion)**: Obscuration of mind, misperceiving the impermanent as permanent, the painful as pleasurable, and the non-self as self.

### V. The Twelve Links of Dependent Origination (*Paṭiccasamuppāda*)
*Conditioned co-arising demonstrating how ignorance compounds into existential distress:*
1. **Avijjā (Ignorance)** $ightarrow$ Volitional Formations (*Saṅkhārā*)
2. **Saṅkhārā** $ightarrow$ Consciousness (*Viññāṇa*)
3. **Viññāṇa** $ightarrow$ Name-and-Form (*Nāmarūpa*)
4. **Nāmarūpa** $ightarrow$ The Six Sense Bases (*Saḷāyatana*)
5. **Saḷāyatana** $ightarrow$ Contact (*Phassa*)
6. **Phassa** $ightarrow$ Feeling (*Vedanā*)
7. **Vedanā** $ightarrow$ Craving (*Taṇhā*)
8. **Taṇhā** $ightarrow$ Clinging / Grasping (*Upādāna*)
9. **Upādāna** $ightarrow$ Becoming (*Bhava*)
10. **Bhava** $ightarrow$ Birth (*Jāti*)
11. **Jāti** $ightarrow$ Aging, Death, Sorrow, Grief & Lamentation (*Jarāmaraṇa*)

### VI. The 37 Requisites of Enlightenment (*Bodhipakkhiyā Dhammā*)
Seven groups of contemplative qualities systematized by the Buddha:
1. **Four Foundations of Mindfulness (*Satipaṭṭhāna*)**: Contemplation of Body (*Kāyānupassanā*), Feelings (*Vedanānupassanā*), Mind (*Cittānupassanā*), and Mental Qualities (*Dhammānupassanā*).
2. **Four Right Efforts (*Sammappadhāna*)**: Preventing unarisen unwholesome states, abandoning arisen unwholesome states, cultivating unarisen wholesome states, maintaining arisen wholesome states.
3. **Four Bases of Spiritual Power (*Iddhipāda*)**: Desire/Intention (*Chanda*), Energy (*Viriya*), Consciousness/Mind (*Citta*), Investigation (*Vīmaṁsā*).
4. **Five Spiritual Faculties (*Indriya*)**: Faith (*Saddhā*), Energy (*Viriya*), Mindfulness (*Sati*), Concentration (*Samādhi*), Wisdom (*Paññā*).
5. **Five Spiritual Powers (*Bala*)**: The same five qualities when fortified against opposing defilements.
6. **Seven Factors of Awakening (*Bojjhaṅga*)**: Mindfulness (*Sati*), Investigation of Dhamma (*Dhamma-vicaya*), Energy (*Viriya*), Joy (*Pīti*), Tranquility (*Passaddhi*), Concentration (*Samādhi*), Equanimity (*Upekkhā*).
7. **The Noble Eightfold Path (*Ariya Aṭṭhaṅgika Magga*)**: Right View, Intention, Speech, Action, Livelihood, Effort, Mindfulness, and Concentration.

### VII. The Five Hindrances (*Pañca Nīvaraṇa*)
Mental obstacles that cloud awareness and prevent meditative absorption (*jhāna*):
* **Kāmacchanda**: Sensual desire $ightarrow$ *Remedy: Asubha contemplation, sensory restraint.*
* **Byāpāda**: Ill-will, resentment $ightarrow$ *Remedy: Mettā (Loving-kindness) meditation.*
* **Thīna-middha**: Sloth and torpor $ightarrow$ *Remedy: Light perception, physical posture adjustment, viriya.*
* **Uddhacca-kukkucca**: Restlessness and remorse $ightarrow$ *Remedy: Calming the breath, tranquil concentration.*
* **Vicikicchā**: Doubt and indecision $ightarrow$ *Remedy: Wise reflection (yoniso manasikāra), Dhamma study.*

### VIII. The Ten Fetters (*Dasa Saṁyojana*) & Four Stages of Awakening
Habitual patterns binding consciousness to conditioned existence:
1. **Sakkāya-diṭṭhi** (Identity view)
2. **Vicikicchā** (Doubt)
3. **Sīlabbata-parāmāsa** (Attachment to rites and rituals)
   *(Fetters 1–3 severed at **Stream-Entry / Sotāpatti**)*
4. **Kāmarāga** (Sensual desire)
5. **Paṭigha** (Ill-will)
   *(Fetters 4–5 significantly weakened at **Once-Returning / Sakadāgāmī**; completely severed at **Non-Returning / Anāgāmī**)*
6. **Rūparāga** (Desire for subtle-material existence)
7. **Arūparāga** (Desire for immaterial existence)
8. **Māna** (Conceit / measuring "I am")
9. **Uddhacca** (Restlessness)
10. **Avijjā** (Ignorance)
   *(Fetters 6–10 severed at **Arahantship / Full Awakening**)*

### IX. The Ten Perfections (*Dasa Pāramī*) & Four Divine Abodes (*Brahmavihāras*)
* **The 10 Pāramīs**: Generosity (*Dāna*), Morality (*Sīla*), Renunciation (*Nekkhamma*), Wisdom (*Paññā*), Energy (*Viriya*), Patience (*Khanti*), Truthfulness (*Sacca*), Determination (*Adhiṭṭhāna*), Loving-kindness (*Mettā*), Equanimity (*Upekkhā*).
* **The 4 Brahmavihāras**:
  * **Mettā**: Loving-kindness $ightarrow$ wishing all beings well-being.
  * **Karuṇā**: Compassion $ightarrow$ holding suffering with tender, non-aversive presence.
  * **Muditā**: Sympathetic joy $ightarrow$ rejoicing in the happiness and fortune of others.
  * **Upekkhā**: Equanimity $ightarrow$ unshakeable balance observing karma and natural conditionality.
