"Hoʻoponopono" is defined in the _Hawaiian Dictionary_ as:

(a) "To put to rights; to put in order or shape, correct, revise, adjust, amend, regulate, arrange, rectify, tidy up make orderly or neat, administer, superintend, supervise, manage, edit, work carefully or neatly; to make ready, as canoemen preparing to catch a wave."

(b) "Mental cleansing: family conferences in which relationships were set right (_hoʻoponopono_) through prayer, discussion, confession, repentance, and mutual restitution and [forgiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forgiveness "Forgiveness")."[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-HDict-13)

Literally, _hoʻo_ is a particle used to make an actualizing verb from the following noun. Here, it creates a verb from the noun _[pono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pono_(word) "Pono (word)")_, which is defined as: "...goodness, uprightness, morality, moral qualities, correct or proper procedure, excellence, well-being, prosperity, welfare, benefit, true condition or nature, duty; moral, fitting, proper, righteous, right, upright, just, virtuous, fair, beneficial, successful, in perfect order, accurate, correct, eased, relieved; should, ought, must, necessary."[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-HDict-13)

_Ponopono_ is defined as "to put to rights; to put in order or shape, correct, revise, adjust, amend, regulate, arrange, rectify, tidy up, make orderly or neat."

So hoʻoponopono can be translated literally as "to make right" or "to make good".

## Ritual[[edit source](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ho%CA%BBoponopono&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Ritual")]

Hoʻoponopono corrects, restores and maintains good relationships among family members and with their god(s) by getting to the causes and sources of trouble. Usually the most senior member of the family conducts it. He or she gathers the family together. If the family is unable to work through a problem, they turn to a respected outsider.

The process begins with prayer. A statement of the problem is made, and the transgression discussed. Family members are expected to work problems through and cooperate, not "hold fast to the fault". One or more periods of silence may be taken for reflection on the entanglement of emotions and injuries. Everyone's feelings are acknowledged. Then confession, repentance and forgiveness take place. Everyone releases (_kala_) each other, letting go. They cut off the past (_ʻoki_), and together they close the event with a ceremonial feast, called _pani_, which often included eating _limu kala_ or [kala seaweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_seaweed "Kala seaweed"), symbolic of the release.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-24)

In a form used by the family of _kahuna_ Makaweliweli of the island of Molokaʻi, the completion of hoʻoponopono is represented by giving the person forgiven a [lei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei_(Hawaii) "Lei (Hawaii)") made from the fruit of the [hala tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hala_(tree) "Hala (tree)").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-25)

### Freedom from karma[[edit source](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ho%CA%BBoponopono&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Freedom from karma")]

[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lapakahi3.jpg "Enlarge")

The site of the partially restored remains of the village of Koaiʻe in the _Lapakahi State Historical Park_ of the island of _Hawaii_, North Kohala district. Beginning in the early 20th century, this village has been a center for lapaʻau

In 1976 [Morrnah Simeona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrnah_Simeona "Morrnah Simeona"), regarded as a healing priest or _[kahuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahuna "Kahuna") lapaʻau_, adapted the traditional hoʻoponopono of family mutual forgiveness to the social realities of the modern day. For this she extended it both to a general problem solving process outside the family and to a psycho-spiritual self-help rather than group process.

Simeona's version is influenced by her Christian (Protestant and Catholic) education and her philosophical studies about India, China and [Edgar Cayce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Cayce "Edgar Cayce"). Like Hawaiian tradition she emphasizes prayer, confession, repentance, and mutual restitution and forgiveness. Unlike Hawaiian tradition, she describes problems only as the effects of negative [karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma "Karma"), saying that "you have to experience by yourself what you have done to others." But that you are the creator of your life circumstances was common knowledge for the people of old as "things we had brought with us from other lifetimes."[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-30) Any wrongdoing is memorized within oneself and mirrored in every entity and object which was present when the cause happened. As the Law of Cause and Effect predominates in all of life and lifetimes, the purpose of her version is mainly "to release unhappy, negative experiences in past [reincarnations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation "Reincarnation"), and to resolve and remove traumas from the 'memory banks'."[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-31) Karmic bondages hinder the evolution of mind, so that "(karmic) cleansing is a requisite for the expansion of awareness".[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-32) Using her 14-step-process would dissolve those bondages.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-33) She did not use [mantras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantra "Mantra") or conditioning exercises.

Her teachings include: there is a Divine Creator who takes care of altruistic pleas of Men; "when the phrase 'And it is done' is used after a prayer, it means Man's work ends and God's begins."[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-34) "Self-Identity" signifies, e.g. during the hoʻoponopono, that the three selves or aspects of consciousness are balanced and connected with the Divine Creator.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-35) Different from egoistic prayers, "altruistic prayers like hoʻoponopono, where you also pray for the release of other entities and objects, reach the Divine plane or Cosmos because of their high vibrations. From that plane the Divine energy or "mana" would come,"[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-36) which would transform the painful part of the memory of the wrong actions in all participants to "Pure Light", on whatever plane they are existing; "all are set free".[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-37) Through this transmutation in the mind the problems will lose their energy for physical effects, and healing or balancing is begun. In this sense, Simeona's mana is not the same as the traditional Polynesian understanding of [mana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mana "Mana").

Pacifica Seminars, founded by [Morrnah Simeona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrnah_Simeona "Morrnah Simeona"), started the first Ho'oponopono seminars in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). Seminars are still held on a regular basis in Germany, Poland, France, and Denmark.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-38)[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-39)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%CA%BBoponopono#cite_note-40)
