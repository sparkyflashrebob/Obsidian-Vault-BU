Hypocognition is a state where someone is unable to use language or cognitive processes to describe ideas or interpret experiences. It can lead to cognitive deficits in memory, explanation, and recognition. Hypocognition can also cause social conflict and chauvinism in political and cultural spheres. [1, 2]

Here are some examples of hypocognition: [1]
## Tahitians and grief
In 1973, anthropologist Robert Levy observed that Tahitians were unable to express grief after the loss of a loved one because they lacked the concept of grief. [1]
## Martinique islanders and refrigeration
The islanders lacked a cognitive representation of refrigeration. [3]
## Type II diabetes
People with Type II diabetes may remain hypocognitive of their illness and fail to seek treatment. They may recognize symptoms like blurry vision, dry mouth, and frequent urination, but lack the concept to unify them into a single alarm. [4]
## Social privilege
People in socially dominant groups, such as White people and men, may be hypocognitive of the social advantage they enjoy. They may be unaware of the discrimination and disadvantage experienced by other groups. [5]
