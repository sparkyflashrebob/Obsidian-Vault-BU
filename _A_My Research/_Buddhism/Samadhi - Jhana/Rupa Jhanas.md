**The [[Rupa Jhāna]]s**

1. **First Jhāna** - To attain this jhāna, the meditator must fix his mind on the meditation object to reduce and eliminate the lower mental qualities which is called the [Five Hindrances](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Hindrances "Five Hindrances") (sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, and doubt) and promote the growth of five jhāna factors ([applied/directed thought](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitakka "Vitakka"), [sustained thought](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicara "Vicara"), [rapture](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piti "Piti"), [bliss](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukha "Sukha") and [one-pointedness](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaggata "Ekaggata")). In this stage, only the subtlest mental movement remains. The ability to form unwholesome intentions ceases.
2. **Second Jhāna** - To attain this jhāna, the meditator must reduce and eliminate the two initial factors of the first jhāna itself ([applied/directed thought](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitakka "Vitakka") and [sustained thought](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicara "Vicara")), the three remaining jhāna factors still possessed by the meditator are the [rapture](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piti "Piti"), [bliss](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukha "Sukha") and [one-pointedness](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaggata "Ekaggata"). In this stage, all mental movement utterly ceases. The ability to form wholesome intentions ceases as well. Acquires complete confidence.
3. **Third Jhāna** - To attain this jhāna, the meditator must reduce and eliminate the third initial factor of the first jhāna itself ([rapture](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piti "Piti")), the two remaining jhāna factors still possessed by the meditator are the [bliss](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukha "Sukha") and [one-pointedness](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaggata "Ekaggata"). Three additional components are possessed by the meditator ([equanimity](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upekkha "Upekkha"), mindfulness and discernment).
4. **Fourth Jhāna** - To attain this _jhāna_, the meditator must reduce and eliminate the fourth initial factor of the first _jhāna_ itself ([bliss](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukha "Sukha")) and replace it with another _jhāna_ factor ([equanimity/neutral feeling](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upekkha "Upekkha")), the two remaining _jhāna_ factors still possessed by the meditator are the [neutral feeling](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upekkha "Upekkha") and [one-pointedness](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaggata "Ekaggata"). In this stage, the meditator enters a state of supreme purity, equanimity, and pure consciousness.
