Clinical Mindfulness approach that does not utilize meditation. 

Langerian mindfulness is defined as the [[process of paying attention on purpose to the present moment]], of being aware of novelty in experiences or situations, and of
perceiving differences in contexts and events [1]. 

In contrast to the conceptualization of mindfulness associated with meditative practices that emphasizes a [[nonjudgmental awareness]] of the present moment [2], Langerian
mindfulness is characterized by a continuous creation of new categories, openness to new information and possibilities, awareness of more than one perspective,
and flexibility in perspective-taking [3]. 

In Langer’s conceptualization, mindfulness is the opposite of [[mindlessness]], the latter considering only a single perspective, of being entrenched in previous categorizations that do not incorporate new information from the current situational context.
