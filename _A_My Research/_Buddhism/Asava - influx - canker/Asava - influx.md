**[[Āsava]]** is a [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali") term ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"): Āsrava) that is used in Buddhist scripture, philosophy, and psychology, meaning "influx, canker." It refers to the [[mental defilements]] of [[sensual pleasures]], [[craving for existence]], and [[ignorance]], which perpetuate _[samsara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsara "Samsara")_, the beginningless cycle of rebirth, _[dukkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukkha "Dukkha")_, and dying again.

_Asavas_ are also translated as "karmic predilections" and "karmic propensities" in Buddhism.[[_A_My Research/Dharma Subjects and Themes/Logic and Philosophy/Catuskoti]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asava#cite_note-Lusthaus2014p73-1)

## Etymology[[edit source](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Asava&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Etymology")]

According to [Bhikkhu Bodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhikkhu_Bodhi "Bhikkhu Bodhi"),

> The commentaries derive the word from a root _su_ meaning "to flow." Scholars differ as to whether the flow implied by the prefix _ā_ is inward or outward; hence some have rendered it as "influxes" or "influences," others as "outflows" or "effluents."[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asava#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBhikkhu_Bodhi2005229-3)

[Ajahn Sucitto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajahn_Sucitto "Ajahn Sucitto") in his book 'Kamma and the end of Kamma' describes asavas as "underlying biases" (that fabricate things, emotions, sensations, and responses), which condition grasping through which samsara operates.

### Samsara[[edit source](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Asava&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Samsara")]

Main article: [Samsara (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsara_(Buddhism) "Samsara (Buddhism)")

The āsavas are mental defilements that perpetuate _samsara_, the beginningless cycle of rebirth, _dukkha_, and dying again. Carr and Mahalingam:

> inflow, influx, influence; mental bias or canker, cankers that keep one bound to the world of _[samsāra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sams%C4%81ra "Samsāra"); used particularly in [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") and [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asava#cite_note-FOOTNOTECarrMahalingam1997948-4)_

Bikkhu Bodhi:

> A stock passage in the suttas indicates the term's real significance independently of etymology when it describes the _āsavas_ as states "that defile, bring renewal of existence, give trouble, ripen in suffering, and lead to future birth, aging and death" (MN 36.47; I 250). Thus other translators, bypassing the literal meaning, have rendered it "cankers," "corruptions," or "taints."[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asava#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBhikkhu_Bodhi2005229-3)

De Silva further explains: The word canker suggests something that corrodes or corrupts slowly. These figurative meanings perhaps describe facets of the concept of _āsava_: kept long in storage, oozing out, taint, corroding, etc.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asava#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDe_Silva200075-5)
