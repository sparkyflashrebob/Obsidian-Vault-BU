MMR#69 - Fabrication and Fabrication

Teachings based primarily on intuitive awareness - allowing things to unfold.  This is part of it.
Westerners, like myself, can easily place too much energy in the practice and therefore come out of balance - fidgety - not settle down .
As we gain a foundation into the practice this changes.
Similes
1- Mindful of Breathing - like turning a piece of wood on a lathe - intention to be sensitive to long and short turns - long and short breaths
2- Mindful for Concentration - like the king’s cook trying to figure out what kind of food the king liked or didn’t like -
3- Dwelling in first jhana - to get pleasure and rapture to suffice the body - like a batman mixing water with a ball of bath powder to try to moisten every particle of powder and not drip outside the ball.
After getting to 1st Jhana other similes to describe less effort
* Spring filla lake with cool waters
* Lotuses immersed in a lake saturated with still, cool water from their roots to their tips
* A person sitting wrapped in a white cloth
* Point being that after the act of spreading rapture and pleasure throughout the body and achieving absorption - with awareness  - it is easier to deepen concentration.
But as we progress thru the levels of concentration attainment 2nd 3rd 4th Jhana and beyond one still needs to emply acts of intentions, desire, decision, and persistence to stay there.
When describing any level of jhana or formless attainments to develop discernment he reverted to a more active simile - the meditator was now like an archer who has mastered the skills of shooting rapidly, great distances and hitting the target with the arrows.

Conflict with modern teachers that insist buddhist meditation is not a matter of doing but of simply allowing things to happen on their own. However without any present effort in getting mindfulness or jhana to develop then these qualities would either be determined by physical laws, determined by ouor past karma, the result of the grace of a divine creator or simply serendipitous: sponpaneous events with no discernible cause at all.
Yet the Buddha make it clear that to believe that present experience come about purely in any of these four ways would allow for no room for a path of practice to the end of suffering to make any sense.
There would be nothing you could do in the present moment to choose such a path or to follow it.  If you believe in the possibility of choosing and following a path to the end of suffering, you have to believe that you can make a difference in the present with your present intentions. Otherwise, the path would be impossible.

"But what are bodily fabrications? What are verbal fabrications? What are mental fabrications?"
"In-and-out breaths are bodily fabrications. Directed thought and evaluation are verbal fabrications. Perceptions and feelings are mental fabrications."

[http://buddhasutra.com/files/cula_vedalla_sutta.htm](http://buddhasutra.com/files/cula_vedalla_sutta.htm)

"In the case of the one who is dead, who has completed his time, his bodily fabrications have ceased & subsided, his verbal fabrications ... his mental fabrications have ceased & subsided, his vitality is exhausted, his heat subsided, & his faculties are scattered. But in the case of a monk who has attained the cessation of perception & feeling, his bodily fabrications have ceased & subsided, his verbal fabrications ... his mental fabrications have ceased & subsided, his vitality is not exhausted, his heat has not subsided, & his faculties are exceptionally clear. This is the difference between one who is dead, who has completed his time, and a monk who has attained the cessation of perception & feeling."
