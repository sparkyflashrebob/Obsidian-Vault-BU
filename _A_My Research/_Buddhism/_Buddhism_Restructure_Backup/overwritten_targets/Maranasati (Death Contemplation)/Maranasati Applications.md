Samyutta Nikaya 55.22

Samyutta Nikaya 55.22 is a sutta that discusses the theme of non-self (anatta) and directly addresses the concept of self-identity and clinging to the notion of a permanent self. In this discourse, the Buddha uses the analogy of a chariot to explain the illusory nature of the self. The sutta explores how just as a chariot is a collection of parts - wheels, axles, reins, etc. - and not a permanent, independent entity, so too is the concept of a self. The Buddha teaches that when we examine the components of what we believe constitutes a "self," we find that there is no essence or unchanging core that can be identified as a self. By contemplating this teaching, practitioners are encouraged to develop insight into the nature of selflessness and to overcome attachments and delusions related to a fixed and enduring self. This understanding is crucial in the practice of reducing suffering and realizing liberation in the Buddhist path. The sutta Samyutta Nikaya 55.22 serves as a profound reflection on the nature of self and impermanence, inviting practitioners to investigate the nature of their existence and experience in a deeper and more insightful way. It highlights the core Buddhist teaching of anatta (non-self) and offers guidance on the path towards liberation from suffering.


SN 22.95

In the Theravada Buddhist tradition, there is a well-known analogy often used by the Buddha in various suttas known as the "Simile of the Clay Pot" or "Simile of the Potter." This analogy is found in several discourses to convey teachings on impermanence, selflessness, and the nature of existence. 
One example can be found in the Khajjaniya Sutta (SN 22.95) from the Samyutta Nikaya, where the Buddha compares the physical body to a clay pot. Just as a clay pot is subject to breaking, the body is also impermanent and subject to decay. 
This analogy illustrates the transient nature of the body and emphasizes the importance of understanding impermanence as a way to reduce attachment and suffering. It serves as a reminder of the temporary and fragile nature of our physical existence, encouraging practitioners to reflect on the impermanent and conditioned nature of all phenomena, including the body.
