
## <font color="#ff0000">HOW TO MAP OUR OWN INTERNAL COMMITTEE</font>

To map your own internal committee, you must treat your mind not as a single, static entity, but as a dynamic <font color="#00b050">**"town meeting,"</font> "unruly throng," or "corrupt city council"** composed of many different power centers that communicate across internal compartments and walls.

Here is a practical, step-by-step guide to mapping and training your own internal voices based on the Dhamma:

###### <font color="#00b0f0">Step 1: Identify the Members and Their "Hungers"</font>

Every member of your internal committee represents a different "self" or identity that you created and nurtured at some point in your past as a **specific strategy to attain happiness or satisfy a particular hunger**. To map a committee member, you must analyze its dual nature:

- **The Self as Consumer:** This is the part of the voice that is hungry, identifying what it needs to feel satisfied (e.g., a hunger for safety, control, praise, or quick sensory pleasure).
- **The Self as Producer:** This is the part of the voice that plans, strategizes, and uses its specific abilities to secure that "food" (e.g., throwing a tantrum, shutting down, or overworking).
- _How to practice:_ When a strong urge or reaction arises, step back and ask: **"Which sub-self is speaking right now?"** Give it a name (e.g., "The Panic Coordinator" or "The Pleaser"). Identifying it as a distinct voice helps loosen your automatic identification with it.

###### <font color="#00b0f0">Step 2: Expose Their Methods and Back-Room Deals</font>

Many committee members are highly strategic, obscure, and devious; they try to hide their real motives out of embarrassment, communicating in **subliminal hints, whispers, and back-room deals**.

- **Physical Hijacking:** Watch how unskillful factions (like anger or anxiety) try to manipulate you. A faction advocating anger will often **hijack your physical breath**, making it tight and uncomfortable, to convince the rest of the committee that you _must_ react unskillfully to find physical relief.
- **Flashing Images:** Be alert to the mental fabrications they use. Unskillful voices will actively **flash mental images and perceptions of danger or injustice** across your inner screen—much like a devious television producer—to make you fear or hate a situation.
- _How to practice:_ When a strong emotion hits, quiet your mind and investigate its physical "footprint". Notice if your breathing has tightened, and observe the specific words or images the voice is using to justify itself.

###### <font color="#00b0f0">Step 3: Exercise Your Veto Power</font>

A crucial realization in this practice is that **just because an unskillful or embarrassing idea is brought to the committee floor does not mean your mind is inherently bad**.

- **The Power of the Veto:** You do not have to assume responsibility for every thought that arises. Your responsibility and freedom lie entirely in **your power to adopt or veto the motion** once it is presented.
- **Alternative Alliances:** Refusing to feed an unskillful desire does not mean you are starving yourself. You can choose to identify with other, more skillful members of the committee who want to seek long-term peace and well-being.
- _How to practice:_ When an unskillful thought arises, mentally say: _"A motion has been proposed to the floor. I see it, but I veto it."_ Immediately anchor your attention back to the breath.

###### <font color="#00b0f0">Step 4: Nourish the Skillful Factions</font>

In an untrained mind, the wise, skillful members of the committee are often scattered and uncooperative—like tiny hummingbirds at a feeder that cannot band together to fight off a larger bird.

- **Feeding the Wise Members:** When you practice meditation, you are providing the skillful members of your committee with **nourishing food** in the form of physical rapture, pleasure, and equanimity.
- **Internal Education:** As these wise members grow stronger, they can band together and **actively train the more short-sighted, unskillful members**, teaching them to look at happiness from a longer-term perspective of peace.
- _How to practice:_ Focus on making the breath feel as comfortable and refreshing as possible. Let the physical ease spread through your body to nurture the mindful, wise parts of your mind.

###### <font color="#00b0f0">Step 5: "Put Them Out to Pasture"</font>

If you try to aggressively fight or repress a troublesome committee member, it will often warn you that if you don't give in, it will **go underground into subconscious repression** and sabotage you later.

- Instead of fighting them, you simply stop feeding them your attention.
- Treat these unskillful sub-selves like **"horses you can no longer use"**. You do not need to shoot them; you simply open the gate and put them out in a meadow to graze on their own.
- By persistently refusing to indulge them, you peel back their defenses, expose how weak their reasoning is, and **gradually pare down the active members of your committee** until the boardroom falls completely silent and you no longer need any constructed, artificial senses of "self" at all.
