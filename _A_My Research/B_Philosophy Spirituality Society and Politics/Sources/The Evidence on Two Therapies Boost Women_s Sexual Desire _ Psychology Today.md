### The Evidence on Two Therapies Boost Women's Sexual Desire

#### Two psychological therapies, not drugs, boost female desire and arousal.

##### Key points

```
*  Many women feel distressed about low—or no—sexual desire.
*  Mindfulness meditation and cognitive behavioral therapy work better and cost less than drugs.
*  The two psychological therapies also relieve several other common sex problems.
```

Some women feel little desire for sex. The current edition of the bible of mental health, the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual* , Fifth Edition, calls this “female [sexual](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/sex) interest-arousal disorder.” The FDA has approved one drug to treat it, Addyi (flibanserin), which produces modest benefits. Meanwhile, two psychological approaches work better and cost less: [mindfulness meditation](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/meditation) (MM) and [cognitive behavioral](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/cognitive-behavioral-therapy) therapy (CBT).
article continues after advertisement
Recently, researchers at several North American universities collaborated on a study that demonstrated the value of online MM and CBT for [low sexual desire](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/low-sexual-desire) in women.
**The Study**
The researchers surveyed the sexual function of 129 women complaining of low desire and difficulties becoming erotically aroused. Then the investigators randomized them into three groups:
•
A 43-person control group was placed on a waitlist.
•
Another group of 43 participated in 12 weeks of MM, including remote meetings with aids who provided support, but not sex therapy or [psychotherapy](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/therapy). MM involves either sitting quietly and focusing intently on the breath or the present moment, or focusing on a single, simple activity, for example, walking, slowly chewing one bite of food.
•
A third 43-person group participated in a CBT program. CBT involves identifying distorted thinking patterns and correcting them.
At the end of the study, all participants completed the survey again.
The waitlist group reported no significant changes in sexual measures, but both the MM and CBT groups reported significant increases in sexual desire, arousal, sexual function, and sexual satisfaction.
**Why Do MM and CBT Work?**
Great sex requires deep relaxation. One big sex killer is its opposite, emotional stress. A great deal of research has shown that both MM and CBT reduce stress and [anxiety](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/anxiety) and promote relaxation (below).
In addition, sexual responsiveness depends on robust blood flow to the genitals. But stress releases the fight-or-flight [hormone](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/hormones), cortisol, which constricts blood vessels throughout the body, including the genitals; extra blood circulates to the arms and legs to either fight the perceived threat or flee it. Stress reduction normalizes blood flow, directing more blood to the genitals, which promotes sexual desire, arousal, and pleasure.
article continues after advertisement
Great sex and meditation have a good deal in common. Both involve taking breaks from daily responsibilities. Both include deep breathing. Both encourage emptying the mind of extraneous thoughts. Both [mindfulness](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/mindfulness) and lovemaking expand [spiritual](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/spirituality) connections—meditators to the world around them, lovers to their partners. And after both, meditators and lovers emerge feeling calm and refreshed, better able to cope with life's vicissitudes.
CBT offers similar benefits. Consider this line from the Bob Dylan song *Maggie's Farm* : “I got a head full of ideas that are driving me insane.” Many people have heads full of sexual beliefs that may not drive them actually crazy, but produce stress sufficient to cause sex problems. CBT corrects mistaken thinking. For example, women might believe they “should” have orgasms solely from intercourse. When they don't, they experience stress that might ruin sex. Actually, few women have orgasms solely from intercourse, and the large majority of women need direct clitoral stimulation. With this change in perspective, women are better able to relax and hopefully obtain the stimulation they need to climax.

**Other Studies Agree**
One study author, professor Lori Brotto at the University of British Columbia, has published several other studies documenting the sexual benefits of MM and CBT:
•
**Low libido** . Brotto and colleagues recruited 117 low-desire women. Forty-nine were placed on a wait list. Sixty-eight women participated in three 90-minute classes over six weeks that educated them about the causes of low libido and provided counseling to minimize these causes and instruction in mindfulness meditation. Between classes, the women practiced daily mindfulness at home. After six months, the treatment group reported significantly greater desire, arousal, and lubrication, easier orgasms, and greater satisfaction.
article continues after advertisement
Investigators at Willamette University in Oregon analyzed eleven studies of MM involving 449 women who complained of low libido and arousal and [orgasm](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/orgasm) difficulties. “All aspects of sexual function and well-being exhibited significant improvement.”
•
**Erectile dysfunction** (ED). Another Brotto team enrolled 10 men suffering erection difficulties in a four-week MM program that provided information, counseling, and MM practice in therapy sessions and daily at home. Most of the men reported significant improvement.

•
**Men in distress because of porn.** Creighton University investigators took 38 men convinced they were [porn addicts](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/porn-addiction) to a rustic retreat center for eight days. They spent 32 hours in CBT. During sessions, the researchers endeavored to correct participants' sexual misconceptions, such as: Only bad people have solo sex. The therapists corrected that mistaken belief, explaining that almost everyone does it, particularly when [stressed](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/stress). Unless it interferes with life responsibilities or partner lovemaking, there's nothing wrong with self-sexing, even daily. After the retreat, the participants reported significantly less sexual distress.
•
**Child sex abuse** . A team led by Brotto enrolled 20 adult survivors of [childhood](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/child-development) sex [trauma](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/trauma) in a CBT program that helped them reframe their stories away from the horror of abuse toward [self-forgiveness](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/forgiveness) and personal empowerment. Half the group also learned MM and practiced it daily. After one month, both groups reported significantly less sexual distress.
article continues after advertisement
Doctors and psychiatrists tend to prescribe drugs rather than emphasize lifestyle and psychological approaches to people's problems. But when it comes to helping women reclaim erotic desire, MM and CBT have been proven superior to drugs.
