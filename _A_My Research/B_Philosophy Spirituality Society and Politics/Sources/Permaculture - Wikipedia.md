Approach to agriculture and land management
A garden cultivated on permaculture principles
Permaculture
is an approach to land management and settlement design that adopts arrangements observed in flourishing natural
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem
. It includes a set of design principles derived using
whole-systems thinking
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems\_theory
. It applies these principles in fields such as
regenerative agriculture
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regenerative\_agriculture
, town planning,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewilding\_(conservation\_biology)
community resilience
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community\_resilience
. The term was coined in 1978 by
Bill Mollison
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill\_Mollison
David Holmgren
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David\_Holmgren
, who formulated the concept in opposition to modern industrialized methods, instead adopting a more traditional or "natural" approach to agriculture.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:3-1
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:7-2
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:4-3
Multiple thinkers in the early and mid-20th century explored
no-dig gardening
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-dig\_gardening
no-till farming
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-till\_farming
, and the concept of "permanent agriculture", which were early inspirations for the field of permaculture.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Ferguson\_Lovell-4
Mollison and Holmgren's work from the 1970s and 1980s led to several books, starting with
Permaculture One
in 1978, and to the development of the "Permaculture Design Course" which has been one of the main methods of diffusion of permacultural ideas.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Scott-5
Starting from a focus on land usage in
Southern Australia
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern\_Australia
, permaculture has since spread in scope to include other regions and other topics, such as
appropriate technology
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appropriate\_technology
intentional community
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentional\_community
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Holmgren\_Essence-6
Several concepts and practices unify the wide array of approaches labelled as permaculture. Mollison and Holmgren's three foundational ethics and Holmgren's twelve design principles are often cited and restated in permaculture literature.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Scott-5
Practices such as
companion planting
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion\_planting
, extensive use of
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perennial
crops, and designs such as the
herb spiral
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb\_spiral
have been used extensively by permaculturists.
Permaculture as a popular movement has been largely isolated from scientific literature, and has been criticised for a lack of clear definition or rigorous methodology.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-For\_Sceptics\_2021-7
Despite a long divide, some 21st century studies have supported the claims that permaculture improves soil quality and biodiversity,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Reiff\_1%E2%80%9314-8
and have identified it as a
social movement
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social\_movement
capable of promoting
agroecological transition
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agroecology
away from conventional agriculture.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Ferguson\_Lovell-4
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Reiff\_1%E2%80%9314-8
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Permaculture&action=edit&section=1
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Permaculture&action=edit&section=2
Franklin Hiram King
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin\_Hiram\_King
introduced the term "permanent agriculture" in 1911.
Franklin Hiram King
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin\_Hiram\_King
Farmers of Forty Centuries: Or Permanent Agriculture in China, Korea and Japan
, describing farming practices of East Asia designed for "permanent agriculture".
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEKing1911Title\_page-9
Joseph Russell Smith
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph\_Russell\_Smith
appended King's term as the subtitle for
Tree Crops: A Permanent Agriculture
, which he wrote in response to widespread deforestation, plow agriculture, and erosion in the eastern mountains and hill regions of the United States. He proposed the planting of tree fruits and nuts as human and animal food crops that could stabilize watersheds and restore soil health.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-jrsjs-10
Smith saw the world as an inter-related whole and suggested mixed systems of trees with understory crops. This book inspired individuals such as
Toyohiko Kagawa
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyohiko\_Kagawa
who pioneered
forest farming
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest\_farming
in Japan in the 1930s.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEHart1996[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidN01940btQAQCpgPA41\_41]-11
Another pioneer,
George Washington Carver
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George\_Washington\_Carver
, advocated for practices now common in permaculture, including the use of
crop rotation
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop\_rotation
to restore nitrogen to the soil and repair damaged farmland, in his work at the
Tuskegee Institute
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuskegee\_Institute
between 1896 and his death in 1947.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-12
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-13
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-14
In his 1964 book
Water for Every Farm
, the Australian agronomist and engineer
P. A. Yeomans
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.*A.*Yeomans
advanced a definition of permanent agriculture as one that can be sustained indefinitely. Yeomans introduced both an observation-based approach to land use in Australia in the 1940s and in the 1950s the
Keyline design
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyline\_design
as a way of managing the supply and distribution of water in semi-arid regions. Other early influences include
Stewart Brand
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart\_Brand
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth\_Stout
Esther Deans
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther\_Deans
, who pioneered
no-dig gardening
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-dig\_gardening
Masanobu Fukuoka
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masanobu\_Fukuoka
who, in the late 1930s in Japan, began advocating
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-till\_farming
orchards and gardens and
natural farming
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural\_farming
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Holmgren\_Essence-6
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-15
Bill Mollison
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill\_Mollison
, who has been described as the "father of permaculture", cites Aboriginal Tasmanian belief systems as an inspiration of the practice.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:3-1
In the late 1960s,
Bill Mollison
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill\_Mollison
, senior lecturer in Environmental Psychology at
University of Tasmania
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University\_of\_Tasmania
David Holmgren
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David\_Holmgren
, graduate student at the then Tasmanian College of Advanced Education started developing ideas about stable agricultural systems on the southern Australian island of
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmania
. Their recognition of the unsustainable nature of modern industrialized methods and their inspiration from Tasmanian Aboriginal and other traditional practises were critical to their formulation of permaculture.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:3-1
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:7-2
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:4-3
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Permaculture\_1991-16
In their view, industrialized methods were highly dependent on non-
renewable resources
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable\_resource
, and were additionally poisoning land and water, reducing
biodiversity
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity
, and removing billions of tons of
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topsoil
from previously fertile landscapes. They responded with permaculture. This term was first made public with the publication of their 1978 book
Permaculture One
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Permaculture\_1991-16
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-17
Permaculture is a philosophy of working with, rather than against nature; of protracted and thoughtful observation rather than protracted and thoughtless labor; and of looking at plants and animals in all their functions, rather than treating any area as a
single product system
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocropping
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-18
— Bill Mollison
Following the publication of
Permaculture One
, Mollison responded to widespread enthusiasm for the work by traveling and teaching a three-week program that became known as the Permaculture Design Course. It addressed the application of permaculture design to growing in major climatic and soil conditions, to the use of renewable energy and natural building methods, and to "invisible structures" of human society. He found ready audiences in Australia, New Zealand, the USA, Britain, and Europe, and from 1985 also reached the Indian subcontinent and southern Africa. By the early 1980s, the concept had broadened from agricultural systems towards
sustainable human habitats
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable\_habitat
and at the 1st Intl. Permaculture Convergence, a gathering of graduates of the PDC held in Australia, the curriculum was formalized and its format shortened to two weeks. After
Permaculture One
, Mollison further refined and developed the ideas while designing hundreds of properties. This led to the 1988 publication of his global reference work,
Permaculture: A Designers Manual
. Mollison encouraged graduates to become teachers and set up their own institutes and demonstration sites.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-19
Critics suggest that this success weakened permaculture's social aspirations of moving away from industrial social forms. They argue that the self-help model (akin to
franchising
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchising
) has had the effect of creating market-focused social relationships that the originators initially opposed.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-20
Foundational ethics
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Permaculture&action=edit&section=3
The ethics on which permaculture builds are:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEMollison19882-21
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:14-22
"Care of the Earth: Provision for all life systems to continue and multiply".
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEMollison19882-21
"Care of people: Provision for people to access those resources necessary for their existence".
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEMollison19882-21
"Setting limits to population and consumption: By governing our own needs, we can set resources aside to further the above principles".
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEMollison19882-21
Mollison's 1988 formulation of the third ethic was restated by Holmgren
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:14-22
in 2002 as "Set limits to consumption and reproduction, and redistribute surplus" and is elsewhere condensed to "
share the surplus"
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-:5-23
Permaculture emphasizes patterns of

Permaculture design is founded in replicating or imitating natural patterns found in ecosystems because these solutions have emerged through evolution over thousands of years and have proven to be effective. As a result, the implementation of permaculture design will vary widely depending on the region of the Earth it is located in. Because permaculture's implementation is so localized and place specific, scientific literature for the field is lacking or not always applicable.

A core theme of permaculture according to Mollison is the idea of "people care". Seeking prosperity begins within a local community or culture that can apply the tenets of permaculture to sustain an environment that supports them and vice versa. This is in contrast, in Mollison's view, to typical modern industrialized societies, where locality and generational knowledge is often overlooked in the pursuit of wealth or other forms of societal leverage.

: Value and employ all available resources: waste nothing.

: Observe patterns in nature and society and use them to inform designs, later adding details.

Hügelkultur
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%BCgelkultur
is the practice of burying wood to increase soil water retention. The porous structure of wood acts like a sponge when decomposing underground. During the rainy season, sufficient buried wood can absorb enough water to sustain crops through the dry season.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-37
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-FOOTNOTEHemenway200984%E2%80%9385-38
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-39
This technique is a traditional practice that has been developed over centuries in Europe and has been recently adopted by permaculturalists.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-40
The Hügelkultur technique can be implemented through building mounds on the ground as well as in
raised garden beds
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raised-bed\_gardening
. In raised beds, the practice "imitates natural
nutrient cycling
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrient\_cycling
found in wood decomposition and the high water-holding capacities of organic detritus, while also improving bed structure and drainage properties." This is done by placing wood material (e.g. logs and sticks) in the bottom of the bed before piling organic soil and compost on top. A study comparing the water retention capacities of Hügel raised beds to non-Hügel beds determined that Hügel beds are both lower maintenance and more efficient in the long term by requiring less irrigation.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permaculture#cite\_note-Scott-5
