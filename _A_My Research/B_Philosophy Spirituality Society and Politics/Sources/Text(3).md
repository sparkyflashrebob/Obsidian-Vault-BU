Anand Giridharadas argues that the wealthy and powerful operate by using "elite do-gooding" (philanthropy, social responsibility) to address societal problems, but in ways that ultimately protect and reinforce the harmful systems (like predatory capitalism) that create those problems, allowing them to benefit from the status quo while appearing virtuous. They reframe social change to avoid challenging fundamental structures, focusing on "win-win" scenarios and systemic tweaks rather than systemic overhaul, thereby making themselves the heroes and maintaining their advantages. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
How They Operate (According to Giridharadas):

1. Elite Do-Gooding: Wealthy individuals and corporations fund solutions (like charter schools, art museums) to issues they often contribute to, gaining moral credit without changing the underlying rules.
2. Hijacking Social Change: By leading change efforts, they define what change is, ensuring it never threatens their power or wealth, framing it as something that benefits everyone (win-win).
3. Whitewashing Harm: Philanthropy serves as a "wingman" to harmful practices, distracting from predatory wealth extraction and creating a "moral glow" that obscures the damage done.
4. Avoiding Systemic Change: They focus on "giving back" or "chipping in" rather than tackling issues like excessive wealth concentration, tax avoidance, or corporate malfeasance that truly undermine democracy and society.
5. Controlling the Narrative: Through thought leadership and funding, they shape discourse, making some solutions (like union power) seem bad while promoting those that don't cost them much (like certain educational reforms).
6. Creating Gated Futures: Advances in technology and medicine benefit a select few, while the majority are left behind, creating a divided society where the rich monopolize progress. [1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11]
   The Core Problem:Giridharadas argues that this approach isn't just insufficient; it's a charade that actively perpetuates inequality, turning the wealthy into saviors of a system they've broken, rather than holding them accountable for the damage they cause. [1, 2, 5, 7, 12]
