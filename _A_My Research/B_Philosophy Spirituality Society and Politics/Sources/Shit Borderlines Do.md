DBT Options for Solving Problems

In DBT, when you are presented with a problem by life, you have four basic options. I’ll go over these options and the skill set that you would use for that option.

Option 1: Stay Miserable

Make Things Worse, by creating other, new problems for yourself.

If you want to stay miserable in the face of a problem, all you have to do is use

skills. To make matters

for yourself, all you need to do is to give in to your negative urges which will most likely end up creating a whole new problem in the process. Obviously, this is not the

course of action, but it’s important to note that it

an option, it

a choice you can make. One of the things that I find is unique about DBT is that

DBT acknowledges that a bad solution is still a solution and should be characterized as such even though it’s not a solution that therapists want us to choose when faced with a problem.

In fact, when many people with BPD enter treatment, we have maladaptive coping mechanisms that embody many of the characteristics of bad solutions, but they are solutions that have worked for us in some way which is why we use them. One of the key parts of DBT is teaching ourselves to not choose the bad solution that causes more problems (even if that bad solution provides short-term gratification or relief) and to explore our other options for dealing with problems.

Option 2: Tolerate The Problem

this involves accepting that the problem is happening, and tolerating both the problem itself and your responses to the problem.

The skill sets you use in this option are Distress Tolerance Skills and Mindfulness Skills. These skills help us stay in the moment, or focus our attention onto a different moment that is not the problem. These skills help us get through particularly difficult situations where we may not have the skills or the capacity to actually solve the problem. Some problems just have to be tolerated and Radically Accepted because there’s nothing more we can do about them. This is also an option you can choose and then choose one or both of the options below to address the problem once you’ve “weathered the storm” of your emotions, so to speak.

Sometimes we need to take care of our emotional needs and decrease the intensity of our emotions before we can work at solving the problem or changing our feelings about the problem.

This is why this option could be your “destination” or it could be part of your journey to your final “destination” for the problem you’re faced with.

Option 3: Feel Better About The Problem

this involves changing or regulating your emotional response to the problem

The skill set you would use in this option is Emotion Regulation Skills. These skills help us change our perspective on problems and life in general, they help us look past immediate problems and help us to see the bigger picture and the better prospects in our future. Basically, Emotion Regulation Skills help us feel better about our lives, even though problems still happen and our skills used in this option do not change the reality of these problems. Like Option 2, this option can be your “destination” or it can be part of your journey to the final option on this list.

Some problems can’t be changed, so we have to Radically Accept them, but unlike the “destination” problems in Option 2,

the problems we face that we can address with Option 3 are ones that we have the capacity to change our feelings towards.

This doesn’t make the problem go away, but it means our attitude and thus our responses are different and make us feel differently about the problem.

It makes the problem less of a problem in our minds and hearts, even though the reality of the problem has not changed.

Option 4: Solve The Problem

this involves changing the situation, or avoiding, leaving, or getting out of the situation for good

There are a variety of skill sets you can use for this option. You can use

Interpersonal Effectiveness Skills

if your problem involved another person or a group of people. You can use the

principles of Walking the Middle Path

(this draws from virtually all elements of DBT but primarily Interpersonal Effectiveness). Or you can use

problem-solving skills

(some of which are covered with Emotion Regulation Skills but there are additional problem-solving skills you could use).

This is the ideal Option to choose, but it’s not always an option. Sometimes we just do not have the skills or the power to solve the problems we’re faced with. But we strive so solve (in some way or another) the problems that life deals us or that we create for ourselves.

This option encourages you to be creative when faced with problems and find new ways of dealing with them if your old methods of solving problems won’t work. Problem-solving is a skill we are always capable of improving and we are constantly being exposed to new solutions and new ways of arriving at solutions.

Problem-solving can also be collaborative: you can seek advice, help, or assistance from friends, family, your therapist, other medical professionals, etc. You don’t have to solve every problem alone.

The Options for solving any problem are 1) Staying Miserable / Making it Worse, 2) Tolerate the Problem, 3) Feel Better about the Problem, or 4) Solve the Problem.
