Here’s a look at our very first Work in Progress issue, focused on compassion fatigue:

Working in the social-impact sector is deeply meaningful, but it can also be emotionally draining. Whether you're working directly with vulnerable populations or supporting a mission behind the scenes, the weight of the work can build quietly over time.

This emotional strain, known as compassion fatigue, is a common and natural response to prolonged exposure to others' trauma and needs. It’s more than burnout—it’s a deep weariness that can leave you feeling numb, disconnected, or even hopeless.

Our organizations rely on us, and our colleagues, to keep things running. It doesn’t matter if the mission is focused on a big, lofty goal like fighting climate change or specifically rooted in local community service; at the end of the day, you can’t help but feel impacted by the work.

Compassion fatigue happens when we constantly extend ourselves outward without replenishing our own emotional reserves. Helping professionals are wired to care, but without intentional boundaries and self-reflection, that care can come at a personal cost. You might find yourself skipping breaks, carrying work home, or feeling guilty for taking time off. Over time, this erodes your capacity to stay present (not only for others, but for yourself, too!).

So how do we manage these feelings of stress, guilt, despair, and fatigue?

“Recognizing Compassion Fatigue in the Helping Professions” dives into a few helpful strategies for managing compassion fatigue and its outcomes head on. For more tips on managing your mental health at work, check out some of our other resources here.
