This opinion piece argues that the Supreme Court's decision in *Louisiana v. Callais* effectively dismantles the protections against racial vote dilution established by Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act (VRA) [1, 2]. While Justice Samuel Alito's majority opinion framed the ruling as a mere update to evidentiary requirements, the authors contend that it **imposes impossible new demands that secretly rewrite the VRA** [2, 3].

The authors highlight two major changes introduced by the ruling:

* **Redefining Racial Bloc Voting:** The ruling requires plaintiffs to **"control for party affiliation" when attempting to prove racial bloc voting** [3, 4]. Instead of looking at whether white and minority voters broadly prefer different political parties, plaintiffs must now prove that racial groups have different voting preferences *within* the same political party [4, 5]. The authors argue this demand is "nonsensical," as it strips VRA protection from minority voters seeking to elect the specific *party* of their choice [5, 6].
* **Protecting Partisan Gerrymandering:** The decision demands that **plaintiffs' proposed alternative voting maps must accommodate a state's "specified political goals,"** such as maintaining a targeted partisan distribution or preserving specific incumbent seats [6, 7]. According to the authors, this makes Section 2 claims logically impossible to win in any state where racially polarized voting and partisan gerrymandering co-occur [7, 8].

The authors trace this shift to the Court's 2018 *Rucho v. Common Cause* decision, arguing that Alito has improperly elevated the right of state legislatures to engage in partisan gerrymandering above the VRA's core purpose [8-10]. By doing so, they conclude that the Court is **overriding Congress's intent and destroying the VRA at every juncture while pretending to uphold it** [10-12].
