In a Social Permaculture context, a "heart sharing" or process meeting is a gathering dedicated entirely to the emotional and psychological well-being of the group, completely separate from logistical, business, or land management meetings [1, 2].

Based on the sources regarding community building and mindful communication, here is how a heart sharing meeting would effectively work:

**1. Set a Regular, Dedicated Time**
Communities often hold these process meetings on a regular schedule, such as every two weeks [2]. The strict focus of this time is to process the emotional realities of transitioning to and sustaining a cooperative way of life [2]. It is a time for personal exploration and group exercises rather than discussing theories or daily tasks [3].

**2. Establish Clear Group Norms for Safety**
Because heart sharing requires vulnerability, the group must create a safe "container" for truth-telling [4]. This typically involves agreeing to a set of communication norms, such as:

* Maintaining strict confidentiality [5].
* Using "I" statements to speak directly from personal experience rather than making assumptions about others [5].
* Committing to air difficult feelings in a spirit of love and wisdom, rather than resorting to blame or criticism [5].

**3. Practice Deep, Mindful Listening**
When one person is sharing, the rest of the group practices active, mindful listening—often described as listening with the "ear of the heart" [6]. This means listeners give their full attention without interrupting, mentally planning their response, or judging the speaker [6, 7]. Listeners also refrain from offering advice or trying to "fix" the speaker's pain unless explicitly asked [7]. This unconditional presence allows the speaker's natural vitality and spirit to emerge [8].

**4. Embrace Both Joys and Difficulties**
While these meetings are crucial for navigating the inevitable friction and "shadow" aspects of a community, they are not exclusively for grievances. A true heart sharing meeting makes space to process both the profound difficulties and the joys of living together [2].

By tending to these "invisible structures" of human relationships, communities ensure that their interpersonal foundations are as resilient as their physical gardens [9]. In fact, participants in intentional communities often report that these heart sharing sessions become their favorite meetings [2].
