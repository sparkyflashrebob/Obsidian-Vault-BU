The 10 Stages of Genocide is a widely recognized framework developed by Dr. Gregory Stanton in 1996 for Genocide Watch. It outlines the sequential, overlapping phases through which genocides progress, serving as an essential tool for recognizing early warning signs and preventing mass atrocities. [1, 2, 3, 4]

The 10 stages include:
Classification: Dividing people into distinct "us" versus "them" categories based on ethnicity, race, religion, or nationality.
Symbolization: Forcing targeted groups to wear symbols or using specific names to visibly separate them from the dominant group.
Discrimination: Using law, custom, and political power to deny civil rights or full citizenship to a targeted group.
Dehumanization: Treating the targeted group as subhuman (e.g., comparing them to vermin or diseases) to strip away their human dignity and justify violence.
Organization: Planning genocides at the state or military level, often training militias and acquiring weapons.
Polarization: Using propaganda to drive groups apart, silencing moderates who attempt to bridge the divide, and forbidding social interaction.
Preparation: Drawing up death lists, expropriating victims' property, and segregating them into ghettos or camps in preparation for mass murder.
Persecution: Forcing targeted groups into dire living conditions, subjecting them to systematic abuse, and carrying out initial massacres.
Extermination: Committing mass murder, where perpetrators view their actions as the justified elimination of an undesirable group.
Denial: The final stage where perpetrators attempt to destroy evidence, deny the atrocities occurred, and blame the victims to escape accountability. [2, 9]

These stages are not strictly linear and often happen simultaneously. Understanding them provides a structural method to intervene and halt the process before it reaches the extermination phase. [2, 3, 10]
