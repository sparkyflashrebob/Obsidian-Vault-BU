MODEL:

Personalizing your writing style relies on combining two foundational documents: the **"Text DNA Bible,"** which dictates the positive rules for what you *always* do, and your **"Writing Guardrails,"** which define the negative rules for what you *never* do [1, 2]. Together, these files act as a "permanent style shield" that replaces generic AI fingerprints with your specific, unique voice [2].

To deeply personalize your writing style, you can customize several core elements of your digital voice:

* **Voice and Tone:** You define your actual spoken register, instructing the AI whether your natural tone is casual, dry, warm, or blunt, so the output sounds like a "real person" rather than someone "trying to sound professional" [3, 4]. You also specify your **humor style** (e.g., dry humor, sarcasm, self-deprecation) so it never feels forced [5], and define **how you handle criticism**, choosing whether you prefer to soften disagreements with context or directly pick a side and stand behind it [5, 6].
* **Vocabulary Preferences:** Personalization involves listing the specific, plain words you actually use in conversation (like using "help" instead of "facilitate") and incorporating your natural filler phrases [7, 8]. Conversely, you must outline the **words you never use**, removing corporate speak, overused phrases, and expressions that make you cringe [6, 8].
* **Sentence Rhythm:** A major part of your digital voice is your **natural sentence rhythm** [4]. You can specify if you naturally think in short, punchy sentences or longer, flowing ones [7]. The baseline recommendation is to vary sentence lengths dramatically—mixing fragments with longer explanations—and never sustaining the same length for more than two consecutive sentences [4, 9].
* **Structuring and Formatting:** You control how your pieces are structured, such as **how you open and close things** [10, 11]. A standard approach is to skip the "wind-up" and start in the middle of a thought, while avoiding summary endings in favor of just stopping when you are done [11]. You also dictate **formatting preferences**, like the maximum length of your paragraphs, when to use bullet points, and whether to include headers or bold text [12].
* **Defining Annoyances:** A helpful way to personalize your style is to list exactly **what you find annoying to read**, such as restating the same point multiple times or over-explaining obvious concepts [10].

Ultimately, this personalization is anchored by your **content philosophy**—what you actually value and optimize for in your writing [12, 13]. By default, this philosophy advocates for **clarity over completeness, specificity over generality, and directness over diplomacy** [13].
