### A Hot and Fiery Decade for Kīlauea

Image of the Day for December 9, 2025
The volcano in Hawaii is one of the most active in the world, and NASA tech makes it easier for volcanologists to monitor new developments.
NASA Earth Observatory
Dec 09, 2025 Article
View more Images of the Day:[Dec 8, 2025](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/islands-of-fire-and-ice-veiled-in-cloud/)[Dec 10, 2025](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/islands-of-fire-and-ice-veiled-in-cloud/)Instruments:

* [Aqua](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/islands-of-fire-and-ice-veiled-in-cloud/) — [MODIS](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-aqua-modis/)
* [Landsat 8](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-aqua-modis/) — [OLI](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-landsat-8-oli/)
* [Landsat 9](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-landsat-8-oli/) — [OLI](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-landsat-9-oli-2/)
* [Terra](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-landsat-9-oli-2/) — [MODIS](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-terra-modis/)
  Topics:
* [Volcanoes](https://science.nasa.gov/science-org-term/eo-sensors-terra-modis/)
  Of the hundreds of active volcanoes that NASA satellites have observed since the early 2000s, [Kīlauea](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea) stands out. It's one of the world's leading emitters of [thermal infrared energy](https://www.eoportal.org/other-space-activities/thermal-imaging#thermalanomalyandso2), according to [observations](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2023.1240107) from the [Aqua](https://aqua.nasa.gov/) and [Terra](https://terra.nasa.gov/about) satellites. (Other top emitters include [Bárðarbunga in Iceland](https://www.earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/85031/growth-of-the-holuhraun-lava-field) and the Democratic Republic of the Congo's [Nyiragongo](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/83954/nyamuragira-volcano-erupts).)
  This chart shows the volcano's daily average [spectral radiance](http://modis.higp.hawaii.edu/daytime.html) from 2016 to 2025, underscoring its frequent activity and variability. The chart draws on data from [MODVOLC](http://modis.higp.hawaii.edu/), an automated volcano monitoring system developed by scientists at the University of Hawai'i. The system processes observations of all the world's volcanoes from [MODIS](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/) (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer) sensors and detects [thermal anomalies](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/descriptions/FIRMS_MODIS_Firehotspots.html), or "hot spots," on a daily basis. Here, Kīlauea'sspectral radiance is shown,depicting the amount of energy emitted in a specific direction, from a specific area, at a specific wavelength.
  From 2016 to 2018 (left side of the chart), Kīlauea showed low but continuous activity due to relatively gentle, long-lived eruptions at two separate vents. At [Halema‘uma‘u](https://www.earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/81781/halemaumau-crater) (below), the summit crater, a previously unstable [lava lake](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp1867A) had stabilized and grown to cover about 42,000 square meters (452,000 square feet). This made it the world's second-largest lava lake, surpassed only by Nyiragongo's. Kīlauea's lava lake frequently sloshed, spattered, and sometimes overflowed, sending lava across the crater floor and producing many low-level thermal anomalies.
  *Past eruptions at Kīlauea have left a mark on the landscape, visible in this image acquired by* *Landsat 9* *on February 2, 2022.* At the same time, lava poured from Puʻu ʻŌʻō *(* above *)* , a cone farther down the mountain in the [East Rift](https://hilo.hawaii.edu/natural-hazards/volcanoes/riftzones.php)—a geologically active area where eruptions are most likely. In this period, lava spilling from Puʻu ʻŌʻō sometimes formed "breakout" flows that drained all the way to the coastline and built a [delta](http://www.usgs.gov/news/volcano-watch-kilauea-volcanos-kamokuna-ocean-entry-reaches-its-one-year-anniversary) at Kamokuna as lava [entered the sea](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/89127/kilaueas-lava-pours-into-the-pacific).
  On April 30, 2018, the situation changed abruptly. Signaling the end of Kīlauea's [longest and most voluminous](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/science/puuoo-eruption-lasted-35-years) known outpouring of lava in 500 years, Puʻu ʻŌʻō's [crater floor collapsed](https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/kilauea-volcano-clear-views-puu-oo) **,** and Puʻu ʻŌʻō stopped erupting. Magma moved through underground conduits farther east into the [Lower East Rift Zone](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/map-kilauea-volcanos-lower-east-rift-zone) (LERZ) rather than flowing south down the hillside and into the sea, as it had since 1983. A few days later, the ground split open in Leilani Estates, and lava flows bulldozed and burned whole neighborhoods in the densely populated subdivision.
  This [false-color image](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-infrared-glow-of-kilaueas-lava-flows-92194/) *, captured by Landsat 8, shows the infrared signal emitted by fresh lava flowing toward the sea on May 23, 2018.* By the time the three-month LERZ eruption wound down, an outpouring of more than [320,000 Olympic-sized pools](https://www.nps.gov/havo/planyourvisit/upload/PrelimSum_LERZ-Summit_2018_508.pdf) of lava had destroyed 700 homes and caused up to [$1 billion](https://recovery.hawaiicounty.gov/resources/2018-eruption) in economic losses. So much lava [spread across the surface](https://livingatlas.arcgis.com/landsatexplorer/#mapCenter=-154.85440%2C19.47606%2C12.984&mode=find+a+scene&mainScene=2018-08-02%7CShort-wave+Infrared+for+Visualization%7C8269414) at once that the eruption pushed the volcano's spectral radiance to more than ten times the 2016 baseline. That was enough to cause [a large spike](https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/1240107/feart-11-1240107-HTML/image_m/feart-11-1240107-g003.jpg) in Earth's total thermal emissions from volcanoes, according to [one global analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2023.1240107) from researchers based in Italy.
  After the big 2018 LERZ eruption, Kīlauea was quiet for a few years. In 2019, a [new water lake formed](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/science/2019-2020-summit-water-kilauea) (below) in the area where Halema‘uma‘u's lava lake had existed, and it slowly deepened until December 2020, when a new eruption at the summit quickly boiled off the water. Over the next three years, a [series of five small eruptions](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/science/december-2020-september-2023-kilauea-summit-eruptions) in Halema‘uma‘u gradually refilled the lava lake.
  A series of [Landsat images](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/146687/a-new-lakewater-not-lavaon-kilauea) *acquired between 2018 and 2020 shows water pooling in the bottom of Halema‘uma‘u crater.* Then, starting in [December 2024](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/science/eruption-information) (below), events took an unexpected turn. Even more intense surges of activity began to occur in Halema‘uma‘u every few weeks, usually in the form of huge lava fountains that lasted for several hours, contributing to the spikes on the right side of the chart.
  One recent eruptive episode, the 37th since December 2024, produced fountains that soared up to 1,000 feet (300 meters), while plumes of particles and volcanic gases swirled into "[volnados](https://nypost.com/2025/11/28/us-news/giant-volnado-emerges-during-most-recent-kilauea-volcano-eruption-in-hawaii/)" that wowed onlookers in November 2025. On December 6, 2025, the 38th episode produced a lava fountain that sprayed beyond Halemaʻumaʻu's rim and [destroyed](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/watch-lava-from-hawaiis-kilauea-volcano-obliterate-a-webcam/) a U.S. Geological Survey camera site.
  "The height of the fountaining we've started to see at Halema‘uma‘u is amazing," said Ian Flynn, a volcanologist at the University of Pittsburgh. Scientists are seeing a surge in volatile-rich magma coming from the volcano since December 2024. This means the magma has more dissolved gases, causing it to rise and "fizz" as it erupts from the surface, much like a shaken bottle of soda, he added. According to MODVOLC data, these frequent spikes in activity often push Kīlauea's spectral radiance to double or triple the baseline levels prior to the 2018 eruption.
  This [false-color image](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/153767/a-fiery-display-at-kilauea) *, acquired by Landsat 8, shows the infrared signature of new lava at Kīlauea's summit crater on December 24, 2024.* It's unclear what Kīlauea's future will bring. For the past 200 years, relatively gentle [effusive eruptions](https://volcanoes.usgs.gov/vsc/glossary/effusive_eruption.html) have dominated, but the [geologic record](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/science/geology-and-history-kilauea) makes clear that Kīlauea cycles into periods of more explosive eruptions, as occurred between [1500 and 1800](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvolgeores.2011.11.009), when groundwater mixed with magma and made eruptions much more violent.
  It is unknown when a similar transition will occur, but whatever happens, scientists will analyze how new developments compare to the volcano's past behavior. Such analysis would be impossible without decades of thermal observations from MODIS and data processing systems like [MODVOLC](http://modis.higp.hawaii.edu/) and [MIROVA](https://www.mirovaweb.it/NRT/) (the Near Real Time Volcanic Hot Spot Detection System), a similar tool developed at the University of Turin. These tools allow researchers and anyone else to freely access and analyze thermal activity observed by MODIS for all the world's active volcanoes.
  As Terra and Aqua age and grow closer to the end of their missions, both the [MODVOLC](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AGUFM.V33E..02W/abstract) and [MIROVA](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22051713) teams are updating their systems to ingest data from a newer generation of sensors called [VIIRS](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/instruments/viirs) (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite) to ensure that they can continue building long-term datasets for individual volcanoes.
  "Long-term datasets and monitoring tools like these are powerful and absolutely critical," said Flynn. "They make it possible to study, on a global scale, how volcanic activity is changing over long periods and to better predict the thermal precursors of eruptions."
  NASA Earth Observatory chart and East Rift Zone image by Michala Garrison, using [MODVOLC data](http://modis.higp.hawaii.edu/) from the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/) and [Landsat data](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/landsat/data-overview/) from the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://earthexplorer.usgs.gov/) *. Story by Adam Voiland.*
