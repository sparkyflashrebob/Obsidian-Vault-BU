Volcano[Kīlauea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%ABlauea)Date November 1790 A.D., around the 17th or 18th[Type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_volcanic_eruptions)[Phreatomagmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_volcanic_eruptions), [Strombolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strombolian_eruption), [Phreatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreatic_eruption)Location[Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_(island)), [Hawaiian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands)[19°24′46″N 155°16′32″W ﻿ / ﻿ 19.41278°N 155.27556°W ﻿ / 19.41278; -155.27556](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands)[VEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands)4 Impact More than 400 deaths MapsThe **Keanakakoi eruption** was a [VEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_Explosivity_Index)-4 eruption that occurred from the summit [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera) of [Kīlauea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%ABlauea)[volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%ABlauea) in or around November 1790.[ 1 ][ 2 ] It has been described as the [deadliest volcanic eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_volcanic_eruptions_by_death_toll) in what is now the United States, with more than 400 people having been killed in the event.[ 2 ][ 3 ] The eruption deposited the Keanakakoi Ash which surrounds the Kīlauea Caldera.[ 2 ]
  Three eruptive phases define the Keanakakoi eruption, all of which were separated by quiescent spells. The first phase of the eruption was [phreatomagmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreatomagmatic_eruption), and involved the deposition of fine-grained, well-bedded [volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash). A [Strombolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strombolian_eruption)-style [scoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoria) fall deposit and phreatomagmatic ash similar to that of the first phase were deposited during the second phase. The third and final phase was [phreatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreatic_eruption) and produced interbedded [pyroclastic fallout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_fall) and [surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_surge) deposits.[ 2 ]
