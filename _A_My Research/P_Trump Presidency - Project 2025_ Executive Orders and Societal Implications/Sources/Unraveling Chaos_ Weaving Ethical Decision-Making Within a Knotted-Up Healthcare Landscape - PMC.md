### Unraveling Chaos: Weaving Ethical Decision-Making Within a Knotted-Up Healthcare Landscape

  PMC Copyright noticePMCID: PMC12056846 PMID: [40096565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40096565/)Health care is a complex adaptive system nested within and connected to other complex systems. It is dynamic and highly networked with interdependent, heterogeneous agents. These system characteristics are why health care and other complex industries are unpredictable despite our best efforts ([Watson & Wilson, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b19-cjon-292-108)). Imagine a tangled pile of different colored yarn or threads. It is difficult to see the start or end of most of the yarn, and there is no apparent order. Picking up one thread will inherently disrupt one or more threads because they are connected.
  Just as pulling one thread in a tangled system disrupts others, policy changes create systemic ripple effects within networked systems—like health care—with unintended and unimaginable consequences. The U.S. Constitution gives the president executive power via executive order (EO) purview, and every president since George Washington has wielded this authority. Trump has signed 87 EOs to date while signing 220 in his first term. Presidents across both parties have executed thousands of EOs in their presidential careers—even up to Franklin D. Roosevelt’s record 3,721 EOs. Much of Trump’s EO work has focused on increasing federal government efficiency, curbing unsustainable national spending, and tackling foreign affairs and social policy ([UC Santa Barbara, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b17-cjon-292-108)). Several of Trump’s EOs are healthcare-specific, and they have far-reaching implications for all Americans but particularly for women, children, and other vulnerable populations.
  EO 14168’s original intention mandated that people recognize only two biologic sexes to protect and defend women ([Federal Register, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b3-cjon-292-108)). However, as a result, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and U.S. Food and Drug Administration removed longstanding national datasets and health information ([Dunbar, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b2-cjon-292-108)), and some U.S. Food and Drug Administration scientists and National Science Foundation staffers reported receiving a list of words commonly used in research that are now prohibited, although an official White House spokesperson has said the words did not need to be removed from communications ([Johnson et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b6-cjon-292-108); [Levy, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b8-cjon-292-108)). The Trump administration banned similar language in 2017 (Sun & Eilperin), likewise expeditiously clarified as nonexistent ([Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b5-cjon-292-108)). One disrupted thread entangles many others.
  EO 14158 established the novel Department of Government Efficiency ([Federal Register, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b3-cjon-292-108)), which has disrupted numerous U.S. industries. In unprecedented moves, the Department of Government Efficiency has conducted mass layoffs of integral U.S. infrastructure, including the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and U.S. Department of Education ([Kinnard, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b7-cjon-292-108))—some of which the administration is now trying to reverse ([Natter, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b10-cjon-292-108)). Many of these original actions are now mired in legal battles, perpetuating tangled uncertainty.
  Although this could be a potential example of creative destruction, which is a tension that makes space for true innovation within systems, it is hard to understand the full impact of complex changes without living and experiencing the effects ([Watson & Wilson, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b19-cjon-292-108)). For example, the National Institutes of Health has already stalled applications for medical research funds, and the administration is seeking to significantly reduce indirect funding limits—including those for cancer ([R. Stein, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b15-cjon-292-108)). This is leading to some universities pausing doctoral research program admissions until they can better understand the financial impact, which can affect future medical discoveries, innovations, and workforce pipelines. All government organizations have stopped collecting sexual orientation and gender identity data in adherence to EO 14168 ([Federal Register, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b3-cjon-292-108)), which may affect numerous aspects of public health. Only time will tell as disrupting multiple threads moves and knots countless others.
  Complex adaptive systems are unpredictable, making decision-making challenging. In times of uncertainty, people want control and often seek as much clarity as possible. However, fewer rules and guidelines—not more—helps spur individuals, teams, and organizations toward a shared vision of the future ([Watson, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b20-cjon-292-108)). Nursing has a professional code of ethics to guide us in decision-making. When all else is unclear, nurses can find this anchoring thread. The 10 provisions of the *Code of Ethics for Nurses* are concise and powerful (<https://codeofethics.ana.org/provisions>).
  Americans have a history of trusting nurses, in large part thanks to our code of ethics ([Saad, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b13-cjon-292-108)). Even in the tangled mess of uncertainty, we never lose sight of the thread—people needing care. Nurses have earned this trust with every interaction we have individually and collectively made in research, publishing, advocating, and serving in visible and invisible ways in almost every setting imaginable. Oncology nurses exemplify the code of ethics, as evidenced by the mission, vision, and values of the Oncology Nursing Society ([ONS, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b11-cjon-292-108)) .
  On January 29, 2025, ONS President Jessica MacIntyre, DNP, MBA, APRN, NP-C, AOCNP®, and ONS Chief Executive Officer Brenda Nevidjon, MSN, RN, FAAN, sent a joint email to ONS members reinforcing ONS’s 50-year commitment to “advocating for policies that improve patient access to high-quality cancer care, supporting evidence-based practices and the advancement of science, and monitoring government and healthcare changes and responding accordingly.” Key threads of decision-making, organizations, journals, and clinicians alike are consistently speaking up and amplifying voices regarding the potentially devastating and far-reaching health effects President Trump’s policies may have on people ([Clark & Abbasi, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b1-cjon-292-108); [International Committee of Medical Journal Editors, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b4-cjon-292-108); [PLOS, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b12-cjon-292-108); [D. Stein, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b15-cjon-292-108); [Wagatsuma, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov#b18-cjon-292-108)).
  As the *Clinical Journal of Oncology Nursing* ( *CJON* ) is a vehicle to advance excellence in oncology nursing and quality cancer care, the journal will continue to publish clinically relevant, evidence-based content for oncology nurses in diverse roles and practice settings to use when caring for those affected by cancer. If anyone is affected by cancer, *CJON* will support the nurse caring for that person to provide the best care possible via relevant evidence and publications. *CJON* will not let oncology nurses work in evidence vacuums nor turn its sights from caring for populations of every kind.
  Despite policy shifts, oncology nurses uphold an ethical duty to provide care without exclusion, recognizing individuals’ inherent dignity and worth. Censorship has no place in health care as we work toward the Quintuple Aim, and it has no place in academia, implementation science, or evidence-based practice as clinicians are pulled by curiosity to inquire more. If you feel like the current policy and social tangles are overwhelming, navigate the seemingly knotted mess by turning back to the 10 ethical provisions to support your oncology nursing care one decision at a time.
