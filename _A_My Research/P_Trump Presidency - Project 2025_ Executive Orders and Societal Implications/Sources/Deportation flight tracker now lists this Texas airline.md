#### The ICE Flight Monitor now mentions Avelo since it started federal deportation flights in May.

By [Andrea Guzmán](https://www.chron.com/author/andrea-guzman/), Texas Brands Reporter Aug 27, 2025Avelo Airlines takes off with first flight between Burbank and Santa Rosa at Hollywood Burbank Airport on April 28, 2021 in Burbank, California.
Joe Scarnici/Getty Images for Avelo Air
Houston-based [Avelo Airlines](https://www.chron.com/culture/article/avelo-airlines-deportation-flights-20263403.php) first started conducting federal deportation flights out of Arizona in mid-May. Now, an analysis of publicly available aviation data looks at Avelo's flight contributions for [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://www.chron.com/culture/article/avelo-airlines-deportation-houston-20294746.php) so far.
Tom Cartwright, a refugee advocate with immigrant rights group Witness at the Border, has been tracking the activity through the [ICE Flight Monitor](https://humanrightsfirst.org/ice-flight-monitor/). The monitor began mentioning Avelo after the airline signed an agreement for a long-term charter program flying domestic and international flights for the Department of Homeland Security.

Cartwright's report notes that ICE Air does not disclose information or data about its flights. Still, the information is based on flight information from the publicly available FlightAware application, FlightRadar24, AirNav and the ADB-S tracking system. To identify the removal flights, Cartwright filters them to the likely ICE Air removal and destination locations, such as Guatemala and Honduras, which represented half of deportation flights in July. "Any errors in our estimations, I believe, are small and certainly immaterial to the analysis," Cartwright says in the monitor, parts of which have been verified by reporters at [the New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/07/31/us/ice-deportation-hub-alexandria-louisiana.html).
Avelo declined to comment, saying the sources are not from valid measurement sites and the numbers are not confirmed.
According to Cartwright's May [report](https://humanrightsfirst.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICEAirMayTHCPDF.pdf), Avelo operated 116 flight legs, including five removal flights, representing 2.5 percent of all removal flights and 11 percent of total ICE Air flights for the half month they had been operating.
As the summer carried on, Cartwright's analysis found that June and July saw a similar mix of ICE Air charters by carrier. In July, GlobalX operated 50 percent of total flights, followed by Eastern Air Express at 24 percent, Avelo at 20 percent, and the military at 2 percent. Others and unallocated represent the rest.

For [July removal flights](https://humanrightsfirst.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ICEAirJulTHCPDF.pdf), defined as a flight from a domestic ICE Air location to an international removal destination, GlobalX was listed at 56 percent, Eastern Air Express at 14 percent, Avelo at 10 percent, military at 9 percent, OMNI, Eastern, and Colombian Air Force each at 2 percent and all others and unallocated making up 5 percent.
The number of detainees on the flights is unknown, as the numbers aren't disclosed, and Cartwright notes there is no way to estimate. But regardless of the number of passengers, Avelo has faced [ongoing protests and criticism](https://www.chron.com/culture/article/avelo-airlines-deportation-lawsuit-20331549.php) over its DHS operations as President Donald Trump [aims to carry out mass deportations](https://www.nbcnews.com/data-graphics/us-immigration-tracker-follow-arrests-detentions-border-crossings-rcna189148).
Deportations aside, domestic flights have garnered attention lately. As the [Wall Street Journal reported](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/ice-detention-migrants-vanish-38fd798f?reflink=desktopwebshare_permalink), domestic flights by ICE—including flights to detention centers and between facilities—are higher now than they ever had been since Cartwright started documenting them in 2020. In July, there were 727 domestic legs. In comparison, the Biden administration saw an average of 350 per month.

Meanwhile, other activists are putting pressure on Avelo. The Coalition to Stop Avelo calls the deportation flights "cruel" and "inhumane," and recently cheered when the carrier announced its plans to [close its base at Hollywood Burbank Airport](https://www.chron.com/culture/article/avelo-california-base-closes-20770582.php). Ultimately, their mission is to convince Avelo to cancel its DHS contract or put the airline out of business through boycotts.
