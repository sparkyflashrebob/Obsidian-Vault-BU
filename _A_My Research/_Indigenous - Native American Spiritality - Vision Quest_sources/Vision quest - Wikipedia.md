vision quest

rite of passage

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite\_of\_passage

Native American

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous\_peoples\_of\_the\_Americas

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous\_peoples\_of\_the\_Americas

. Individual Indigenous cultures have their own names for their rites of passage. "Vision quest" is an English-language

umbrella term

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella\_term

, and may not always be accurate or used by the cultures in question.

Among Native American cultures who have this type of rite, it usually consists of a series of

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremony

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North\_American\_Indigenous\_elder

and supported by the young person’s community.

The process includes a complete

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting

for four days and nights, alone at a

sacred site

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred\_space

in nature which is chosen by elders for this purpose.

Some communities have used the same sites for many generations. During this time, the young person prays and cries out to the spirits that they may have

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vision\_(spirituality)

, one that will help them find their

purpose in life

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purpose\_in\_life

role in a community

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social\_role

, and how they may best serve the People.

Dreams or visions may involve

natural symbolism

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious\_symbol

– such as animals or forces of nature – that require

interpretation

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream\_interpretation

After their passage into adulthood, and guided by this experience, the young person may then become an apprentice or student of an adult who has mastered this role.

When talking to

Yellow Wolf

Lucullus Virgil McWhorter

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucullus\_Virgil\_McWhorter

came to believe that the person fasts, and stays awake and concentrates on their quest until their mind becomes "comatose."

It was then that their

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyekin

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez\_Perce\_people

word) revealed itself.

Use by non-Native Americans

https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Vision\_quest&action=edit&section=1

Non-Native,

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_Age

and "wilderness training" schools offer what they call "vision quests" to the non-Native public.

However, despite the name, these experiences may bear little resemblance to the traditional ceremonies beyond fasting and isolation.

Such use of the term "vision quest" has been criticized as "

cultural appropriation

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural\_appropriation

", with those leading the exercises derided as "

plastic shamans

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic\_shaman

Such exercises may include New Age versions of a

sweat lodge

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweat\_lodge

, which has at times led to untrained people causing

harm and even death

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweat\_lodge#Risks

, such as in the

James Arthur Ray

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James\_Arthur\_Ray

manslaughter incident, which involved a 36-hour, non-Native idea of a vision quest, for which the participants paid almost $10,000.

Like a number of other Indigenous ceremonies, the vision quest has been mentioned in statements by Indigenous leaders concerned about the protection of ceremonies and other

Indigenous intellectual property

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous\_intellectual\_property

rights; one of these documents is the 1993 Declaration of War Against Exploiters of Lakota Spirituality.

In 2007 the

United Nations

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United\_Nations

adopted the

Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration\_on\_the\_Rights\_of\_Indigenous\_Peoples

(UNDRIP), which has given further support to Indigenous people's rights to protect their cultures and ceremonies, and address restitution when intellectual, religious and spiritual property is taken without their free, prior and informed consent or in violation of their laws, traditions and customs.
