### Outline
### Introduction to the RAIN Practice and Selecting a Personal Difficulty

- Participants are guided to sit in a comfortable, alert, and upright position before beginning the practice.

- A personal difficulty is to be brought to mind, specifically one that evokes emotional reactivity such as fear, anger, or hurt.

- Suitable difficulties may include relational conflicts, misunderstandings, addictive tendencies, or work-related feelings of failure, jealousy, or anxiety.

- A common denominator across these difficulties is identified as self-aversion, where one is critical or harsh toward oneself.

### The R and A of RAIN Recognizing and Allowing

- Recalling a recent situation in which one was triggered is encouraged as the entry point into the practice.

- Recognition, the first step of RAIN, involves acknowledging what is happening internally without judgment, such as noticing when one feels stuck.

- Whatever emotions or feelings are most prominent in one's experience are to be identified and named.

- Allowing, the second step represented by the letter A, involves pausing and making space for the experience rather than trying to fix or escape it.

- Simply acknowledging what is present and agreeing to experience it fully, even momentarily, is the essence of this step.

### Investigating and Nurturing Through the Full RAIN Practice

- Re-entering the triggering experience is used as a means to observe whether one's relationship with it has shifted.

- Having previously explored the full RAIN practice, including investigation and nurturing, one is invited to notice where they currently stand with the difficulty.

- Any inner call to deepen attention or offer kindness toward oneself is to be noticed and honored.

- Making an inward gesture of kindness, understanding, or compassion is introduced as a meaningful act within the practice.

- The distinction between being completely absorbed in a difficult experience and resting in open, caring presence is highlighted as central to the practice.

### Closing Reflection on Presence and Inner Clarity

- A poem by Wu-Man is offered as a closing reflection, evoking images of ten thousand flowers in spring, the moon in autumn, a cool breeze in summer, and snow in winter.

- The poem conveys that when the mind is not clouded by unnecessary things, any moment can become the best season of one's life.

- This closing reinforces the broader intention of the practice, which is to cultivate an unobstructed, present, and compassionate awareness.

### Next Steps

- Continued engagement with the RAIN practice is implied, building on the prior day's full exploration of all four steps.

- Deepening one's capacity to recognize, allow, investigate, and nurture difficult inner experiences remains the overarching direction.

- Cultivating a quality of inner kindness and presence as a habitual orientation, rather than a one-time exercise, is the broader aspiration emerging from the session.

### Detailed Subject Matter
**The RAIN Practice Overview**
- RAIN is presented as a practice of mindfulness and compassion applied to personal difficulties
- Participants are guided to bring a specific difficulty to mind — one that generates emotional reactivity such as fear, anger, or hurt
- The practice is applicable to relational conflicts, addictive tendencies, work-related anxieties, feelings of failure, or jealousy
**R — Recognize**
- The first step is to recognize what is happening in one's experience
- This involves acknowledging being stuck or triggered
- Recognition focuses on whatever is most predominant in the emotional or felt experience
**A — Allow**
- Allowing means letting the experience be as it is without trying to fix it or escape it
- It is described as a quality of pausing and making space
- The instruction is to simply acknowledge and give space to what is present
- Participants are invited to notice what it is like to agree to experience something as it is
**I — Investigate**
- Referenced as having been explored in a prior session
- Involves deepening attention into the experience that has been triggering
**N — Nurture**
- Involves bringing compassion and kindness to the inner experience
- Participants are invited to make an inward gesture of kindness, understanding, and compassion
- The nurturing quality is connected to a sense of presence and care
**Re-entering a Triggering Experience**
- Participants are guided to re-enter a recent situation in which they were triggered
- The purpose is to sense any shift in relationship to that experience after having worked with the full RAIN practice
**Presence and Openness**
- A distinction is drawn between being totally stuck inside an experience and being in a state of openness, presence, and care
- This openness is described as expressing the truth of who one is
### Spiritual and Societal Insights
**Mindfulness as a Path Through Difficulty**
- The RAIN practice offers a structured approach to sitting with difficulty rather than avoiding or suppressing it
- Emotional reactivity — fear, anger, hurt — is treated not as something to eliminate but as something to be recognized and held with awareness
**Self-Aversion as a Common Thread**
- Tara Brach identifies self-aversion as a common denominator across many personal difficulties
- Whether the difficulty involves relationships, work, or behavior, the underlying experience often involves being down on oneself
**Compassion Directed Inward**
- The practice emphasizes that compassion is not only directed outward but must also be cultivated inwardly
- Making a gesture of kindness toward oneself is presented as a meaningful and transformative act
**Presence as True Nature**
- The state of openness and care that arises through the RAIN practice is described as expressing the truth of who one is
- This points to a spiritual understanding that one's essential nature is characterized by presence and compassion rather than reactivity
**The Wisdom of Wu-Man's Poem**
- The closing poem points to the idea that when the mind is not clouded by unnecessary things, every season and moment becomes the best of one's life
- This reflects a non-dual spiritual perspective in which clarity of mind reveals the inherent beauty of ordinary experience
