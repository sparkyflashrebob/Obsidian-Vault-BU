# MMTCP Mod 03 Chest Opening Seated Movement Sequence -  J Foust - Oct, 15

### Chest Opening Seated Movement Sequence
📅 October 15, 2026
👥 Jonathan Foust (Main Teacher)
### Outline
### The Physical Effects of Prolonged Sitting and Forward Posture

- Chronic sitting, especially with back support, causes the head to drift forward and creates an imbalance between muscles that become overly lengthened and those that become chronically shortened.

- Referenced in the discussion is Lee Alpert's book "Yoga for Pain Relief," which argues that forward bend postures are largely unnecessary in modern life because daily activities such as using devices and sitting at desks already place the body in a constant forward bend.

- Most people consequently develop muscles that are too short in the front of the body and too long in the back, which can lead to pain, particularly in the neck, when beginning a meditation practice.

### Seated Posture Adjustments to Support Spinal Alignment

- Rolling up a towel or sweater and placing it at the lower back while seated tilts the pelvis forward and creates a significant improvement in spinal alignment compared to sitting without lumbar support.

- Hip position relative to knee height is an important factor, as hips sitting lower than the knees causes the body to compensate by leaning forward, compounding postural imbalance.

- Adjusting seat height or adding a cushion so that the hips are level with or higher than the knees helps to maintain a more neutral and supportive seated position.

### Principles for Leading Movement Sequences Safely

- Participants must always be reminded not to strain, and if strain is felt, they should back off immediately as it indicates the movement is being performed incorrectly.

- Sustaining a stretch for approximately one minute is recommended, and using a slow five-second inhale and five-second exhale breath cycle helps maintain the position across five or six breath rounds.

- Connecting breath to movement is described as critical, because disconnecting from the breath leads to disconnecting from the body and its intelligence, which is when injury is most likely to occur.

- This type of movement sequence is presented as a practical tool that can be offered as a segue into seated meditation or as a standalone few-minute practice for people who work at a desk.

### Single-Arm Overhead Chest Opening Variation

- One arm at a time is brought up so that the forearm rests on the top of the head with the palm facing upward, which contracts muscles that are typically overly lengthened in people who sit frequently.

- This variation may be too constricting for some participants, and they should be encouraged to explore modifications rather than forcing the position.

- Pressing down through the pelvis and drawing the navel in slightly are foundational actions that support the movement throughout all variations.

- Opening the elbow slightly out to the side is offered as a subtle refinement, while maintaining the instruction to avoid any forcing or strain.

- After holding for approximately one minute with slow deep breaths, the hand is gently lowered with awareness and placed on the lap, followed by a moment of internal observation to notice any difference between the two sides of the body.

- The second arm is then performed in the same manner, with an optional variation of lifting the chin slightly, which Jonathan Foust refers to as the "My God" posture as named by Lee Alpert.

- Working at approximately 70 to 75 percent of maximum effort is the target range, where engagement is felt without any accompanying strain.

### Interlaced Hands on Top of Head Variation

- Both arms are raised overhead with fingers interlaced and palms resting on the top of the head, while the breath continues at a slow five-count inhale and exhale.

- Exploring the opening of the elbows out to the side within this position allows participants to find their individual edge without strain.

- Drawing the shoulder blades slightly closer together is introduced as an additional refinement to deepen the contraction through the back of the body.

- After releasing the hands and allowing them to float down, attention is directed inward to notice any subtle shift, including a possible sense of openness through the belly and a gentle stretch along the midline.

### Interlaced Hands at the Back of the Head Variation

- This final variation involves reaching the arms overhead, interlacing the fingers, and bringing the hands to the back of the head with the thumbs resting at the occipital ridge at the base of the skull.

- Sensitivity is required when leading this variation as it will not be accessible to participants with restricted shoulder mobility, and any alternative modification should be encouraged.

- Elbows are opened out to the side while the breath deepens, and attention is directed toward relaxing and then lengthening through the back of the neck.

- A very gentle pressure is applied by pressing the back of the head into the hands, creating a subtle isometric engagement without any forcing.

- Upon releasing, the hands float down to the thighs, the breath is fully relaxed, and awareness is guided through the back of the neck, base of the skull, and back of the head to observe any shift in ease of breathing and internal alignment.

### Next Steps

- Participants are expected to practice leading the chest opening seated movement sequence, including its variations, as a tool they can offer in their own sessions.

- The sequence can be integrated either as a transition into seated meditation or as a standalone practice for individuals who spend extended time at a desk.

- Exploring personal postural adjustments such as lumbar support and seat height is encouraged as an ongoing practice to support spinal alignment during sitting.

### Detailed Subject Matter
**Modern Posture and Forward Bend Problem**
- Most people live in a constant forward bend due to device use, desk work, and leaning back in chairs
- When leaning back, the head comes forward, creating muscles that are chronically lengthened in the back and chronically shortened in the front
- Reference made to Lee Alpert, author of Yoga for Pain Relief, who suggests forward bend postures are largely unnecessary given how much we already live in that position
**Seated Posture Corrections**
- Rolling up a towel or sweater and placing it at the lower back tilts the pelvis forward and improves spinal alignment
- Hips should be level with or higher than the knees to prevent forward lean compensation
- A cushion can be used to raise the seat if the chair is too low
**Breath and Movement Connection**
- Slow five-second inhale and five-second exhale is recommended throughout the movement sequence
- Connecting breath to movement is described as critical to staying connected to the body's intelligence
- Disconnecting from the breath leads to disconnecting from the body, which increases risk of injury
- Sustaining a stretch for approximately one minute, using five to six slow breath cycles, is recommended
**Chest Opening Sequence — Variations**
- _Single Arm Variation:_ Forearm placed on top of the head with palm facing up; elbow opens out to the side; held for approximately one minute with slow breath; repeated on both sides
- _"My God" Posture:_ Chin lifted slightly while in the single arm variation; named by Lee Alpert
- _Interlaced Fingers on Top of Head:_ Both arms raised, fingers interlaced, palms resting on top of the head; elbows open out to the sides; shoulder blades drawn together
- _Hands at Back of Head:_ Arms raised overhead, fingers interlaced, hands brought to the back of the head with thumbs at the occipital ridge; gentle pressure applied from the back of the head into the hands; elbows open out to the sides; back of the neck relaxed and lengthened
**Leading Movement Sequences**
- Participants should always be instructed not to strain; if strain is felt, they are not doing it correctly and should back off
- Encourage variations for those with shoulder constriction or other limitations
- The sequence can serve as a segue into seated meditation or as a standalone desk-worker practice
- Takes only a few minutes and can help realign the spine
**Post-Sequence Awareness**
- After each variation, attention is directed inward to notice subtle shifts in alignment, breath ease, and sensation
- Observations may include: sternum lifting, shoulders moving slightly back, shoulder blades settling down, belly feeling more open, a subtle stretch through the midline

### Physical, Mental Challenges, Affects on Practice
**Physical Challenges**
- Many people beginning meditation develop neck pain due to postural imbalances from prolonged sitting
- The single-arm overhead variation may be too painful or constricted for some individuals due to shoulder tightness
- The hands-behind-head variation may not be accessible for those with significant shoulder constriction
- Hips lower than knees in a chair causes the body to compensate by leaning forward, compounding postural problems
- Chronic shortening of front-body muscles and lengthening of back-body muscles is identified as a widespread physical issue
**Guidance for Managing Challenges**
- Participants are instructed to back off immediately if strain is felt
- Props such as rolled towels, sweaters, or cushions are recommended to support correct alignment
- Variations are encouraged throughout so that all participants can engage safely regardless of physical limitation
