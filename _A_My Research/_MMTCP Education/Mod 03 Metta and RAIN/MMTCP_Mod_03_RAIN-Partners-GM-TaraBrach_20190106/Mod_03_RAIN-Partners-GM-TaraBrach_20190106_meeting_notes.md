# MMTCP Mod 03 RAIN-Partners-GM-TaraBrach 20190106 - Oct, 15

### RAIN Partners Guided Meditation with Tara Brach
📅 January 6, 2019
👥 Main Teacher: Tara Brach
### Outline
### Introduction and Preparation for the RAIN Practice

- Deciding who will serve as speaker number one and listener number one for the two sharing periods is the first step before beginning the session.

- Sitting comfortably with eyes closed, participants are guided to come into stillness and scan the body for obvious areas of tension to release.

- Attention is then directed to the senses, including breath, body sensations, or sounds, to quiet and settle the mind.

- A reflective inquiry is introduced by asking the question "What is between me and true well-being?" as a way to surface emotional material for the practice.

### The R of RAIN - Recognize

- Participants are invited to scan their lives for an area, such as relationships, work, or health, where they feel emotionally stuck and would benefit from insight, compassion, or healing.

- Getting in touch with the specific circumstances of the emotional tangle is encouraged before moving into the first step of the practice.

- Speaker number one opens their eyes and shares their situation aloud, naming the primary emotions that have become activated.

- The listening partner is asked to offer an accepting and open-hearted presence during the three-minute sharing.

- After speaker one finishes, speaker number two shares their own situation and names the primary emotions activated for them, while the other partner listens with the same open-hearted presence.

### The A of RAIN - Allow

- Following each sharing period, participants are guided to close their eyes and pause, holding together what has been named with a sense of spaciousness.

- Pausing and giving space to what is present is described as the beginning of allowing, which forms the foundation of the next steps.

- Full allowing is defined as the willingness to stay present with life exactly as it is, without resistance or judgment.

- Mentally whispering phrases such as "This too," "Yes," or "I can hold this" is offered as a tool to support the allowing presence.

### The I of RAIN - Investigate

- Investigating is described as an inquiry into the felt sense of experience rather than a cognitive or analytical process.

- Returning attention to the body and to the sensations of wherever one feels most vulnerable is emphasized as the core of this step.

- Gentleness and genuine curiosity are identified as the qualities most needed during investigation.

- Inquiry begins with questions such as "What is the worst part of this?" and "What most wants my attention?" to access deeper layers of experience.

- Exploring the most difficult or painful belief being held, such as "I am unlovable," "I am unworthy," or "I will always fail," is encouraged as a way to surface core emotional material.

- Noticing the emotions that arise in response to these beliefs, such as fear, anger, grief, or hurt, and locating where they are felt in the body, deepens the investigation.

- Describing the felt sense of these emotions as physical sensations, such as raw, hot, sore, aching, empty, or squeezed, is suggested to make the experience more tangible.

- Assuming the facial expression and body posture that best reflects the feelings is offered as a further way to access and notice what is present.

### The N of RAIN - Nurture

- Staying connected with the vulnerable experience inside, participants are guided to ask what the most hurting part of them would express if it could communicate.

- Inquiring how that vulnerable part wants to be met, and what it needs from oneself or from a larger source of love and wisdom, forms the bridge into nurturing.

- Adjusting posture and breathing consciously is suggested to help access the most awake and compassionate expression of oneself.

- This compassionate inner presence is described as one's future self, Buddha nature, or the most evolved expression of who one is.

- Calling on a wise and compassionate inner self, or alternatively a trusted friend, family member, pet, teacher, or spiritual figure, is offered as a way to engage the nurturing step.

- Offering inwardly the love, acceptance, forgiveness, compassion, or protection that the vulnerable part most needs is the central act of nurturing.

- Extending care through words, such as a message affirming one's basic goodness or communicating "You're here, you're not going away, I care," is one form this nurturing can take.

- Touch, such as placing a hand gently on the heart, and imagery, such as seeing one's inner child embraced or surrounded by light, are offered as additional ways to deliver care.

- Sensing a trusted other's warmth and love flowing through the hand into the heart, bathing the place of vulnerability, is described as a way to be both the holder and the held.

### After the RAIN - Resting in Presence

- The final phase of the practice involves ceasing all doing and resting in the presence and heart space that has emerged from the RAIN process.

- Relaxing back and allowing that presence to fill one's being, while becoming familiar with its quality, is the invitation of this closing phase.

- Any new or residual difficulty that arises is met with the same acknowledgement and care extended throughout the practice.

- Attending to the quality of one's presence and asking "What is the sense of my being, of who I am?" invites reflection on how one's inner state has shifted since the beginning of the meditation.

### Reflective Partner Sharing

- Before moving into partner sharing, participants are encouraged to stretch, write notes in a journal, and drink water.

- Speaker number one responds to two questions while the partner listens silently, those being what was challenging about the practice and what was experienced or learned about oneself that is worth remembering.

- Speaker number two then responds to the same two questions in a subsequent three-minute sharing period.

- After both partners have shared, any remaining reflections about the experience, including what it was like to listen to and share with one another, are invited in a final open exchange.

### Next Steps

- Participants are encouraged to carry forward any insights or self-knowledge gained during the practice as material for ongoing personal reflection.

- Expressing gratitude to one another in whatever way feels authentic is identified as the closing act of the session.

- Journaling key observations from the practice is recommended as a way to consolidate and remember what emerged.


### Key Points, Subjects and Themes
- The RAIN meditation framework as a structured contemplative practice
- Recognizing and naming activated emotions in relation to personal situations
- Allowing and pausing as foundational to emotional healing
- Investigating the felt sense of experience through body-centered inquiry
- Identifying core limiting beliefs and their emotional resonance
- Nurturing the vulnerable self through compassionate inner presence
- The "After the RAIN" phase as a period of restful, open awareness
- Partner-based sharing as a vehicle for mutual witnessing and presence
- Gratitude and reflective sharing as closing practices
### Detailed Subject Matter
- **The RAIN Acronym and Structure**
  - RAIN stands for Recognize, Allow, Investigate, and Nurture
  - The practice is structured as a guided meditation with partner sharing components
  - Participants are instructed to designate Speaker One and Listener One before beginning
- **R — Recognize**
  - Participants scan their lives and inquire: "What is between me and true well-being?"
  - Focus areas include relationships, work, health, or emotional stuckness
  - Speaker One shares their situation and names primary emotions that have become activated
  - The listener offers an accepting and open-hearted presence
  - Each speaker is given three minutes to share
- **A — Allow**
  - Allowing is described as the willingness to pause and stay present with life as it is
  - Participants are guided to mentally whisper phrases such as "This too," "Yes," or "I can hold this"
  - Pausing and giving space to what is present is identified as the beginning of allowing
  - Allowing sets the ground for the next step of investigation
- **I — Investigate**
  - Investigation is described as inquiring into the felt sense of experience rather than a cognitive or analytic process
  - Participants are guided to return attention to the body and to sensations of vulnerability
  - Guiding questions include:
  - "What is the worst part of this?"
  - "What most wants my attention?"
  - "What is the most difficult or painful thing I'm believing?"
  - Core limiting beliefs are explored, such as "I'm unlovable," "I'm unworthy," or "I'll always fail"
  - Participants are guided to notice emotions arising from beliefs: fear, anger, grief, hurt
  - Body sensations are explored using descriptors such as raw, hot, sore, aching, empty, squeezed
  - Participants are invited to assume the facial expression and body posture that best reflects their feelings
- **N — Nurture**
  - Participants are guided to connect with a compassionate inner presence
  - This presence is described as one's most wise and compassionate self, future self, or Buddha nature
  - Alternatively, participants may call in a trusted friend, family member, pet, teacher, or spiritual figure
  - Nurturing is offered through words, touch (such as placing a hand on the heart), or imagery (such as an inner child embraced or surrounded by light)
  - The vulnerable part is asked: "If it could communicate, what would it express?" and "How does this part want me to be with it?"
  - Care is extended inwardly through love, acceptance, forgiveness, compassion, or protection
- **After the RAIN**
  - Participants are guided to cease doing and rest in the presence and heart space that has emerged
  - Participants are asked to notice the quality of their presence and sense of being
  - Any residual difficulty is met with acknowledgment and care
  - Participants are encouraged to stretch, journal, and drink water before partner sharing
- **Reflective Partner Sharing**
  - Each speaker responds to two questions while the partner listens silently:
  - "What was challenging about this practice?"
  - "What did I experience or learn about myself that I want to remember?"
  - Each speaker is given three minutes
  - Final open sharing invites anything else about the experience that feels important, including what it was like to listen to and share with a partner
  - The session closes with an expression of gratitude between partners
### Spiritual and Societal Insights
- **The Nature of Allowing**
  - Tara Brach presents allowing not as passive resignation but as an active willingness to remain present with experience exactly as it is, without trying to change or fix it
  - This quality of presence is positioned as the essential ground from which deeper healing can arise
- **Investigating Through the Body**
  - The practice emphasizes somatic inquiry over intellectual analysis, directing attention to physical sensations as the most direct pathway to emotional truth
  - This reflects a broader contemplative understanding that the body holds emotional experience in ways the thinking mind cannot fully access
- **Core Beliefs and Suffering**
  - The practice surfaces the role of deeply held beliefs — such as unworthiness or unlovability — as central drivers of emotional suffering
  - By naming and feeling these beliefs rather than suppressing or analyzing them, participants are guided toward liberation from their grip
- **Compassionate Self as Inner Resource**
  - The concept of one's most wise and compassionate self, described also as Buddha nature or one's most evolved expression, is presented as an always-available inner resource
  - This framing bridges psychological self-compassion practices with spiritual traditions
- **Witnessing as a Healing Act**
  - The partner structure of the practice highlights the healing power of being truly heard and witnessed without judgment
  - The listener's role — offering open-hearted, silent presence — is treated as a meaningful spiritual and relational act in itself
- **Being the Holder and the Held**
  - Tara Brach uses the phrase "be the holder and the held," pointing to the non-dual nature of compassionate presence — one can simultaneously offer and receive care from within
- **Gratitude as Closing Practice**
  - The session closes with an invitation to express gratitude to one's partner in any way that resonates, framing gratitude as a natural expression of the heart space cultivated through the practice

### Physical, Mental Challenges, Affects on Practice
- Participants are guided to scan for and relax obvious areas of physical tension at the outset of the meditation
- Posture adjustments are encouraged during the Nurture phase to support comfort and openness
- Participants are invited to stretch, write in a journal, and drink water between the meditation and the reflective sharing, acknowledging the physical and mental demands of the practice
- The practice explicitly engages with emotionally difficult material including core beliefs of unworthiness, unlovability, and fear of failure, which may surface significant psychological vulnerability
- The listener role is framed as requiring sustained attentional and emotional presence, which carries its own mental demand
- Residual difficulty following the meditation is acknowledged and participants are guided to meet it with continued care rather than dismissal
