[[Journaling Practice - Interpersonal Conflict and Anger]]

Reflect on your learnings from the past two (2) modules on mindful communications and interpersonal conflict.
What are some communication challenges or conflicts you have experienced in your role as a teacher, whether you were involved or it was between students?
Can you now discern what went wrong?
How could it have gone better?
What could you have done to help steer the communication in a healthier way?
Please note this journal entry will serve as the foundation for your November 2022 Peer Group.

Journaling Practice - Ethics
Reflect on the MMTCP Code of Ethics.

What are the areas that are challenging to you?
How can you teach ethics to your students in a way that is uplifting and not judgmental?
How do your relationships with peers, Mentors, or teachers help you to be accountable to your values, ethics, and commitment to not cause harm as a teacher?

[[Journaling Practice - Teacher's Role]]

The role of a teacher can trigger self-doubt and inflation, as well as expectations and projections from your students.
Which of these seems most challenging for you as you step into the teacher role?
What might be helpful for you to navigate these challenging experiences?

[[Journaling Practice - The Art of Practice]]

How do effort and judgment manifest in your practice?
How do you work with judgments when they arise?

[[Journaling Practice - Self-Compassion and RAIN]]

What are 3 actions you could take to help sustain self-compassion in your life?
Which of these might you suggest to future students?
What, if anything, have you found challenging in your exploration of applied mindfulness and self-compassion?

[[Journaling Practice - Navigating Conflict in Group and Community]]
In this training and in your future groups as a teacher, what guidelines and agreements do you feel are essential for ensuring good communication and addressing conflict when it arises?

[[Journaling Practice - Mindfulness of Thoughts]]

What do you find challenging when working with mindfulness of thoughts?
What are the thoughts that most put you in trance?
What are the thoughts that keep you awake at night?
How might you advise a student struggling with challenging thoughts?

[[Journaling Practice - Mindfulness of the Body]]

Where in your body do you find it easiest to access sensations and to feel aliveness?
Where is it more difficult?
What helps you in awakening awareness inside your body?

[[Journaling Practice - Mindfulness of Emotions]]

How we relate to emotions is often shaped by our family and culture.
What was your family history with emotion?
What was allowed in your family?
What wasn’t allowed?
What range of emotions were okay for you?
What is outside your comfort zone?
Knowing this for yourself as a teacher will help you be more aware of your own norms as you relate to your students and help them uncover theirs.

[[Journaling Practice - Gratitude and Generosity]]
What helps you to cultivate generosity and gratitude? What blocks or interrupts the flow of generosity or gratitude for you? Consider both internal and external obstacles to the cultivation of these qualities.

[[Journaling Practice - Expanding from Internal to External/Personal to Collective Mindfulness]]

What communication guidelines would you need to establish with others to allow you to stay present with difficult topics?
What would allow you to be as open as possible in complex and potentially intense conversations around race, inclusion, and equity?
Notice what takes you out of your comfort zone. Where does it live in your body?

[[Journaling Practice - Forgiveness of Others]]

Imagine a situation where your communication has a negative impact on a person in a less privileged position than you.
How might your new awareness of privilege shape your response around asking for forgiveness in that
situation?
How might you intervene as a leader in your community when this situation is playing out between two
members?

[[Journaling Practice - Impermanence, Grief, and Loss, Part Two]]

Getting familiar with your own inner territory around grief and loss will help you be more present for your students, and to add stories from your own experience to your talks.
Where might you have an ungrieved loss?
How have you managed vulnerability and avoided grieving?
What might be asking for acceptance or inclusion?
What might help you internally and externally in your life to give more attention to this area?

[[Journaling Practice - Compassion for Others]]

What is your familial and cultural conditioning around compassion?
Thich Nhat Hanh says that “compassion is a verb”—it includes compassionate response. 
What helps you access and act on compassion in this troubled world without numbing out or being overwhelmed?

[[Journaling Practice - Vipassana Out Loud]]

Go to the Vipassana Out Loud discussion forum to find a partner with whom you can practice Vipassana Out Loud—or try the practice with your existing RAIN partner and then journal on your experience using the prompts below:
Have you found Vipassana Out Loud to be helpful in your personal practice?
How comfortable are you in guiding individuals in inquiry and Vipassana Out Loud?
What would help you develop this skill?
What have you learned about guiding inquiry from working with RAIN partners?

[[Journaling Practice - Trauma and Strong Fear]]

Reflect on something that brings up fear or anxiety for you.
How have you been relating to these feelings?
Which of your attitudes or responses have you found helpful?
Which have not been helpful?
If you live with historical racial or ethnic trauma, how do you navigate these feelings?

As teachers, we are still people—people who have had triggering experiences in this world. You may get triggered by your students! Knowing your own triggers will help you be less reactive to others when you are in the teacher's seat.

[[Journaling Practice - The Role of a Teacher]]

Bring to mind several teachers that you respect.
What are the key qualities that characterize their way of teaching and relating informally with students?
What steps might you take to develop these qualities in yourself?

[[Journaling Practice - Standing and Walking Meditation]]

What do you discover?

How is this different from practicing still or seated meditation?
