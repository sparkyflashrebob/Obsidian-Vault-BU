### Outline
### Mindfulness Through the Five Fields of the Body

- Settling presence and awareness downward into the feet and ankles is the opening step of the practice, allowing connection with the weight and pressure of the body.

- Attention flows gently upward from the feet and ankles through the lower legs, knees, and upper legs, with movement described as easy and spontaneous.

- From the legs, awareness moves into the hips and pelvis, engaging with this field as a space of life and vitality through breath and movement.

- Continuing upward, the upper body is brought into awareness, encompassing the internal organs, ribs, heart, lungs, shoulder blades, arms, wrists, and fingers, all moving with ease.

- Presence is finally extended through the whole field into the head and neck, with the eyes kept open in a soft gaze throughout the practice.

- Completion of this sequence involves settling awareness downward once more to reconnect with the earth.

### Standing Meditation Posture and Alignment

- Three key fields are held in awareness during standing meditation, namely the head, the heart, and the energetic center known as the hara, all connected with the earth.

- A plumb line flowing through the core of the body along the lines of gravity serves as the guiding image for alignment, with the form relaxed rather than rigid.

- Knees are kept soft and unlocked to maintain openness in the lower body, while the weight of the upper body flows downward through the hips and pelvis.

- Natural abdominal breathing is encouraged, with the breath allowed to deepen on its own as the body softens.

- A gentle lift runs along the length of the spine through the head and neck, balanced by a simultaneous settling downward, described as similar to a flower opening to the sun.

- Bringing the chin in slightly, without closing the throat, allows the back of the crown of the head to lift effortlessly while softening the back of the neck.

- Consciously softening the tongue, which rests naturally against the upper palate, is described as creating a ripple of ease through the entire field of the body.

- Eyes are held in a soft, open gaze, and even when closed, a sense of open peripheral vision is maintained to cultivate spaciousness.

- Hands may rest at the sides or be placed at the hara field below the navel, and the same alignment principles apply equally to both standing and sitting postures.

### Radiant Body Breathing - First Aspect

- Beginning from the gesture of standing meditation, the hands flow out to the sides with an in-breath, with palms turning upward at the shoulders.

- On the exhalation, the palms smooth downward along the front field of the body, settling the energy downward.

- Movement throughout this practice is kept slow, soft, smooth, and continuous, with any sense of hurry or urgency released.

- Allowing the whole body to breathe and staying with the flow of the breath are emphasized as the guiding qualities of this aspect.

### Radiant Body Breathing - Second Aspect

- Breathing upward along the energetic center channel, out through the head, and settling downward to the earth forms the basis of the second aspect of radiant body breathing.

- One hand is held in front of the other as the breath flows up from the center column along the front of the body.

- Relaxing the face, throat, and crown of the head allows the energy to settle downward like a fountain on the exhalation.

- Returning to standing meditation after completing this aspect, the hands may rest at the sides or settle at the hara.

### Qigong of Pulsing from the Core - Forward Wrapping Aspect

- The first aspect of the Qigong of pulsing from the core involves wrapping forward with the backs of the hands inclining toward one another along the center line.

- Bending the knees, settling the tailbone downward, lightly bringing the chin in, and lifting the crown of the head define the foundational gesture of this practice.

- Softness through the length of the arms, shoulders, elbows, and wrists is maintained, keeping these areas open and spacious throughout.

- On an in-breath, the body straightens and lengthens, the shoulders roll lightly back, and the palms settle downward with a little more presence in the heels.

- This pulsing and wrapping movement works along the whole length of the spine into the head, described as nourishing the brain and nervous system.

- Underdoing the range of motion while staying focused on the feeling of the pulse is specifically recommended to maintain the quality of ease.

### Qigong of Pulsing from the Core - Back Opening Aspect

- The balancing gesture of pulsing from the core begins by opening the front line of the body, with the shoulder blades drawing gently toward the spine.

- This opening encourages relaxation in the throat, heart field, solar plexus, abdominal field, and the base of the spine along the front of the body.

- From this open position, the shoulders lift gently, the body straightens, wraps forward with the backs of the hands inclining inward, and the knees bend as the palms draw back.

- A ripple travels up the length of the spine, over the top of the head, and settles down the front of the body, with the energetic flow described as moving up the back, over the crown, and down the front.

- This soft pulsing movement is described as gently releasing the spine and nervous system while massaging the exit points of the central nervous system along the length of the spine.

### Settling and Integration in Standing Meditation

- Returning to standing meditation after completing the pulsing practices, attention is directed toward connecting with what is present in the moment.

- Allowing whatever sensations are moving to continue moving, and noticing the wave-like, impermanent qualities of the sensational field, are offered as the orientation for this settling phase.

### Closing Practice - Settling in the Hara and Balancing in the Heart

- Hands flow out to the sides with palms up, then align facing inward toward the body and flow down the center line, coming to rest below the navel at the front gateway of the hara.

- Settling into the hara is described as resting in a reservoir of presence, with easy and natural breathing and a quality of kindness and inner smile.

- Completing the session, the fingertips are brought together and the feet are brought together in an ancient mudra of balance.

- This closing gesture is offered as a moment to connect with one's intention for well-being, for oneself and for all of life.

- An aspiration is expressed that all beings together may know and express loving kindness, compassion, joy, mindfulness, and equanimity.

### Next Steps

- Continued practice of the full standing movement sequence, including the five fields of the body, standing meditation alignment, both aspects of radiant body breathing, and both aspects of the Qigong of pulsing from the core, is implied as the ongoing path.

- Integrating the alignment principles of standing meditation into both standing and sitting postures in daily practice is encouraged.

- Carrying forward the qualities of ease, softness, and underdoing the range of motion into future sessions is highlighted as a key principle for deepening the practice.

### Physical, Mental Challenges, Affects on Practice
- Knees locking is identified as something to avoid; the instruction is to keep the knee space open
- Closing the throat is noted as something to avoid when bringing the chin in; the adjustment is made carefully to soften the back of the neck instead
- The instruction to underdo range of motion is given to prevent overexertion and to keep the practitioner in the felt sense of the pulse rather than in mechanical movement
- Hurry and urgency are named as qualities to release during the breathing practice
- The standing meditation posture is explicitly described as not rigid, addressing a potential tendency toward effortful or tense holding
- After the pulsing practices, residual energetic movement may continue to be felt in standing meditation; practitioners are instructed to allow it to be exactly as it is
