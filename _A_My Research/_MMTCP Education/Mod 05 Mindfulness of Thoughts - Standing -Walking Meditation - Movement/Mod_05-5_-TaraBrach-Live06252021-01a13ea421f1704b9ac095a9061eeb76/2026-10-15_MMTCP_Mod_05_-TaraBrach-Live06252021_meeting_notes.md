### Outline
### The Nature and Function of Limiting Beliefs

- Beliefs function as mental filters that prevent individuals from experiencing peace in the present moment, with stronger beliefs creating greater barriers to authentic experience.

- Confirmation bias drives people to seek out information that affirms existing beliefs while ignoring contradictory evidence, reinforcing siloed realities.

- The negativity bias explains why the survival brain disproportionately generates fear-based beliefs, as the brain operates as a predicting machine oriented toward anticipating what will go wrong.

- Biochemical reward chemicals are released when beliefs are confirmed, creating an addiction to certainty that makes people cling tightly to their beliefs even when those beliefs cause pain.

- Periods of rapid change and uncertainty intensify the tendency to grasp at beliefs, including painful ones, as a way of maintaining a sense of stability and control.

- Beliefs are not inherently true or false but are tools of the mind, representations of the world rather than the world itself, captured in the phrase "the map is not the territory."

- Quantum physicist Carlo Rovelli is cited to illustrate that mental maps bear the same relationship to reality as sailors' charts do to the fury of the ocean, emphasizing the fragility and limitations of conceptual frameworks.

- Negative self-beliefs originate in early wounds from interactions with caregivers and are further compounded by societal messaging that devalues individuals from non-dominant populations.

- Fear-based beliefs directly affect the degree to which individuals trust themselves, others, and the world, making intimate relationships difficult when those beliefs are dominant.

### The Looping Mechanism of Belief-Driven Suffering

- Suffering caused by beliefs operates through a self-reinforcing loop in which fear-based thoughts generate painful feelings in the body, which in turn trigger more fear-based beliefs.

- Emotions naturally arise and pass within approximately 1.5 minutes according to neuroscientists, but underlying beliefs prevent this natural resolution and cause emotions to solidify into sustained moods such as despair or depression.

- Recognizing and being able to describe this looping pattern to students is presented as a foundational and highly practical teaching tool.

### Using RAIN to Investigate and Disidentify from Limiting Beliefs

- RAIN, standing for Recognize, Allow, Investigate, and Nurture, is introduced as a structured mindfulness and compassion practice for working with limiting beliefs.

- Bringing beliefs above the line of conscious awareness is described as the essential first step, drawing on the image of a circle divided by a line where anything below represents what is outside of consciousness.

- Sri Nisargadatta's teaching that "illusion exists because it is not investigated" is offered as a foundational rationale for the investigative approach.

- When using RAIN with beginning students, the investigation phase should focus approximately 98% of the time on guiding the student to locate the belief as a felt sense in the body rather than engaging in cognitive analysis.

- Asking a student "what are you believing?" must be followed immediately by the question "how do you experience that in your body?" to prevent the session from devolving into intellectualization and to preserve the embodied quality that makes RAIN transformational.

- Beliefs must serve as a portal back into the body rather than an entry point into narrative, as embodiment is what enables genuine transformation through the practice.

- A personal example is shared in which physical discomfort triggers catastrophic thinking, and deeper investigation reveals a secondary belief of "I am bad for how I am relating to this," which is the belief that actually sustains the suffering.

- Bringing the hidden secondary belief into conscious awareness generates sadness, which then allows compassion to arise spontaneously, shifting identification from the fearful self to a field of tenderness.

- Once a student has moved through the RAIN process and is resting in compassionate presence, the inquiry "who would you be if you didn't believe this?" is offered as a powerful closing question to consolidate the shift.

- The Dalai Lama's willingness to meet with a delegation from the San Francisco gay community and ultimately change his publicly stated position on homosexuality is presented as a real-world example of bringing a belief above the line, feeling the suffering it causes, and allowing compassion to dissolve a previously held certainty.

### Guided Group RAIN Practice on Personal Limiting Beliefs

- Participants are invited to identify a limiting belief that is keeping them from living fully, which could range from core beliefs such as "I am unworthy" to more specific beliefs such as "I need to change in order to be loved."

- The guided practice moves sequentially through recognizing the belief and any accompanying emotions, allowing them to be present without resistance, and then investigating the core of the belief by inviting it forward fully.

- Participants are guided to let the part of themselves that most strongly holds the belief step forward and to sense through the body what happens when that belief is fully inhabited.

- Placing a hand on the heart, throat, or belly is offered as a way of accompanying the painful feeling with a physical gesture of presence and care.

- The inquiry "is it really true?" is introduced during the investigation phase as a gentle challenge rather than a dismissal, designed to create a small opening of uncertainty around the belief.

- Witnessing the impact of the belief on relationships, on one's relationship with oneself, and on one's relationship with the living earth is included as part of the investigation to deepen felt understanding.

- A message or prayer from the wise and caring place within is invited as the nurturing component, with participants encouraged to listen inwardly for what they most need to hear.

- Participants are guided to notice the shift from the small fearful self identified with the belief to the space of compassionate presence, and to ask who they are when they are not believing the belief.

### Working with Eduardo on the Belief of Being Fundamentally Flawed

- Eduardo's limiting belief centers on feeling fundamentally flawed, awkward, and unable to trust himself, with a pattern of losing jobs or clients serving as recurring evidence that reinforces the belief.

- A specific triggering incident is explored in which a brief email exchange with his boss leads Eduardo to immediately conclude he has said something stupid and that this may be the beginning of losing his position.

- Physical sensations of curled toes, hunched shoulders, and chest tension are identified as the somatic expression of the belief when it is activated.

- Tara guides Eduardo to give his body permission to fully take on the posture and feelings associated with the belief rather than resisting them, and to listen for what most wants attention within that contraction.

- A young inner part, estimated to be around six or seven years old, surfaces with the message "enough pain, stop beating me up, just believe in me and be proud of me."

- Hearing that this part is doing the best it can produces a softening and a feeling of compassion toward it, demonstrating the natural arising of the N of RAIN when investigation reaches the vulnerable core.

- Eduardo offers the inner part a commitment that he will not give up on it no matter what, which opens waves of warmth and compassion throughout his body.

- A fear arises that the transformation will not be complete, and Tara guides Eduardo to include that fear rather than suppress it, modeling the principle that all experience is allowed within the RAIN framework.

- The observation is made that Eduardo's warmth is immediately perceptible and that trusting and sinking into that warmth is the antidote to the story of being flawed, since the belief is sustained not by the story itself but by the withholding of that warmth from others and from himself.

### Working with Sarah on the Belief of Scarcity and Separation Around Money

- Sarah's limiting belief involves feeling that she cannot have enough money and must suffer to earn it, which she experiences as a conflict between abundance-oriented entrepreneurial thinking and Buddhist non-attachment.

- Through the session's earlier practice, Sarah identifies a more core belief underlying the money fear, namely that she is fundamentally separate from others and from the flow of resources in the universe rather than interconnected with it.

- Tara affirms the value of identifying the core belief beneath the surface belief and instructs Sarah to use the surface fear around money as a doorway to feeling the deeper belief of separateness in her body each time it arises.

- Somatic investigation reveals tension around the eyes, jaw, and belly, along with an inward shrinking that Sarah recognizes as a familiar and historically safe protective posture.

- The vulnerable place being protected is identified as the quirky, creative, and unknown parts of herself that want to shine but that she fears will lead to ostracism if fully seen, mirroring the very isolation she creates by withdrawing.

- Sarah is guided to close her eyes and send her aspiration directly to the place in her that most needs to hear it, with her stated aspiration being to let herself be fully seen and to trust in interconnection and unlimited resources flowing to support all beings.

- An inner critic voice arises during the aspiration, echoing parental messaging that she should know better by now, which Tara reframes as an opportunity to practice a different quality of inner parenting rather than a disruption to the process.

- The concept of spiritual reparenting is introduced to describe the practice of holding oneself from a large, wise, and compassionate perspective rather than from the perspective of the original critical conditioning.

### Working with Rick on Imposter Syndrome and the Belief of Not Being Qualified to Teach

- Rick's presenting belief is that he is an imposter as a teacher of mindfulness, rooted in his identity as a recovering addict who is still learning and acquiring skills.

- Hearing Eduardo and Sarah speak shifts Rick's awareness toward a broader pattern of wearing a mask and struggling with authenticity across multiple areas of life, not only in the context of teaching.

- Imposter syndrome is identified as an extremely widespread experience among mindfulness teachers, with Tara noting that even the Buddha faced the same final challenge of doubt about his own worthiness.

- Rick describes having had significant aversion to the investigation phase of RAIN, finding it frightening to dig deeply, and having initially resisted moving past the recognition and allowing stages.

- The shift toward more embodied investigation, informed by teachings from a recent Power of Awareness course and from a module taught by Jonathan, is described as having meaningfully advanced Rick's ability to work with the practice.

- A connection between Rick's yoga practice and his meditation practice is noted as beginning to emerge, and this integration is identified as a potential pathway for how he might teach in a way that feels authentic.

- Tara recommends that Rick begin each RAIN session with a brief body scan to ensure full embodied presence before entering the practice, as this will support his ability to move through investigation without avoidance.

- Setting a clear intention or prayer at the outset of practice, such as "may I stay present and may I be compassionate," is offered as a way of harnessing Rick's evident aspiration for healing to energize and sustain the investigative process.

### Closing Teachings on Beliefs and the Path of Remembering

- Rumi's poem "Wean Yourself" is read as a teaching on the progressive movement from the narrow certainty of conditioned beliefs toward an ever-wider encounter with reality, using the image of an embryo unable to conceive of the world outside the womb.

- Thomas Merton's statement that life is simple and that the divine is shining through an absolutely transparent world at all times is offered as a description of what becomes accessible when limiting beliefs are released.

- The entire arc of working with limiting beliefs is framed as a path of remembering, in which practices of recognition, investigation, and compassion restore access to a presence that has always been present but has been obscured by the looping of fear-based thought.

- Participants are guided to gaze at one another on screen and to hold a silent wish that each person may be free of any belief that boxes them in, free to let their heart love what it loves, and free to let their spirit flourish.

- A collective metta closes the session, extending the aspiration that the benefits of the practices and teachings may ripple outward to touch countless beings and that all beings everywhere may awaken and be free.

### Next Steps

- Practitioners are advised to deepen their trust in the warmth and compassionate presence they discover through the practice, as familiarity with that larger beingness is what progressively loosens the grip of limiting beliefs.

- Beginning each RAIN session with a body scan and a stated intention is identified as a foundational preparation step, particularly for practitioners who experience resistance during the investigation phase.

### Detailed Subject Matter
- **Beliefs as Filters on Reality**
  - Tara Brach described how beliefs — whether about astral planes, Buddha realms, or self-worth — act as filters that prevent direct experience of the present moment
  - The stronger the belief, the more it blocks authentic experience of who we are and what is here
  - People become addicted to certainty and hold tightly to beliefs even when those beliefs cause pain
- **The Brain as a Predicting Machine**
  - Neuroscientists describe the brain as a predicting machine that uses beliefs to navigate reality
  - The survival brain relies most heavily on predictions about what will go wrong, which is the root of the negativity bias
  - When beliefs are confirmed, the brain releases biochemical reward chemicals, reinforcing confirmation bias
- **Confirmation Bias and Negativity Bias**
  - Confirmation bias causes people to absorb information that affirms existing beliefs and ignore what challenges them
  - Negativity bias means beliefs are disproportionately oriented toward anticipated threats or failures
  - Both biases intensify during periods of uncertainty, impermanence, and rapid change
- **The Looping Cycle of Suffering**
  - Fear-based thoughts generate painful feelings in the body, which in turn trigger more fear-based beliefs
  - This looping is what transforms a passing emotion into a sustained mood of despair, depression, or hopelessness
  - Neuroscientists note that emotions naturally arise and pass in approximately 1.5 minutes; it is the underlying beliefs that cause them to persist
- **Beliefs as Maps, Not Truth**
  - Beliefs are tools of the mind to help navigate; they are representations of the world, not truth itself
  - The phrase "the map is not the territory" was offered as a key transmission for teachers to share with students
  - Quantum physicist Carlo Rovelli was cited as describing mental maps as a clumsy tool for navigating infinite mysteries
- **The RAIN Practice Applied to Limiting Beliefs**
  - R — Recognize: Identify the belief and any related feelings present
  - A — Allow: Let whatever is present be there without resistance; a radical act of acceptance
  - I — Investigate: Inquire into the core belief and, critically, find how it lives as a felt sense in the body
  - N — Nurture: Offer care, tenderness, and compassion to the vulnerable place discovered in investigation
  - Tara emphasized that investigation must return to the body to avoid purely cognitive processing
  - The question "What are you believing?" should always be followed by "How do you experience that in your body?"
- **Bringing Beliefs Above the Line of Awareness**
  - Tara used the image of a circle with a line through it: below the line is unconscious, above the line is conscious awareness
  - As attention deepens, the line moves and more comes into awareness
  - Sri Nisargadatta was cited: "Illusion exists because it is not investigated"
- **Core Beliefs Beneath Surface Beliefs**
  - Surface beliefs such as "I can never have enough money" often rest on deeper core beliefs such as "I am separate from others and from the source of abundance"
  - Teachers were encouraged to help students identify the core belief beneath the presenting belief
  - Once the core belief is identified, the student should feel it in the body rather than analyze it cognitively
- **Embodiment as Essential to RAIN**
  - "The issues are in the tissues" — healing requires somatic engagement, not just cognitive understanding
  - Students should be guided to feel where a belief lives in the body: throat, chest, belly, posture
  - Allowing the body to take the position it is driven into by the belief is part of the investigation
- **Spiritual Reparenting**
  - Sarah Berkeley introduced the term "spiritual reparenting" to describe the process of offering oneself the wise, compassionate perspective that may not have been received in childhood
  - Tara affirmed this framing, noting that everyone needs to see and experience themselves from a larger place
  - The inner critic voice that arose during Sarah's practice was acknowledged as an opportunity to consciously choose a different quality of inner parenting
- **Imposter Syndrome Among Teachers**
  - Rick Stoker raised the experience of feeling like an imposter in teaching, questioning who he is to teach the practice
  - Tara noted this is extremely common and described it as part of deep conditioning
  - She referenced the Buddha's final challenge before enlightenment as also involving doubt about worthiness
  - Teachers were encouraged to set an intention of compassion before beginning RAIN, and to use aspiration as an energizing force
- **Societal Sources of Negative Self-Beliefs**
  - Negative self-beliefs are formed through early interactions with caregivers and through societal conditioning
  - For people in non-dominant populations — Black, trans, Dalit, and others — the beliefs formed about self-worth through societal messaging are described as particularly violent and demeaning
  - What we believe affects trust, relationships, openness, and the capacity for intimacy
- **The Dalai Lama and Belief Revision**
  - The Dalai Lama initially stated that Buddhism does not approve of homosexuality
  - After meeting with a delegation from the San Francisco gay community, listening deeply, and allowing himself to feel the embodied suffering caused by his stated belief, he publicly changed his position
  - He stated: "I was speaking in accordance with traditional Buddhist teachings, but now I believe they are misguided"
  - This was offered as a model of bringing a belief above the line, feeling its impact, and allowing compassion to shift the position
- **Closing Guidance for Teachers**
  - Teachers are not expected to replicate the full complexity of the session with their own students
  - Staying with the basic RAIN structure — keeping students in the body, asking how they feel, encouraging them to keep the feeling company, and offering nurturing — is sufficient and powerful
  - Beliefs are undone through many rounds of practice, not a single session; students should not be discouraged when a belief resurfaces
### Spiritual and Societal Insights
- **The Universality of Fear-Based Beliefs**
  - Every person who is suffering is, at that moment, believing something that comes from fear and constricts their world
  - This is not a personal failing but a function of the survival brain and its evolutionary design
  - The more insecure a person is, the more tightly they cling to certainty, even when that certainty causes pain
- **Uncertainty as a Catalyst for Belief-Clinging**
  - Rapid societal change, existential threats to the planet, and personal impermanence all intensify the grip of fear-based beliefs
  - These are described as times that lead people to grasp even more tightly to painful beliefs
  - Awareness of this dynamic is itself a form of compassion for oneself and for students
- **The Transparency of the Divine**
  - Thomas Merton was cited: "Life is just simple. We are living in a world that is absolutely transparent and the divine is shining through it all the time."
  - The spiritual path is described as a path of remembering — remembering the vastness, mystery, love, and awareness that shines through everything
  - Limiting beliefs cause forgetting; RAIN practice supports remembering
- **Consciousness Is Larger Than Belief**
  - After moving through RAIN, the sense of being rests in a field of compassionate presence rather than in identification with the fearful self
  - From this larger awareness, beliefs can be seen as beliefs — they are delinked from identity
  - The more familiar a person becomes with this beingness, the more porous beliefs become
- **Societal Violence of Negative Self-Beliefs**
  - The beliefs formed about self-worth at a societal level are described as incredibly violent and demeaning for those in non-dominant populations
  - This was named explicitly in relation to Black people, trans people, and Dalits in India
  - Healing limiting beliefs is therefore not only a personal practice but a matter of justice
- **Collective Aspiration and Metta**
  - The session closed with a collective metta practice wishing that all beings might be free of beliefs that box them in, that their hearts might love what they love, and that their spirits might flourish
  - The aspiration was extended to justice and peace on earth and the awakening of all beings
  - Gazing at one another on screen was offered as a practice of sensing the light and goodness shining through each person
