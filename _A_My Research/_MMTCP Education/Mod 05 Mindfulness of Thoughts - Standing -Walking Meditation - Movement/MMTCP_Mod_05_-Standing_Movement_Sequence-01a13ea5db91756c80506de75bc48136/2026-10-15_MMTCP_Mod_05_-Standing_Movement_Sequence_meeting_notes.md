# MMTCP Mod 05 -Standing Movement Sequence - Oct, 15

### MMTCP Mod 05 – Standing Movement Sequence
📅 October 15, 2026
👥 **Main Teacher:** Jonathan Foust
Tags: Standing Meditation, Movement Sequence, Breathwork, Yoga, Mindfulness, Warrior Pose, Mountain Pose, Body Awareness, Somatic Practice, Mind-Body Connection
### Outline
### Breath Awareness as the Foundation of the Practice

- Paying close attention to the breath is identified as the primary focus throughout the entire standing movement sequence.

- Full, deep, and smooth breathing is encouraged, with particular emphasis on matching the length of the inhalation to the length of the exhalation.

- A slow count of five for both the inhalation and the exhalation is recommended as the target breathing rhythm.

- Naturally forgetting the breath is acknowledged as expected, though returning attention to it as the center of experience is emphasized for maximizing the benefit of the postures.

### Safety Guidelines and Personal Boundaries

- The sequence is described as designed to be both very safe and profoundly beneficial when practiced with care.

- Never extending beyond 75% of one's physical capacity is presented as one of the most important principles for ensuring safety throughout the practice.

- Any movement that does not feel appropriate for the individual body should be ignored in favor of what feels right personally.

### Warm-Up Swaying Movement

- Beginning with the feet slightly wider than hip width apart, the arms are allowed to hang relaxed by the sides like empty coat sleeves.

- The torso is guided to sway gently from side to side without any forcing, allowing the arms to move freely with the momentum.

- Lifting the opposite heel off the ground is suggested as a way to deepen and expand the range of the swinging movement.

- Deepening the breath during the sway is encouraged, including exhaling gently to each side as the movement flows.

### Standing Meditation Alignment and Body Awareness

- Feet are positioned approximately six to eight inches apart, with the soles imagined as relaxed and wide against the earth.

- Hyperextending or locking the knees back is identified as a common standing habit to be avoided, with a soft bend and gentle lengthening of the legs recommended instead.

- Tightening the buttocks is used as a technique to draw the tailbone under, after which the clench is released while maintaining the downward extension of the tailbone.

- Softening the belly is introduced as a further point of relaxation within the standing alignment.

- Relaxing the back of the neck and the base of the skull is guided, with a sense of lengthening imagined in that area.

- Hands are felt as floating in space, and the overall quality of the standing meditation is described as both rested and alert.

### Centering Breath Practice

- A structured centering breath is introduced, guiding slow inhalations and exhalations each to a count of five.

- Smoothing out the breath as much as possible is emphasized, with attention drawn to the effect that a smooth, deep breath has on the quality of the mind.

### Flowing Arm Movement Coordinated with Breath

- Palms are turned to face outward to either side or forward depending on the available space, preparing for the arm sweep.

- On the inhalation, the arms are swept up overhead with fingertips and palms coming together, then drawn down through the midline of the body on the exhalation.

- Drawing the breath up under the collarbones to fill the upper lobes of the lungs is cued as part of the inhalation during the arm movement.

- Lifting the chin slightly as if looking up into the light is offered as an optional enhancement to the upward movement.

- Intimate connection between breath and movement is emphasized, with the exhalation used as an opportunity to notice internal sensations.

### Mountain Pose with Active Engagement

- Long, slow, deep breathing with matched inhalation and exhalation lengths is re-established as the foundation entering Mountain Pose.

- Tightening the buttocks to draw the tailbone under and drawing the navel in toward the spine are cued as active engagements of the lower body.

- Palms are turned outward and the shoulder blades are actively drawn close together, with the shoulders drawn down slightly.

- Breathing the heart forward is introduced as a key image for the posture, encouraging an opening through the front of the chest.

- Lifting the chin slightly is offered as an optional addition to deepen the sense of full contraction through the back of the body.

- Exploring how much more active and engaged the posture can become is encouraged through one final full, slow, deep breath before releasing.

### Warrior Pose on Both Sides

- Sufficient space to step forward is noted as a practical requirement before entering the Warrior Pose.

- Selecting a focal point approximately six to twelve feet ahead is recommended to support balance and concentration during the posture.

- Stepping forward with the right foot and turning the left foot out approximately 30 degrees establishes the stance for the first side.

- Ensuring the knee does not extend beyond the ankle is emphasized as an important safety alignment point.

- Hands placed on the hips are used to align the hip bones straight forward, described as pointing headlights directly ahead.

- Drawing the shoulder blades together, lowering the shoulders slightly, and breathing the heart forward are carried into the Warrior Pose from the Mountain Pose principles.

- Lifting the chin slightly and exploring what might open or lengthen on the inhalation and what new sensations arise on the exhalation are encouraged as ways to deepen the experience.

- Pressing down into the forward thigh with both hands is used as a technique to assist stepping forward out of the pose safely.

- The same sequence of alignment, breath focus, shoulder blade engagement, and heart-forward breathing is repeated on the second side with the left foot stepping forward and the right foot turned out.

### Return to Flowing Arm Movement and Closing Standing Meditation

- Following the Warrior Pose sequence, the flowing arm movement coordinated with breath is revisited as a transitional practice.

- Arms are drawn up overhead on the inhalation with fingertips and palms together, then released down the midline on the exhalation, repeated with attention to drawing breath up under the collarbones.

- Drawing awareness to the sense of space both inside and outside the body during the breath and movement is encouraged.

- Returning to standing meditation, the feet are placed directly beneath the hips approximately six to eight inches apart, with the eyes closed or softly gazing.

- The full sequence of body alignment points is revisited, including relaxing the feet wide against the earth, softening the backs of the knees, drawing the tailbone under, softening the belly, and relaxing the back of the neck.

- The closing standing meditation is framed as an opportunity to feel the imprint and effect of the breath and movement practice, and to experience the natural transition into stillness.

### Next Steps

- Continued practice of the standing movement sequence is implied, with breath awareness, safety boundaries, and personal body attunement established as the guiding principles to carry forward.

- Integration of the Mountain Pose and Warrior Pose alignment principles into ongoing practice is encouraged as a means of deepening engagement and benefit over time.

### Detailed Subject Matter
**Breath Principles**
- Breath should be full, deep, and smooth throughout the sequence
- Inhalation and exhalation should be matched in length
- Preferred rhythm is a slow count of five on the inhale and five on the exhale
- The breath is described as the foundation and center of the entire practice
- Breath is intimately connected to movement throughout the sequence
**Safety Guidelines**
- The sequence is designed to be safe and profound
- Practitioners should never extend beyond 75% of their capacity
- If any posture does not feel right for the body, participants are encouraged to ignore the instruction and do what feels right for them
**Warm-Up Movement**
- Feet placed wider than hip-width apart
- Arms relaxed at the sides, swaying like empty coat sleeves
- Lifting the opposite heel allows for a fuller swinging movement
- Breath may deepen and exhale can be released to each side during the sway
**Standing Meditation**
- Feet approximately six to eight inches apart, hip-width
- Eyes closed or with a soft gaze
- Soles of the feet imagined as relaxed and wide against the earth
- Backs of the knees kept soft rather than hyperextended or locked
- Buttocks gently tightened to draw the tailbone under, then released while maintaining the tailbone's downward extension
- Belly softened and relaxed
- Back of the neck and base of the skull lengthened and relaxed
- Hands felt as floating in space
- Quality of presence described as rested yet alert
**Centering Breath**
- Slow inhalation to the count of five
- Slow exhalation to the count of five
- Attention directed to smoothing out the breath and observing its effect on the mind
**Flowing Arm Movement**
- Palms turned out to the sides or facing forward
- On inhalation, arms sweep up overhead
- Fingertips and palms come together at the top
- Hands trace down the midline of the body on exhalation
- Chin may be lifted as if looking up into the light
- Breath drawn up under the collarbones to fill the upper lobes of the lungs
**Mountain Pose**
- Long, slow, deep breath established
- Buttocks tightened, tailbone drawn under
- Navel drawn in toward the spine
- Palms turned out to the sides
- Shoulder blades actively drawn close together
- Shoulders drawn down slightly
- Heart breathed forward
- Chin lifted slightly
- Full contraction felt through the back of the body
- Exploration of how much more active and engaged the posture can become
**Warrior Pose**
- Sufficient space required to step forward
- Eyes open recommended, with a focal point six to twelve feet ahead
- Right foot steps forward, left foot turned out approximately 30 degrees
- Knee must not extend beyond the ankle
- Hands placed on hips, hip bones aligned straight forward like headlights
- Shoulder blades drawn together, shoulders down slightly
- Heart breathed forward, chin lifted slightly if desired
- Arms may remain on hips or open out to the sides
- Inhalation used to explore what might open or lengthen
- Exhalation used to relax and scan for new sensations
- Release by pressing both hands down into the forward thigh before stepping forward
- Sequence repeated on the left side with the same alignment and breath cues
**Return to Standing Meditation**
- Flowing arm movements repeated as a transition back to stillness
- Feet returned to hip-width, six to eight inches apart
- Eyes closed or soft gaze
- Breath fully released and relaxed
- Feet imagined as relaxed and wide against the earth
- Backs of knees softened
- Tailbone drawn under then released downward
- Belly softened
- Back of neck and base of skull relaxed
- Hands felt floating in space
- Attention directed to the imprint and effect of breath and movement as the body transitions to stillness

### Physical, Mental Challenges, Affects on Practice
**Physical Considerations**
- Hyperextension or locking of the knees is identified as a common standing habit that should be avoided; a slight knee bend is recommended to keep the backs of the knees soft
- The knee must not extend beyond the ankle in Warrior Pose to protect the joint
- Practitioners are advised not to exceed 75% of their physical capacity to prevent strain or injury
- Participants are given explicit permission to modify or skip any posture that does not feel right for their body
**Mental and Attentional Challenges**
- It is acknowledged that practitioners will naturally forget the breath during the sequence, normalizing lapses in attention and encouraging a return to breath awareness without judgment
**Effects on Practice**
- Smoothing out the breath is noted to have a direct observable effect on the quality of the mind
- The sequence of active postures followed by standing stillness is designed to allow practitioners to feel the imprint and effect of breath and movement as the body and mind settle
