**Summary:**

Jack Kornfield continues a session on mindfulness, focusing on the **mindfulness of emotions and feelings**, the second foundation. He emphasizes that understanding and skillfully working with feelings is crucial for both personal well-being and for guiding others. Kornfield explains that primary feelings (pleasant, unpleasant, neutral) give rise to a vast stream of secondary emotions and feelings, many of which operate unconsciously, profoundly influencing our decisions and experiences.

The core of the practice involves **witnessing these feelings with loving awareness, without judgment or immediate reactivity**. This includes normalizing all feelings as part of the human experience, identifying them, sensing them in the body, and holding them with compassion. He highlights common "hindrances" like desire, aversion, restlessness, sleepiness, and doubt, suggesting ways to meet them mindfully.

Kornfield uses numerous stories and examples (e.g., Achaan Chah on rage, the Marshmallow Experiment, Frank Ostaseski with a grieving family, his own experience of fear) to illustrate how this practice allows us to expand our "window of tolerance." The goal is not to eliminate difficult feelings but to develop the courage and trust to be present with the full spectrum of human emotion. This presence, he suggests, allows for a deeper understanding and a wiser, more compassionate response to life, ultimately leading to a form of liberation where feelings are seen as impermanent, like weather, rather than fixed realities. He also touches on the idea that mindfulness of feelings ultimately informs wise action in the world.

**Detailed Breakdown:**

**I. Introduction: The Importance of Mindfulness of Feelings & Emotions**
    *   **Continuing the Session:** This session focuses on mindfulness of emotions and feelings, essential for working with students and for personal life.
    *   **Foundation for Further Learning:** This understanding is necessary for approaching difficulties, the RAIN process, and topics like diversity and inclusion.
    *   **Skill in Dealing with Feelings:** Requires understanding and skill.

**II. The Second Foundation: Mindfulness of Feelings (Vedanā)**
    *   **Follows Mindfulness of Body:** The traditional sequence of the Four Foundations of Mindfulness.
    *   **Primary Feelings:**
        *   Awareness of pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral feelings arising.
        *   This is about *witnessing* and *observing* without immediate judgment or reactivity (e.g., "This is pleasant, this is unpleasant, this is neutral").
        *   This is distinct from being "hooked" by them.
    *   **Secondary Feelings/Emotions:** These primary feelings give rise to a vast stream of more complex emotions and feelings.

**III. The Nature of Emotions and How We Experience Them**
    *   **A River of Experience:** Feelings are a constant, flowing stream within us.
    *   **Sensing Feelings:**
        *   **Mind:** Some people primarily experience feelings as thoughts or mental states.
        *   **Heart:** Others feel them more in the chest, as love, longing, anger, etc.
        *   **Body:** Many experience feelings as physical sensations (e.g., anxiety as a knot in the stomach).
    *   **Unconscious Feelings:** Many of our feelings operate unconsciously, yet drive our behavior.
        *   **Justice William O. Douglas:** "90% of our decisions [at the Supreme Court] are made on the basis of what we feel about things... the other 10% is our rationalization."
    *   **Normalizing Feelings:** It's crucial to reassure people (and ourselves) that whatever they are feeling is human and normal.

**IV. Working with Feelings in Practice and Teaching**
    *   **Identifying and Locating:**
        *   "Where do you feel that in your body?"
        *   "What images or stories come with this feeling?"
    *   **Holding with Compassion:** Can you hold this feeling with compassion?
    *   **The Hindrances (as examples of common feeling-states):**
        *   **Desire/Grasping:** The "pickpocket meets a saint" story – seeing only what desire dictates. We scan for what we want/don't want.
        *   **Aversion/Resistance:** "I don't like this, I'm afraid of it." Pushing away experience.
        *   **Restlessness:** Mindfully noticing "restless, restless."
        *   **Sleepiness/Dullness:** Normalizing it as tiredness or a natural state.
        *   **Doubt:** "I can't do this," "It's the wrong day/practice," "I'm not worthy."
    *   **Difficult Emotions:**
        *   Fear, shame, sorrow, longing, guilt, anxiety, addiction, jealousy, being hurt, anger, rage.
        *   **Achaan Chah on Rage:** "Oh rage, huh? Interesting... Go back into your little hut... close the windows... put on all your robes. If you're going to be angry, do it right. Sit in the middle of it for as long as it lasts and see what happens to it." (Emphasizes direct, non-judgmental experience).
    *   **Respect for Feelings:**
        *   Feelings are powerful and demand respect.
        *   Story of US soldiers in Iraq taking a knee before an angry crowd, a gesture of respect that de-escalated the situation.
    *   **Trust and Confidence:** The space of awareness and compassion is big enough to hold all feelings.
    *   **Expanding the Window of Tolerance:** Mindfulness helps us expand our capacity to be with a wider range of feelings without being overwhelmed.

**V. Stories and Illustrations of Working with Feelings:**
    *   **Tom (Man on Retreat):** Hadn't felt much, but when guided to his body, realized anxiety. Wrote a letter to himself revealing deep insecurities, fears about his wife's Parkinson's, and unresolved childhood issues. This opened him to compassion.
    *   **Frank Ostaseski and Grieving Parents (Jamie):**
        *   Parents were afraid to touch their deceased 7-year-old son due to fear of their own grief.
        *   Frank modeled gentle presence by kissing Jamie's forehead.
        *   He guided them through a ritual of washing their son's body with herbs.
        *   This allowed their grief to flow, held in a space of love. The grief became a "fierce fire of their own love."
    *   **Fado (Portuguese Song):** Jane Hirshfield's poem describes how Fado balances joy and sorrow, like putting "every life in the room on one pan of a scale and itself in the other, and the copper bowls balance."
    *   **Marshmallow Experiment:** Illustrates the "torture" of desire and the effort of impulse control in young children. A picture of meditation in some ways.

**VI. The Role of Feelings in Wise Living and Action**
    *   **Mary Oliver Poem:** "Meditation is old and honorable... But can one be passionate about the just, the sublime, the holy, and yet commit no labor to its cause? I don't think so... Be ignited or gone." (Mindfulness should lead to wise action, not just passive observation).
    *   **Molly Ivins Quote:** "So keep fighting for freedom and justice, beloveds, but don't forget to have fun doing it... Be sure to tell those who follow how much fun it was." (Embracing joy even amidst struggle).
    *   **Voltaire Quote:** "The art of medicine primarily consists of amusing the patient while nature does the healing." (Trusting the inherent capacity for healing and self-regulation within the body and mind).

**VII. The Impermanent Nature of Feelings and Liberation**
    *   **No Feeling is Final:** Feelings, like weather, arise and pass. They are not solid, fixed entities.
    *   **The Path to Awakening:** This understanding and practice of meeting feelings with mindful, loving awareness is a path to liberation.
    *   **"This too can be held":** The ultimate message is that the space of awareness is vast enough for all experiences.
