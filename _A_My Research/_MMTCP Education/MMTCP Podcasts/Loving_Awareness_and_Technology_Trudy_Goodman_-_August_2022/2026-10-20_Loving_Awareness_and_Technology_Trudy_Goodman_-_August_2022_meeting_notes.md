### Outline
### Introduction to the Topic of Loving Awareness and Technology

- Trudy Goodman-Cornfield introduces the subject of maintaining loving awareness in the age of technology as something she has been examining passionately.

- Increased time spent reading news across multiple platforms following the 2016 presidential election is identified as the origin of her inquiry into this topic.

- Observations of two teenage grandchildren and their evolving relationship with social media further deepened her concern about technology's impact.

- The talk is framed around the question of what role mindfulness can play in addressing the effects of technology on human attention and behavior.

### The Blind Men and the Elephant as a Framework for Wholeness

- An ancient story appearing across spiritual traditions is introduced to illustrate how individuals perceive only partial truths from their unique perspectives.

- Each person in the story touches a different part of the elephant and draws a different conclusion, leading to conflict over who holds the correct understanding.

- A wise woman resolves the conflict by explaining that each person is right about their portion of the truth, but that seeing the whole requires collective wisdom.

- The moral drawn is that human beings need each other to perceive the full picture, and that listening and learning from one another is essential to understanding.

- This framework is connected to the purpose of mindfulness, which is described as the capacity to perceive the whole inner and outer context of human experience.

### Mindfulness as the Foundation of Conscious Attention

- Jon Kabat-Zinn's definition of mindfulness is cited as paying attention on purpose, in the present moment, and non-judgmentally.

- Mindfulness is described as a way of perceiving thoughts and feelings without judgment and with calm acceptance of the full spectrum of human experience.

- Unfiltered awareness is defined as awareness not filtered through preferences of liking, disliking, or choosing to ignore.

- Concentration is identified as the necessary partner to mindfulness, enabling practitioners to remain present long enough to understand the nature of experience.

- The concept of loving awareness, coined by Ram Dass, is introduced as a guiding principle that Trudy and Jack Kornfield have adopted in their practice and teaching.

### How Technology Undermines Focused Attention and Conscious Choice

- A central concern is raised about whether technology is actively undermining the human capacity for focused attention and concentration.

- Focused attention is described as the foundation of human freedom, specifically the ability to consciously choose where and how attention is directed.

- The possibility that this capacity is being eroded in ways beyond conscious control and outside of awareness is presented as a serious problem.

- Werner Heisenberg's principle is quoted to illustrate that what is observed depends on the method of questioning, reinforcing that awareness shapes perception.

- Whatever falls outside of awareness is described as outside of conscious control, making the erosion of attention a matter of significant concern for practitioners.

### Filter Bubbles and the Restriction of Perspective

- Filter bubbles are defined as the restriction of a user's perspective created by personalized search technologies and algorithmic content curation.

- A Wikipedia definition is cited describing filter bubbles as a state of intellectual isolation resulting from websites algorithmically guessing what a user wants to see based on location, click behavior, and search history.

- Personalized results mean that different individuals searching the same term will receive different information, narrowing each person's view of reality.

- The concern is that this narrowing happens without the user's conscious choice or consent, making it a form of manipulation that operates outside of awareness.

- This dynamic is connected back to the elephant parable, as filter bubbles prevent individuals from seeing the whole picture and reinforce partial truths.

### The Addictive Design of Technology and Its Commercial Motivations

- Algorithms are described as engineered to capture attention by appealing to lower brain functions, including fear, desire, hope, and sensationalism.

- The more attention a platform captures, the more advertising revenue it generates, meaning users are the product rather than the customer.

- Neuroscientists and engineers are described as working together to make technology more actively addictive, a practice that Tristan Harris, a former Google engineer, has publicly exposed.

- Harris is credited with sounding the alarm about the deliberate design of addictive technology and dedicating his career to raising public awareness after leaving Google.

- Powerful voices including Mark Benioff, CEO of Salesforce, are noted as calling for regulation of platforms like Facebook on the grounds that they are addictive and harmful.

- Early investors in Facebook are described as feeling a sense of responsibility to address the dangers of what they helped create.

### The Impact of Technology on Children, Relationships, and Society

- Technology's influence on children and teenagers is identified as a growing area of concern, particularly regarding social media use and imitative behavior.

- The broader societal impact includes political consequences, as algorithmically curated news and social media shape public opinion in ways that are not transparent.

- Spending more and more time in a reality mediated by algorithms means spending less time in face-to-face human connection.

- The cumulative effect on relationships, creative work, and engagement with the natural environment is presented as a reason for mindfulness teachers to take this issue seriously.

- Technology's impact is compared to climate change in that it is already reshaping lives regardless of whether individuals are fully aware of it.

### The Potential of Technology Used with Compassion and Wisdom

- Technology is acknowledged as enabling remarkable possibilities, including global connection, learning, and the ability to conduct the session from Hawaii.

- A group that Trudy and Jack Kornfield are part of is described as actively exploring how to bring mindfulness and compassion into the design of devices and virtual assistants.

- Questions are raised about whether compassionate AI interfaces, such as a more empathetic Siri or Alexa, could help people feel more compassionate toward themselves and others.

- Virtual reality is highlighted as having significant potential to increase empathy, with an example of an immersive installation at LACMA that places viewers in the experience of crossing the border.

- Video game design is cited as an area where researchers are working to cultivate skills like connection and compassion rather than aggression.

- The historical arc of technology, from telegraph to telephone to television, is used to frame current developments as part of a long pattern of tools that both unite and challenge humanity.

### Loving Awareness Meditation Practice

- A brief guided meditation is offered as a way to embody the principles discussed throughout the talk.

- Participants are invited to lower their gaze or close their eyes as a signal to the body that meditation is beginning.

- The Loving Awareness Meditation taught by Ram Dass is introduced, beginning with the silent repetition of the phrase "I am loving awareness."

- Over time the "I am" is dropped, leaving only the words "loving awareness" paired with the exhalation.

- When the mind becomes distracted, practitioners are guided to shift the body slightly back into a posture of receptivity rather than tipping forward in search of the next moment.

- The practice is connected to the concept of the backward step described by 13th-century Zen Master Dogen Zenji, meaning turning attention inward rather than outward.

- Each breath, sound, sensation, thought, and feeling is described as a manifestation of the whole elephant, the vast dimension of universal life that all beings share.

### Benefits of Technology and What Mindful Use Looks Like

- The benefits of technology are described as numerous and captivating, including global connectivity, learning, and the ability to gather across distances.

- Mindful use of technology is described as educating oneself about the ways attention may be manipulated and supporting efforts to make technology more humane and compassionate.

- Being an early adopter of email is shared as evidence that the concerns raised come from a place of genuine love for technology rather than opposition to it.

- The distinction is drawn between being aware of technology's risks and being against technology itself, with the former framed as an act of care.

### Loving Awareness as a Deeper State of Consciousness

- Loving awareness is described as a deeper level of consciousness than the discursive thinking mind that constantly and automatically judges every experience.

- The brain is acknowledged as a judgment-making machine, and discernment is distinguished from automatic judgment as a wise and necessary function.

- Clear comprehension and conscious discernment are identified as essential foundations of mindfulness practice, whereas automatic liking and disliking are described as filtering and limiting awareness.

- Ignorance is defined as the fog created by habitual reactions of liking, disliking, or spacing out, which prevents clear seeing.

- Loving awareness is described as more fulfilling and enriching than ordinary discursive consciousness, and as a state in which genuine learning becomes possible.

### Practical Strategies for Disengaging from Mindless Technology Use

- Awareness of the moment one picks up a device habitually is identified as the first and most important step in breaking the cycle of mindless use.

- Making an agreement with a partner or buddy to limit technology use is described as a highly effective strategy, particularly because powerful forces are designed to work against such changes.

- Gradually reducing time spent on news and social media rather than making abrupt large changes is recommended as a more sustainable approach.

- Switching all devices to yellow light after sunset is suggested as a practical measure to prevent blue light from disrupting the body's natural sleep signals.

- Avoiding social media personally and delegating posting to an assistant is shared as a strategy that acknowledges the strength of the pull toward engagement.

- The humanetech.com website, specifically its "take control" section, is recommended as a resource offering a wealth of practices for individuals and teachers.

### Challenges in Teaching These Ideas to Students

- Naming the emotional difficulty of hearing unwelcome information about things one loves is identified as an important first step when introducing these ideas to students.

- Sharing one's own struggles and speaking from a place of shared human experience rather than from a position of perfection is described as reducing resistance in listeners.

- Transparency about why a teacher cares about a topic makes the message more relatable and opens people to receiving it more fully.

- Acknowledging that change is hard and that powerful forces are working against it creates a compassionate container for students to engage with the material honestly.

### Fear of Missing Out as a Mindfulness Challenge

- Fear of missing out is identified as a real and powerful psychological force, particularly for teenagers and young people for whom social belonging is especially important.

- Having friends or a small group commit to unplugging together is recommended as the most effective approach for younger individuals who cannot easily face this fear alone.

- Sitting with the fear for a short period before acting on it, and gradually extending that time, is offered as a practice for working with FOMO directly.

- Unpacking the fear layer by layer to identify its deeper roots, such as fear of rejection or marginalization, is described as a way to understand and soften its grip.

- FOMO is described as a deliberately engineered response, built into technology to keep users engaged longer, making it important to recognize it as such rather than as a personal failing.

- All the tools of mindfulness practice, including RAIN and compassionate attention to difficult emotions, are described as applicable to working with FOMO as with any other difficult emotion.

- Personal experience of feeling contraction and fear around low social media participation is shared as evidence that this feeling is universal and deserves tenderness and compassionate attention.

### The Path Forward for Technology and Humanity

- The momentum of technological progress is described as unstoppable, making awareness and intentional engagement more important than resistance.

- Groups of technologists, neuroscientists, mindfulness teachers, and business leaders are described as actively working on ways to make technology more compassionate and humane.

- George Soros and other high-profile figures are noted as speaking out publicly about the dangers of internet platforms, signaling a growing movement for accountability.

- Virtual assistants are envisioned as potentially reminding users to pause for compassion meditation or to seek out perspectives that help them see the whole elephant.

- A safe space for engineers troubled by what they are designing is noted as a new development that supports ethical reflection within the technology industry.

- The path forward is described as partly in the hands of individuals who can demand better, and partly in the hands of those with the technical expertise and moral commitment to build it.

### Next Steps

- Mindfulness teachers are encouraged to incorporate awareness of technology's impact on attention and focus into their teaching as a matter of ongoing practice.

- Participants are directed to explore the humanetech.com website, particularly the "take control" section, for practical resources to use personally and with students.

- Engagement with communities and groups working to bring compassion and mindfulness into technology design is identified as a meaningful way to contribute to the path forward.

- Continued personal practice and authentic engagement with these teachings is affirmed as the foundation from which effective teaching and positive ripple effects can emerge.

### Stories
- **The Blind Men and the Elephant**
  Shared by Trudy Goodman-Cornfield. An ancient story appearing across spiritual traditions. Blind people in a village hear there is an elephant and go to discover what it is by touch. Each touches a different part — tail, leg, belly, ear, tusk — and declares it to be a rope, a pillar, a wall, a fan, a spear respectively. They begin to fight over who is right. A wise woman arrives, sees the whole elephant, and explains that each person is touching a piece of the truth. They are relieved and stop fighting. Trudy applies this as a metaphor for partial perspectives, the need for collective understanding, and the purpose of mindfulness to perceive the whole picture.
- **Tristan Harris and Google**
  Shared by Trudy Goodman-Cornfield. Tristan Harris was an engineer at Google who became profoundly uncomfortable with what he was designing. He raised the alarm internally; the company listened but did not change anything. He left and devoted subsequent years to publicly educating people about how neuroscientists and engineers work together to make technology more addictive. Trudy met him at Wisdom 2.0. He founded timewellspent.io, later associated with humanetech.com.
- **Alejandro Inarritu's VR Installation at LACMA**
  Shared by Trudy Goodman-Cornfield. A virtual reality installation by filmmaker Alejandro Inarritu at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art. Six minutes of total immersion in the experience of crossing the border and being caught and arrested by Border Patrol. Described as so vivid that people come out of the experience unable to feel the same way about borders as they did before. Offered as an example of technology's potential to increase empathy and compassion.
- **Ram Dass in His Chair**
  Shared by Trudy Goodman-Cornfield. Trudy is broadcasting from Ram Dass's house in Hawaii, seated in his chair. Ram Dass is present in the house and has been listening to the talk throughout. At the close of the session, Trudy acknowledges him directly, noting that the group performed his Loving Awareness Meditation together while he was there.
- **Trudy's Personal Experience with Social Media**
  Shared by Trudy Goodman-Cornfield. While having breakfast with a tech-savvy mindfulness teacher colleague who emphasized the importance of social media engagement for teachers today, Trudy noticed a contraction in her heart and a fear arising about whether her teaching would reach people if she remained off social media. She used this as a personal example of FOMO and how mindfulness creates space around the feeling without making it disappear.
### Physical, Mental Challenges, Affects on Practice
- **Erosion of Concentration**
  Trudy raises the concern that technology is undermining the capacity for focused attention, which is the foundation of mindfulness practice. She and Jack Kornfield both find it harder and harder to disengage from devices despite years of mindfulness practice.
- **Compulsive News Consumption**
  After the 2016 presidential election, Trudy and Jack found themselves spending increasing amounts of time reading news across multiple sites, feeling it was not fulfilling and was displacing more creative activities.
- **Technology-Induced ADD**
  Trudy describes the experience of opening a phone to make a call, getting distracted by texts, and forgetting the original intention — likening this to attention deficit disorder induced by device use.
- **Sleep Disruption**
  Blue light from devices activates the brain and makes it difficult to fall asleep, contributing to a tendency to stay online late into the night. Switching to yellow/warm light after sunset is recommended as a countermeasure.
- **Social Media Addiction**
  Trudy acknowledges that social media is stronger than her own willpower, describing the pull to check grandchildren's posts, like friends' content, and stay engaged as something she cannot moderate — leading her to abstain entirely.
- **FOMO as a Painful Emotion**
  Fear of missing out is identified as a real and intense psychological challenge, particularly for teenagers. It is described as deliberately engineered into technology. Trudy personally experienced it in relation to her low participation in social media and concern about her teaching's reach.
- **Grandchildren's Technology Use**
  Trudy observes her 16-year-old granddaughter and 13-year-old grandson and notes with concern how the younger child imitates his sister's increasing engagement with texts and social media, reflecting on the broader impact on young people's development.
