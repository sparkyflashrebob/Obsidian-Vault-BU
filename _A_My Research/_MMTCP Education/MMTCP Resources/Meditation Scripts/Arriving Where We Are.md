MINDFULNESS MEDITATION TEACHER CERTIFICATION PROGRAM 

Script for Guided Meditation 
(adapted from The Power of Awareness with Jack Kornfield and Tara Brach) 

Arriving Where We Are (10 minutes) 
Let’s start for a moment with just a little practice, arriving where we are. Find a posture in which you are relaxed and alert for this practice period. You can sit on a chair. You can sit on the floor, on a cushion; you can lie down or stand up—whatever way your body is comfortable and at ease on the earth. And if, as we go through these meditations and trainings, you find your body uncomfortable, you can notice that and work with it mindfully for a time. And then feel free to change posture or move. Just do so mindfully. So keeping this invitation for presence now, allow yourself first to look around where you are, opening mindfulness, awareness to the body and senses. See the colors around you in the room, the computer screen, the windows, and the light or darkness. And as you look around, relax and let yourself just take in the environment.
[Pause] 
And when you’re ready, then bring your attention back to yourself here. And allow your eyes to close. And relax more fully. Let the eyes and face be soft.
[Pause] 
Loosen the jaw. Let the shoulders relax and the arms and hands rest easily, so you’re arriving with a sense of dignity and alertness, and at the same time, a deep invitation of presence and relaxation. And begin to notice the play of experience as you practice quietly. Notice the sounds that come and go. 
[Long pause] 
You can also notice if there’s any sense of smell or taste, odors from the roses on the table nearby, or someone cooking down the street, or even the words that I’m using that evoke the sense. You can notice the body sensations, areas of ease or tightness, warmth or cool, vibration or stillness, pleasure or pain, the feel of this body received with a kind attention. 
[Long pause] 
You can notice the state of the heart as well. Is there interest, or gratitude, or sadness and grief, or excitement, or tenderness? Does the heart feel open or closed? Without any judgment, just noticing. 
[Pause] 
Then notice the state of mind that’s present now—planning, excitement, relaxed, doubting or questioning, remembering, curious, obsessed. 
[Pause] 
And finally, again, feel your body here halfway between heaven and earth, with presence and dignity. And realize that you can notice the play of experience you have, with a spacious and kind attention, that you’ve come more fully into the present. 
[Ring bell, followed by a pause] 
What did you notice? Almost everyone will notice how much things change. The sounds come and go. The body sensations and vibrations open and move and change. The moods and states of the heart will arise, get stronger, change to another. The states of mind and thought stream through. 
[Pause] 
This is the reality of our human experience. As you get quiet, you find a stillness in the attention, and at the same time, the ever-changing stream of thoughts and sounds, of feelings and sensations. In this practice of meditation, your heart beats, the Earth turns, and you take your seat in the midst of it. And as you relax and open your senses, you open your eyes to the world around you as well, what one Zen poet called “the love letters sent by the wind and the rain, by the blossoms and the moon.” And by becoming present, you also become attentive to one another. So there is a blessing to be able to pay attention to yourself, to your own body, to the environment around you, to the states of your heart and mind. And this is just where we begin, exactly where we are.

Thank you. 
[Ring bell to close the practice]
