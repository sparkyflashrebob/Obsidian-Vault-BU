Peer Group Guidelines & Instructions

  

# Peer Group Guidelines

In the Peer Group meetings, you’ll be gathering with the same group of six people from your Mentoring Group, but without your mentor. Peer Groups will meet once per month for 90 minutes online via video conferencing of your group's choosing. Peer Group meetings are a vital part of this mindfulness training. You will be engaging in skill-building practices, discussions, and exercises together that align with the month’s curriculum. 

  

As a group, you will need to self-organize and find a consistent meeting time that works for the entire group. We highly recommend finding a regular time so that everyone can plan ahead. We recommend having one person volunteer to take the lead on scheduling using a free and easy online scheduling platform called Doodle. Each group member will be expected to prioritize the attendance of this meeting. Once the group has decided on a time, please post your Peer Group meeting schedule for the duration of the program to the Newsfeed in your group.

  

You will need to self-organize to find a video conferencing platform to use for your group. It is important that the group be on video rather than just audio as seeing each other and being seen is an important part of learning and receiving feedback. We recommend using Google Hangouts, Skype, Zoom, or similar free services.

  

Attendance at Peer Group meetings is required. If you need to miss a session, please let your group members know in advance. The maximum number of Peer Group meetings that you can miss is three. After that, your certification may be jeopardized.

  

### Peer Group - A Practice in Wise Speech

The Peer Group meetings are an opportunity to support each other in the program. You will be both giving and receiving support with the other members in your group. When a group is aligned in a common spirit of support the experience of sangha happens— appreciation, trust, a spirit of generosity and genuine connection. The whole then becomes greater than the sum of its parts.

  

A key to this happening is seeing the group as a collective exercise in Wise Speech. The more you can embody this practice, the better you will be able to teach it to others. We strongly encourage your meetings to be a time whereas a group you practice wise, skillful communication. The basic guidelines for Wise Speech are to say what's truthful and useful in a kind way so that your communication can be really received by others.

  

Please carefully review and adhere to the Agreements for Multicultural Interactions that you have discussed in your Mentor Groups. Use these guidelines to remind yourselves and each other of your commitments to Wise Speech. Check in periodically around which guidelines you are practicing well as a group, and which you could be practicing even better.

  

### Working With Conflict Within the Peer Group

As in any community, there are times when miscommunication or microaggressions occur. We invite you to hold the perspective that when conflict occurs, it’s an opportunity to deepen in relationship with one another. Working through conflict and repairing harm is as important a part of Wise Speech as striving for harmony. It is especially important to hold, and even explicitly state, a shared intention of goodwill as the group processes things-while also taking accountability for impacts, even unintentional ones.

  

When conflict arises, use your Peer Group time together to slow down and discuss the conflict openly. You are all training to be teachers of mindfulness and compassion. It’s essential that you embody the principles and practices that support these qualities with one another.

  

We suggest the following:

  

First, sit quietly for five minutes.

Take some time to get in touch with your intentions. Take two minutes to silently reflect on what you want to accomplish.

Finally, take turns sharing your intentions for communication between you in the moment and the group meetings going forward. Make it a practice to deeply listen to each other.

As you speak, consider the intention behind your words. Are you mainly trying to be “right”? Do you want to have a greater understanding between you? Can your words come from valuing the relationships that you share?

  

You can all be “vibes watchers,” If anyone feels the energy is getting uncomfortable, name what you are experiencing, especially if the communication feels harmful. Then everyone can take three mindful breaths in silence before continuing.

  

If you can’t resolve the conflict within one session, please reach out to your Mentor immediately to let them know what’s arising within the group. They can help facilitate another conversation, or if necessary reach out to the MMTCP leadership team for additional specific support.

  

For additional guidelines on resolving Peer Group conflict, please see the Handouts and Scripts within the Resources section in the student portal (MMTCP - Class of 2023).

  

## Suggested Peer Group Process and Format

Each meeting has a facilitator; all members take turns in this role. The role of the facilitator is to keep time for skill-building exercises and group discussions, keep the group on track with that month’s Peer Group assignment, follow up with anyone who may have missed that session, take attendance and report attendance to their Mentor with a short (3-4 sentence) summary of the Peer Group meeting. Each group also can appoint a scribe for taking notes on the meeting.

  

In the first month of the program, the group creates (and shares with their Mentor) a schedule of students who will do skill-building presentations (i.e. guided meditations or short talks) for Mentor Group as well as Peer Group meetings. It is simplest to do this alphabetically unless a student knows in advance they will have a scheduling conflict. As you create this schedule, please look ahead at the upcoming assignments as not all months have a skill-building presentation by a student, although most do.

  

Example

  

MENTOR GROUP PEER GROUP

JANUARY Bledsoe Hopf

FEBRUARY Ford Turk

MARCH Hopf Urdaneta

APRIL Turk Baltazar

MAY Urdaneta Bledsoe

JUNE Baltazar Ford

Sample suggested schedule (there will be some variance due to the Peer Group assignments:

(5 minutes) Silent Sit - 5 minute. Facilitator rings a bell at the end.

(20 minutes) Check in - 3 minutes per person--facilitator uses a bell to signal the end. Person wraps up their thoughts if they’re not finished. Then picks the next person to speak.

Suggested check in:

  

How is your mindfulness practice helping you meet the challenges and joys in your life?

What are you learning and how is the program supporting you in that learning?

Any elements of the program you would like to bring up for clarification or discussion.

(30-35 minutes) Assigned skill-building practice (i.e. guided meditation, talk, etc.) as listed in your monthly Peer Group assignment, followed by feedback from group members. (Note: some months may have two skill-building exercises which may take longer.)

  

When giving feedback, use the four-step feedback process you have learned in your Mentor Group

  

Speak from the heart, with kindness, and from your own experience

Be respectful of the views of others

Use the guidelines for Wise Speech

To support the feedback process, review the MMTCP guidelines for meditations and talks

(20-25 minutes) General discussion - You can use this time to discuss any topics that have been assigned for this month’s Peer Group meeting. If no topics have been assigned, you can take the time to discuss any topics that come up regarding the curriculum content, assignments, the program in general, practice answering questions, about your teaching (once Practicums start) or any other topic that arises.

General questions could include:

  

What questions would you like to explore about the current module?

How are things going for you so far in the program?

Where are you finding inspiration?

What has been challenging for you?

(10 minutes) Opportunity for Feedback on the Meeting - After the main discussion, take some "process time" for members to express how the meeting was for them. Often this will take just a few minutes, sometimes longer. It gives a chance for people to give feedback about what is or is not working for them in the group, and to review how the group is holding Guidelines for Multicultural Interactions and addressing conflict. That way everyone can benefit from individual observations and hear any ideas that would make the group stronger, rather than anyone leaving the meeting feeling disconnected from the group. Saying what's truthful and useful, without blame, can make for a deeper sense of honesty and mutual commitment.
