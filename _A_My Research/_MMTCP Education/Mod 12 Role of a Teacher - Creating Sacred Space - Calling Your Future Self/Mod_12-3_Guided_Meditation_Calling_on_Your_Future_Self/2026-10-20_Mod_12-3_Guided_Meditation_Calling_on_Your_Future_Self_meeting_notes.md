# Mod 12-3 Guided Meditation Calling on Your Future Self - Oct, 20

### Mod 12-3 Guided Meditation: Calling on Your Future Self
📅 October 20, 2026
👥 @Tara Brach (Lead Meditation Guide/Teacher)
Tags: Guided Meditation, Future Self, Mindfulness, Compassion, Bodhicitta, Refuge, Awakened Heart, Teaching Practice, Visualization, Breath Work
### Outline
### Introduction to the Guided Practice and Setting Intention

- Participants are invited to sit comfortably, close their eyes, and come into stillness before beginning the practice.

- The session is titled "Calling on Your Future Self," with the facilitator offering alternative terms such as "high self," "awakened heart," or "manifestation of bodhicitta" for those who prefer different language.

- All suggested terms are framed as pointing toward the most evolved expression of one's being, allowing each participant to choose what resonates personally.

### Breathwork and Physical Relaxation

- Several long, deep breaths are guided to help collect attention, with emphasis on filling the chest and lungs fully on each inhale.

- Slow out-breaths are encouraged as a means of releasing accumulated tension from the body.

- Continued awareness of the breath's natural rhythm is introduced as a foundation for deepening physical and mental relaxation.

### Visualization of the Future Self

- A journey into the future is invited, with participants encouraged to imagine themselves five, ten, or twenty years ahead depending on their current age.

- The future self is described as an older, more awake, and more evolved version of oneself, representing how one's awakened heart manifests over time.

- Participants are guided to visualize their future self in a teaching role, whether with one person, a small group, or a large group, in either a formal or informal setting.

- Attention is drawn to specific physical details of the future self, including clothing, hair, facial expression, posture, and the quality of presence in their eyes.

- The future self is depicted as engaged in teaching activities such as giving a talk, responding to individual questions, or listening attentively to students.

### Dialogue with the Future Self

- Once the students are imagined to have departed, participants are guided to sit one-on-one with their future self in a space of kindness and complete welcome.

- Participants are encouraged to share with their future self their emerging experience of teaching mindfulness and compassion, including whatever feels most challenging, vulnerable, insecure, or uncertain.

- Any questions about fulfilling one's potential in teaching are invited to be asked openly during this inner dialogue.

- A period of deep listening with an open heart is then introduced, allowing participants to receive whatever arises from the encounter.

### Receiving the Message and Wisdom of the Future Self

- Participants are guided to sense the most important message their future self wishes them to remember, one that can serve them in the moments of their current life.

- Openness is encouraged to receiving this message in various forms, whether as words, a gesture, a touch, or a visual image.

- The awareness, wisdom, and heart of the future self are then imagined as filling the participant entirely, bathing every cell and the spaces around them.

- Resting in this expanded sense of beingness is offered as an opportunity to simply be present with all that one is.

### Integration and Closing Reflection

- Awareness is gently returned to the breath, the body, and the physical space shared with others in the room.

- The love and wisdom of one's most evolved self are affirmed as already living within the participant, accessible at any moment as a source of refuge.

- Turning toward what one loves is presented as the path through which that love is discovered, lived from, and taught from in one's own unique way.

- The session closes with an inward bowing to what each participant loves, as a gesture of reverence and commitment.

### Next Steps

- Participants are encouraged to carry forward the insight and message received from their future self as an ongoing inner resource in daily life.

- Returning to the sense of one's most evolved being is presented as a practice that can be revisited whenever support or guidance is needed while serving others.

- Continued engagement with teaching mindfulness and compassion is affirmed as a path that deepens through turning toward what one loves.

### Spiritual and Societal Insights
- **Bodhicitta and the Awakened Heart**
  - The Future Self is equated with the manifestation of bodhicitta, the awakened heart in Buddhist tradition
  - The practice suggests that one's highest spiritual potential is not distant but accessible through inner visualization and receptivity
- **The Teacher as Embodiment of Practice**
  - The Future Self is consistently portrayed as a teacher, suggesting that teaching is seen as a natural expression of spiritual maturity
  - The quality of presence and transmission is emphasized over content or technique, pointing to the relational and energetic dimensions of teaching
- **Vulnerability as Part of the Path**
  - Participants are explicitly invited to bring their insecurities, uncertainties, and vulnerabilities into the dialogue with the Future Self
  - This normalizes doubt and challenge as inherent to the path of teaching mindfulness and compassion
- **Taking Refuge in the Self**
  - The concept of "taking refuge" is reframed here from an external orientation toward an internal one — refuge found in the fullness of one's own being
  - This parallels traditional Buddhist refuge-taking but directs it inward toward one's own awakened nature
- **Love as the Foundation of Teaching**
  - The closing instruction to turn toward what one loves and teach from it positions love as both the motivational source and the pedagogical foundation
  - The inward bow to what one loves serves as a ritual gesture of commitment and reverence
- **Transmission Beyond Words**
  - The message from the Future Self is described as potentially arriving through gesture, touch, or image rather than words alone
  - This points to non-verbal, somatic, and intuitive modes of knowing as valid and valued
