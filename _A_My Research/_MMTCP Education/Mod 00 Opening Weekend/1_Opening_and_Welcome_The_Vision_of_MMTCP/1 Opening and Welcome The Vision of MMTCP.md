#### Outline

#### Overview and Scale of the MMTCP Teacher Training Program

- Launched as a two-year mindfulness and compassion teacher training program, the MMTCP was designed to meet a growing global need for qualified teachers in these disciplines.
- Initial expectations projected a cohort of one to two hundred participants, but demand grew far beyond that, with the most recent cohort reaching 1,450 trainees and the current cohort comprising 2,550 participants from 75 countries.
- Over 6,000 applications were received for the current cohort, reflecting an extraordinary global demand for the skills and transformation that mindfulness and compassion practices can offer.
- The program is co-led by Jack Kornfield and Tara Brach, who have invested extensive time and their most refined teachings into building the curriculum over the course of many years.
- Conda Mason serves as one of the major teachers in the program and is participating in her second cohort, having mentored participants through the previous graduating class.

#### The Societal Need Driving Mindfulness and Compassion Education

- A growing crisis of emotional and mental health underlies the demand for this training, with the US Surgeon General noting that more than half of all illnesses brought to hospitals and clinics are rooted in emotional problems.
- Tens of billions of dollars have been invested in recent decades to address suicide, depression, mental illness, and addiction, yet according to a senior scientist from the National Institutes of Health, these efforts have barely moved the needle.
- Humanity is described as being at a crossroads where technological advancement, including computers, artificial intelligence, biotechnology, and nanotechnology, cannot on its own resolve warfare, racism, environmental destruction, or tribalism, as all of these problems have roots in the human heart.
- The Chairman of the US Military Joint Chiefs of Staff is quoted as describing the nation as "nuclear giants and ethical infants," underscoring the urgent need to match outer technological development with inner human development.
- Healing at a societal level is understood to require not pills, consumer goods, or more technology, but rather the cultivation of inner capacities such as attention, kindness, and compassion.

#### The Transformative Power of Mindfulness and Attention Training

- A brief experiential exercise was offered to demonstrate that human attention can be deliberately directed, moving from the feet, to the hands, to the surrounding environment, and finally to the heart.
- This exercise illustrated that attention can be trained and filled with positive qualities such as kindness, love, and understanding, which stands in contrast to conventional education that focuses almost exclusively on outer subjects.
- Neuroscience research supports the capacity to develop steadiness of mind and caring attention, and teachers in the program will be equipped to use this research when explaining these processes to their own students.
- Mindfulness and compassion are presented as opening pathways not only to stress reduction and emotional regulation but also to deeper existential questions about consciousness, identity, freedom, and what truly matters in life.
- Stepping out of self-absorption and the "body of fear" is described as possible through practice, allowing individuals to feel connection to the whole and to live with dignity, freedom of heart, and collective wellbeing.

#### Core Values and Design Principles of the MMTCP Program

- The seeds of the program were planted in 2012 when Jack Kornfield and Tara Brach reflected together on what the world most needed and recognized that an online teacher training with genuine spiritual depth could serve a growing global community of practitioners.
- A central design principle is that the program centers on relationships, both in its curriculum content and in the relational culture cultivated among participants themselves, as much of the suffering students bring to teachers involves conflict and disconnection with those close to them.
- Trauma sensitivity is built into the program as a foundational element, recognizing that trauma is pervasive across all populations and that facilitating groups and guiding meditation requires specific skills to create safety, grounding, and a container for deep practice.
- Compassion-based engaged spirituality is named as a core value, grounded in the understanding that inner awakening and outer freedom cannot be separated from care for the most vulnerable members of the broader community.
- Dedication to Diversity, Equity, Inclusivity, and Access, referred to as DEIA, is described as the ground of all other values and as a direct expression of love in action, representing a shared commitment to honor the dignity and sacredness of every being.
- For those from dominant cultural backgrounds, DEIA work involves the ongoing effort of waking up from conditioning that separates and excludes, while for those from non-dominant populations it involves healing and building resilience in the face of ongoing violation.
- Research on mindfulness is cited as demonstrating its capacity to help practitioners become aware of deeply conditioned biases, including racial bias, and to develop the attentional depth needed to avoid acting from those biases.

#### Agreements for Multicultural Interactions

- A set of agreements titled "Agreements for Multicultural Interactions," used at the East Bay Meditation Center as part of a framework called radical inclusivity, was introduced as a foundational guide for all communication throughout the two-year program.
- Participants were invited to listen to each agreement and identify which felt most natural and which might present the greatest personal challenge.
- The first agreement, "Try It On," asks participants to be willing to engage with new ideas or ways of doing things that may be unfamiliar or uncomfortable, recognizing that views are deeply conditioned and that enlarging perspective requires tolerating discomfort.
- "Practice Self-Focus," the second agreement, calls on participants to speak only from their own experience and to avoid making assumptions about the experiences of others, acknowledging that real and significant differences in history and conditioning exist across individuals and communities.
- The third agreement, understanding the difference between intent and impact, emphasizes that acknowledging the harm caused by one's words or actions is more important than defending one's intentions, and that this principle is especially critical in conversations involving power and privilege.
- Equally, those who experience harm are encouraged to remain open to the possibility that harmful intent was not present, as the agreement operates in both directions to support genuine understanding.
- The fourth agreement, "Practice Both And," encourages substituting the word "and" for "but" in speech, as this linguistic shift honors multiple realities simultaneously and validates the complexity of what others have expressed rather than erasing it.
- Refraining from blaming and shaming oneself and others is named as essential because guilt and shame shut down communication, particularly in conversations about race and privilege, and the goal is to engage in ways that are generative rather than paralyzing.
- The "Move Up, Move Back" agreement encourages full participation by inviting those who speak frequently to create more space, and those who speak less to contribute more, framing this as an act of generosity and a practice of dana.
- Mindful listening is presented as a practice of setting aside the habit of preparing one's response while another person is speaking, and instead being willing to be surprised, to learn something new, and to respond from the heart in the moment.
- Confidentiality is described as a form of protection that allows participants to be vulnerable and honest, with the guideline that learnings may be taken home but no one other than oneself should be identified, and that any follow-up conversation with another participant requires their explicit permission.
- The right to pass is affirmed as a respectful practice that allows any participant to remain silent when not ready to speak, honoring the natural rhythm of one's own heart and the appropriate timing of self-expression.

#### Heart Reflection and Aspiration Practice

- Participants were guided through a body-based meditation focused on the breath at the heart, inviting a release of unconscious physical tension and a deepening of inward attention.
- A visualization was offered in which participants imagined walking down a path and rounding a bend to encounter an image, landscape, animal, sound, or felt sense that reflected the current state of their heart.
- Participants were then invited to sense what their heart most longs for and to identify the deepest aspiration that brought them to this training, with the option to journal their reflections either electronically or by hand.
- Following the individual reflection, participants were paired in dyads and given four minutes each to share what they discovered about the state of their heart and their aspiration, while their partner held a space of presence and silence.
- Aspirations shared in the chat upon returning from dyads included deepening compassion, expanding understanding of others' perspectives, strengthening personal practice, helping others find peace and connection, and coming home to oneself.

### Stories

**Jack Kornfield — His Own Journey**

Jack Kornfield shares that his family life was painful growing up, with a violent and abusive father. In college, he encountered the teachings of the Buddha through Asian studies and felt an urgent personal need to understand the cause of suffering and the path to its end. Upon graduating, he asked the Peace Corps to send him to a Buddhist country. He worked on rural health and medical teams in the Mekong River Valley in northeast Thailand during a time of war, found a teacher, and became a monk. In meditation he learned to steady his mind and cultivate compassion and forgiveness, while facing his own anger, fear, loneliness, and confusion. He describes this as more necessary than anything he learned in his Ivy League education.

**Jack Kornfield — Christina Figueres and the Paris Climate Accord**

Jack Kornfield recounts the story of Christina Figueres, the United Nations Special Representative for climate change. Desperate, she was advised to visit Plum Village, Thich Nhat Hanh's community in the South of France. There she had a deep realization that the countries fighting over who was the victim and who was the perpetrator were in fact interdependent, like one family. She returned to Paris and, practicing mindful listening rather than blame, was able to bring 190 countries together to create the Paris Climate Accord.

**Conda Mason — Her Own Journey**

Conda Mason grew up in a loving household in Southern California in a neighborhood shared by Black and Mexican families. At age 12, her family moved to an all-white suburb. She experienced name-calling and racial hostility she did not understand, and describes her inner light going out one by one. She carried this wound through college until she attended a kundalini yoga class at UC Berkeley, where lying in savasana she felt the aliveness she had known as a child return. She went on to study Tibetan Buddhism, became a yoga teacher at Kripalu in Western Massachusetts, and in 1996 received a call from Jack Kornfield inviting her to co-lead a retreat for activists of color. This began her long mentorship with Jack and her connection to Spirit Rock and the East Bay Meditation Center.

**Conda Mason — Loss of Family**

Conda Mason shares that her family carried a rare kidney disease and that almost all of her family members died from it. She lost her parents and brothers in back-to-back deaths, leaving only one sister. She describes the practice of mindfulness and compassion as what allowed her to mourn in a healthy way and understand something larger, while not stopping the grief itself.

**Tara Brach — Her Own Journey**

Tara Brach was a type-A student involved in social activism and tenants' rights work with Black and Latino organizers, planning to attend law school. She began yoga classes on Tuesday nights while attending political rallies on weekends, noticing the contrast between the aggression of political fervor and the stillness of meditation. One spring night, walking home after yoga class, she stopped under a fruit tree and realized her body and mind were in the same place at the same moment. She felt a peace and connectedness that led her, at age 21, to move into a spiritual community in the United States rather than attend law school. She stayed for ten years, then left, had a child, began Buddhist retreats, started her doctorate in psychology, and helped found a DC meditation community.

**Tara Brach — The Black Medical Student Near George Floyd's Murder**

Tara Brach shares the story of a Black friend who was attending medical school in Minneapolis, living blocks from where George Floyd was murdered. During his years there, in most encounters with white people outside a small circle of friends, he felt he was receiving the message "You're bad. You're dangerous." He told Tara this made him hate himself, despite being a deeply good person. His refuge was the practice of loving-kindness: repeating "May I love myself just as I am" as his pathway home to remembering who he was.

**Tara Brach — The Dying Man at Zen Hospice**

Tara Brach recounts a story from Frank Ostaseski, founder of Zen Hospice in San Francisco. Frank was guiding an elderly man dying of cancer who was in a lot of pain and wanted to learn mindfulness. Frank offered to place his hands on the man's belly to help him hold the pain. The man said it still hurt. Frank moved his hands further away, holding the pain in more space. The man said it was a little better. Frank moved his hands even further away, and the man said "That's lovely." Frank invited him to rest there. The man said "Just rest in love," which became a mantra for his remaining days.

**Tara Brach — The Seeds of MMTCP**

In 2012, Jack Kornfield and Tara Brach were in Tara's kitchen reflecting on priorities for the coming years. They recognized that the teachings were exactly what the world needed and that a growing number of students and friends around the globe wanted to share them. They conceived of an online teacher training that would have real spiritual depth and not hold back from exploring true freedom on the path. A team quickly formed around them to develop the program.
