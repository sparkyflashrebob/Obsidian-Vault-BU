# Mod 26-3 Dharma In Motion with Jonathan Relucio - Oct, 20

### Mod 26-3 Dharma In Motion with Jonathan Relucio
📅 October 20, 2026
👥 **Main Teacher:** Jonathan Relucio
**Tags:** Mindfulness, Movement Practice, Pranayama, Four Elements, Yoga, Meditation, Sangha, Gratitude, Equanimity, Dharma, Body Awareness, Breathwork, Sun Salutations, Warrior Pose, Loving-Kindness
### Outline
### Grounding Practice Through the Earth Element

- Attention is directed toward the physical sensations of the body making contact with the floor and chair as an entry point into the practice.

- Solidity and density of the body are identified as expressions of the earth element within one's being.

- Gravity is introduced as the force connecting the body to the earth, with the minerals in bones described as linking directly to the minerals of the earth.

### Breath Awareness and Connection to the Air Element

- Hands are placed on the chest and belly to direct attention inward toward the movement of the breath.

- The air element is characterized by qualities of lightness, expansiveness, and changeability as experienced through inhalation and exhalation.

- Belly breathing is introduced as a pranayama technique in which the belly expands on the inhale and relaxes on the exhale.

- Natural, uninstructed breathing by infants is offered as a model for how belly breathing should feel, with babies described as natural teachers of this technique.

### Gratitude and Caring Touch Practice

- Participants are invited to offer gratitude to each part of the body through gentle touch, beginning at the head and moving toward the soles of the feet.

- An additional option of gentle massage is offered alongside light touch as a way of deepening the expression of care toward the body.

- Attention is drawn to any sensations arising from the practice, including tingling, warmth, or coolness, as indicators of the body's responsiveness.

- The body is acknowledged as something that supports and serves every moment, making it a worthy recipient of care and gratitude.

### Shoulder Movement and Introduction of the Water Element

- A dynamic shoulder-rolling movement is introduced in which shoulders draw back on the inhale and roll forward on the exhale.

- Fluidity and flow are highlighted as the defining qualities of the water element as it is experienced through this movement.

- Water is identified as present throughout the body in the form of blood, fluids, eyes, saliva, and sweat, and is described as having healing and cleansing properties.

### Bell Sound as a Mindfulness Anchor

- A bell is rung and participants are invited to listen until the very last trace of sound fades completely.

- Sound is presented alongside the body and breath as one of three reliable anchors for returning to the present moment.

### Dynamic Twisting Movement and the Fire Element

- A side-to-side twisting movement with arms parallel to the floor is introduced, with a puff of breath released on each twist.

- Both seated and standing options are offered, with the standing variation including a heel lift on each twist to deepen the movement.

- The fire element is described as expressing itself through temperature, warmth, vibration, and heat within the body.

- Participants are guided to notice sensations of warmth or tingling in the arms, hands, fingertips, spine, legs, feet, and toes as expressions of the fire element.

- Fire is connected to inner strength, commitment to practice, and the passions and beliefs that motivate one's path.

### Arm Flopping Movement and Further Fire Element Awareness

- Arms are raised to shoulder height on the inhale and allowed to flop toward the earth on the exhale in a releasing movement.

- Both seated and standing variations are offered, with options to bend at the knees or at the waist for those standing.

- Following the movement, attention is again brought to the fire element through noticing vibration and changes in temperature in the body.

### Half Sun Salutations Integrating All Four Elements

- Four rounds of half sun salutations are guided, with each round dedicated to mindful awareness of a specific element.

- The first round focuses on the earth element, with attention directed to the muscles, bones, and solid points of contact between the body and the floor.

- Fire element awareness in the second round involves tuning into warmth, changes in temperature, and sensations around the heart space and belly.

- Curiosity and kindness are named as the foundational attitudes of the practice, with participants reminded that not feeling specific sensations is equally valid.

- The third round connects to the water element by acknowledging the natural quality of flow occurring within the body at all times, along with water's healing and cleansing qualities.

- Air element awareness in the fourth round emphasizes lightness, changeability, and expansiveness, with breath recognized as life-sustaining and shared among countless beings.

- The inseparability of all beings through the shared air element is offered as a contemplative reflection during the final round.

### Warrior Pose Variations and Elemental Choice

- A warrior pose is introduced with multiple options ranging from a seated variation with bent knee to a fully standing version with extended leg.

- Movement is added to the pose by raising the arms on the inhale and floating them over the legs on the exhale while gazing over the fingertips.

- Participants are encouraged to independently explore which element to connect with based on what feels most needed in their own practice at that moment.

- The relationship between elements is described as dynamic, with the suggestion that an excess of one element may call for the balancing quality of another.

### Hip Opener with Gentle Twist

- A hip-opening posture is introduced with knees wide, aligned with the ankles and second toes, and hands resting lightly on the inner thighs.

- A subtle twisting movement is added by alternately dropping each shoulder toward the floor while applying gentle pressure to the corresponding inner thigh.

- Participants are invited to connect with whichever element is emerging most prominently in their awareness during this movement.

- Equanimity is named as a central quality cultivated through the practices explored across the session, with gratitude and kindness to oneself and the community identified as supporting factors.

### Rhythmic Thoracic Breathing with Self-Embrace

- Arms are extended wide on the inhale to open the chest, with the option to lift the gaze toward the sky, and then wrapped around the body in a self-embrace on the exhale.

- Rhythm is added to the breath using a ratio of three counts for the inhale and six counts for the exhale, with a 4-8 ratio offered as an alternative.

- The inhalation is associated with sending kindness inward to oneself, while the exhalation is associated with sending kindness outward to the entire community.

- The practice of self-embrace is described as an opportunity to parent oneself, acknowledging that this kind of self-care is needed frequently.

### Closing Meditation and Dedication of Merit

- Participants are invited into stillness in their chosen posture, whether seated, standing, or lying down, with a blanket and pillow offered as support.

- Belly breathing with rhythmic breath ratios is reintroduced to prepare the mind and body for meditation, drawing on the Raja Yoga teaching that asana prepares the body and pranayama prepares the mind.

- The teacher BK Bose is cited as the source of the teaching that mindful movement and breathing techniques work together to cultivate steadiness and ease.

- The session closes with a dedication of merit through a reading of a poem by Zenju Earthlyn Manuel entitled For All Beings, which offers wishes for all beings to be cared for, loved, listened to, accepted, freed from fear and hatred, housed, fed, and able to live fully without scarcity.

- All beings are wished to be awake, to experience silence and stillness, and to receive and share the gifts of life.

### Next Steps

- Participants are encouraged to continue exploring the relationship between the four elements in their personal practice, using awareness of imbalance as a guide for which element to engage.

- The integration of belly breathing, rhythmic breath ratios, and mindful movement is recommended as an ongoing personal practice to cultivate steadiness and ease.

- Continued connection to nature through elemental awareness and grounding practices is identified as a supportive approach to maintaining equanimity.

### Spiritual and Societal Insights
**The Four Elements as a Framework for Self-Awareness**
- The body itself is understood as composed of the four classical elements — earth, air, fire, and water
- Each element offers a distinct quality: earth brings stability and density; air brings lightness and changeability; fire brings warmth, inner strength, and commitment; water brings flow, healing, and cleansing
- Practitioners are invited to notice which element they may be lacking and to use movement and breath to cultivate balance
**Mindfulness as Present-Moment Awareness**
- The body, breath, and sound are each named as doorways into the present moment
- The bell is used as a traditional mindfulness tool to anchor awareness in the here and now
- Stillness is framed not merely as the absence of movement but as something to be actively received and embodied
**Self-Compassion and Inner Parenting**
- The practice of offering gratitude and gentle touch to one's own body is framed as an act of care
- Practitioners are described as having the opportunity to parent their own selves through the practice
- Friendliness and curiosity are named as the foundational qualities — toward the breath, the body, and whatever arises
**Equanimity in the Context of the Pandemic**
- The pandemic is named as a period that called for the cultivation of equanimity across communities and circles
- Practices of gratitude, kindness, grounding in nature and the elements, and connection to Sangha are offered as supports for steadiness and ease
**Interconnection Through the Air Element**
- The air element is described as life-sustaining and shared — countless beings are sustained by it
- Practitioners are described as inseparable from all other beings through this shared element
**Loving-Kindness Extended Outward**
