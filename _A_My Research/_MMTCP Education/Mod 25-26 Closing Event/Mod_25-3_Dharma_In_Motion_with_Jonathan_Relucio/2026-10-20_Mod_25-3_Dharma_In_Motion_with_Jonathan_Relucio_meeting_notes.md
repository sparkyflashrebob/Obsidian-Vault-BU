### Outline
### Opening Grounding Practice with Gratitude

- Participants were invited to find a comfortable seat in a chair or to practice standing, based on what felt good for their body.

- Hands were placed gently on the head to begin a body gratitude practice, offering kind and gentle touch to each area.

- Gratitude was progressively offered to the ears, eyes, nose, face, neck, and all other parts of the body, moving downward at each participant's own pace.

- Extra care and tender loving attention were encouraged for any areas of the body that felt they needed additional focus.

- The practice concluded by reaching the soles of the feet, framing the body gratitude sequence as a way of coming home to oneself.

### Centering and Breath Awareness Practice

- Attention was drawn to the weight of the feet on the floor and the weight of the body on the chair as an anchor for centering.

- Shoulders were relaxed over the hips and ears over the shoulders to establish a grounded, aligned posture.

- One hand was placed on the chest and one on the belly to direct awareness inward toward the breath.

- For those for whom the breath was not accessible, grounding attention on the body's points of contact with the floor or chair was offered as an alternative anchor.

- Movement in the shoulders, chest, ribs, and belly during breathing was noted as something to observe without judgment.

### Belly Breathing Instruction

- Participants were guided to allow the belly to expand like a balloon on all sides during inhalation and to relax toward the spine on exhalation.

- The movement of the belly was described as something that could be large or small, or even invisible to the eye, with permission given for natural movement.

- Sensations of the belly were invited to be felt both from the outside and from the inside during the breathing practice.

- Two final full breaths were taken before returning to a natural, unguided breathing pace.

- Hands were then released to wherever they felt comfortable on the body.

### Loving-Kindness Introduction with Buddha's Words

- A quote attributed to the Buddha was shared, stating that no one in the entire universe is more deserving of love and affection than oneself.

- A bell was rung as a mindfulness cue, with participants invited to listen to the sound until its very last trace faded.

- Listening to the bell was framed as a practice for finding and anchoring oneself in the present moment.

- Loving-kindness phrases were introduced as something participants could say silently, out loud, or recreate in a personally resonant way.

### Arm Sweep Movement Practice

- Arms were raised to approximately shoulder height on the inhalation and allowed to fall toward the earth using gravity on the exhalation.

- An audible exhalation was offered as an option to accompany the falling of the arms.

- Three standing variations were demonstrated, including a straight-leg option, a bent-knee option, and a hip-fold option, described as all being equal in value.

- The sweeping motion was framed as a way of releasing anything that was not serving participants on that day.

- Five additional rounds were completed before participants were invited to let go of the movement and receive stillness.

- Awareness was then brought to the arms, hands, and fingertips to notice any tingling or pulsing sensations as the language of the body.

### Trunk Twist Movement Practice

- Arms were held parallel to the floor and participants were guided to gently twist from left to right in coordination with the breath.

- Inhalation was aligned with returning to center and exhalation was aligned with each twist to the left or right.

- A faster variation was offered as an option for those practicing standing, with the heels lifting alternately during each twist.

- Twists were gradually slowed until participants returned to stillness.

- A teaching from Jonathan's teacher Janice Gates was shared, offering the phrases "Pause and feel" and "What's here now" as invitations for self-inquiry.

- Breath speed after the twists was noted as something to observe, whether faster, slower, or unchanged.

### Shoulder Rolling Movement Practice

- Shoulders were rolled back with the heart lifting toward the sky on the inhalation, and rolled forward with the back rounding on the exhalation.

- A second option was offered in which the gaze lifted toward the sky during the back roll and the chin came toward the chest during the forward roll, opening the throat and the back of the neck.

- Participants were encouraged to make choices in their practice so that it felt satisfying and met the needs of their body.

- Sensations in the chest, shoulder blades, and upper back were highlighted as areas to bring awareness to during the movement.

- After completing the rounds, stillness was received and participants were invited to notice sensations at the level of the chest and shoulder blades.

### Interlaced Finger Shoulder Stretch

- Fingers were interlaced at the waist and palms were taken toward the sky on the inhalation, extending the arms overhead.

- Participants were encouraged to work at or away from their edge, finding a height and extension that felt appropriate for their body.

- The tailbone was dropped downward as the arms extended upward to create length through the spine.

- A balance between ease and effort was emphasized as a guiding principle for the movement.

- Awareness was brought to the shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, and fingers after releasing the movement.

- A loving-kindness phrase was offered, "May I be safe and protected from inner and outer harm," to be said silently, aloud, or adapted personally.

### Eagle Arms Shoulder Stretch

- Arms were extended on the inhalation and then crossed at the elbows on the exhalation, with the left arm over the right, to hug the shoulder blades apart.

- Three options were offered, including crossing the arms at the elbows, placing the backs of the hands together, or resting the fingertips of one hand on the palm of the other.

- Elbows were lifted as much as comfortable and gently pushed forward to round the back and open the shoulder blades.

- Three to four full breaths were held in the position before releasing, with the option to come out at any time.

- The sequence was then repeated on the opposite side with the right arm over the left.

- After releasing, participants were invited to notice any changes in breathing and to observe whether the inhalation or exhalation was longer.

### Neck Lateral Stretch Movement

- The right ear was allowed to fall toward the right shoulder on the exhalation to open the left side of the neck, returning to center on the inhalation.

- The movement was then mirrored to the left side, alternating at each participant's own pace in coordination with the breath.

- Range of motion on both sides was noted as something to observe with kindness rather than judgment.

- A teaching from Jonathan's teacher Ralph Gates was shared, stating that wise attention is kind attention, as an invitation to practice with gentleness.

- A loving-kindness phrase was offered, "May I be healthy in body and heart," to accompany the practice.

### Lateral Side Bend Movement Practice

- Arms were taken high on the inhalation and the body was leaned to the right on the exhalation, opening the left side of the body.

- The movement was then mirrored to the left side, continuing at each participant's own pace.

- Bending and flexing like bamboo in the wind was used as an image to convey strength, flexibility, and resilience without breaking.

- Feeling the strength in length and finding the middle between ease and effort were offered as guiding intentions.

- Awareness was brought to the arms moving through space and the feet pressing into the floor throughout the movement.

- Palms were brought to the chest after the final round and participants were invited to pause and feel the sensations along the sides of the body.

- Sensations were invited to be noticed as pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral as part of the mindfulness practice.

### Half Sun Salutation Sequence

- Arms were taken high on the inhalation and the body folded forward on the exhalation, with hands resting on the shins, ankles, or floor and the head relaxed.

- The core was engaged on the inhalation to come halfway up, and the body folded forward again on the exhalation.

- An optional back bend was offered by drawing the shoulders back, lifting the heart toward the sky, and arching the arms back as much as comfortable.

- Palms were brought to the chest at the end of each round as a moment of arrival and stillness.

- After completing the rounds, awareness was expanded to include the quality of the mind, noticing whether it felt calm or otherwise.

- A loving-kindness phrase was offered, "May I live with ease and well-being," to accompany the transition into stillness.

### Hip Opener and Side Warrior Practice

- A seated side warrior variation was demonstrated with the right knee over the right ankle and the left knee over the left ankle, legs at right angles.

- The right arm floated over the right leg and the left arm extended as a continuation of the right arm along one line, with the gaze over the right fingertips.

- A second seated option was offered by extending the left leg and allowing the arms to float freely.

- For the standing variation, the right heel was aligned with the arch or heel of the back foot, with the right knee over the right ankle.

- The right forearm rested on the right thigh on the exhalation, with the left arm extending over the left ear, with the option to gaze forward or toward the sky.

- An alternative arm option was offered by placing the left hand on the waist instead of extending it overhead.

- The sequence was repeated on the opposite side with the same options applied to the left forearm and right arm.

- After completing both sides, the spine was gently lengthened back to neutral and the feet were brought back to hips distance apart.

- The connection between breath, feeling, and thought was highlighted as something to notice in the stillness following the movement.

### Rhythmic Thoracic Breathing with Self-Embrace

- Arms were extended wide on the inhalation, lifting the heart, and then wrapped around the body in a self-hug on the exhalation.

- The arms were alternated so that a different arm was on top with each embrace, practicing receiving oneself with care.

- Rhythm was added to the breath by inhaling for a count of three and exhaling for a count of six, making the exhalation twice as long as the inhalation.

- Two rounds were completed at each participant's own pace, with the inhalation dedicated to sending kindness to oneself and the exhalation to sending kindness to everyone in the collective community.

- A final self-embrace was given before slowly releasing the arms to a resting position.

- Participants were invited to notice how the breath, body, and heart felt after completing the sequence.

### Closing Loving-Kindness Dedication

- A series of loving-kindness phrases were offered collectively, including "May we be safe and protected from inner and outer harm," "May we be healthy in mind, body, and spirit," and "May we live with ease and well-being."

- A final phrase was offered, "May we find true happiness and freedom," as a closing dedication for the group.

- Gratitude was expressed for the shared practice and the joy of being together in that way.

### Spiritual and Societal Insights
**Self-Love as a Foundational Teaching**
- Buddha's words were used to anchor the session in the principle that each person is as deserving of love and affection as anyone else in the universe
- The practice of directing kindness inward before extending it outward was woven throughout the session
**Loving-Kindness as a Practice Structure**
- Loving-kindness phrases were not presented as affirmations but as invitations, with participants given freedom to recreate them in personally resonant ways
- The phrases moved from personal safety and health to ease, well-being, and ultimately happiness and freedom
**Wise Attention as Kind Attention**
- Teacher Ralph Gates was cited: "Wise attention is kind attention"
- This principle was applied to noticing range of motion and physical sensation without judgment
**Breath as a Bridge Between Body and Mind**
- The relationship between breath, feeling, and thought was explicitly named — how breath connects to feeling, feeling connects to thought, and thought affects breath
- Belly breathing and rhythmic thoracic breathing were offered as tools to regulate this connection
**Friendliness as a Quality of Practice**
- Friendliness toward the body and breath was repeatedly invited throughout the movement sequences
- The session modeled an approach to movement that emphasizes choice, self-determination, and meeting the body's needs
**Community and Collective Kindness**
- During rhythmic thoracic breathing, the outbreath was dedicated to sending kindness to the collective community
- The closing phrases shifted from "I" to "we," extending the practice outward
