# Mod 26-2  Mindful Movement with Jonathan Foust - Oct, 20

### Mindful Movement with Jonathan Foust
📅 October 20, 2026
👥 Jonathan Foust — Main Teacher and Facilitator
Tags: Mindful Movement, Yoga, Breathwork, Meditation, Coherence Breathing, Spinal Mobility, Body Awareness, Presence, Relaxation, Standing Practice, Seated Practice
### Outline
### Welcome and Guiding Principles for the Session

- Participants are encouraged to ignore any instructions that do not resonate with their body, with Jonathan Foust framing this as a sign of safety and self-awareness.

- Exploring the edge of effort is introduced as a key principle, described as the point of engagement without strain.

- A recommendation is made to work at approximately 75% of capacity, finding a place of active effort without pushing beyond comfort.

- Softening on the inside while exploring what can lengthen on the inhalation and what can be felt on the exhalation is presented as a foundational approach.

- Breath awareness is established as the third core principle guiding the entire session.

### Coherence Breathing Introduction

- Ujjayi pranayama, now referred to in modern science as coherence breathing, is introduced as the foundational breathing technique for the session.

- Participants are guided to notice the natural, free-flowing quality of the breath and identify where it is felt most strongly.

- Breathing in to a count of four or five and exhaling to the same count is introduced, with the goal of matching the length of the in-breath and out-breath.

- Smoothing out the transition between the in-breath and out-breath is emphasized as a way to deepen the calming effect on the mind.

- This form of breathing is described as beneficial not only in meditation but also as a tool to maintain connection between awareness and bodily sensation during movement.

### Standing Warm-Up and Shaking Practice

- A lateral torso swing with arms hanging loosely like empty coat sleeves is introduced as the opening standing movement, with the option to lift the opposite heel for greater engagement.

- A soft gaze with eyes open is suggested during the swinging movement to allow a natural blur of vision that supports inward awareness.

- Standing meditation is introduced following the swing, with attention directed to softening the soles of the feet, the backs of the knees, the belly, and the tailbone.

- Awareness is guided to the occipital ridge and base of the skull, with an invitation to soften this area and imagine a gentle elongation through the back of the neck.

- A shaking practice is introduced as an energizing preparation for meditation, beginning with one hand and progressively moving up through the elbow and shoulder.

- The shaking is expanded to both arms, then both legs, and finally into jogging in place, with encouragement to breathe more fully and allow any sounds to emerge.

- Participants are invited to increase the intensity for a final 15 seconds, releasing anything they are holding on to before coming to stillness.

- Following the shaking, stillness is held to allow natural absorption, with attention returned to the breath, soft feet, relaxed knees, and an expressionless face.

### Three-Part Inhalation and Standing Flow

- A three-part inhalation technique is introduced, involving three sequential inhales followed by a full exhalation, with the option to bend the knees slightly and involve the whole body.

- Arms are swept overhead following the breath, with encouragement to breathe in to a count of four and exhale to a count of four while drawing the navel toward the spine.

- A lateral side stretch to the left at 75% capacity is guided, with the in-breath used to explore what wants to lengthen and the out-breath used to scan internal sensations.

- The same lateral stretch is then performed to the right side, with emphasis on maintaining engagement without anxiety or strain in the body.

- A standing flow is introduced in which the palms face outward, arms rise overhead on the inhalation, and palms come together descending through the midline on the exhalation.

- Participants are encouraged to slow down the movement progressively and correspondingly slow the breath, observing the effect this has on the quality of the mind.

- The sequence concludes with a return to standing meditation, with an invitation to relax, soften, and simply feel before transitioning to seated practice.

### Seated Spinal Movements

- The six fundamental movements of the spine are introduced as the framework for the seated practice, covering lateral movement, twisting, forward bending, and chest opening.

- A seated spinal twist to the left is guided by placing the right hand on the outside of the left thigh, with the breath used to explore lengthening on the inhalation and scanning new sensations on the exhalation.

- Relaxing through the occipital ridge and back of the neck is highlighted as a way to deepen the elongation, with the chin drawn parallel to the floor before gently rotating toward the left shoulder.

- Light palm pressure against the outside of the leg is suggested to support the twist, with attention given to the internal massage of the organs.

- The twist is then performed to the opposite side, with the left hand placed on the outside of the right thigh and the chin eventually drawn toward the right shoulder.

- Breath is described as the bridge between awareness and life force throughout the twisting sequence.

- A seated forward bend is introduced as the fifth spinal movement, with palms resting on the tops of the thighs and the torso invited forward over the legs.

- Relaxing along the base of the skull and back of the neck is emphasized in the forward bend, with the posture described symbolically as placing the head beneath the heart in an act of surrendering the mind into presence.

- An option to widen the legs and deepen the forward bend is offered for those with greater flexibility, along with an invitation to open the jaw wide, move it side to side, and release tension.

- Two options for returning from the forward bend are provided, one involving pressing the back up as a single unit and the other rolling up one vertebra at a time with the head coming up last.

- A chest-opening posture is introduced as the sixth spinal movement, beginning with arms reaching overhead and stretching to each side before bringing the elbows to shoulder height with palms facing inward.

- Shoulder blades are actively drawn together and slightly downward while breathing in to a count of four and exhaling to a count of four, targeting muscles that are chronically lengthened from prolonged sitting.

- The breath is directed up under the collarbones on the inhalation to lengthen the chest muscles that become shortened from excessive sitting, with the navel drawn in on the exhalation to expel stale air.

- Approximately 60 to 90 seconds of holding the contraction is identified as the threshold at which a meaningful shift begins to occur in the body.

- Arms are released overhead once more following the contraction, then allowed to float down as attention is turned inward to notice what has shifted through breath and movement.

### Transition into Meditation and Deep Relaxation

- Hands are brought to chest height with palms facing inward as a bridge between movement and meditative absorption, with the breath allowed to flow freely.

- Attention is directed to feeling the hands from the inside out, relaxing the thumbs, palms, and the space between the thumbs and forefingers as an anchor for deep relaxation and concentration.

- An ultra-slow descent of the hands toward the lap is guided, with the felt sense of the palms, fingers, thumbs, and micro-movements serving as the meditative anchor.

- Body scanning is introduced progressively, moving through the lips, tongue, jaw, lower back, buttocks, belly, arms, hands, pelvis, legs, soles of the feet, and heels.

- Relaxing the root of the tongue down into the throat while simultaneously softening the jaw is offered as a specific technique for deepening physical release.

- Participants are invited to escort attention to an anchor of their choosing and explore the balance of deep relaxation alongside alert awareness.

### Deepening Presence and Open Awareness

- In the final phase of meditation, participants are invited to refresh their practice by noticing anything that could relax, soften, or let go.

- A lens of non-preferential awareness, described as the witness or observer, is introduced as a quality of being both deeply relaxed and alert simultaneously.

- The practice is guided from a sense of doing toward a sense of being, releasing the anchor and any sense of manipulating experience.

- Resting in presence is offered as the culminating invitation, with the question posed of whether anything in the body, mind, or heart could relax or soften even further.

- Breath awareness is revisited, with participants guided to observe where the breath is felt most prominently and optionally lengthen it to a count of four on both the inhalation and exhalation.

### Closing and Return to Awareness

- Gentle head movements side to side are guided to begin the return from stillness, sensing the motion from the inside.

- Participants are invited to let the body move and stretch in whatever way feels most natural and pleasurable, including reaching the arms overhead.

- A lighthearted suggestion is offered to smile as a way to consciously shift state and complete the transition back to full wakefulness.

### Next Steps

- Participants are expected to transition immediately following the session to a subsequent session led by another facilitator who is described as being ready and excited to continue the program.

- The mindful movement and meditation practices introduced in the session, including coherence breathing, the 75% effort principle, and the six spinal movements, are intended to be carried forward as ongoing personal practices.

### Detailed Subject Matter
**Coherence Breathing**
- Formerly known in ashram traditions as ujjayi pranayama, now updated by modern science to coherence breathing
- Involves matching the length of the in-breath with the length of the out-breath, typically to a count of four or five
- Breath is kept smooth with slow, gentle transitions between inhalation and exhalation
- Described as helpful in both meditation and movement practice
- Disconnecting from the breath is equated with disconnecting from the body
**The 75% Capacity Principle**
- Participants are encouraged to go to 75% of their capacity rather than pushing to the limit
- The goal is to be engaged without strain or anxiety in the body
- Described as finding the edge where it "hurts good" — a yoga concept
- Backing off the inclination to push is described as powerful and productive
**Standing Movement Practices**
- Lateral torso swinging with arms as empty coat sleeves, optionally lifting the opposite heel
- Standing meditation with attention to softening the soles of the feet, backs of the knees, belly, tailbone, and occipital ridge
- Shaking practice: progressive shaking beginning with one hand, moving up through elbows and shoulders, then the opposite arm, then legs, then jogging in place
- Shaking described as building energy and providing contrast when returning to stillness
- Three-part inhalation breath exercise followed by arm sweeps overhead and lateral side stretches
**Seated Spinal Movement — Six Fundamental Movements**
- Lateral movement explored during standing practice
- Spinal twist: right hand to outside of left thigh, then left hand to outside of right thigh; attention to elongation on the in-breath and internal organ massage
- Forward bend: torso over thighs, option to widen legs; symbolic of placing the head beneath the heart and surrendering the mind into presence; jaw release and tongue extension offered
- Chest opening: elbows at shoulder height, palms facing inward, elbows drawn back; shoulder blades actively drawn together and slightly down; navel drawn toward spine on exhalation; held for approximately 60 to 90 seconds described as the point when shifts begin to occur
**Transition to Meditation**
- Hands held at chest level, palms facing inward, eyes closed
- Ultra-slow descent of hands toward the lap as an anchor for concentration
- Body scan including lips, tongue, jaw, lower back, buttocks, belly, arms, hands, legs, soles of feet
- Invitation to rest in presence, releasing any sense of doing or manipulating experience
- Non-preferential awareness described as a lens of the witness or observer
- Final practice of deepening breath to a count of four, sensing space around the body, and gentle head movements side to side

### Physical, Mental Challenges, Affects on Practice
- Participants with lower back concerns are offered a modification for returning from the forward bend — pressing hands into the back and rising as one unit rather than rolling up vertebra by vertebra
- Hyper-extension of the knees is flagged as something to avoid; softening the backs of the knees is specifically cued
- Breath-holding during movement is noted as a common unconscious habit — Jonathan directly calls attention to it during the shaking sequence
- Chronic shortening of chest muscles from prolonged sitting is identified as a physical concern addressed by the chest-opening posture
- Chronic lengthening of muscles along the back from slumping or prolonged sitting is also identified and addressed by the same posture
- The 75% capacity guideline is offered as a safeguard against strain or injury throughout all movements
