### Outline
### Mindfulness as an Integrative Quality Across Movement Practices

- Mindful movement is not a single defined practice but rather a quality of awareness that the practitioner brings to any form of movement, including yoga and Qigong.

- Brought intentionally, mindfulness can transform yoga, Qigong, and Neigong into deeply contemplative and health-supporting disciplines.

- These practices serve the practitioner's physical, emotional, and mental health and well-being when approached with mindful intention.

- The Dharma aspect of the practice is oriented toward unwinding and release, allowing the practitioner to move toward wholeness.

### Core Principles of Qigong Practice

- Relaxed intention is identified as the guiding principle of movement, with dynamic relaxation cultivated throughout each gesture.

- Softness and ease in practice are described as working more deeply into the fabric of the being than forceful effort would.

- The meditative and contemplative dimensions of practice are meant to inform every cell of the body, not merely the cognitive mind.

- Relaxing into gestures and movement opens the intelligence of the being, allowing it to resonate with the deeper fabric of the practice.

- All practices in relation to the spine and movement are to be performed by listening to the body first, without pushing against resistance or into pain.

- The principle of underdoing range of motion is emphasized as a means of staying present and avoiding tension in the joints and muscles.

### Standing Meditation Posture and Alignment

- The foundational standing posture aligns the body with the energy of the earth below and the sky above, a form present across all cultures of cultivation.

- Feet are kept relatively parallel to align energy through the body and support connection with the earth.

- The central column of the body opens through a gently lifted crown of the head, creating a natural spinal alignment.

- A light, gentle pulse or shake of the whole body is used to settle the system, relax the breath, and ease into the standing form.

- Between movement gestures, returning to the standing meditation posture maintains an unbroken quality of mindful presence in both stillness and motion.

### Lao Gong Activation and Hand Opening Exercises

- Bringing the fingertips and thumbs together activates the Lao Gong, the energetic center located in the palm of the hand.

- A light pressing of the palms toward one another stretches the backs of the hands, tendons, and ligaments while opening this important energetic gateway.

- Extending the hands to each side in the traditional gassho position gently opens the inner forearm and extends the upper forearm on the opposite side.

- Extending the hands forward and drawing them back opens the radial and ulnar lines of the forearms and hands respectively.

### Drawing Down the Heavens Practice

- Drawing Down the Heavens is described as a yang, activating, and accessible practice that grounds energy to the earth while opening the practitioner to the sky.

- Hands rise on the in-breath and descend with elbows leading straight down, releasing all the way to the earth on the exhalation.

- The speed of the gesture remains consistent and unhurried throughout the entire flow, without acceleration at any point.

- Palms face lightly inward as the elbows lead the chi down through the central column of the body during the descent.

### Drawing the Tai Chi and Wu Chi Circle

- Drawing the Tai Chi and Wu Chi circle originates from the Xingyi tradition of health practices and involves gently raising the arms without reaching upward.

- The upward movement of the hands corresponds to a deep in-breath, and the downward settling corresponds to the exhalation.

- A quality of mindful kindness is brought to the field of the arms, keeping the movement open and soft throughout.

- Reversing the direction of the hands on the downward phase completes a full circular cycle of the practice.

- For seated practitioners, the hands are modified to move in front of the body's center rather than in a full arc.

- The Wu Chi standing posture can be held with hands flowing softly to the sides, maintaining a sense of openness along the lateral planes of the body, or with one hand resting over the other below the navel.

### Full Spinal Breathing Practice

- Full spinal breathing opens the spine along both its front and back surfaces, from the base of the spine through the occiput and crown of the head.

- This practice is described as deeply nourishing to the spinal system, the nervous system, and the brain.

- Palms turn upward as the elbows draw straight back, lifting the heart, lungs, and sternum on the in-breath.

- On the exhalation, the spine spirals and gently rounds, with the tailbone settling downward and the chin drawing in.

- Gentleness in execution allows the practice to open more completely along the full interior length of the spine and spinal cord, extending into what is described as the rivers of light of the nervous system.

### Yin Yang Breathing Practice

- Yin Yang breathing, also referred to as opening and closing breath, is a practice designed to benefit the heart and lungs and release congestion in the center of the body.

- Hands extend outward to the sides on the in-breath, with a sense of roundness maintained from the shoulder through the elbow, wrist, and fingers, without locking the elbows.

- Spiraling the palms outward encourages the shoulder blades to smooth out across the back plane, releasing held tension in that area.

- On the exhalation, a gentle forward bow is taken with the chin drawing down, inviting a sense of roundness through the upper back.

- The shoulder joint remains relaxed throughout so that no tension develops in that area of the body.

### Microcosmic Orbit and Energetic Circuit

- Resting the tongue so that its flat upper surface lightly touches the upper palate is a practice shared by both yoga and Qigong traditions.

- This tongue position helps complete the energetic circuit known as the microcosmic orbit or small circulation.

- The microcosmic orbit describes the natural flow of prana and chi, the life force, moving through the body.

### The Concept of Dao Yin and the Three Elements of Practice

- In ancient times, the practices now referred to as Qigong and Neigong were called Dao Yin, meaning leading and guiding the chi.

- Dao Yin is not a top-down directive approach but rather a collaborative working with the practices to integrate intention, breath, and form.

- Intention is defined as the orientation toward essential well-being encompassing body, spirit, heart, mind, emotions, mental states, and consciousness.

- Breath practices connect with and animate the intention, while the skill of the form provides the third contributing element.

- Together, these three elements of intention, breath, and form contribute to a greater sense of wholeness and fullness in the practitioner.

- Modern understanding of bioelectrical energy in the body is acknowledged, while the subtle systems described by ancient yogis are noted as still slightly ahead of contemporary science.

### Seated Dao Yin Sequence Integrating the Three Practices

- A seated Dao Yin sequence weaves together full spinal breathing, yin yang breathing, and drawing down the heavens into a unified flow.

- This integrated sequence is described as a launch pad for contemplative practice, settling and relaxing the cognitive churning of the mind.

- Full spinal breathing initiates the sequence with palms up and elbows drawing back, performed more gently than in the standing form.

- Transitioning into yin yang breathing, the hands extend to the sides on the in-breath and round forward on the exhalation, smoothing the shoulder blades across the back.

- Drawing down the heavens completes the sequence, with palms pressing down along the left and right channels and then floating outward on the in-breath.

- The sequence concludes with hands resting on the knees or in a chosen meditation gesture, entering a state of pure allowing and essential mindful presence.

### Contemplative Meditation on the Elements

- Following the movement sequence, a period of seated stillness invites connection with the felt sense of the body's structure and elemental composition.

- Connecting with the bones, spine, skull, face, arms, and feet is presented as a meditation on the earth element, with bones understood as generated from the elements of the earth.

- The water element is then brought into awareness through the fluid systems present in every cell of the body, including the bones, felt as fluid movement within.

- Breath is identified as the animating energy through which chi moves into and out of the body, connecting the water element with the waves of respiration.

- The fire element is introduced as the warmth and intelligence of the radiant being, offering an opportunity to connect with the quality of being prior to conditioning.

- Turning the attention to presence itself, described as the Deep Witness or the awareness of awareness, concludes the meditative sequence.

### Detailed Subject Matter
**Mindful Movement**
- Mindfulness is not inherent to any single movement practice; it is a quality the practitioner brings
- Yoga, Qigong, and Neigong can all become mindful practices through the application of mindful guidance
- Mindful movement serves personal health and well-being, emotional and mental health
**Dharma and the Principle of Release**
- The Dharma aspect of practice centers on unwinding and release
- Relaxed intention is the guiding principle of movement
- Dynamic relaxation works deeper into the fabric of being for release, wholeness, and health
- Practices are easy to access but profound in depth when the meditative and contemplative side is engaged
- The intelligence of the body opens when one relaxes into gestures and movement — this is not a cognitive process
**Daoyin — Ancient Roots of Qigong**
- In ancient times, what is now called Qigong and energy practice was called Daoyin
- Daoyin means "leading and guiding the chi"
- It is not a top-down approach but works with intention, breath, and form to integrate essential well-being
- Well-being encompasses body, spirit, heart, mind, emotions, mental states, and consciousness
**Three Elements of Practice**
- Intention: essential well-being of body, spirit, heart, mind, emotions, mental states, and consciousness
- Breath: connected with the practices of breathwork
- Skill of form: the physical gestures and movements
- Together these three contribute to a greater sense of wholeness and fullness
**Standing Meditation Posture**
- Feet relatively parallel to align energy through the body and connect with the earth
- Central column opens through an open crown of the head
- Hands can rest one over the other below the navel in the field of the lower dantian
- Eyes soft, peripheral vision slightly open
- Tongue rests naturally with the flat upper surface touching the upper palate to complete the energetic circuit
**Lao Gong / Palm Chakra Activation**
- Fingertips and thumbs brought together with a light press
- Opens and stretches the backs of the hands, tendons, and ligaments
- Opens the energetic center in the palm, important for extending Qi and keeping life force flowing
**Drawing Down the Heavens**
- Hands rise and come down with elbows leading straight down
- Hands release down to the earth
- Described as a yang, activating, and beautiful practice
- Helps ground energy to the earth and opens to the sky
**Wu Chi / Tai Chi Circle Drawing**
- From the Xingyi tradition of health practices
- Arms come up gently, lubricating the shoulders
- Deep breath in as hands rise; exhale as they settle down
- Cultivates open, soft quality in the field of the arms
- Direction reverses in subsequent cycles
**Full Spinal Breathing**
- Palms turn up, elbows come straight back, heart and lungs lift, sternum opens on in-breath
- Exhale: spiral and gently round the spine, tailbone settles, chin comes in
- Done very gently to open completely along the whole length of the spine
- Nourishes the spinal system, nervous system, and brain
- Opens the spinal cord and the rivers of light of the nervous system
**Yin Yang Breathing / Opening and Closing Breath**
- Wonderful practice for the heart and lungs
- Releases congestion in the center of the body
- Hands extend out to the side, palms open and facing upward on in-breath
- Exhale into a gentle bow, chin down, shoulder blades smoothing out on the back plane
- Elbows not locked; roundness and openness maintained from shoulder through fingers
- Principle of underdoing range of motion to stay present
**Microcosmic Orbit**
- Tongue placement on the upper palate completes the energetic circuit
- Known in both yoga and Qigong as the microcosmic orbit or small circulation
- Supports natural flow of prana / chi / life force through the body
**Seated Modifications**

- Hands may come just in front of the center rather than full extension
