### Outline
### Opening Meditation and Grounding Practice

- Participants were welcomed into the session with an invitation to settle into stillness or engage in gentle movement such as stretching or walking meditation.

- Attention was directed toward the breath, inviting deep inhalations through the nose and exhalations through the mouth to calm the nervous system.

- Awareness was guided toward sensory experience, including sounds, smells, temperature on the skin, and other bodily sensations.

- The practice concluded by inviting participants to allow the breath to find its natural rhythm before gradually reintroducing awareness of the external environment.

### Session Intent and Pedagogical Approach

- The Mentor Leadership Team shared that the session title, Reflections from the Mentor Leadership Team, was chosen months in advance for its flexibility, as the team was uncertain what would feel most appropriate to offer at this stage of the program.

- The word "reflections" was selected intentionally to encompass both an inner quality of listening and the act of mirroring back to one another.

- Planning for the session began with a shared meditation among the mentor leaders, followed by aloud reflection on what mentees had found meaningful throughout the two-year program.

- Consideration was given to where participants would be in the arc of the day, having already engaged with several hours of rich content from multiple presenters.

- A decision was made to focus on dimensions of practice that are intuitive, embodied, nonlinear, and expressive, including movement, music, and celebration.

- The session was structured using a method called "parental teaching," in which content is offered first and then followed by an explanation of why it was offered, a technique considered especially useful when addressing experienced practitioners and teachers.

### Deka Poetry Practice

- Sulwazi Johnson introduced a form of poetry called deka, created by Dharma teacher Rachel Bagby, designed to help practitioners access their unique voice and distill experience into shareable wisdom.

- The word deka is derived from the Greek word for ten, and a deka poem consists of exactly ten syllables arranged across three lines, with two syllables in the first line, three in the second, and five in the third.

- Deka poems are considered most potent when created in community and are intended to be spoken aloud to another person in order to come fully to life.

- Examples of deka poems were shared, including "Knowledge / And acknowledge / Action coming together," "Thank you / For the smile / You bring out in me," and "Dear class / Finally / Continuation."

- Participants were sent into breakout rooms organized by last name for approximately ten minutes to introduce themselves to group partners and collaboratively create at least one deka poem.

- Upon returning to the main session, selected deka poems were harvested from the chat, including "We are / Radiant / Loving awareness," "Let go / Turn inward / What you seek is here," and "Up / Practice compassion / And loving kindness."

- The practice was framed as a meaningful way to mark a shared experience and give it poetic expression.

### Mahasati Hand Movement Meditation

- Deb Kerr introduced a hand movement meditation called Mahasati as a practice that can serve as an alternative to walking meditation and can be used anytime simple movement is desired as an anchor.

- The form can be practiced in a seated or lying down position and is described as a powerful method for calming the body and cultivating concentration.

- The movement sequence begins with the right hand turned on its outer edge, lifted, and placed on the belly, followed by the same action with the left hand.

- From the belly, the right hand slides up to the heart, opens out over the thigh, lowers, and returns palm-down to a resting position, with the left hand following the same path.

- Participants were encouraged to bring focused attention into the hands to feel sensations as they moved through the air and made contact with the body.

- An element of loving kindness was then layered into the practice by adding the whispered phrase "kind touch" each time the hands made contact with the body.

- Participants were invited to pause at the belly and hold themselves in kindness, and then to imagine radiating kindness outward from the heart as the hand moved away.

- The practice concluded with the loving kindness phrases "May we be safe, may we be peaceful, may we be happy, may we be free," extending care to the entire community.

### Freeform Movement Practice and Dance

- Ann Cushman introduced a freeform movement practice described as "moving from the inner impulse," inviting participants to feel the aliveness in the body and allow it to express itself through movement.

- This practice was framed as directly relevant to the role of a teacher, as it mirrors the process of sensing what is alive and juicy and surrendering to being moved by something larger than oneself.

- Participants were guided to begin in stillness and empty out, releasing the tendency to micromanage, control, or plan, and instead drop into the living, vibrating field of the body.

- Attention was directed to one hand, inviting awareness of tingling, temperature, and sensation, and then allowing the hand to move from the inside, beginning with subtle movements of the fingers, wrist, or a single finger.

- Movement was then invited to travel up the arm, through the shoulder, across the chest, down into the belly and hips, along the spine, and into the legs, with participants encouraged to linger wherever felt most alive.

- Participants were guided to trust the way life force energy moves through their unique body, exploring qualities of movement such as fast or slow, staccato or fluid.

- Following a return to stillness, music was introduced and participants were invited to join a virtual dance party on screen by clicking the raise hand button, which would allow their image to be shared as a panelist.

- The dance portion was intended to combine the inner impulse of movement with the outer stimulus of music, creating a sense of collective celebration and community.

- A faster piece of music was played for approximately two minutes to energize the movement, followed by a slower piece to guide participants back into stillness and reflection.

- After the dance, participants were invited to notice sensations, emotions, and qualities such as warmth, joy, and effervescence arising in the mind, body, and heart.

- An option was offered to place a hand on the belly or chest as a gesture of kind touch while resting in the presence of the global community gathered virtually.
