  From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia 1957 publication by Paul Reps
  **Zen Flesh, Zen Bones** is a 1957 publication by [Paul Reps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Reps) combining four separate texts on [nondual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonduality_(spirituality)) practice:
* [101 Zen Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonduality_(spirituality))
* [The Gateless Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonduality_(spirituality)) (Mumonkan)
* [Ten Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonduality_(spirituality))
* [Centering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonduality_(spirituality)) ([Vigyan Bhairav Tantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijnana_Bhairava_Tantra))

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zen_Flesh,_Zen_Bones&action=edit&section=1)]

##### 101 Zen Stories

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zen_Flesh,_Zen_Bones&action=edit&section=2)]
*101 Zen Stories* is a 1919 compilation of [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen)[koans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen)[\[ 1 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) including 19th and early 20th century [anecdotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anecdote) compiled by [Nyogen Senzaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyogen_Senzaki),[ 2 ] and a translation of Shasekishū,[ 1 ][ 3 ] written in the 13th century by [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) Zen master [Mujū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muj%C5%AB) (無住) (literally, "non-dweller").[ 3 ] The book was reprinted by [Paul Reps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Reps) as part of *Zen Flesh, Zen Bones* .[ 3 ][ 4 ] Well-known koans in the collection include *A Cup of Tea* (1), *The Sound of One Hand* (21), *No Water, No Moon* (29), and *Everything is Best* (31).

##### Gateless Gate

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zen_Flesh,_Zen_Bones&action=edit&section=3)]
*The Gateless Gate* ([Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Mandarin): 無門關 *Wúménguān* ; [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language): 無門関 *Mumonkan* ), more accurately translated as *The Gateless Barrier* , is a collection of 48 [Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) ([Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen)) [koans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koan) compiled in the early 13th century by the Chinese Zen master [Wumen Huikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wumen_Huikai) (無門慧開; Japanese: *Mumon Ekai* ; 1183–1260). Wumen's preface indicates that the volume was published in 1228. Each koan is accompanied by a commentary and verse by Wumen.

##### Ten Bulls

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zen_Flesh,_Zen_Bones&action=edit&section=4)]
*Ten Bulls* or *Ten Ox Herding Pictures* (十牛; [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language): *jūgyū* , [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): *shíniú* ) is a series of short poems and accompanying pictures used in the [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) tradition to illustrate the stages of a practitioner's progression towards the purification of the mind and [enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi),[ web 1 ] as well as his or her subsequent return into the world while acting out of wisdom.
An equivalent series of stages is depicted in the [Nine Stages of Tranquility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samatha#Nine_mental_abidings),[ web 2 ] used in the [Mahamudra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamudra) tradition, in which the mind is represented by an elephant and a monkey.[ web 3 ][ web 4 ] This formulation originates with [Asaṅga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asanga) (4th CE), delineating the nine mental abidings in his *Abhidharmasamuccaya* and the *Śrāvakabhūmi* chapter of his Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra. It is also found in the Mahāyānasūtrālaṅkāra of [Maitreyanātha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitreyan%C4%81tha), which shows considerable similarity in arrangement and content to the Bodhisattva-bhūmi-śāstra.[ note 1 ] The *Dharma Fellowship'* , a [Kagyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagyu) (Mahamudra) organisation, notes that the practice starts with studying and pondering the dharma, where-after the practice of meditation commences.[ web 5 ]

##### Vigyan Bhairav Tantra

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zen_Flesh,_Zen_Bones&action=edit&section=5)]
The Vigyan Bhairav Tantra is a chapter from the *Rudrayamala Tantra* . It was introduced to the west by [Paul Reps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Reps), a student of [Lakshman Joo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshman_Joo). Reps brought the text to wider attention by including an English translation in his popular book *Zen Flesh, Zen Bones* . Cast as a discourse between the god [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) and his consort [Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi) or [Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakti), it briefly presents 112 meditation methods or centering techniques (dharanas).[ 5 ]
