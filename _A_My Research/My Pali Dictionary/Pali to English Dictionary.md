Anālayo, Ajahn Sucitto, Ajahn Pasanno, Ajahn Brahm**, and the classical Pāli Canon.

## A

### **Abhidhamma** *(Skt: Abhidharma)*
Tags: #abhidhamma #canon---philosophy #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Higher Teaching / Ultimate Doctrine**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Higher or further (abhi) doctrine (dhamma)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Canon / Philosophy`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The third basket (Piṭaka) of the Theravada Buddhist Canon, comprising a meticulous philosophical and psychological analysis of mind, consciousness (citta), mental factors (cetasika), matter (rūpa), and liberation (nibbāna).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Distinguishes between conventional reality (vohāra-sacca) and ultimate reality (paramattha-sacca). Often referenced in mapping the 52 cetasikas and mind-moments.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Dhamma`, `Citta`, `Cetasika`, `Paramattha`

---

### **Adhiṭṭhāna** *(Skt: Adhiṣṭhāna)*
Tags: #adhi--h-na #p-ram----practice #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Determination / Resolute Resolve**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Standing firm, establishing, foundation*
* **Category / Domain:** `Pāramī / Practice`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** One of the Ten Perfections (pāramī). An unshakeable, resolute decision and commitment to stay on the path of Dhamma, endure hardships, and fulfill spiritual goals regardless of obstacles.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah and Ajahn Sumedho emphasize adhiṭṭhāna during arduous monastic training—such as undertaking sitting through the night (all-night vigil) or adhering strictly to the Vinaya.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Pāramī`, `Viriya`, `Khanti`, `Sacca`

---

### **Ajahn** *(Skt: Ācārya)*
Tags: #ajahn #monastic-title #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Teacher / Mentor (Thai / Pali)**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Master, spiritual guide, preceptor (Pali: Ācariya)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Monastic Title`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** In the Thai Forest Tradition, a title of respect given to a senior monk who has spent at least ten rains retreats (vassa) in the robe and is qualified to guide students and train junior monastics.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Used for venerated lineage masters such as Ajahn Chah, Ajahn Sumedho, Ajahn Amaro, Ajahn Pasanno, Ajahn Sucitto, Ajahn Brahm, and Ajahn Lee.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Ācariya`, `Thera`, `Luang Por`, `Kalyāṇamitta`

---

### **Akusala** *(Skt: Akuśala)*
Tags: #akusala #ethics---psychology #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Unwholesome / Unskillful**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Not (a-) skillful, healthy, or intelligent (kusala)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Ethics / Psychology`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** Mental states, intentions, actions, and speech that are rooted in the three unwholesome roots: greed/attachment (lobha), aversion/hatred (dosa), and delusion/ignorance (moha). These states cause suffering and obstruct awakening.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Mindfulness practice observes akusala states without judgment or guilt, seeing them as impermanent mental conditions to be understood and released.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Kusala`, `Lobha`, `Dosa`, `Moha`, `Kamma`

---

### **Anattā** *(Skt: Anātman)*
Tags: #anatt #three-characteristics--tilakkha-a #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Non-Self / Not-Self / Impersonality**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Not (an-) self / soul (attā)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Three Characteristics (Tilakkhaṇa)`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The core Buddhist insight that all physical and mental phenomena (the five aggregates) are devoid of an enduring, autonomous, permanent ego, soul, or core entity. Things arise dependent on conditions, perform functions, and pass away without an owner.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Sumedho emphasizes that noticing 'I am angry' vs 'There is anger' shifts awareness from identity to anattā. Ajahn Chah asked, 'If this body is yours, why can't you command it not to age or get sick?'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Tilakkhaṇa`, `Anicca`, `Dukkha`, `Khandha`, `Sakkāyadiṭṭhi`

---

### **Anicca** *(Skt: Anitya)*
Tags: #anicca #three-characteristics--tilakkha-a #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Impermanence / Inconstancy**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Not (an-) continuous or permanent (nicca)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Three Characteristics (Tilakkhaṇa)`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The universal truth that all conditioned phenomena (saṅkhārā)—physical sensations, emotions, thoughts, perceptions, and outer structures—are in a constant state of flux, arising, changing, and passing away.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah called anicca 'the standard of the Dhamma'. His famous phrase 'Mai Nae' (Thai for 'It's not sure, it's uncertain') is the direct contemplation of anicca in daily monastic life.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Tilakkhaṇa`, `Anattā`, `Dukkha`, `Vaya-dhamma`, `Udayabbaya`

---

### **Anusaya** *(Skt: Anuśaya)*
Tags: #anusaya #defilements---psychology #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Latent Tendencies / Underlying Biases**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Lying dormant (anu-śī)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Defilements / Psychology`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The seven deep-seated, dormant defilements that sleep in the subconscious mind until triggered by sensory contact: sensual passion (kāmarāga), aversion (paṭigha), views (diṭṭhi), doubt (vicikicchā), conceit (māna), desire for existence (bhavarāga), and ignorance (avijjā).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Meditation purifies not merely surface thoughts, but uproots the dormant anusayas that generate repetitive karmic reactivity.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Kilesa`, `Āsava`, `Saṁyojana`, `Taṇhā`, `Avijjā`

---

### **Anāgāmī** *(Skt: Anāgāmin)*
Tags: #an-g-m #stages-of-awakening #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Non-Returner**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *One who does not return (an-āgāmin)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Stages of Awakening`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The third of the four stages of enlightenment (ariya-puggala). The non-returner has eradicated the first five lower fetters (saṁyojana), including sensual craving (kāmarāga) and ill-will (paṭigha). Upon physical death, an anāgāmī is reborn in the Pure Abodes (Suddhāvāsa) and attains Nibbāna there.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Represents the total transcendence of sensual desire and anger.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Sotāpanna`, `Sakadāgāmī`, `Arahant`, `Saṁyojana`

---

### **Appamāda** *(Skt: Apramāda)*
Tags: #appam-da #core-virtue #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Heedfulness / Diligence / Vigilance**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Non-intoxication, non-negligence (a-pamāda)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Virtue`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** Unflagging alertness, moral care, and earnest diligence in cultivating wholesome states and abandoning unwholesome ones. The Buddha declared appamāda to be the root of all wholesome qualities, and his dying words were: 'Vayadhammā saṅkhārā appamādena sampādetha' (All conditioned things are subject to decay; strive on with diligence).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah and Bhikkhu Bodhi translate this as being wide awake to the present consequences of our actions, guarding the mind as a mother guards her only child.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Sati`, `Viriya`, `Sampajañña`, `Kusala`

---

### **Arahant** *(Skt: Arhat)*
Tags: #arahant #stages-of-awakening #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Worthy One / Fully Enlightened Being**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *One who is worthy (arahati) of homage, offerings, and reverence; one who has slain the defilements*
* **Category / Domain:** `Stages of Awakening`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** A person who has eradicated all ten fetters (saṁyojana), completely destroyed all defilements (āsavas), attained full Nibbāna in this life, and ended the cycle of rebirth (saṁsāra).
* **Context from the Teachers:** The highest spiritual archetype in early Buddhism, exemplified by the historic Buddha's chief disciples and the revered masters of the Thai Forest Tradition (such as Ajahn Mun Bhuridatto).
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Nibbāna`, `Āsavakkhaya`, `Sotāpanna`, `Sakadāgāmī`, `Anāgāmī`

---

### **Ariya** *(Skt: Ārya)*
Tags: #ariya #doctrinal-term #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Noble / Spiritual Aristocrat**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Noble, exalted, honourable, pure*
* **Category / Domain:** `Doctrinal Term`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** That which is spiritually noble, pure, and leading to liberation. Applied to the Four Noble Truths (Ariya-saccāni), the Noble Eightfold Path (Ariya-aṭṭhaṅgika-magga), and Noble Disciples (Ariya-sāvaka) who have entered the stream of awakening.
* **Context from the Teachers:** In Buddhism, nobility is defined not by birth or social class, but by spiritual purity, ethical conduct (sīla), and wisdom (paññā).
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Ariyasacca`, `Aṭṭhaṅgikamagga`, `Ariya-puggala`, `Sotāpanna`

---

### **Ariyasacca** *(Skt: Āryasatya)*
Tags: #ariyasacca #core-doctrine #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **The Four Noble Truths**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Noble (ariya) truths (sacca)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Doctrine`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The fundamental framework of the Buddha's teaching: (1) Dukkha: The truth of suffering/unsatisfactoriness; (2) Samudaya: The origin of suffering in craving (taṇhā); (3) Nirodha: The cessation of suffering in Nibbāna; (4) Magga: The Noble Eightfold Path leading to the cessation of suffering.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Sumedho's seminal work 'The Four Noble Truths' outlines the 3 turnings and 12 aspects of the Four Noble Truths as an empirical guide for investigating daily life.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Dukkha`, `Samudaya`, `Nirodha`, `Magga`, `Taṇhā`

---

### **Attā** *(Skt: Ātman)*
Tags: #att #philosophy #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Self / Soul / Ego**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Self, soul, individual identity*
* **Category / Domain:** `Philosophy`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The conventional concept of an individual self or ego. In classical Vedic and Brahmanical thought, the Ātman was believed to be an eternal, unchanging essence. The Buddha declared all phenomena to be not-self (anattā).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Buddhism accepts self (attā) as a useful conventional designation for everyday communication, but denies its ultimate existence.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Anattā`, `Sakkāyadiṭṭhi`, `Māna`, `Ahamkāra`

---

### **Avijjā** *(Skt: Avidyā)*
Tags: #avijj #core-defilement---dependent-origination #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Ignorance / Delusion / Spiritual Blindness**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Not (a-) seeing, knowing, or understanding (vijjā)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Defilement / Dependent Origination`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The fundamental lack of true insight into the Four Noble Truths, the Three Characteristics, and Dependent Origination. It is the primary root of all suffering and the first link (nidāna) in the chain of Dependent Origination (Paṭiccasamuppāda).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah: 'Ignorance is simply not knowing things as they actually are—taking the impermanent to be permanent, the painful to be pleasant, and the non-self to be a self.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Moha`, `Paṭiccasamuppāda`, `Taṇhā`, `Vijjā`, `Kilesa`

---

### **Aṭṭhaṅgika-magga** *(Skt: Aṣṭāṅgamārga)*
Tags: #a--ha-gika-magga #core-doctrine---practice #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Noble Eightfold Path**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Eight (aṭṭha) fold/limbed (aṅga) path (magga)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Doctrine / Practice`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The Fourth Noble Truth. The middle way that avoids the extremes of sensual indulgence and self-mortification. Divided into three trainings: Wisdom (Right View, Right Intention), Ethics (Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood), and Meditation (Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah taught that the eight factors of the path are not sequential steps, but come together in a single unified moment of awareness: 'When the path comes together, it's like eight strands of rope braided into one.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Magga`, `Sammādiṭṭhi`, `Sīla`, `Samādhi`, `Paññā`

## B

### **Bhavana** *(Skt: Bhāvanā)*
Tags: #bhavana #practice #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Mental Cultivation / Meditation / Spiritual Development**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Causing to become, bringing into being, cultivation (bhāveti)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Practice`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The systematic development of wholesome qualities of mind through meditation. Divided traditionally into Samatha-bhāvanā (cultivation of serenity and concentration) and Vipassanā-bhāvanā (cultivation of wisdom and insight).
* **Context from the Teachers:** In Thai Forest teachings, bhāvanā encompasses not just formal sitting on a cushion, but 24/7 internal cultivation while sweeping, walking, eating, and interacting.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Samatha`, `Vipassanā`, `Sati`, `Samādhi`, `Citta`

### **Bhikkhu** *(Skt: Bhikṣu)*
Tags: #bhikkhu #monasticism---sa-gha #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Monk / Fully Ordained Alms-gatherer**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *One who shares or lives on alms (bhikkhati)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Monasticism / Saṅgha`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** A male who has taken higher ordination (upasampadā) in the Theravada Buddhist monastic order, committing to live by the 227 rules of the Pāṭimokkha (Vinaya).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Lives a life of voluntary simplicity, celibacy, poverty, and dedication to meditation and communal service.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Bhikkhunī`, `Saṅgha`, `Vinaya`, `Pāṭimokkha`, `Sāmaṇera`

---

### **Bhikkhunī** *(Skt: Bhikṣuṇī)*
Tags: #bhikkhun #monasticism---sa-gha #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Nun / Fully Ordained Female Monastic**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Female alms-gatherer*
* **Category / Domain:** `Monasticism / Saṅgha`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** A fully ordained female monastic in the Buddhist tradition, observing the rules of the Bhikkhunī Pāṭimokkha (311 rules in Theravada).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Founded by the Buddha at the request of Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī and Venerable Ānanda; revitalized in modern Theravada scholarship and practice.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Bhikkhu`, `Saṅgha`, `Vinaya`, `Siladhara`

---

### **Bojjhaṅga** *(Skt: Bodhyanga)*
Tags: #bojjha-ga #core-doctrine---meditation #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Seven Factors of Enlightenment**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Factors (aṅga) of awakening / enlightenment (bodhi)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Doctrine / Meditation`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The seven mental qualities that, when balanced and cultivated, lead directly to Nibbāna: (1) Sati (Mindfulness), (2) Dhammavicaya (Investigation of Dhamma), (3) Viriya (Energy/Effort), (4) Pīti (Joy/Rapture), (5) Passaddhi (Tranquility), (6) Samādhi (Concentration), (7) Upekkhā (Equanimity).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Brahm and Ven. Analayo highlight the Bojjhaṅgas as the engine of awakening—the activating factors (Investigation, Energy, Joy) balancing the calming factors (Tranquility, Concentration, Equanimity), with Mindfulness overseeing all.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Bodhi`, `Sati`, `Dhammavicaya`, `Viriya`, `Pīti`, `Passaddhi`, `Samādhi`, `Upekkhā`

---

### **Brahmavihāra** *(Skt: Brahmavihāra)*
Tags: #brahmavih-ra #heart-practices---meditation #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Four Sublime Abodes / Immeasurables**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Divine / noble (brahma) abodes / dwellings (vihāra)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Heart Practices / Meditation`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The four boundless virtues of the enlightened heart: (1) Mettā (Loving-kindness / Benevolence), (2) Karuṇā (Compassion for suffering), (3) Muditā (Sympathetic / Altruistic Joy), (4) Upekkhā (Equanimity / Impartiality). Radiated in all directions to all living beings without boundary.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Amaro, Ajahn Sucitto, and Ajahn Pasanno teach Brahmavihāras as the direct antidote to fear, resentment, and cynicism, opening the heart into unconditioned peace.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Mettā`, `Karuṇā`, `Muditā`, `Upekkhā`, `Appamañña`

---

### **Buddho** *(Skt: Buddha)*
Tags: #buddho #meditation-mantra---thai-forest #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **The One Who Knows / The Awake**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Awakened, enlightened; knowing*
* **Category / Domain:** `Meditation Mantra / Thai Forest`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** In the Thai Forest Tradition of Ajahn Mun and Ajahn Chah, 'Buddho' is the primary meditation word (parikamma) used to anchor the mind to the breath (Bud- on in-breath, -dho on out-breath). It also refers to the innate quality of pure, unconditioned knowing presence within the heart (citta).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah: 'Buddho means the one who knows. When you repeat Buddho, you are calling upon and resting in the knowing heart.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Buddha`, `Citta`, `Sati`, `Ānāpānasati`, `Knowing Heart`

## C

### **Cankama** *(Skt: Caṅkramaṇa)*
Tags: #cankama #meditation---monastic-practice #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Walking Meditation**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Walking up and down a path, pacing*
* **Category / Domain:** `Meditation / Monastic Practice`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** Formal walking meditation practiced along a straight path (usually 20 to 30 paces). Cultivates continuous mindfulness (sati) and clear comprehension (sampajañña) in the moving body.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah and Ajahn Sumedho stressed alternating equal periods of sitting and walking meditation to balance physical energy and prevent sleepiness.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Sati`, `Sampajañña`, `Kāyagatāsati`, `Iriyāpatha`

---

### **Cetasika** *(Skt: Caitasika)*
Tags: #cetasika #abhidhamma---psychology #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Mental Factors / Mental Concomitants**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Belonging to the mind (ceto / citta)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Abhidhamma / Psychology`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The 52 mental qualities that arise and pass away in conjunction with consciousness (citta). Classified into 7 universal, 6 occasional, 14 unwholesome (greed, anger, conceit, etc.), and 25 beautiful/wholesome factors (mindfulness, faith, non-greed, equanimity, wisdom).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Cetasikas are the fleeting emotional textures that color consciousness; pure awareness itself is distinct from the cetasikas arising within it.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Citta`, `Abhidhamma`, `Kilesa`, `Kusala`, `Sati`

---

### **Citta** *(Skt: Citta)*
Tags: #citta #core-psychology---thai-forest #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Mind / Heart / Consciousness / Core Awareness**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *That which is conscious, that which thinks or knows; the heart-mind*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Psychology / Thai Forest`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The seat of awareness, emotion, and knowing in Buddhist psychology. In the Thai Forest Tradition, often translated as 'heart-mind'. In its conditioned state, it is soiled by visiting defilements (kilesas); in its radiant, purified state (pabhassara citta), it is liberated from all attachments.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Maha Boowa and Ajahn Chah speak of the citta as that which knows the passing flow of thoughts, memories, and sensations: 'Thoughts are not the mind; they are visitors that come and go in the citta.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Cetasika`, `Mano`, `Viññāṇa`, `Buddho`, `Pabhassara Citta`

## D

### **Dana** *(Skt: Dāna)*
Tags: #dana #ethics---p-ram #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Generosity / Giving / Almsgiving**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Giving, gift, liberality*
* **Category / Domain:** `Ethics / Pāramī`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The practice of open-handed generosity without expecting a return. The first of the Ten Perfections (pāramī) and the initial step on the Buddhist spiritual path. Destroys stinginess (macchariya) and prepares the mind for concentration and wisdom.
* **Context from the Teachers:** In Thai monastic culture, the mutual relationship between lay supporters offering food/shelter and monastics offering Dhamma exemplifies pure dāna.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Pāramī`, `Cāga`, `Punya`, `Macchariya`, `Sīla`

---

### **Defilements** *(Skt: Kleśa)*
Tags: #defilements #psychology---defilements #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Kilesa (Defilements / Mental Poisons)**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *That which stains, torments, or soils*
* **Category / Domain:** `Psychology / Defilements`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** See Kilesa. Mental impurities that cloud the natural clarity of awareness and drive unwholesome karma.
* **Context from the Teachers:** The work of meditation is not to battle defilements with aggression, but to see their impermanent nature and let them drop.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Kilesa`, `Lobha`, `Dosa`, `Moha`, `Āsava`, `Anusaya`

---

### **Dependent Origination** *(Skt: Pratītyasamutpāda)*
Tags: #dependent-origination #core-doctrine #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Paṭiccasamuppāda (Dependent Co-Arising)**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Arising (samuppāda) dependent upon (paṭicca)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Doctrine`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** See Paṭiccasamuppāda. The twelve-linked chain describing how ignorance (avijjā) gives rise to mental formations, consciousness, mind-and-matter, six senses, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and the entire mass of suffering.
* **Context from the Teachers:** The Buddha said: 'Whoever sees Dependent Origination sees the Dhamma; whoever sees the Dhamma sees Dependent Origination.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Paṭiccasamuppāda`, `Idappaccayatā`, `Avijjā`, `Taṇhā`, `Dukkha`

---

### **Dhamma** *(Skt: Dharma)*
Tags: #dhamma #triple-gem---core-concept #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Truth / Natural Law / Teaching of the Buddha / Phenomena**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *That which upholds or sustains (dhar); truth; the way things are*
* **Category / Domain:** `Triple Gem / Core Concept`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** A multi-layered term in Buddhism meaning: (1) The ultimate Truth or Law of Nature; (2) The teachings, discourses, and doctrine of the Buddha; (3) All conditioned and unconditioned phenomena/things (dhammā); (4) Moral duty and righteousness.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah defined Dhamma as 'the way things are'—nature itself in its raw, unfolding reality. When we align with how nature is, suffering ceases.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Buddha`, `Saṅgha`, `Dhammacakka`, `Tilakkhaṇa`, `Yathābhūta`

---

### **Dhammavicaya** *(Skt: Dharmapravicaya)*
Tags: #dhammavicaya #seven-factors-of-enlightenment--bojjha-ga #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Investigation of Dhamma / Mindful Inquiry**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Investigation, discrimination, or deep scrutiny (vicaya) of phenomena/teachings (dhamma)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Seven Factors of Enlightenment (Bojjhaṅga)`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The second factor of enlightenment. The active, intelligent scrutiny of experience—investigating whether mental states are wholesome or unwholesome, examining how phenomena arise and cease, and discerning the three characteristics (anicca, dukkha, anattā).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Sumedho refers to this as 'inner investigation'—the curious question 'What is this?' that looks into emotion and physical sensations without bias.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Bojjhaṅga`, `Sati`, `Paññā`, `Yoniso Manasikāra`, `Vipassanā`

---

### **Dosa** *(Skt: Dveṣa)*
Tags: #dosa #three-unwholesome-roots--akusala-m-la #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Aversion / Hatred / Anger / Ill-Will**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Corrupting, burning, hurting, fault*
* **Category / Domain:** `Three Unwholesome Roots (Akusala-mūla)`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** One of the three primary poisons of the mind. Ranges from mild annoyance, irritation, impatience, and boredom to burning rage, hatred, resentment, and cruelty. It pushes away reality and desires destruction.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Antidoted by Loving-kindness (mettā) and Compassion (karuṇā). Ajahn Chah taught: 'When anger arises, don't follow it and don't fight it. Just watch it burn itself out.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Lobha`, `Moha`, `Vyāpāda`, `Paṭigha`, `Mettā`

### **Dukkha** *(Skt: Duḥkha)*
Tags: #dukkha #four-noble-truths---three-characteristics #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Suffering / Unsatisfactoriness / Stress / Friction**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Difficult to bear (du-kham), poorly fitting axle-hole (friction, bumpiness)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Four Noble Truths / Three Characteristics`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The First Noble Truth. Encompasses all forms of physical pain, emotional grief, distress, dissatisfaction, and the existential friction of conditioned existence that fails to provide permanent security. Divided into: (1) Dukkha-dukkhatā (obvious physical/mental pain), (2) Vipariṇāma-dukkhatā (suffering due to change/impermanence), (3) Saṅkhāra-dukkhatā (pervasive unsatisfactoriness of conditioned formations).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Sumedho: 'Dukkha is the noble truth to be understood, not a personal flaw to be ashamed of.'
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Ariyasacca`, `Anicca`, `Anattā`, `Taṇhā`, `Nirodha`

* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Doubt, uncertainty, wavering, perplexity*
* **Category / Domain:** `Five Hindrances / Ten Fetters`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The fifth hindrance and second fetter. An agitated, wavering state of mental uncertainty regarding the path, the teachings, one's teacher, or one's own capacity to practice. Paralyzes spiritual progress by questioning endlessly without practicing.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah compared doubt to standing at a crossroad arguing about which road to take rather than taking a step. Overcome through wise reflection, inquiry (dhammavicaya), and sustained practice.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Nīvaraṇa`, `Saṁyojana`, `Saddhā`, `Dhammavicaya`, `Sotāpanna`

### **Vinaya** *(Skt: Vinaya)*
Tags: #vinaya #monasticism---canon #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Monastic Discipline / Code of Conduct**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Leading out, removing (defilements), training, discipline (vi-nī)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Monasticism / Canon`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The first basket of the Tipiṭaka (Vinaya Piṭaka). The ethical discipline, procedural guidelines, and behavioral rules established by the Buddha for Buddhist monks and nuns to support harmony, simplicity, and liberation.
* **Context from the Teachers:** In the Thai Forest Tradition, strict adherence to the Vinaya is regarded not as legalism, but as a living meditation cushion—a container that purifies bodily and verbal actions.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Bhikkhu`, `Bhikkhunī`, `Pāṭimokkha`, `Sīla`, `Saṅgha`, `Tipiṭaka`

### **Vipassanā** *(Skt: Vipaśyanā)*
Tags: #vipassan #meditation---core-practice #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Insight Meditation / Clear Seeing**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Seeing in a special way, clear seeing, penetrating vision (vi-passati)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Meditation / Core Practice`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The direct meditative insight into the true nature of reality—specifically seeing all physical and mental phenomena as impermanent (anicca), unsatisfactory (dukkha), and not-self (anattā). Disenchants the mind from clinging and leads to Nibbāna.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Chah and Ajahn Amaro teach that vipassanā is not a dry intellectual exercise, but the heart's direct realization of the unburdened nature of reality.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Samatha`, `Paññā`, `Tilakkhaṇa`, `Satipaṭṭhāna`, `Anattā`

* **Doctrinal Definition:** The natural, inevitable fruition and psychological result of intentional actions (kamma). Wholesome intentions mature as peace and pleasant experience; unwholesome intentions mature as suffering.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Understanding vipāka brings profound acceptance of life's pleasant and unpleasant circumstances without blame or bitterness.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Kamma`, `Cetanā`, `Kusala`, `Akusala`, `Saṁsāra`

* **Category / Domain:** `Five Faculties / Seven Factors / Eightfold Path`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The unflagging vigor, courage, and persistent application of energy in abandoning unwholesome states and cultivating wholesome states. Present in Right Effort (Sammā-vāyāma), the Seven Factors of Enlightenment, and the Ten Perfections.

* **Doctrinal Definition:** The progressive fading away of feverish passion, craving, and clinging as wisdom reveals the unsatisfactoriness and impermanence of conditioned things. A direct synonym for and pathway to Nibbāna.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Not cold indifference or depression, but the profound cooling of spiritual freedom where the heart is no longer addicted to sensory highs.

### **Yoniso Manasikāra** *(Skt: Yoniśo Manaskāra)*
Tags: #yoniso-manasik-ra #core-practice---wisdom #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Wise Attention / Appropriate Reflection / Radical Inquiry**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Attending (manasikāra) right from the origin/source (yoni)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Core Practice / Wisdom`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The mental faculty of directing attention deeply to the root nature of experience—attending to impermanence rather than permanent stories, attending to not-self rather than ego dramas. The Buddha declared yoniso manasikāra to be the primary internal condition for the arising of Right View and Awakening.
* **Context from the Teachers:** Ajahn Sumedho and Ajahn Sucitto define this as 'appropriate attention'—looking at what is arising not to judge it, but to understand its conditions and nature.

### **Ānāpānasati** *(Skt: Ānāpānasmṛti)*
Tags: #n-p-nasati #meditation---core-practice #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Mindfulness of Breathing**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *Mindfulness (sati) of in-and-out breathing (āna-apāna)*
* **Category / Domain:** `Meditation / Core Practice`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** The foundational meditation practice taught systematically by the Buddha in the Ānāpānasati Sutta (MN 118). ### **Āsava** *(Skt: Āśrava)*
Tags: #sava #defilements #pali-dictionary

* **Primary Translation:** **Inflows / Taints / Fermentations / Cankers**
* **Literal Root Meaning:** *That which flows in or leaks out; intoxicating fermented brew*
* **Category / Domain:** `Defilements`
* **Doctrinal Definition:** Deep, intoxicating pollutants of the mind that sustain the cycle of rebirth: (1) Kāmāsava (taint of sensuality), (2) Bhavāsava (taint of becoming/existence), (3) Diṭṭhāsava (taint of dogmatic views), (4) Avijjāsava (taint of ignorance). Their total destruction is the definition of Arahantship (āsavakkhaya).
* **Context from the Teachers:** Bhikkhu Bodhi translates āsava as 'taints', Thanissaro Bhikkhu as 'effluents/fermentations', and Ajahn Amaro as 'outpourings'.
* **Related Pāli Terms:** `Avijjā`, `Kilesa`, `Anusaya`, `Arahant`, `Nibbāna`
