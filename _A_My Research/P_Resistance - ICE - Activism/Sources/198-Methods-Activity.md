##### Inspired by Gene Sharp’s 198 Methods of Nonviolent Action, this dynamic resource provides the framework for facilitators, educators, and community members to lead an experiential activity that helps participants get to know one another, learn about different nonviolent strategies, and connect nonviolence theory to real world examples.

##### Duration: 20-120 minutes Group Size: 2-200

##### 32 unique, double-sided handouts. The front of each handout contains the following:

##### - 1 of 32 categories of nonviolent action as identified in Gene Sharp’s 198 Nonviolent Methods. - List of specific methods that fall under the respective category - Real-world example of one of the methods - Discussion question

##### The backside of each handout features a uniform checklist of the 32 categories. As participants talk with each other and learn about different nonviolent actions, they can check off each of the 32 categories.

##### Contents:

##### About:

##### Objectives: -Participants will teach other group members about the method(s) of nonviolent action that has been assigned to them. -Participants will connect each method of nonviolent action to at least one real-world example.

# 198 METHODS OF NONVIOLENT ACTION

##### Instructions:

##### 1. Hand out 1, double-sided nonviolent methods handout to each participant in the group.

##### Adaptations: Speed Dating Format -- After giving each participant a nonviolent methods handout, place them into concentric circles facing each other. Give the outside circle 2 minutes to explain their nonviolent method to their partner and discuss to find real-world examples. After two minutes have passed, allow the other partner to do the same. When both partners have finished teaching, ask the outside circle to move one partner to the left and repeat.

##### Walking Workshop -- This workshop also works well when a large group is walking from one destination to the next as part of a memorial march or protest. After giving each participant a nonviolent methods handout, ask them to mingle with other participants so they can teach and learn while they walk to their final destination. Encourage participants to try to check off as many boxes on the back side of their handout as they can by talking to as many other participants as possible during the walk.

##### MK Gandhi Institute for Nonviolence | Rochester, NY | www.gandhiinstitute.org

##### 4. At the end of the 15 minutes, bring the group together and lead a group discussion to reflect on the activity. What did they learn? What surprised them? What questions do they have?

##### 3. When group members have finished reading their handout, give them 15 minutes to stand up and mingle with each other. During this time they should explain their nonviolent methods and answer the discussion question with at least three other people. In the process of teaching, they will also be learning about at least 3 other categories of nonviolent action.

##### 2. Give each person time to read their handout since they will be responsible for explaining it to others in the group.
