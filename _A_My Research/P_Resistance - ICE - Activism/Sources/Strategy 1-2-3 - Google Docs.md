*A starter-kit for strategic planning.*
A DISCLAIMER FOR THE DISRUPTIVE This card deck is not a substitute for a full-fledged strategic planning process. But *you can use it to begin to craft a winning strategy, by stepping through the 3* Strategy Cards, and also connecting with Beautiful Trouble for live support and trainings at beautifultrouble.org/training .
Choose your own adventure. Depending on how much prep your group has already done together and how much time you have for planning, you will start playing in different places. If your group has been around for a while, you may have already completed the initial steps below. If your group is just forming, you’ll likely want to take it from the top.
MATERIALS Large blank paper or flip-chart • Pens & paper • Cards
STRATEGY PYRAMID

1. Draw a Strategy Planning Pyramid (if possible on a large piece of blank paper). *Refer to image on Strategy Card 1 game play card.*
2. Fill in the top two levels:
   Vision : What future do you want? Be big, bold, and solution-oriented. (Instead of saying “Stop fracking!”, say “A 100% renewable energy future.”) Values : List the underlying values that can guide your organizing work. Then, go through the Principle and Theory cards to provoke ideas, and consider whether there’s anything else you’d like to add. Mission : How will your group’s purpose enact your vision? What is your group’s unique role (and core commitment) in helping to solve the problem?
   ANALYSIS AND GOALS
   Now it’s time to analyze what you’re up against and where your path to victory may lie.
3. Review these Methodology Cards:
   *Pillars of Power • Power Mapping • Spectrum of Allies • Narrative Power Analysis • Onion Tool • SWOT*
4. Choose the Methodologies most relevant to your campaign. If you only have time to do one assessment, do SWOT .
5. Assess. Follow each Methodology’s pop-up code to get in-depth how-to instructions; complete each analysis, using your Vision, Values & Mission as guides.
6. Identify your goals based on your findings above. What does winning look *like? To increase the likelihood of success, use the SMART Objectives Methodology to make sure your goals are: S pecific, M easurable , Achievable ,* R elevant, & T ime-bound.
   PLAN YOUR CAMPAIGN
   At this stage, many groups will go on to plan a roadmap toward victory that will often include a multi-pronged series of campaigns and actions. Your SMARTT goals guide the steps of your campaign (for example: build a group; write letters; have meetings; do a sit-in…) Since a target rarely gives in after one event, it is necessary to strategically escalate step-by-step, drawing upon a diverse mix of tactics. Each new action re-emphasizes the larger demand, builds your strength to take the next escalated step, and reminds the target that the people will not give up until there is *a satisfactory solution. For more, read Principle: Escalate Strategically .*
   *1. Review your Vision, Values & Mission from Strategy 1 , and your analysis & SMART goals from Strategy 2 .*
7. Map out your campaign using these cards:
   *Choose your target wisely • Points of Intervention • Choose tactics that support your strategy • Escalate strategically • Build people power, then negotiate • Develop an inside-outside strategy*
8. Now , you’re ready to start thinking about tactics and creative actions. *Proceed to the Design an Action game & get going!*
   OPTIONS: *● Risk Card . Use the Risk Card to do a mini-safety-check on your proposed*
   tactics. ● Additional Methodologies. As you move towards planning specific actions,
   you might find these Methodologies particularly useful: Points of Intervention, Action Star, and possibly others.
   *● Get SMARTT . Add an additional “ T ” (for “ T heory of Change”) to your SMART Objectives to make “SMART T ” Objectives. Read Methodology: Theory of Change and write out a few for your campaign.*

   TIPS FOR FACILITATORS ● Have each team do one Methodology. During the Analysis & Goals phase
   *( Strategy 2 ), break the group into teams (groups of 3-7 people each) and* have each team do a different methodology. Each team should read their selected Methodology together and follow the directions. For a quick analysis, give each team 20 minutes. Ask teams to prepare a brief presentation on their work (with visual aid on flip chart paper recommended). Give teams 5 minutes each to report back. Hand out sticky notes to participants to write highlights (one per note) of what they hear/learn.
   ● Tip for quick use of methodologies. When you’re short on time (i.e. trying to do a Methodology in a brief 20 minutes), make sure to fill in something in every category, pie slice or row, rather than going very deep in one place. For *example, if using the Methodology: Spectrum of Allies , list a minimum of two* specific groups for each of the pie slices (active allies, passive allies, neutrals, etc.) Make sure to distinctly identify each group. (“Local 1199 Baltimore rank and file” is specific and useful; “labor union” much less so.)
   *● For a SWOT analysis , you could choose to do a whole-group brainstorm, or* divide into four teams and give each team one of the four main categories : Strengths , Weaknesses , Threats , and Opportunities . (Remember, some items could end up in more than one category!) Consider sketching the SWOT matrix on a large sheet and writing the SWOT items on sticky notes and then placing them in the appropriate boxes. Next, take some time to figure out how your Strengths and your Opportunities intersect, and write down those ideas in the appropriate scenario box (see diagram
   above/below). Do this for the remaining scenario boxes ( Strengths + Threats , Weaknesses + Opportunities , Weaknesses +Threats ). Note that the ideas that come from the intersection of Strengths + Opportunities fall in the “best case” box, and could be considered “low-hanging fruit” — actions that could be done fairly easily, with minimal effort or expenditure of resources. Where weaknesses and threats intersect you’ll find your “Worst Case” scenarios, which you should try to avoid if *possible! For more information, see Methodology: SWOT Matrix .*
   ● Encourage participants to learn more. Each card includes a unique “pop-up code.” Turn your smartphone camera on and hover over the card. See what happens?! Your smartphone automatically pops up a link to the full version of that card in the Beautiful Trouble online toolbox.
   ● Don’t forget to capture your best ideas. For all of these activities, you may want to designate a notetaker who can write down — or quickly take pictures with their phone — any ideas your team generates during the process.
