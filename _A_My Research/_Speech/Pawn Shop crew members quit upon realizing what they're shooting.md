In a move that might hopefully inspire some real-life ICE members to follow suit, several crew members have quit *Pawn Shop* upon realizing that they were [making a pro-ICE propaganda piece](https://www.avclub.com/the-daily-wire-pawn-shop-shoot-run-hide-fight-action-movies). This is per [a new report in *IndieWire*](https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/pawn-shop-crew-script-disclosure-pro-ice-movie-1235220533/), which features quotes from one person still employed in the production. That crew member (who speaks under the condition of anonymity because they signed an NDA) estimates that about a dozen people have quit a production that we might call “beleaguered” but is probably just another day to The Daily Wire.

While [Ben Shapiro may claim](https://www.avclub.com/ben-shapiro-production-resumes-daily-wire) that any obstacle toward making propaganda is a violation of his First Amendment rights, he apparently wasn’t all that keen on using that freedom of speech to tell people what the hell they were signing up for. “I was asked my availability, was told it was a ‘fun action movie’ in downtown Livingston. Great crew, great whatever. That was the whole pitch,” says the anonymous crew member. “So, I just said, ‘Yep, I’m available.'” It wasn’t until they were into the job that they learned what they were really shooting, and they say, “If I had known this, then I would not have even accepted the job.”

And while many have already walked off the job, there are pretty obvious obstacles in place to keep other crew members—who are typically paid relatively little and may have long gaps between jobs—from doing the same. Many of the workers are not from Montana, where the production is taking place, and had to pay for their own travel home, according to local activist Brooklyn Wilde. Those who remain are in a pretty unpleasant situation, with the anonymous member interviewed saying they were trying to figure out how to withdraw gracefully or whether to use a pseudonym in the credits (similar to what [many involved with *Melania* did](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/two-thirds-melania-movies-crew-043040881.html)). It doesn’t sound like the production has really done much to improve the safety for the people working, either. The crew member says that when they asked what the protocol was when they were shouted at or approached by angry protestors, “I was laughed at.” Yeah, that [sounds like a Daily Wire movie](https://www.avclub.com/jonathan-majors-fell-through-window-daily-wire-movie-set-strike) alright. You can read *IndieWire* ‘s whole report [here](https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/pawn-shop-crew-script-disclosure-pro-ice-movie-1235220533/).

Asha Sharma’s tenure as executive vice president and chief executive officer of Xbox has been short but busy: She’s [overseen a staggering amount of layoffs and the divestment of long-running studios](https://www.avclub.com/dungeons-dragons-magic-the-gathering-union) absorbed by the company; she helped [woo apex developer Hideo Kojima over to Xbox](https://www.avclub.com/hideo-kojima-moves-physint-to-xbox) after Sony Interactive Entertainment withdrew its backing for the project; and now, Sharma is expanding Xbox’s franchises beyond consoles and PCs with “XP,” a new entertainment division, according to internal emails and memos obtained [by *The Hollywood Reporter*.](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/xbox-xp-film-tv-adaptations-minecraft-call-of-duty-halo-ip-1236725288/)

XP, or “Experience Points”, is the latest development to come out of the company’s tumultuous restructuring phase, and will be helmed by current Mojang (*Minecraft*) president Kayleen Walters, with Maria Angelidou-Smith boarding Mojang as its new CEO. Walters is old hat at this corporate-synergistic hullabaloo: She’s the former vice president of franchise marketing and integrated planning at Lucasfilm and served as an executive producer on *A Minecraft Movie* and its upcoming sequel. With Prime Video’s *Fallout* adaptation entering its third season and Peter Berg and Taylor Sheridan [seemingly quite serious about making their *Call Of Duty* movie](https://www.avclub.com/call-of-duty-movie-modern-warfare-peter-berg-taylor-sheridan) —not to mention [Netflix’s announced *Diablo* animated series](https://www.avclub.com/netflix-announces-diablo-animated-series-blizzard) and Kojima’s *Physint* “already looking toward film and TV adaptation opportunities” (per an internal memo sent out by Sharma)—Sharma’s Xbox is making an aggressive push into a franchise arena similar to that of Marvel Comics or LEGO. However, *THR* stresses that XP’s function won’t be as a production studio, but a division that pairs production studios with their game franchises and IP.

Walters took the lead in announcing XP to the company. “XP was created from a simple but powerful belief: the world’s most beloved gaming franchises have an opportunity to reach people far beyond the boundaries of the game itself,” she wrote in a company email. “Fans don’t just play our franchises. They watch them, wear them, attend them, celebrate them, and connect with them in countless ways. XP brings together the teams responsible for helping Xbox franchises show up in the world in bigger, more meaningful ways.”

“XP is a play on ‘Experience Points’ and formalizes four investment areas for us,” Sharma wrote in the memo. “XBOX PICTURES for film and television adaptations; XBOX PRODUCTS for licensing and merchandise; XBOX PARTNERSHIPS for sponsors and brand partners; and XBOX PLACES for our marquee broadcast and in-person events, theme parks, and other location-based experiences. Our franchises can mean more to people than the time they spend playing them, and Kayleen knows how to build those experiences with the industry without losing what made people care about the franchise in the first place.”

With a title like *Christmas At The Kringles*, savvy holiday movie watchers might expect a standard, Hallmark-style snooze full of tinsel, good cheer, and perhaps a big city gal learning to appreciate the burly charms of a small town hunk (who may or may not actually be Santa). But the first full trailer for this film from director Kevin Lewis ([*Willy’s Wonderland*](https://www.avclub.com/nic-cage-is-a-silent-brute-in-the-chintzy-five-nights-a-1846256412)) detonates those lowered expectations with a faux ’70s grainy exploitation vibe and gouts of blood and gore, all showering over a promising cast filled with comic character actors. (There is also quite a bit of tinsel.)

The film sees an average family, led by Ron Livingston and Rachel Bilson, moving in next door to the fanatically Christmas-obsessed Kringles, whose over-the-top holiday decorations flood the neighborhood with twinkling cheer all year long. Played by the always welcome [Thomas Lennon](https://www.avclub.com/thomas-lennon-random-roles-interview-1849889943) and [Arden Myrin](https://www.thejvclub.org/episodes/2017/9/18/episode-87-arden-myrin), the Kringles are more than just *into* Christmas. They’re murderously fanatical about ensuring their new neighbors get into the holiday spirit, even if they have to employ Christmas-themed implements like brightly colored chainsaws, a *Saw* -jawed human-sized Nutcracker, and a festive Christmas gimp to enforce their singular brand of mandatory joy.

Featuring [*Scrubs*](https://www.avclub.com/scrubs-season-10-review-abc-tv-revival) ‘ Donald Faison as a wary fellow neighbor, Rob Ruggle as a gruff, doom-saying Santa, Lennon’s [*The State*](https://www.avclub.com/the-state-the-complete-series-1798206592) pal David Wain, and Beverly D’Angelo (no stranger to [disastrous Christmases)](https://www.avclub.com/national-lampoon-s-christmas-vacation-proves-that-perfe-1798242619), *Christmas At The Kringles* arrives under your tree on October 30, just in time for Halloween, naturally.

Though he probably could have just included “Psycho Killer” or “Life During Wartime” and called it a day, [David Byrne](https://www.avclub.com/david-byrne-brady-corbet-concert-film) has produced some new music for Alex Gibney’s epic documentary *Musk*. Teaming with composer Daniel Pemberton, the former Talking Head has debuted “Let Me Sell You A Dream.” The title alone should communicate the general feelings these musicians have toward Elon Musk. But if the title and lyrics (which include phrases like “My daddy used to tell me / tell me I was no good”) don’t drive the point home, there’s no misunderstanding [the music video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BzZfEbA1_Po), which features the potential model for Iron Man donning the armor to vaporize the employees at Twitter.

Byrne’s no stranger to soundtrack work; a few years ago, he (and Mitski and Ryan Lott) received an Oscar nomination for “This Is A Life,” which came from the soundtrack to *Everything Everywhere All At Once*. That award ultimately went to “Naatu Naatu” from *RRR*, but perhaps “Let Me Sell You A Dream” can get Byrne back in the Dolby Theater. Someone has to compete against Taylor Swift’s *Toy Story* song, after all.

*Musk* opens in theaters in New York and Los Angeles tomorrow, and opens nationwide on October 16.
