MODEL:

Nate Hagens' worldview was fundamentally shaped by ten core learnings (though he lists eleven total in the discussion) that he acquired over thirty years, beginning when he started to "shed my Gilligan's Island and Donkey Kong and, wall Street, exterior" [1].

The discoveries that changed Nate's view, drawing directly from his presentation, are:

### I. The Reality of Energy and Resources

1. **The Role of Oil and Externalized Costs:** Nate's deep dive into oil in the 1990s was the concept that caused the **"loss of my wide boundary virginity"** and "changed everything about how I see the world" [2, 3]. He identified three main takeaways:
   * **Oil's Power:** He learned that society confuses the price of energy with its value [4]. A single barrel of oil contains 1700 kilowatt hours of energy potential, roughly the equivalent of **five years of human work**, yet sells for less than a dinner in New York City [4]. Combined with machines, fossil fuels function as an "army of half a trillion human workers" [4].
   * **Oil's Finiteness:** He realized that oil is a "one-time bank account from Earth's deep past" that took millions of years to form, but is being burned through quickly [4, 5]. Our cultural stories imply it is a flow, rather than a stock that is rapidly depleting [5].
   * **Oil's Externalized Costs:** None of the pollution, global heating, or ecological damages from burning oil appear in its market price, meaning that prices, incentives, and cultural expectations are based on the wrong long-term prices because depletion and pollution are excluded [2, 5].
2. **Energy Primacy:** He saw that the issue was not just about one fuel, but about **energy itself** [3]. Every civilization is at its core a physical process of transforming energy and materials into things humans value (food, shelter, status, etc.) [3, 6]. Beneath the money, every transaction is an energy transformation [6]. This revealed how **"deeply energy blind"** society is, having mistaken cheap, available energy for a background condition, like gravity or air [6]. **Energy is the economy**; every dollar of GDP represents a physical transformation powered by energy [7].
3. **Net Energy (Earning vs. Spending):** This concept became the central focus of his PhD thesis [8]. He learned that every living system depends on gaining more energy than it spends [8]. The **surplus energy** (net energy) left after extraction costs are paid is what actually runs civilization, building cities and powering services [9, 10]. He discovered that the energy cost share of total energy began to **rise again in the 21st century** (headed toward 15% from a low of 4% in 1999) because easier resources are exhausted [10, 11]. This means more energy must be spent simply to maintain the extraction system [11].
4. **Technology Accelerates Access, Not Wealth Creation:** Nate realized that real wealth comes from the sun, soil, water, and natural resource accounts [12]. Technology doesn't create this wealth; instead, it functions like a **"straw to access it,"** accelerating the speed at which stored wealth is converted into income [12, 13]. Furthermore, when technology makes a process more efficient, it often increases total consumption, known as the rebound effect or Jevons Paradox [14].

### II. The Structure of Finance and Human Behavior

5. **The Paradox of Wanting vs. Having:** Observing his wealthy clients on Wall Street, he noticed a "peculiar paradox": billionaires often seemed anxious and unsatisfied, while lower-paid clerks seemed lighter in spirit [15]. He realized that **"wanting seemed a stronger force than having,"** and people with more actually wanted more [16, 17]. He understood that the current economy runs on the loop where dopamine drives pursuit, not fulfillment [17]. People are chasing the same mix of neurotransmitters, and money simply buys a different path to the same temporary chemical highs [18, 19].
6. **Money's Growth Imperative:** He discovered that the fractional reserve banking model was flawed, realizing that banks **create new money** with every loan based on the promise of future growth [20, 21]. The problems are two-fold: creating new money does not simultaneously create more "stuff" (soil, oil, copper, forests) [21, 22], and the interest required for repayment creates a built-in **growth imperative** for the entire system [22]. This realization "shattered a spell," showing him that the global economy runs on "jewels, atoms, and functioning ecosystems," not credit ledgers and spreadsheets [23, 24].
7. **Multi-Level Selection (Cooperation):** This addressed the question of whether humans are selfish or cooperative, revealing the answer is both [25]. Through studying multi-level selection theory, he understood that evolution acts on groups as well as individuals; **groups that cooperate effectively tend to outcompete those that don't** [25]. He realized that the widely known "selfish gene story" is incomplete, and the essential dynamic is the balance between individual winning and collective belonging [26, 27].

### III. Deep Time and Planetary Scale

8. **Made of Stars (Deep Time):** The discovery that he was **"recycled star matter"** cracked open his sense of deep time [28]. He realized that the heavy elements composing our bodies were forged in dying Suns billions of years ago [29]. This perspective allowed him to perceive himself and everything else as part of an **"unbroken, continual story"** stretching back to the universe's birth [28]. Conscious life is, profoundly, "how the universe observes itself" [30].
9. **Evolved Psychology and Evolutionary Scripts:** Researching the origin of blue eyes (a trait that spread through attraction, not adaptation) [31, 32] led him to the realization that humans are **not born as blank slates** [32]. Our mental and physical structures follow deeply conserved evolutionary scripts of mating choice, status games, and social learnings [32]. These often irrational but predictable evolved behavioral tendencies explain a great deal of the current human and planetary predicament (e.g., status addiction, collective action tragedy) [33].
10. **Humanity as the Ecosystem (Planetary Reallocation):** Learning about ecology provided a "gut punch" realization regarding humanity's dominion [34]. He learned that humans and their livestock now **outweigh all wild land mammals by roughly 80 to one** [34]. This represents a 3,000-fold swing in biomass composition over an evolutionary "I blink" [35]. Humanity now directs 30 to 40% of the net primary productivity of the sunlight hitting Earth toward human purposes [35]. He concluded that **"We've become the ecosystem,"** consuming the wild world and remaking it in our image [36, 37].
11. **The Economic Superorganism:** The most recent and ongoing realization is that *humanity as a whole* functions differently than *humans as individuals* [37]. While individuals are often decent and cooperative locally [38], at scale, **emergent systems (markets, politics) function like a "mindless, unthinking, economic Superorganism"** driven by its own need for continuous growth and material throughput [39]. This system reinforces short-term power and rewards individuals with "dark triad traits" [39, 40]. This insight, while unsettling, suggests that becoming aware of ourselves as a planetary force is part of a potential "evolutionary transition" [41].

These realizations dismantled Nate's previous layer of naivete and led him to a deeper understanding of the **human predicament**, prompting him to see humans as stardust, as animals, as energy systems, and as social organisms, integrating these truths to enter into conscious membership with the story of life on earth [41-43].

**Analogy:** Nate's shift in worldview is like moving from playing a simple video game (like *Donkey Kong* or living on *Gilligan's Island* [1]) where the rules seem obvious (get money, level up), to suddenly discovering the complex, hidden code and the underlying power grid that runs the entire game system—realizing that the economic score is just an abstraction built atop finite, depleting physics, and that the players are all running on ancient, wired programming.
