Person-First and Identity-First Language - Employer Assistance and Resource Network

Source guide

The Employer Assistance and Resource Network on Disability (EARN) provides a comprehensive framework designed to help organizations recruit, hire, retain, and advance individuals with disabilities. Beyond offering structural guidance for the employment lifecycle, the resource emphasizes the importance of respectful communication by distinguishing between person-first and identity-first language. While person-first language highlights the individual before the condition, identity-first language is often embraced as a point of pride; therefore, the text stresses that personal preference should always dictate which terms are used. Ultimately, this source serves as both a practical toolkit for workplace inclusion and a guide for fostering an inclusive environment through sensitive and affirmative terminology.

Person-First and Identity-First Language

Learn more about the use of person-first and identity-first language when communicating about people with disabilities.

Getting Started / Resources

Person-first language emphasizes the person before the disability, for example “person who is blind” or “people with spinal cord injuries.” Identity-first language puts the disability first in the description, e.g., “disabled” or “autistic." Person-first or identify-first language is equally appropriate depending on personal preference. When in doubt, ask the person which they prefer.

It is important to note that while person-first language is often used in more formal writing, many people with disabilities, particularly younger people, are choosing to use identity-first language. How a person chooses to self-identify is up to them, and they should not be corrected or admonished if they choose not to use person-first language.
