  From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia Ideological and political wings of the Republican Party
  The [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) includes several [factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_faction), or wings. During the 19th century, Republican factions included the [Half-Breeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Breeds_(politics)), who supported civil service reform; the [Radical Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Republicans), who advocated the immediate and total abolition of slavery, and later advocated civil rights for freed slaves during the [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era); and the [Stalwarts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalwarts), who supported machine politics.
  In the 20th century, Republican factions included the Progressive Republicans, the [Reagan coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_coalition), and the liberal [Rockefeller Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Republicans).
  In the 21st century, Republican factions include conservatives (represented in the House by the [Republican Study Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Study_Committee) and the [Freedom Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Caucus)), moderates (represented in the House by the [Republican Governance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Governance_Group), [Republican Main Street Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Main_Street_Partnership), and the Republican members of the [Problem Solvers Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_Solvers_Caucus)), and libertarians (represented in Congress by the [Republican Liberty Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Liberty_Caucus)). During the first presidency of [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump), [Trumpist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism) and [anti-Trumpist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Trump) factions arose within the Republican Party.

#### 21st century factions

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=1)] [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:U.S._President_Donald_Trump_with_a_KAG_hat.jpg), the 45th and 47th [President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States), has been widely credited for [realigning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_realignment) the Republican Party away from [conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism) and more toward [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism).[ 1 ][ 2 ][ 3 ]During the presidency of [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama), the Republican Party experienced internal conflict between its governing class (known as the Republican establishment) and the anti-establishment, small-government [Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement).[ 4 ][ 5 ][ 6 ][ 7 ] In 2019, during the presidency of [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump), Perry Bacon Jr. of FiveThirtyEight.com asserted that there were five groups of Republicans: Trumpists, Pro-Trumpers, Trump-Skeptical Conservatives, Trump-Skeptical Moderates, and Anti-Trumpers.[ 8 ]
In February 2021, following Trump's 2020 loss to Democrat [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden) and the [2021 United States Capitol attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_Capitol_attack), Philip Bump of The Washington Post posited that the Republican Party in the U.S. House of Representatives consisted of three factions: the Trumpists (who voted against the [second impeachment of Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_impeachment_of_Donald_Trump) in 2021, voted against stripping [Marjorie Taylor Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Taylor_Greene) of her committee assignments, and supported [efforts to overturn the results of the 2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efforts_to_overturn_the_results_of_the_2020_presidential_election)), the accountability caucus (who supported either the Trump impeachment, the effort to discipline Greene, or both), and the pro-democracy Republicans (who opposed the Trump impeachment and the effort to discipline Greene but also opposed efforts to overturn the 2020 presidential election results).[ 9 ] Also in February 2021, Carl Leubsdorf of the Dallas Morning News asserted that there were three groups of Republicans: Never Trumpers (including [Bill Kristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Kristol), Sen. [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney), and governors [Charlie Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Baker) and [Larry Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Hogan)), Sometimes Trumpers (including Senate Minority Leader [Mitch McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_McConnell) and former U.N. Ambassador [Nikki Haley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikki_Haley)), and Always Trumpers (including Sens. [Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz) and [Josh Hawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hawley)).[ 10 ]
In March 2021, one survey indicated that five factions of Republican voters had emerged following Trump's presidency: Never Trump, Post-Trump G.O.P. (voters who liked Trump but did not want him to run for president again), Trump Boosters (voters who approved of Trump, but identified more closely with the Republican Party than with Trump), Die-hard Trumpers, and *Infowars* G.O.P. (voters who subscribe to conspiracy theories).[ 11 ] In November 2021, [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) identified four Republican-aligned groups of Americans: Faith and Flag Conservatives, Committed Conservatives, the Populist Right, and the Ambivalent Right.[ 12 ]
As of 2023, congressional Republicans refer to the various House Republican factions as the Five Families.[ 13 ][ 14 ][ 15 ][ 16 ] Derived from The Godfather, the term refers to Mafia crime families.[ 14 ] The Five Families consist of "the right-wing House [Freedom Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Caucus), the conservative [Republican Study Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Study_Committee), the business-minded [Main Street Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Main_Street_Partnership), the mainstream [Republican Governance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Governance_Group)", and the Republican members of the bipartisan [Problem Solvers Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_Solvers_Caucus). The House Republican factions overlap with one another, and some members belong to no caucus.[ 15 ]

##### Conservatives

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=2)] Main article: [Conservatism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States)See also: [Cultural conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_conservatism), [Fiscal conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_conservatism), [Movement conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_conservatism), [Neoconservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoconservatism), and [Social conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism)"Conservative Republicans" redirects here. For the 19th century faction of this party, see [Conservative Republicans (Reconstruction era)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Republicans_(Reconstruction_era)). For other uses, see [Conservative Republican Party (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Republican_Party_(disambiguation)). [William F. Buckley Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:William_F._Buckley,_Jr._Public_Domain.jpg) is considered one of the earliest and most influential figures of the [conservative movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States).
The [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) wing grew out of the 1950s and 1960s, with its initial leaders being Senator [Robert A. Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Taft), [Russell Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Kirk), and [William F. Buckley Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Buckley_Jr.) Its central tenets include the promotion of individual liberty and [free-market economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-market_economics) and opposition to [labor unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_union), high [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxes), and [government regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_regulation).[ 17 ] The Republican Party has undergone a major decrease in the influence of its [establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment#United_States) conservative faction since the election of Donald Trump in 2016.[ 18 ][ 19 ][ 20 ]
In economic policy, conservatives call for a large reduction in [government spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_spending), less regulation of the economy, and privatization or changes to [Social Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_(United_States)). Supporters of [supply-side economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-side_economics) and [fiscal conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_conservatism) predominate, but there are [deficit hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficit_hawk) and [protectionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism_in_the_United_States) within the party as well. Before 1930, the Northeastern industrialist faction of the GOP was strongly committed to [high tariffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff_History_of_the_United_States), a political stance that returned to popularity in many conservative circles during the [Trump presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump).[ 21 ][ 22 ] The conservative wing typically supports [socially conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism) positions, such as supporting [gun rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_keep_and_bear_arms) and [restrictions on abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion), though there is a wide range of views on such issues within the party.[ 23 ]
Percent of self-identified [conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) by state in 2010:[ 24 ] 49% and above 45–48% 41–44% 37–40% 33–36% 32% and under
Conservatives generally oppose [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action), support increased military spending, and are opposed to [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_the_United_States). On the issue of [school vouchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_voucher), conservative Republicans split between supporters who believe that "[big government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_government) education" is a failure and opponents who fear greater government control over private and church schools. Parts of the conservative wing have been criticized for being [anti-environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-environmentalism)[\[ 25 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-environmentalism)[\[ 26 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-environmentalism)[\[ 27 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-environmentalism) and promoting [climate change denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial)[\[ 28 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial)[\[ 29 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial)[\[ 30 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial) in opposition to the general [scientific consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_opinion_on_climate_change), making them unique even among other worldwide conservative parties.[ 30 ]
Long-term shifts in conservative thinking following the election of Trump have been described as a "new [fusionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusionism)" of traditional conservative ideology and right-wing populist themes.[ 31 ] These have resulted in shifts towards greater support of [national conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism),[ 32 ] protectionism,[ 33 ]cultural conservatism, a more [realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_(international_relations)) foreign policy, skepticism of [neoconservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoconservatism), reduced efforts to roll back entitlement programs, and a disdain for traditional checks and balances.[ 31 ][ 34 ]

##### Neoconservatives

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=3)] Main article: [Neoconservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoconservatism)See also: [American imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism) and [Interventionism (politics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interventionism_(politics))President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) (middle), Vice President [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney) (right) and Secretary of Defense [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld) (left) were considered central figures of the [war on terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_terror) following the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks). The [Bush-Cheney administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush-Cheney_administration) marked the height of [Neoconservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoconservatism) during the 2000s.
[Neoconservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoconservatism) promote an [interventionist foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interventionism_(politics)) and democracy or American interests abroad. Neoconservatives have been credited with importing into the Republican Party a more active international policy. They are amenable to [unilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilateralism) military action when they believe it serves a morally valid purpose (such as the spread of democracy or deterrence of human rights abuses abroad). They grounded in a [realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_(international_relations)) philosophy of "[peace through strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_through_strength)."[ 35 ][ 36 ][ 37 ][ 38 ] Many of its adherents became politically famous during the Republican presidential administrations of the late 20th century, and neoconservatism peaked in influence during the administration of [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) and [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney) during the 2000s, when they played a major role in promoting and planning the [2003 invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq).[ 39 ]
Prominent neoconservatives in the [Bush-Cheney administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush-Cheney_administration) included [John Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bolton), [Paul Wolfowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wolfowitz), [Elliott Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Abrams), [Richard Perle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Perle), and [Paul Bremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bremer). During and after [Donald Trump's presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump), neoconservatism has declined and [non-interventionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-interventionism) and [right-wing populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_populism) has grown among elected federal Republican officeholders.[ 40 ][ 41 ] However, after Trump took office, some neoconservatives joined his administration, such as [John Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bolton), [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo), [Elliott Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Abrams)[\[ 42 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Abrams) and [Nadia Schadlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Schadlow).
Neoconservatives' role remains key in foreign policy issues. The [Hudson Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Institute) has been described as neoconservative,[ 43 ][ 44 ] whose researchers and foreign policy experts have played a key role in [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) administrations since the 2000s. Other organizations associated with this faction include the [American Enterprise Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Enterprise_Institute),[ 45 ] the [Foundation for Defense of Democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_for_Defense_of_Democracies),[ 46 ][ 47 ][ 48 ] the [Henry Jackson Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Jackson_Society)[\[ 49 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Jackson_Society) and the [Project for the New American Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_for_the_New_American_Century).[ 50 ]

##### Christian right

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=4)] Main articles: [Christian right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_right) and [Social conservatism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism_in_the_United_States)See also: [Christian nationalism § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism#United_States), [United States anti-abortion movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_anti-abortion_movement), and [2020s anti-LGBT movement in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020s_anti-LGBT_movement_in_the_United_States)[Jerry Falwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020s_anti-LGBT_movement_in_the_United_States), whose founding of the [Moral Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_Majority) was a key step in the formation of the [New Christian Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Christian_Right)The Christian right is a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States)[Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States)[political faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) characterized by strong support of [socially conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism) and [Christian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism) policies.[ 51 ][ 52 ][ 53 ] Christian conservatives seek to use the teachings of [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) to influence law and public policy.[ 54 ]
In the United States, the Christian right is an informal coalition formed around a core of [evangelical Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism_in_the_United_States) and conservative [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_United_States), as well as a large number of [Latter-day Saints (Mormons)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints).[ 55 ][ 56 ][ 57 ][ 58 ] The movement has its roots in [American politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_politics) going back as far as the 1940s and has been especially influential since the 1970s.[ 59 ] In the late 20th century, the Christian right became strongly connected to the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)).[ 60 ] Republican politicians associated with the Christian right in the 21st century include Tennessee Senator [Marsha Blackburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsha_Blackburn), former Arkansas Governor [Mike Huckabee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Huckabee), and former Senator [Rick Santorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Santorum).[ 61 ] Many within the Christian right have also identified as [social conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatives), which sociologist [Harry F. Dahms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_F._Dahms) has described as Christian doctrinal conservatives ([anti-abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion_movement), [anti-LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_opposition)) and [gun-rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_keep_and_bear_arms_in_the_United_States) conservatives (pro-[NRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rifle_Association)) as the two domains of ideology within social conservatism.[ 62 ] Christian nationalists generally seek to declare the U.S. a Christian nation, enforce [Christian values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_values), and overturn the [separation of church and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state_in_the_United_States).[ 52 ][ 53 ]

##### Libertarians

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=5)] Main articles: [Libertarian Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Republican) and [Right-libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-libertarianism)See also: [Libertarian conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_conservatism), [Libertarianism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism_in_the_United_States), [Republican Liberty Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Liberty_Caucus), and [Tea Party Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_Movement)Senator [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater) had a substantial impact on the [libertarian conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_conservative) movement of the 1960s and beyond.
[Libertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_conservative) make up a relatively small faction of the Republican Party.[ 63 ][ 64 ] In the 1950s and 60s, [fusionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusionism)—the combination of [traditionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_conservatism_in_the_United_States) and [social conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism_in_the_United_States) with political and economic [right-libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-libertarianism)—was essential to the movement's growth.[ 65 ] This philosophy is most closely associated with [Frank Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Meyer_(political_philosopher)).[ 66 ]Barry Goldwater also had a substantial impact on the conservative-libertarian movement of the 1960s.[ 67 ]
Libertarian conservatives in the 21st century favor cutting taxes and regulations, repealing the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act), and protecting [gun rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_the_United_States).[ 68 ] On social issues, they favor [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_privacy), oppose the [USA Patriot Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Patriot_Act), and oppose the [war on drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_drugs).[ 68 ] On foreign policy, libertarian conservatives favor [non-interventionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-interventionism).[ 69 ][ 70 ] The [Republican Liberty Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Liberty_Caucus), which describes itself as "the oldest continuously operating organization in the Liberty Republican movement with state charters nationwide", was founded in 1991.[ 71 ] The [House Liberty Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Liberty_Caucus) is a [congressional caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_caucus) formed by former Representative [Justin Amash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Amash), a former Republican of Michigan who joined the [Libertarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_(United_States)) in 2020 before returning in 2024.[ 72 ]
Prominent libertarian conservatives within the Republican Party include New Hampshire Governor [Chris Sununu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Sununu),[ 73 ][ 74 ] Senators [Mike Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Lee) and [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul), Representative [Thomas Massie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Massie), former Representative and Governor of South Carolina [Mark Sanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Sanford),[ 75 ] and former Representative [Ron Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul)[\[ 76 \]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul) (who was a Republican prior to 1987 and again from 1996 to 2015, and a Libertarian from 1987 to 1996 and since 2015). Ron Paul ran for president [once as a Libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul_1988_presidential_campaign) and [twice more recently as a Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul_2012_presidential_campaign).
The libertarian conservative wing of the party had significant cross-over with the [Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement).[ 77 ][ 78 ]
During the [2024 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_elections), the Republican Party adopted pro-[cryptocurrency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency) policies, which were originally advocated by the libertarian wing of the party.[ 79 ] As the Republican presidential nominee, [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) addressed the [2024 Libertarian National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Libertarian_National_Convention), pledging support for cryptocurrency, opposing [central bank digital currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank_digital_currency) and expressing support for the commutation of [Ross Ulbricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Ulbricht). Trump's 2024 campaign featured greater influence from [technolibertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technolibertarian) elements, particularly [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk), who was subsequently nominated to lead the [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency) (DOGE).[ 80 ][ 81 ][ 82 ] 2024 Republican presidential candidate [Vivek Ramaswamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivek_Ramaswamy), who was chosen to lead DOGE alongside Musk, has called for a synthesis between nationalism and libertarianism within the Republican Party, while opposing [protectionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionist) elements.[ 83 ][ 84 ] Musk and Ramaswamy have clashed with elements within the right-wing populist faction over high-skilled [legal immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_immigration) to the United States.[ 85 ][ 86 ]

##### Moderates

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=6)] Main articles: [Centrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism) and [Center-right politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center-right_politics)See also: [Republican Governance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Governance_Group), [Moderate conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderate_conservatism), and [Republican Main Street Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Main_Street_Partnership)Secretary of State [Colin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Powell) was one of the highest-ranking [moderate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_moderate) Republicans in recent history, and left the party in January 2021 following the [storming of the United States Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol).
Moderate Republicans tend to be conservative-to-moderate on fiscal issues and moderate-to-liberal on social issues, and usually represent [swing states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state) or [blue states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states). Moderate Republican voters are typically [highly educated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_attainment_in_the_United_States),[ 87 ] affluent, socially moderate or liberal and often part of the [Never Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Trump_movement) movement.[ 88 ] Ideologically, such Republicans resemble the [conservative liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_liberalism) of Europe.[ 89 ]
While they sometimes share the economic views of other Republicans (i.e. [lower taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_cuts), [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation), and [welfare reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_reform)), moderate Republicans differ in that some support [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action_in_the_United_States),[ 90 ]LGBT rights and same-sex marriage, legal access to and even public funding for [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_debate), [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_the_United_States) laws, more [environmental regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_regulation) and action on [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change), fewer restrictions on [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration) and a path to citizenship for illegal immigrants, and [embryonic stem cell research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell_controversy).[ 91 ][ 92 ] In the 21st century, some former Republican moderates have switched to the Democratic Party.[ 93 ][ 94 ][ 95 ]
Prominent 21st century moderate Republicans include Senators [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) of Arizona, [Lisa Murkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Murkowski) of Alaska and [Susan Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Collins) of Maine[ 96 ][ 97 ][ 98 ][ 99 ] and several current or former governors of northeastern states, such as [Charlie Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Baker) of Massachusetts[ 100 ] and [Phil Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Scott) of Vermont.[ 101 ] Another moderate Republican is incumbent governor of Nevada [Joe Lombardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Lombardo), who was previously the [Sheriff of Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriff_of_Clark_County).[ 102 ] Moderate Republican Representatives include [Brian Fitzpatrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Fitzpatrick_(American_politician)),[ 103 ]Mike Lawler,[ 104 ] and [David Valadao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Valadao).[ 105 ]
One of the most high-ranking moderate Republicans in recent history was [Colin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Powell) as Secretary of State in the first term of the [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) administration (Powell left the Republican Party in January 2021 following the [2021 storming of the United States Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol), and had endorsed every Democrat for president in the general election since 2008).[ 106 ]
The [Republican Governance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Governance_Group) is a caucus of moderate Republicans within the House of Representatives.[ 14 ]

##### Trumpists

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=7)] Main articles: [Trumpism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism) and [List of politicians associated with Trumpism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_politicians_associated_with_Trumpism)See also: [Radical right (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_(United_States)), [National conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism), and [Freedom Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Caucus)Vice President [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance), Donald Trump's [vice presidential pick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_vice_presidential_candidate_selection) for his [2024 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign). Although initially critical of Trump, Vance became a staunch advocate of [Trumpism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism) later into Trump's first term and by 2021, was described as a [right-wing populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_populist).
Sometimes referred to as the MAGA or "America First" movement,[ 107 ][ 108 ] Trumpists are the dominant faction in the Republican Party as of 2024.[ 109 ][ 18 ][ 110 ][ 20 ][ 111 ] It has been described as consisting of a range of right-wing ideologies including but not limited to [right-wing populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_populism),[ 112 ][ 113 ][ 114 ]national conservatism,[ 115 ]neo-nationalism,[ 116 ] and [Trumpism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism), the political movement associated with [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) and his base.[ 117 ][ 118 ] They have been described by some commentators, including Joseph Lowndes, James A. Gardner, and Guy-Uriel Charles, as the American political variant of the [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_(United_States)).[ 119 ][ 120 ][ 121 ]
Rachel Kleinfeld, senior fellow at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, characterizes Trumpism as an authoritarian, antidemocratic movement which has successfully weaponized cultural issues, and that cultivates a narrative placing white people, Christians, and men at the top of a status hierarchy as its response to the so-called "[Great Replacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Replacement_in_the_United_States)" theory, a claim that minorities, immigrants, and women, enabled by Democrats, Jews, and elites, are displacing white people, Christians, and men from their "rightful" positions in American society.[ 122 ] In international relations, Trumpists support [U.S. aid to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_relations#United_States_aid) but not to Ukraine,[ 123 ][ 124 ] are generally supportive towards [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia),[ 125 ][ 126 ][ 127 ] yet claim to favor an [isolationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolationism) "[America First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_First_(policy))" foreign policy agenda.[ 128 ][ 129 ][ 130 ][ 131 ] They generally reject compromise within the party and with [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)),[ 132 ][ 133 ] and are willing to oust fellow Republican office holders they deem to be too moderate.[ 134 ][ 135 ] Compared to other Republicans, the Trumpist faction is more likely to be immigration restrictionists,[ 136 ] and to be against [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade),[ 137 ]neoconservatism,[ 138 ] and [environmental protection laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism).[ 139 ]
The Republican Party's Trumpist and far-right movements emerged in occurrence with a global increase in such movements in the 2010s and 2020s,[ 140 ][ 141 ] coupled with entrenchment and increased partisanship within the party since 2010, fueled by the rise of the [Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement) which has also been described as far-right.[ 142 ] The election of Trump in 2016 split the party into pro-Trump and [anti-Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Trump_movement) factions.[ 143 ][ 144 ]
When conservative columnist [George Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Will) advised voters of all ideologies to vote for Democratic candidates in the Senate and House elections of November 2018,[ 145 ] political writer Dan McLaughlin at the *National Review* responded that doing so would make the Trumpist faction even more powerful within the Republican party.[ 146 ] Anticipating Trump's defeat in the U.S. presidential election held on November 3, 2020, [Peter Feaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Feaver) wrote in Foreign Policy magazine: "With victory having been so close, the Trumpist faction in the party will be empowered and in no mood to compromise or reform."[ 147 ] A poll conducted in February 2021 indicated that a plurality of Republicans (46% versus 27%) would leave the Republican Party to join a new party if Trump chose to create it.[ 148 ] Nick Beauchamp, assistant professor of political science at [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University), says he sees the country as divided into four parties, with two factions representing each of the Democratic and Republican parties: "For the GOP, there's the Trump faction—which is the larger group—and the non-Trump faction".[ 149 ]
Lilliana Mason, associate professor of political science at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), states that Donald Trump solidified the trend among [Southern white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Southerners)[conservative Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Southerners) since the 1960s of [leaving the Democratic Party and joining the Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_strategy): "Trump basically worked as a lightning rod to finalize that process of creating the Republican Party as a single entity for defending the high status of white, Christian, rural Americans. It's not a huge percentage of Americans that holds these beliefs, and it's not even the entire Republican Party; it's just about half of it. But the party itself is controlled by this intolerant, very strongly pro-Trump faction."[ 150 ]
Julia Azari, an associate professor of political science at [Marquette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquette_University), noted that not all Trumpist Republicans are public supporters of Donald Trump, and that some Republicans endorse Trump policies while distancing themselves from Trump as a person.[ 151 ]
In a speech he gave on November 2, 2022, at Washington's Union Station near the U.S. Capitol, President Biden asserted that "the pro-Trump faction" of the Republican Party is trying to undermine the U.S. electoral system and suppress voting rights.[ 152 ][ 153 ]

##### Anti-Trump faction

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=8)] Main article: [Never Trump movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Trump_movement)Senators [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) and [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney), both former Republican presidential nominees, were two of the most prominent early voices within the Republican Party to publicly condemn Donald Trump and [his ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism).
A divide has formed in the party between those who remain loyal to Donald Trump and those who oppose him.[ 154 ] A recent survey concluded that the Republican Party was divided between pro-Trump (the "Trump Boosters," "Die-hard Trumpers," and "Infowars G.O.P." wings) and anti-Trump factions (the "[Never Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Trump_movement)" and "Post-Trump G.O.P." wings).[ 11 ] Senator [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) was an early leading critic of Trumpism within the Republican Party, [refusing to support the then-Republican presidential nominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Republicans_who_opposed_the_Donald_Trump_2016_presidential_campaign) in the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election).[ 155 ]
Several critics of the Trump faction have faced various forms of retaliation. Representative [Liz Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Cheney) was removed from her position as Republican conference chair in the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives), which was perceived as retaliation for her criticism of Trump;[ 156 ] in 2022, she was defeated by a pro-Trump primary challenger.[ 157 ] Representative [Adam Kinzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Kinzinger) decided to retire at the end of his term, while Murkowski faced a pro-Trump primary challenge in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_Senate_election_in_Alaska) against [Kelly Tshibaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Tshibaka) whom she defeated.[ 158 ][ 159 ] A primary challenge to Romney had been suggested[ 160 ] by [Jason Chaffetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Chaffetz), who has criticized his opponents within the Republican Party as "Trump haters".[ 161 ] Romney chose not to run for re-election in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Utah).[ 162 ]
Representative [Anthony Gonzalez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Gonzalez_(politician)), one of 10 House Republicans who voted to impeach Trump over the [Capitol riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_Capitol_attack), called him "a cancer" while announcing his retirement.[ 163 ] Former Governor of New Jersey [Chris Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Christie), who was [running against Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Christie_2024_presidential_campaign) in the [2024 Republican primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries), called him "a lonely, self-consumed, self-serving, mirror hog" in [his presidential announcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Christie_2024_presidential_campaign).[ 164 ] Indiana senator [Todd Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Young) is one of few elected Republican senators that [did not support Trump's 2024 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Republicans_who_opposed_the_Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign).[ 165 ]
Organizations associated with this faction include [The Lincoln Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lincoln_Project),[ 166 ]Republican Accountability Project[ 167 ] and [Republicans for the Rule of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicans_for_the_Rule_of_Law).[ 168 ]

##### Stalwarts

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=11)] Main article: [Stalwarts (politics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalwarts_(politics))Further information: [Blaine faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine_faction)The [Stalwarts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalwarts_(politics)) were a traditionalist faction that existed from the 1860s through the 1880s. They represented "traditional" Republicans who favored [machine politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_politics) and opposed the [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service) reforms of [Rutherford B. Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_B._Hayes) and the more progressive [Half-Breeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Breeds_(politics)).[ 182 ] They declined following the elections of Hayes and [James A. Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Garfield). After Garfield's assassination by [Charles J. Guiteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_J._Guiteau), his Stalwart Vice President [Chester A. Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_A._Arthur) assumed the presidency. However, rather than pursuing Stalwart goals he took up the reformist cause, which curbed the faction's influence.[ 183 ]

##### Half-Breeds

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=12)] Main article: [Half-Breeds (politics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Breeds_(politics))Further information: [Mugwumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugwumps)The [Half-Breeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Breeds_(politics)) were a reformist faction of the 1870s and 1880s. The name, which originated with rivals claiming they were only "half" Republicans, came to encompass a wide array of figures who did not all get along with each other. Generally speaking, politicians labeled Half-Breeds were moderates or progressives who opposed the machine politics of the Stalwarts and advanced civil service reforms.[ 183 ]

##### Radical Republicans

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=13)] Main article: [Radical Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Republicans)Further information: [Butlerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butlerism)President [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) worked closely with [Radical Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Republicans) to protect African Americans.
The [Radical Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Republicans) were a major factor of the party from its inception in 1854 until the end of the [Reconstruction Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_Era) in 1877. The Radicals strongly opposed [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States), were hard-line [abolitionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism), and later advocated equal rights for the [freedmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedmen) and women. They were often at odds with the moderate and conservative factions of the party. During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Radical Republicans pressed for abolition as a major war aim and they opposed the moderate Reconstruction plans of [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) as too lenient on the [Confederates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America). After the war's end and Lincoln's assassination, the Radicals clashed with [Andrew Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Johnson) over Reconstruction policy.[ 184 ]
After winning major victories in the [1866 congressional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_elections,_1866), the Radicals took over Reconstruction, pushing through new legislation protecting the civil rights of African Americans. [John C. Frémont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Fr%C3%A9mont) of Michigan, the party's first nominee for president in 1856, was a Radical Republican. Upset with Lincoln's politics, the faction split from the Republican Party to form the short-lived [Radical Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Democratic_Party_(United_States)) in 1864 and again nominated [Frémont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Fr%C3%A9mont) for president. They supported [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) for president in 1868 and 1872, who worked closely with them to protect African Americans during Reconstruction. As [Southern Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Democrats) retook control in the South and enthusiasm for continued Reconstruction declined in the North, their influence within the GOP waned.[ 184 ]

##### Progressive Republicans

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=14)] See also: [Progressive Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era)President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt), a leader of the [Progressive Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era) who later joined the short-lived [Bull Moose Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Moose_Party)Historically, the Republican Party included a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) wing that advocated using government to improve the problems of modern society. [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt), an early leader of the [progressive movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States), advanced a "[Square Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Deal)" domestic program as president (1901–09) that was built on the goals of controlling corporations, protecting consumers, and conserving natural resources.[ 185 ] After splitting with his successor, [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft), in the aftermath of the [Pinchot–Ballinger controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinchot%E2%80%93Ballinger_controversy),[ 186 ] Roosevelt sought to block Taft's re-election, first by challenging him for the [1912 Republican presidential nomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries), and then when that failed, by entering the [1912 presidential contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election) as a [third party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_party_(United_States)) candidate, running on the [Progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_(United_States,_1912)) ticket. He succeeded in depriving Taft of a second term, but came in second behind [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States))[Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)).
After Roosevelt's 1912 defeat, the progressive wing of the party went into decline. Progressive Republicans in the [U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) held a "[last stand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_stand)" protest in December 1923, at the start of the [68th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/68th_United_States_Congress), when they refused to support the [Republican Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives_Republican_Conference) nominee for [Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives), [Frederick H. Gillett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_H._Gillett), voting instead for two other candidates. After eight ballots spanning two days, they agreed to support Gillett in exchange for a seat on the [House Rules Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Rules_Committee) and pledges that subsequent rules changes would be considered. On the ninth ballot, Gillett received 215 votes, a majority of the 414 votes cast, to win the election.[ 187 ]
In addition to Theodore Roosevelt, leading early progressive Republicans included [Robert M. La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette), [Charles Evans Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Evans_Hughes), [Hiram Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Johnson), [William Borah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Borah), [George W. Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Norris), [William Allen White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Allen_White), [Victor Murdock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Murdock), [Clyde M. Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_M._Reed) and [Fiorello La Guardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_La_Guardia).[ 188 ]

##### Old Guard

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=15)] Main article: [Old Right (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Right_(United_States))The Old Guard was the conservative faction of the Republican Party between 1945 and 1964. They coalesced around their opposition to the shifts in traditional economic and foreign policy under the presidency of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt). This opposition most noticeably directed to the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal), which was variously derided by Old Guard lawmakers as communist, socialist, or overreaching, seeing its programs as unwanted, unconstitutional, unwise, and politically unprofitable.[ 189 ]
To counter the New Deal, Republicans of the Old Guard espoused [Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanism_(ideology)), which entailed a [strict construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_constructionism) of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States), [fiscal responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_budget), and state and local over federal regulation. Politically, they opposed federal regulation of state, local, or business interests. They viewed “[big government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_government)” as a threat to liberty, which they interpreted as [economic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_freedom), which they saw as critical to incentivizing individuals to improve their material welfare and develop the pioneer virtues of [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism) and self-reliance. The Old Guard also espoused a [unilateralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilateralism) foreign policy, eschewing alliances that entailed advance military commitments while “go[ing] it alone” in foreign engagements. This also entailed [economic self-sufficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-sustainability), prioritizing American financial interests, and thus partially informed the Old Guard’s support for tariffs on imports and opposition to foreign aid.[ 189 ]
While sharing the above overarching goals, figures affiliated with this movement varied in their policy stances. These included [Bruce Fairchild Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Fairchild_Barton), [John W. Bricker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Bricker), [Styles Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styles_Bridges), [Joseph McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy), [Everett Dirksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett_Dirksen),[ 190 ]Walter Judd,[ 191 ] and [Robert A. Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Taft).

##### Rockefeller Republicans

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=17)] Main article: [Rockefeller Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Republican)Vice President [Nelson Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Rockefeller), namesake of the Rockefeller Republicans
Moderate or liberal Republicans in the 20th century, particularly those from the Northeast and West Coast, were referred to as "The Eastern Establishment" or "Rockefeller Republicans", after [Nelson Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Rockefeller), Vice President during the [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) administration.[ 195 ][ 196 ][ 197 ]
With their power decreasing in the final decades of the 20th century, many Rockefeller-style Republicans were replaced by conservative and moderate Democrats, such as those from the [Blue Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Dog_Coalition) or [New Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democrat_Coalition) coalitions. Massachusetts Republican [Elliot Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Richardson) (who served in several cabinet positions during the [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) administration) and writer and academic [Michael Lind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lind) argued that the liberalism of Democratic President [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton) and the rest of the [New Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democrats_(United_States)) movement were in many ways to the right of [Dwight Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Eisenhower), Rockefeller, and [John Lindsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lindsay), Republican Congressman and Mayor of New York City in the late 1960s.[ 198 ][ 199 ]

##### Reagan coalition

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=18)] Main article: [Reagan coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_coalition)President [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan), namesake of the Reagan coalition
According to historian [George H. Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Nash), the Reagan coalition in the Republican Party, which centered around [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan) and his administration throughout all of the 1980s (continuing in the late 1980s with the [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) administration), originally consisted of five factions: the libertarians, the traditionalists, the anti-communists, the neoconservatives, and the religious right (which consisted of Protestants, Catholics, and some Jewish Republicans).[ 17 ][ 200 ]

##### Tea Party movement

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Factions_in_the_Republican_Party_(United_States)&action=edit&section=19)] Main article: [Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement)See also: [List of politicians affiliated with the Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_politicians_affiliated_with_the_Tea_Party_movement)Former Representative [Ron Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul), known as the "intellectual godfather" of the Tea Party movement
The Tea Party movement was an American [fiscally conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_conservatism)[political movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_conservatism) within the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) that began in 2009 following the election of Barack Obama as President of the United States.[ 201 ][ 202 ] Members of the movement have called for lower [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_the_United_States), and for a reduction of the [national debt of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_debt_of_the_United_States) and [federal budget deficit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_budget_deficit) through decreased [government spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_budget).[ 203 ][ 204 ] The movement supports small-government principles[ 205 ][ 206 ] and opposes [government-sponsored universal healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care).[ 207 ] It has been described as a popular [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalism) movement.[ 208 ]
On matters of foreign policy, the movement largely supports avoiding being drawn into unnecessary conflicts and opposes "liberal internationalism".[ 209 ] Its name refers to the [Boston Tea Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Tea_Party) of December 16, 1773, a watershed event in the launch of the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution).[ 210 ] By 2016, Politico said that the modern Tea Party movement was "pretty much dead now"; however, the article noted that it seemed to die in part because some of its ideas had been "co-opted" by the mainstream Republican Party.[ 211 ]
Politicians associated with the Tea Party include former Representatives [Ron Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul), [Michele Bachmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Bachmann) and [Allen West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_West_(politician)),[ 212 ][ 213 ] Senators [Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz), [Mike Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Lee), [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul) and [Tim Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Scott),[ 214 ][ 215 ][ 216 ] former Senator [Jim DeMint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_DeMint),[ 215 ] former acting White House Chief of Staff [Mick Mulvaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Mulvaney),[ 217 ] and 2008 Republican vice presidential nominee and former Alaska Governor [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin).[ 213 ] Although there has never been any one clear founder or leader of the movement, Palin scored highest in a 2010 Washington Post poll asking Tea Party organizers "which national figure best represents your groups?".[ 218 ] Ron Paul was described in a 2011 Atlantic article as its "intellectual godfather".[ 219 ] Both Paul and Palin, although ideologically different in many ways, had a major influence on the emergence of the movement due to their separate 2008 [presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul_2008_presidential_campaign) and [vice presidential general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_presidential_candidacy_of_Sarah_Palin) runs respectively.[ 220 ][ 209 ]
Several political organizations were created in response to the movement's growing popularity in the late 2000s and into the early 2010s, including the [Tea Party Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_Patriots), [Tea Party Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_Express) and [Tea Party Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_Caucus).
