  Especially for White Men (and anyone interested in the intersection of patriarchy and race)
  suggestions made by Gregory Mengel
* The best book I know of on this topic is I Don't Want to Talk About It by Terry Real. Though on
  one level it's about male depression, it is very much about Patriarchy and uses the term
  throughout.
* Another really good one is Paul Kivel's Men's Work: How to Stop the Violence that Tears Our
  Lives Apart. It is a great analysis and includes introspection and practices one could use with a
  group. It was based on his work with the Oakland Men's Project.
* And then there is The Man They Wanted Me to Be by Jared Yates Sexton. It is a memoir of
  growing up in a white working class family and all the toxic pressures he experienced to
  conform to a very narrow version of "mrkin" manhood. It's a great read, but maybe not as
  relatable as Real or Johnson (see below).
* Allan Johnson's Not From Here is actually not about Patriarchy, per se. It is a short-ish memoir
  reflecting on place and belonging and the challenge he experienced, as a white American,
  feeling like he was really from any particular place. It is really beautiful.
* Last but not least, all of Michael Kimmel's books are about Patriarchy in that he is a sociologist
  in Masculinity Studies. Angry White Men from 2013 is good and sort of prescient. The men he
  talks about are the same men who populate Sexton's books - white men who feel their country
  is being taken from them. Guyland is perhaps a better fit. It's about the culture of masculinity
  that young (mostly white) men must navigate in becoming adults - rape culture and such. He's
  a sociologist, and while he is a brilliant academic, he doesn't really show up personally in his
  work.
