# The Upaya of Timing: Wisdom in Compassionate Speech

In Buddhist philosophy, the necessity of waiting for the proper time to share a disagreeable truth is rooted in the concept of \*\*Skillful Means\*\* (\*upaya\*), which emphasizes adapting your actions and words to the unique needs and circumstances of others \[1, 2\]. This concept dictates that wisdom (knowing what is true) and compassion are absolutely inseparable \[3\]. 

While a statement may be factually accurate and ultimately helpful, delivering it at the wrong moment can make it "true but not right" \[4\]. The sources explain the reasoning for waiting in the following ways:

\*   \*\*Preventing Unnecessary Suffering:\*\* Expressing a truth must always be tempered with kindness, empathy, and an awareness of the impact it will have on the listener \[3\]. Delivering a harsh or disagreeable truth abruptly, without considering the listener's emotional state, can cause them unnecessary suffering rather than being constructive \[3, 4\]. 
\*   \*\*Ensuring the Speech is Actually Beneficial:\*\* The ultimate goal of sharing beneficial knowledge is to guide the other person toward liberation from their own suffering \[1, 3\]. If a person is currently unready, defensive, or overwhelmed, delivering a difficult truth will not have its intended positive effect. 

Therefore, waiting for the "proper time" ensures that the circumstances are right for the difficult truth to be received, absorbed, and utilized effectively \[5, 6\]. By using a slightly less direct approach or simply waiting for the listener to become receptive, the speaker ensures that their communication remains a compassionate tool for healing rather than a weapon that causes pain \[3\].
