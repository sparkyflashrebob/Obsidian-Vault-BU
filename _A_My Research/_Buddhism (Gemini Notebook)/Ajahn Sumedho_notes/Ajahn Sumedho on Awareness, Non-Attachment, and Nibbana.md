# Ajahn Sumedho on Awareness, Non-Attachment, and Nibbana

Ajahn Sumedho describes the Path to Liberation as a process centered entirely on cultivating \*\*awareness\*\* and realizing \*\*non-attachment\*\* to conditioned phenomena in the present moment \[1-4\]. This path leads directly to the ultimate reality of the deathless, often referred to as Nibbana or the Unconditioned \[5-8\].

The essence of the path is encapsulated in the recognition that awareness itself is the gateway to the deathless \[4, 9-12\].

\*\*\*

### 1. The Foundation: Awakened Awareness (Sati and Puto)

Liberation is achieved not through external doctrines or achievements, but through the development of \*\*awakened attention\*\* and mindful investigation of reality \[13-16\].

#### Awareness as the Unconditioned Reality
Ajahn Sumedho emphasizes that awareness (\*Sati\*) is the individual's direct access point to the ultimate truth \[17, 18\].

\*   \*\*Awareness is innate and timeless:\*\* It is pure consciousness, which is here and now, timeless, and uncreated \[19-26\]. It is not something you have to develop or attain, as trying to "become" more aware is a delusion (\*sakkyaditti\*) \[20, 23, 25, 27\].
\*   \*\*The Unborn and Unconditioned:\*\* A core reflection is the existence of the unborn, uncreated, unformed, unconditioned \[6, 13, 19, 28\]. Because this unconditioned reality exists, there is an escape from the born, created, and conditioned realm \[6, 13\]. This recognition of the unconditioned nature of awareness is the essence of enlightenment \[29\].
\*   \*\*Trusting Awareness:\*\* The practice involves establishing confidence and \*\*trusting in awareness\*\* \[8, 20, 26, 30-35\]. Views, opinions, emotions, and the body are unreliable and untrustworthy, but the conscious awareness of them is pure and constant \[8, 32, 35\].

#### The Witnessing Position (Puto)
A key method for cultivating this awareness is adopting the position of the witness or observer, referred to by the Thai Forest tradition mantra, \*\*Puto\*\* (or Buddha, meaning "the awakened" or "one who knows the knowing") \[36-41\].

\*   \*\*Non-Judgmental Observation:\*\* Being the witness involves observing experiences—thoughts, feelings, sensations, and conditions—\*\*without judgment\*\* \[7, 15, 25, 26, 42-44\]. It is a state of open, receptive attention \[21, 26, 45\].
\*   \*\*"It is like this":\*\* The witnessing position involves recognizing "the way it is" (\*dhamma\*), which is a non-critical and impersonal reminder of the present moment \[42, 46-50\].
\*   \*\*Pure Consciousness:\*\* By resting in the witnessing position and letting go of all conditioned phenomena (\*sankharas\*), one accesses pure consciousness, which is empty and wide open \[51-53\]. This realization is the ultimate freedom of "nobody, no thing" \[52, 54\]. Pure consciousness does not suffer \[54, 55\].

\*\*\*

### 2. The Core Practice: Non-Attachment and Cessation

The purpose of awareness is to lead to \*\*non-attachment\*\* (\*Nibbana\*) \[15, 56-58\]. Non-attachment is the realization of the cessation (\*nirodha\*) of suffering, which is the Third Noble Truth \[59, 60\].

#### Suffering, Ignorance, and Attachment
The sources confirm that the fundamental cause of suffering is not desire itself, but \*\*attachment to desire\*\* \[61, 62\]. This attachment arises out of \*\*ignorance\*\* (\*avicha\*) of \*Dhamma\*—the true nature of reality \[14, 22, 63-66\].

\*   \*\*Letting Go of Self-View:\*\* The path requires seeing through and letting go of identity with the personal "I am" narrative \[67-69\]. This delusion of a separate self (\*sakkayaditthi\*), which is the first fetter, is the self we create out of ignorance in consciousness \[23, 24, 43, 70-72\].
\*   \*\*Desires of the Ego:\*\* Attachment encompasses sensual desires (\*kama-tanha\*), the desire for becoming or attainment (\*bhava-tanha\*), and the desire to get rid of things or annihilate defilements (\*vibhava-tanha\*) \[64, 73-75\]. Ajahn Sumedho notes that the desire to get rid of desire is itself still desire and part of the death realm \[74, 76\].
\*   \*\*The Act of Letting Go:\*\* Letting go is the prescription for the cessation of suffering \[61\]. It is described as allowing things to go, not destroying or throwing away, and it is crucial for realizing the cessation of suffering \[77, 78\].

#### Realizing Impermanence and Non-Self
The mechanism of letting go relies on realizing the true nature of all phenomena (\*sankharas\*) as impermanent (\*anicca\*), unsatisfactory (\*dukkha\*), and non-self (\*anatta\*) \[42, 50, 79-85\].

\*   \*\*Sankharas:\*\* All compounded things, including physical bodies, thoughts, feelings, memories, language, views, opinions, and even Buddhist scriptures, are \*sankharas\* \[50, 79, 86-92\]. These are conditions that arise and cease \[50, 79, 85\].
\*   \*\*Non-Self (Anatta):\*\* The aim is to realize non-self and non-identity \[80, 93\]. When conditions cease, what is left is awareness—which is non-self and without boundary \[94-96\]. This is known through direct realization, not intellectual deduction which can lead to nihilism \[52, 58, 97\].
\*   \*\*Dying Before You Die:\*\* The path involves "dying before you die," which means observing the ending of desires and fears within the mind and allowing the personality and identifications to cease \[98-102\]. This contemplation of impermanence serves as a gateway to the deathless \[62\].

### 3. Practical Tools and the Middle Way

The path to liberation is achieved through the \*\*reflective style\*\* of investigation encouraged by the Buddha \[13, 103-105\].

#### The Four Noble Truths
The Four Noble Truths are the essential teaching of the Buddha \[62, 106-108\]. They are not doctrines to be believed, but practical lenses to be actively applied to one's direct, present-moment experience \[29, 109-113\].

1.  \*\*Suffering (\*Dukkha\*):\*\* Suffering is to be understood, not avoided or run away from \[107, 113-117\]. Understanding suffering means accepting it and recognizing it objectively ("there is this suffering"), taking the "person" out of the experience \[68, 113, 117, 118\].
2.  \*\*Cause of Suffering (\*Samudaya\*):\*\* The cause (attachment to desire) should be let go of \[61\]. One must investigate desire to know what it is before letting it go \[56, 76, 113\].
3.  \*\*Cessation of Suffering (\*Nirodha\*):\*\* Cessation should be realized \[59, 119\]. This is the realization of non-attachment here and now \[57\].
4.  \*\*The Way Leading to Cessation (\*Magga\*):\*\* The Eightfold Path should be cultivated \[119, 120\]. This is the \*\*way of non-suffering\*\* \[121, 122\].

#### Right Effort and Right Understanding
The Fourth Noble Truth culminates in \*\*Right Understanding\*\* (\*Samma Ditthi\*) and \*\*Right Effort\*\* (\*Samma Vayama\*) \[123-125\].

\*   \*\*Right Understanding\*\* is intuitive understanding and discernment of reality, not a viewpoint or a concept \[94, 120, 125\]. It is wisdom that guides conduct \[95, 124\].
\*   \*\*Right Effort\*\* is described paradoxically as \*\*no effort\*\* \[78, 126, 127\]. It is not forceful striving, which creates tension and restlessness \[128-130\]. Instead, it is the wise, gentle energy of simply remembering to pay attention and actively letting go of the struggle \[24, 129-131\].
\*   This path is the \*\*Middle Way\*\* (not compromise, but transcendence of dualistic thinking), avoiding the extremes of sensuality and ascetic torture \[97, 111, 122, 132\].

#### Specific Tools for Direct Insight
Ajahn Sumedho offers concrete tools to facilitate this awareness and reflection:

\*   \*\*The Sound of Silence:\*\* Listening to the resonating sound of silence provides a constant, natural reference point for awareness \[9, 20, 51, 54, 63, 113, 133-135\]. It helps stop the thinking mind, which is a key barrier to insight \[133, 136\].
\*   \*\*Self-Inquiry:\*\* The path encourages constant self-reflection and questioning the nature of existence ("Who am I?") \[86, 109, 113, 137\]. This reflective practice should continue patiently and persistently ("reflect again and again until the penny drops") to create the conditions for direct, intuitive insight (\*Vipassanā\*), or the "penny drop" moment \[103, 105, 109, 138-140\].
