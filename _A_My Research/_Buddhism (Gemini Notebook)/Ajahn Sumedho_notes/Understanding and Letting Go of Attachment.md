# Understanding and Letting Go of Attachment

Based on the provided sources, Attachment or Clinging (Upadana) is presented in Ajahn Sumedho's teachings as a \*\*fundamental problem and the direct cause of suffering (Dukkha)\*\* \[1-9\]. It is seen as a \*\*deeply ingrained habit\*\* that arises primarily out of \*\*ignorance (Avijja)\*\* \[1, 3, 6, 8, 10-14\] and is strongly linked to the \*\*illusion of a permanent self or personality view (Sakaya Ditti)\*\* \[1, 4, 7, 8, 14-26\].

Here's what the sources say about Attachment/Clinging:

1.  \*\*Attachment as the Cause of Suffering:\*\* Attachment (Upadana or grasping) is explicitly identified as the \*\*cause of suffering (Dukkha)\*\*, particularly "attachment to desire" (not desire itself) \[1, 3, 7-9, 27\]. Suffering arises from our grasping at desire \[2\]. When we attach to the conditioned phenomena, especially the compounded creations like our personality or views, the result is Dukkha because there is a sense of something missing or a lack \[4\]. We create suffering around experiences like aging or sickness when our relationship to the body remains personal, based on identification and attachment \[1\]. Attachment, along with ignorance, craving, and identifying with the self-view, leads to the suffering of being "somebody," a person defined by conditions \[18\]. Attachment to anything in the monastic system can remove joy and make life feel difficult \[28\].
2.  \*\*The Nature of Attachment:\*\* Attachment is described using various terms:
    \*   \*\*Clinging (Upadana)\*\* \[3, 7-9, 14, 28, 29\].
    \*   \*\*Grasping\*\* \[2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 28-34\].
    \*   \*\*Identifying with\*\* \[1, 8, 12, 14, 18, 20, 21, 25, 30, 32, 35, 36\]. This is a key aspect, leading to the illusion of self \[20, 23\].
    \*   \*\*Holding on to\*\* \[1, 2, 8, 23, 27, 37-39\].
    \*   \*\*Commitment\*\* \[5, 28\].
    \*   \*\*Obsession\*\* \[3, 6, 28\].
    \*   It is linked to \*\*clinging to opinions and views\*\* \[23, 31, 40\].
    \*   It is influenced by \*\*cultural conditioning, personal views, and the ego\*\* \[8, 23, 30, 41, 42\]. Identity with nationality, gender, social position, etc., is bound into the ego and its attachments \[8\].
    \*   It manifests as feeling anxious if what you are attached to is threatened, or feeling angry, indignant, or jealous \[28\].

3.  \*\*Attachment and Ignorance (Avijja):\*\* Attachment arises "out of ignorance" \[1, 3, 6, 8, 10-14\]. Ignorance, particularly ignorance of the Four Noble Truths \[17, 22\], affects mental formations and consciousness, leading to desire and attachment \[3\]. The fundamental sense of a self (Sakaya Ditti) is created out of ignorance, and the grasping/attachment that operates from this self-position is the root cause of suffering \[as derived from the connection between Sakaya Ditti, Avijja, and suffering mentioned in multiple sources, e.g., 3, 4, 7, 23, 41\].
4.  \*\*Attachment vs. Desire (Tanha):\*\* The sources emphasize that the \*problem\* is \*\*attachment to desire (Upadana)\*\*, not desire (Tanha) itself \[1, 6, 7, 9\]. Desire is described as normal in this realm \[1\]. Ajahn Sumedho highlights three categories of desire: sensual desire (Kamatanha), desire for becoming (Bhavatanha - ambition, achieving), and desire for getting rid of things (Vibhavatanha - suppression, rejection, annihilation) \[6, 9, 27, 29\]. Even the desire to become enlightened, or to get rid of defilements, is still a creation of the mind and can be an object of attachment, leading to suffering \[9\]. The desire to get rid of bad thoughts or fear is specifically identified as Vibhavatanha \[7, 9, 27, 29, 43\].
5.  \*\*Attachment and Self-View (Sakaya Ditti):\*\* The sense of "I am" or self-view (Sakaya Ditti) is fundamentally linked to attachment and is the first of the ten fetters \[14, 16, 17, 21\]. The ego, which is the attachment to this sense of a self as a person, is a major source of suffering, manifesting as wanting to be right or feeling threatened \[4, 18\]. Interpreting experience through the ego involves identifying with and attaching to feelings, body, memories, and personality \[25\]. Blaming others or focusing on "my problems" or "poor me" is Sakaya Ditti thinking, which is based on illusion and attachment \[8, 26\]. Identifying with stages of attainment (like being a stream-enterer) is also Sakaya Ditti \[14\].
6.  \*\*The Path to Non-Attachment (Nibbana/Cessation):\*\* The path to liberation involves realizing non-attachment \[1, 3, 31, 38, 39\]. This realization is called Nibbana or the cessation of suffering (Dukkha Nirodha) \[3, 27, 31, 38, 44\]. The key practice is \*\*letting go of attachment\*\* \[3, 7-9, 27, 31, 43, 44\].
    \*   \*\*Letting Go:\*\* Letting go is \*\*not getting rid of, destroying, suppressing, or throwing away\*\* out of aversion \[27, 29, 31, 43, 44\]. It is about \*\*laying aside\*\*, \*\*allowing things to go\*\*, allowing them to be \[27, 44, 45\]. Attempting to "get rid of" attachment or suffering often stems from another form of desire (Vibhavatanha) and creates more suffering \[7, 9, 27, 29, 44\]. Embracing or accepting suffering or unpleasantness is a way of letting go \[19, 27, 45\].
    \*   \*\*Knowing and Understanding Attachment:\*\* Before you can let go, you must \*\*know what attachment is\*\* \[3\]. This involves \*\*observing attachment directly\*\* \[3, 5, 7, 28, 43\], \*\*studying it, admitting it, feeling it, experiencing it\*\* \[3, 5, 28\]. It's about becoming an "expert" on desire and attachment by observing its taste and power \[6, 29\]. You must \*\*be the observer of attachment rather than the owner\*\* \[28, 46\]. This understanding, derived from direct experience of the suffering caused by grasping, leads to a natural movement of letting go \[4, 5, 8, 28\]. Letting go is through understanding, not aversion \[28\].
    \*   \*\*Awareness (Sati) and Wisdom (Panna):\*\* Awareness is crucial because it allows you to \*\*see attachment as a condition\*\* \[46\], a mental object \[9\], without identifying with it \[23, 25, 30\]. Awareness allows you to see that the knower (awareness) and the desire/attachment are distinct \[43\]. Trusting in this awareness, which is not the self \[47\], and cultivating it allows you to see through the illusions created by self-view and attachment \[13, 22, 23, 26, 30, 47\]. Wisdom, arising from this awareness and reflection, allows one to respond spontaneously from understanding rather than reacting from habit and attachment \[41\]. Wisdom comes through seeing things through Dhamma rather than attachment \[25\].
    \*   \*\*The Four Noble Truths as a Tool:\*\* The Four Noble Truths provide a framework for investigating suffering and its cause – attachment \[8, 23\]. By contemplating experience through mindfulness using this framework, one observes attachment and its results \[1, 7, 17, 19, 22\]. The second Noble Truth is explicitly used for reflection on desire and attachment \[6, 9, 43\].

In essence, Ajahn Sumedho emphasizes that \*\*attachment (Upadana), fueled by ignorance and the illusion of self, is the core mechanism that creates suffering\*\*. The path to liberation is not about destroying or suppressing attachment, but about \*\*cultivating awareness and wisdom to fully understand its nature, its causes, and the suffering it produces\*\*. Through this direct knowing and understanding, the grasping naturally loosens, leading to the realization of non-attachment and the cessation of suffering. The Buddhist teachings and practices serve as skillful means for this investigation and letting go \[25, 34, 48\].
