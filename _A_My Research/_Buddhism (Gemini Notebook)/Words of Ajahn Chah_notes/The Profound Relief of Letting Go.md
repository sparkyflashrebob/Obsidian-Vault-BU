# The Profound Relief of Letting Go

The story you are referring to actually involves a \*\*heavy branch\*\*, though Ajahn Chah frequently used a \*\*heavy rock\*\* as a powerful analogy to teach the same lesson about "letting go." 

\*\*The Story of the Heavy Branch:\*\*
One day, Ajahn Chah and a disciple were moving a large, heavy branch that was blocking their path \[1\]. While they were holding it up, ready to throw it into the forest, Ajahn Chah looked at his disciple and asked, \*\*"Is it heavy?"\*\* \[1\]. After they flung the branch away, he asked again, \*\*"Now, is it heavy?"\*\* \[1\]. He used this simple, everyday action to practically demonstrate the Dhamma and show the immediate relief that comes from "letting go" \[1\].

\*\*The Analogy of the Heavy Rock:\*\*
When teaching about the mind, Ajahn Chah regularly compared \*\*our pride and our sense of self to carrying a heavy rock\*\* \[2, 3\]. He noted that the teaching of "letting go" or "working with an empty mind" is often the hardest for people to understand because it conflicts with worldly opinions \[4, 5\]. People are afraid to let go of their sense of self because they fear that without it, there will be nothing left \[2, 3\]. 

He explained that we carry this heavy burden around, and even if someone advises us to drop it, we stubbornly clutch it, arguing, "If I throw it away, I won't have anything left!" \[4, 5\]. We endure the weight until it becomes so unbearably heavy and we become so exhausted that we finally have to drop it \[4, 5\]. \*\*It is only after we drop the rock that we immediately experience the lightness, ease, and comfort of not clinging\*\* \[2, 3, 6, 7\]. Ajahn Chah emphasized that before you actually let go of the burden, you cannot possibly know the profound benefits of doing so \[6, 7\].
