# Attaining and Developing Right View

is presented as a foundational and crucial aspect of the path, requiring specific considerations and practices.

#### Here are the various considerations regarding attaining right view:

#### Understanding the Nature of Right View:

#### Worldly Right View:

This type is

affected by taints

, partakes of merit, and ripens on the side of attachment \[1, 2\]. It involves the understanding that there is fruit and result of good and bad actions, there is this world and the other world, there are spontaneously reborn beings, and there are virtuous recluses and brahmans who have realized these things by direct knowledge \[1, 2\].

#### Noble or Supramundane Right View:

This type is

taintless, supramundane, and a factor of the path

\[2\]. It is described as the

, the faculty and power of wisdom, and the investigation of states enlightenment factor, present in one whose mind is noble and taintless and who is developing the noble path \[2\]. This right view is rooted in the understanding of the

Four Noble Truths

: suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the way leading to the cessation of suffering \[3, 4\]. It involves

seeing the origin and cessation of the world as it really is

with correct wisdom, transcending the notions of existence and non-existence \[5\]. With this view, one does not become engaged and cling through mental standpoints, adherence, or underlying tendencies, and there is no perplexity or doubt that what arises is only suffering arising and what ceases is only suffering ceasing \[5\].

#### Recognizing the Importance of Right View:

Right view is considered the

forerunner and precursor

\[6\] and the

for the arising and development of skillful states \[7\]. Conversely, wrong view is the basis for the arising and development of unskillful states, leading to undesirable results \[7\]. Cultivating right view leads to results that are desirable, pleasant, agreeable, yielding benefit, and conducive to the ending of suffering \[7\]. The path itself, including factors like right intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration, is presented as sequentially arising from right view in the disciple's higher training \[8\]. Right view also

slopes, slants, and inclines towards Nibbana

\[9\]. It is impossible for someone possessed of right view to regard any formation (conditioned phenomena) as permanent, a source of happiness, or as a self \[10\].

#### Practicing the Effort to Abandon Wrong View and Cultivate Right View:

Attaining right view involves the

effort to abandon wrong view and to enter upon right view

\[2, 8, 11, 12\]. This is one of the

four occasions for diligence

: giving up bad conduct in deeds, speech, and thought, and cultivating good conduct in deeds, speech, and thought; and giving up wrong view and cultivating right view, not being negligent in that \[10\].

#### Developing Mindfulness and Clear Comprehension:

Mindfulness and clear comprehension are presented as a

nutrient for proper attention

\[13\]. Mindfully, one abandons wrong view and enters upon and abides in right view \[2\]. When mindfulness and clear comprehension are present, they lead sequentially to sense restraint, virtue, true concentration, knowledge and vision of how it really is, disinterest, dispassion, and ultimately knowledge and vision of liberation \[14\]. Lack of mindfulness and clear comprehension, conversely, is a nutrient for improper attention, which nourishes lack of faith, listening to wrong teachings, association with bad people, lack of sense restraint, wrong conduct, the five hindrances, ignorance, and the craving for becoming \[15, 16\].

#### Cultivating Proper Attention (Yoniso Manasikara):

direct nutrient for mindfulness and clear comprehension

\[13\]. Proper attention is crucial because attending unwisely causes unarisen taints to arise and arisen taints to increase \[17, 18\].

Wise attention

involves attending to things fit for attention, which leads to the non-arising of unarisen taints and the abandonment of arisen taints, such as sensual desire, being, and ignorance \[3, 18\]. Things fit for attention include the Four Noble Truths \[3\]. Attending wisely to these leads to the abandonment of the fetters of personality view, doubt, and adherence to rules and observances, which are referred to as taints abandoned by seeing \[3\].

#### Fostering Faith (Saddha):

Proper attention nourishes faith, which is a nutrient for listening to the true dharma \[13\].

Faith in the Tathagata's enlightenment

is one of the five factors of striving, contributing to one's ability to bear the strain of striving and leading to welfare and happiness \[19\]. Lack of faith, stemming from improper attention, nourishes listening to wrong teachings and association with bad people \[16\]. This highlights how faith supports the diligence and effort needed for right view.

Listening to the True Dharma (Saddhamma Savana) and Associating with Superior People (Sappurisa Savana):

Proper attention, leading to faith, also nourishes listening to the true dharma and association with superior people \[13\]. This is part of the upstream conditions that support the development of wholesome states, including right view \[13\]. Conversely, improper attention leads to listening to wrong teachings and association with bad people \[16\].

#### Cultivating Virtue (Sila):

Right view is

helped by virtue

\[5, 10\]. Restraint of the senses is a nutrient for good conduct, which is a nutrient for the four foundations of mindfulness, which are a nutrient for the seven factors of enlightenment, which are a nutrient for liberation by supreme knowledge \[13\]. This chain underscores the supportive role of virtue for the practices leading to and involving right view.

#### Developing Tranquility (Samatha) and Insight (Vipassana):

Right view is

helped by tranquility and insight

\[10\]. While one person may excel in tranquility and another in insight, they can learn from each other, and the ideal is to gain both internal tranquility of mind and higher wisdom of insight into things \[20, 21\].

#### Understanding Impermanence (Anicca) and Non-Self (Anatta):

A person possessed of right view cannot regard any formation as permanent or as a self \[10\]. Cultivating the perception of impermanence helps establish the perception of non-self \[20\]. Right view involves seeing the impermanence of phenomena \[10\] and being free from the notion of self \[5\].
