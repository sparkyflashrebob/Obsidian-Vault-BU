Chapter 10, the heritage of the sassa. These 37 requisites of enlightenment treated in the preceding chapters are the heritage of the Buddha. They are the heritage of the sassana. They constitute gems of the sassana that are priceless and invaluable. I shall now examine what constitutes this heritage of the sassana. Sasana daja. By heritage is meant property given as legacy by parents to their children who are fit to receive it as heirs. Dada. This applies also to the heritage of the sassa which is the Buddha's heritage. Buddha daja. As to the nature of that heritage there are two kinds worldly amisa and dhama heritage. The worldly heritage consist of the four requisites of a biku namely arms food, robes, dwelling place and medicines. The dharma heritage are the three trainings sikka, sila, samadi, pa. The seven purifications and the 37 requisites of enlightenment. There are two kinds of dharma heritage namely mundane lokia and supra mundane locutara. The Mundane one consists of the above three trainings on the mundane level. The six mundane purifications and the requisites of enlightenment as far as associated with those mundane purifications. The supra mundane dharma heritage consists of the three trainings on the supra mundane level. The supra mundane seventh purification and the super mundane requisites of enlightenment. Mundane dharma heritage. may be divided into one that dependent on the round of rebirths and two that tending towards the ending of the round of rebirths we tan or into one stable dharma heritage nata and two unstable dharma heritage the practice of the tree trainings morality etc if d directed towards the attainment of worldly positions such as mentor or teacher of kings or governments or towards the acquisition of dignity, titles, degrees, power, retinue and property or towards the attainment of rebirth as noble and highly placed humans and das. This is called dharma heritage dependent on the round of rebirths. There are three forms of the round of rebirths, the round of defilements klesawata, the round of kama kamaata and the round of kama resultants. Vivata means nibbana which is the end of these three rounds of rebirths. The practice of morality, concentration and wisdom directed towards the ending of these rounds of rebirths is called dharma heritage tending towards the end of the round of rebirths. Persons who lack faith, sadda and zeal do not even conceive the idea that the higher attainments of the purification are the heritage which they can acquire in this very life because they lack energy. Vya they are reluctant to put forth effort that involves viations. They are liable to reject such effort as impossible because they are weak of will. Their minds are not fixed on such kind of work. They change their mind whenever they listen to various theories and expositions. Because they lack knowledge and wisdom, they reject such work as being beyond their capabilities. Therefore, the Buddha has urged all beings to strengthen their weak idipadas based of success such as zeal etc. Only then can new desires and new thoughts arise. Only those who possess one or other of the four idipadas as foundation can enjoy the full benefits of the Buddha's heritage. Others who are without any of these idipadas will get the opportunity to enjoy only some of the superficial benefits without the chance of enjoying the essence of the heritage. Some may not even have the opportunity to enjoy those superficial benefits because they have squandered the heritage and thus become severed from the Buddhas and the sass's heritages. The heirs of the sassa may be classified into one stable or constant heirs and two unstable or inconstant heirs. People who never obtain knowledge of imp permanence and not self within themselves are called unstable heirs. They may be disciples or heirs of the Buddha today and may become disciples or heirs of another teacher tomorrow. They may scorn and harm the Buddha sassa. Even in the present world there are persons who have changed their faith from the Buddha sassa to other religions and who scorn and undermine the sassana. How easily they can change after after death and in another birth can be imagined. Such a one can be a disciple of the Buddha this month and a disciple of another teacher next month and a disciple of the Buddha this year and the disciple of another teacher next year. A disciple of the Buddha this life and the disciple of another teacher in the next. Therefore, it is said that the putas worldlings are so-called because they look up to the faces of various teachers. This means that in the unfathomable past samsara, worldlings have never been constant in the choice of a teacher in whom they have taken refuge. The occasions on which they have approached a Buddha and taken refuge in him are very few indeed. Sometimes they took refuge in Brahma, sometimes in Saka Indra, sometimes in various deities, sometimes in planets, spirits and ogres and they have done so as if these refugees were almighty. The number of false teachers are very numerous in the world and so is the number of existences in which worldlings have taken refuge in such false teachers. While worldlings continue to wander and drift in samsara replete with false attachments to personality, belief they will continue to change their teachers. How frightful, terrible and repellent is the state of a worldling. Whenever a whirling changes his teacher and refuge, a change also occurs in the doctrines and principles on which he depends for his guidance. Sometimes worldling accept the purified morality adila of a Buddha but more often they accept the moral practices of numerous other teachers. Also in the matter of views, the existences in which they accept right view are extremely few while the lives in which they depend on wrong views are extremely numerous. Of the countless errors and perversities possessed by worldlings, that of seeking refuge in false teachers is one of the gravest errors causing them great harm. This is because taking refuge in wrong teachers leads to wrong moral principles and practices and thus the precious and rare achievement of rebirth as a human being. Manusa dulaba becomes entirely like a tree producing the evil fruits of rebirth in the worlds of misery instead of being like a great wishing tree bearing the fruits of good rebirths. This shows the future path of unstable heirs of the sassa. But those person Persons who perceive in themselves the characteristics of impermanence ana and not self anata are freed from the realm of personality belief. They become stable heirs of the sassa. Stable means here that throughout their future lives in sanssara they are no longer inclined to seek refuge in false teachers. They become true children and heirs of the Buddha throughout the future succession of their rebirths. They become members of b Tinsan family. Their views of the incomparable qualities of the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sana become clearer and brighter from one rebirth to another. All the Dharma heritages will prosper and increase in their minds. That is the three path categories morality etc. The seven purifications and the 37 requisites of enlightenment. They are beings who will invariably ascend to anupadisa nibbana the extinction of defilements without any groups or existence remaining. This shows the undeviating path of stable heirs of the sassa. Good and virtuous persons did not put forth effort in past existences which was aimed at their becoming heirs of bad heritages of the sassa. They did not practice morality, concentration and wisdom. in order to become heirs of unstable temporary heritages but because they wished to become heirs of the stable heritages. Taking these facts into account and taking heed of the fact that the Buddha disapproved of the bad heritages of the sassa. Those persons who have now become disciples of the Buddha should not permit themselves to become bad heirs or to become temporary unstable heirs. They should attempt to become heirs of the good heritages which are the requisites of enlightenment. The bodhic pakia dhhamas they should attempt to become stable heirs. As regards to person deficient in wisdom the mere performance of good and meritorious acts has to be encouraged as beneficial. But as to those persons who possess wisdom, if they desire to become stable heirs either in this life or the next, then they should establish lishing themselves firmly. Ajiva set up mindful body contemplation and try for at least 3 hours a day to achieve perception of the three characteristics of existence in the five aggregates of personality. If they perceive any of the three characteristics, they can become stable airs and attain to the status of bonsin, a stream enterer. Here ends the manual of the requ of enlightenment.
