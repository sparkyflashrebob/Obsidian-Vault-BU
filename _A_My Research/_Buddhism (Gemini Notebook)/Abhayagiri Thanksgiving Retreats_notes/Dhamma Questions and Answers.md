\*   \*\*Question:\*\* How to balance \*saṅvega\* (urgency) and patience. \[9\]
    \*   \*\*Summary:\*\* Balancing urgency and patience is assessed by the quality of mind it produces \[9\]. One should cultivate the quality that increases wholesome states (skillful, good, positive) and decreases unwholesome states (negativity, fear, tension, complacency) \[9\].
    \*   \*\*Details:\*\*
        \*   When contemplating balance, the standard is to come back and reflect on the \*\*quality of mind that is present or is created\*\* \[9\].
        \*   The increase of wholesome \*dhammas\* and a decrease of unwholesome \*dhammas\* is considered a good thing \[9\].
        \*   If one is putting attention onto \*saṅvega\* (urgency) and dispassion but what is created is more unwholesome states (negativity, fear, tension), then one has to throttle back a bit and be willing to be more patient \[9\].
        \*   Or, put attention on something that will bring up more wholesome and bright states of mind \[9\].
        \*   Similarly, if one is being patient to the point of complacency, and there is a lack of wholesome interest and urgency to practice or willingness to put forth wholesome effort, then it's out of balance \[9\].
        \*   The investigation is around the quality of mind and the \*\*balance of the wholesome and unwholesome\*\* \[9\].
        \*   One is always trying to encourage that which is wholesome, skillful, good, positive states of mind \[9\].
