# Key Instructions of the Buddha's Teaching

Based on the sources, the concept of "gradual instruction," or

anupubbi katha

in Pali, is a teaching method often found in the sutras \[1\]. It describes how the Buddha would give instruction in a step-by-step manner to accommodate the nature of the human mind, allowing understanding and the ability to comprehend things clearly and deeply to build progressively \[1\]. This is compared to using a set of stairs or an elevator to reach a top floor, rather than expecting someone to get there instantly \[1\].

This graduated teaching typically begins with topics such as generosity (

), virtue (

), heavenly states, drawbacks of worldly things, and renunciation, culminating in the Four Noble Truths \[1\]. This gradual approach is considered appropriate for the human condition and helps lay a foundation of goodness, virtue, and right practice \[1\]. The Noble Eightfold Path is also seen as another expression of this gradual instruction and training \[2\].

While the term "general talks" isn't explicitly defined as distinct from "dharma instructions," the sources describe dharma talks as the means by which these teachings are conveyed, explained, and explored \[3, 4\]. These talks draw upon and elaborate on the specific instructions found in the Buddha's teachings (the sutras) and experienced through practice \[3\].

The instructions provided within the context of these dharma talks encompass a wide range of practices, principles, and contemplations aimed at cultivating understanding and liberation. These include:

#### Core Teachings:

#### The Four Noble Truths:

This is considered the most important or central teaching of the Buddha \[5, 6\]. The instructions regarding the Four Noble Truths involve specific "duties" or "responsibilities" (

) towards each truth: the truth of suffering (

) is to be apprehended or understood; the truth of the cause of suffering is to be abandoned or relinquished; the truth of the cessation of suffering is to be realized; and the truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering is to be cultivated \[6-8\]. Reflecting on suffering and its cessation serves as a key point of investigation \[6\].

#### The Noble Eightfold Path:

This is another structured path presented by the Buddha \[2, 9-11\]. The term "right" in the Eightfold Path (such as right view, right intention, etc.) is often understood as being "attuned" or "in tune" with the Dhamma and harmonizing with other factors of the path and the world, rather than exclusively meaning correct as opposed to wrong \[2, 9, 12\]. It is considered the "stream" that leads to stream entry \[13\].

#### Foundational Practices and Principles:

Taking refuge in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha is a central piece of the teaching and practice \[14\]. It involves recollecting the qualities of the Buddha (awakened, knowing, bright, clear, wisdom, compassion, purity), the Dhamma (the true nature of things, truth, teachings), and the Sangha (those who practice well, the community of practitioners, virtue, integrity) \[14-20\]. This provides a container for practice and helps step out of a personal, self-centered perspective \[14, 18, 20\].

Keeping precepts is a fundamental basis and anchor for practice \[18, 21-24\]. They cultivate virtue, integrity, honesty, harmlessness, and simplicity \[14, 18, 20, 25\]. Keeping precepts leads to non-remorse and well-being \[14, 23\]. Precepts are seen as a training, a way to educate oneself on skillful living, rather than strict commandments \[26\].

#### Cultivation of Mental Qualities:

Mindfulness (

Cultivating mindfulness is a foundation and a distinguishing characteristic of the Buddha's teaching \[27\]. It involves ardency (effort to burn up defilements), clear comprehension or awareness, and heat \[27-29\]. Mindfulness is alert to the quality of the mind and adjusts effort accordingly \[28\]. It is presented as a path to the deathless \[30\].

Concentration (

This is defined as the firm establishing or unification of the mind \[31\]. A settled, peaceful mind is able to understand things clearly \[32, 33\]. Developing concentration can lead to pleasant abiding in the present moment and facilitate deeper insight \[32, 34, 35\]. Learning to allow the mind to settle is key \[33, 36\].

Wisdom/Discernment (

This is the ability to see clearly, understand principles of Dhamma, and discern one's experience \[35, 37-40\]. It is essential for distinguishing wholesome from unwholesome states based on whether they lead to suffering or well-being \[41-43\]. Wisdom, cultivated through practice, is considered a deeper and more reliable understanding than knowledge gained through hearing or thinking alone \[25\]. It must be balanced with faith \[38\].

This refers to the direct penetration and understanding of the nature of reality, particularly the three characteristics of existence \[35, 44-46\]. Deep reflection on impermanence, unsatisfactoriness, and not-self leads to insight \[44, 47\]. Insight can arise from contemplating various aspects of experience and helps to dismantle clinging and identification \[37, 44, 45, 47-50\].

Relinquishment/Letting Go (

This is presented as a powerful tool and a necessary aspect of the practice \[37, 51\]. It involves learning how to put things down without pushing them away or picking them up again \[51\]. Relinquishment balances the aspect of holding on, as in keeping precepts \[52\]. It is seen as essential for freedom from suffering \[7, 23, 47, 53-57\].

Wise Reflection/Appropriate Attention (

Yonisomanasikara

This is the skill of directing one's attention in a skillful way, attending to the root of things \[58-62\]. It is an internal condition that supports progress on the path, including the realization of stream entry \[58, 59, 61, 62\].

#### Specific Practices and Contemplations:

Mindfulness of Breathing (

Anapanasati

This is a key meditation object presented with 16 steps tied to the four foundations of mindfulness (body, feeling, mind, dhammas) \[63-67\]. It is a practice of great fruit and benefit \[65\]. These steps can be used as entry points for practice and exploration \[67\].

#### Contemplation of the Three Characteristics:

Reflecting deeply on impermanence (

), unsatisfactoriness (

), and not-self (

) is highly encouraged \[8, 35, 44, 47, 49, 50, 68\]. This helps to see conditions as they truly are and undermine clinging \[44, 47, 49, 50\]. It can be done by mentally "tossing" experiences into the "waste bins" of these characteristics \[44, 69\].

Loving Kindness (

) and the Divine Abidings (

Brahma Viharas

Cultivating loving kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity are wholesome practices \[70-72\]. Loving kindness can be developed through phrases directed towards oneself and others \[71\]. These qualities help gladden the mind and are considered protective meditations \[72-74\].

Contemplation of the Body (

Kayanupassana

This involves focusing attention on the physical body as an anchor for mindfulness \[66, 75-77\]. Contemplations like the 32 parts of the body can be used \[76\].

Investigation of Phenomena (

Dhamma Vicaya

Applying wise reflection to various themes and aspects of experience, such as the five hindrances, dependent origination, wholesome/unwholesome states, and the nature of the mind itself, is an important practice \[6, 35, 37, 39, 43, 50, 56, 57, 60, 69, 76, 78-90\].

Sense Restraint (

Indriyasamvara

Training the mind to not grasp at the signs and features of sense objects, maintaining balance between attraction and aversion, is crucial \[91\]. This is understood as restraint of the mind, not just physical avoidance \[91\].

#### Attitudinal and Supportive Instructions:

#### Steady Practice:

Maintaining continuity and consistent application of effort is considered the essence of right practice \[92\].

#### Balancing Qualities:

Recognizing that various spiritual faculties (faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom) and practices (calm/insight, precepts/Dhamma) need to be in balance and work together \[10, 33, 38, 74, 93-95\].

#### Practicing Dhamma in Accordance with Dhamma:

Ensuring that one's practice is grounded in the truth of the teachings rather than personal preferences or projections \[13, 43, 48, 58, 59, 62, 84, 96, 97\].

#### Delighting in Non-Complication:

Recognizing and actively choosing to simplify the mind, as the default human habit is often to complicate things \[20, 23, 88, 98\].

#### Learning to Get Out of the Way:

Letting go of self-importance and allowing the fundamental truths to resonate in the heart is an essential part of the practice \[17, 88\].

Cultivating gratitude is encouraged as a quality that pulls away from selfishness \[22, 99\].

#### Listening to Dhamma:

Approaching the hearing of teachings as a practice in itself, with an open and receptive heart, is important \[3, 100\].

#### Skillful Use of Thought:

Employing thought mindfully and with discernment for beneficial purposes, rather than getting lost in random proliferation \[52, 76, 87, 101\].

These instructions are not merely theoretical but are meant to be investigated and applied through personal experience and integrated into all aspects of daily life \[29, 43, 50, 52, 89, 102, 103\]. The dharma talks serve as the vehicle for presenting, clarifying, and encouraging the application of these instructions \[3, 4\].
