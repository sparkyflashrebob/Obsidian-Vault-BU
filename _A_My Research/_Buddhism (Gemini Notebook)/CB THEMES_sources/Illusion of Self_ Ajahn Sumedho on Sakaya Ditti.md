# Illusion of Self: Ajahn Sumedho on Sakaya Ditti

These are what Ajahn Sumdho has talked about in the 108 Talks Series.

Based on the provided sources,

Self / Personality, referred to in Pali as Sakaya Ditti, is a central concept

discussed by Ajahn Sumedho within the larger context of his teachings on practice and liberation. It is consistently presented as an

illusion, a delusion, and a significant obstacle

to realizing the awakened state.

#### Here's what the sources say about Self / Personality (Sakaya Ditti):

#### Definition and Nature:

Sakaya Ditti is translated as

personality view or self view

It is described as a

created sense of a self

separate sense of self

It involves the

blind assumption or unquestioned allegiance

that "I am" certain conditions.

This created self is something

acquired after birth

and becoming conscious; it is

conditioned and artificial

, not natural.

It is built upon

identifying with the five

(aggregates)

: the physical body, feelings, perceptions, mental formations (conceptions), and consciousness. It's seeing these aggregates in terms of belonging to "this person".

It is the identification with one's body, memories, thoughts, emotional habits, tendencies, inclinations, preferences, likes, dislikes, cultural background, position, gender, assumptions, attitudes, ideals, and identities.

The use of pronouns like

"me and mine," "I am," and "tomato"

reinforces this sense of self. Saying "I have to think I am" highlights that this sense of self is a thought.

Society and conventional reality affirm this sense of self.

#### Sakaya Ditti as an Obstruction (Fetter):

Sakaya Ditti is the

first of the Ten Fetters (Syojanas)

that bind beings to suffering.

It is described as a

big obstruction

fetter that binds us to delusion and suffering

Breaking through this delusion is seen as crucial, particularly for the first stage of realization, Stream Entry (Sodapanna).

#### Consequences of Operating from Sakaya Ditti:

Operating from Sakaya Ditti or being

blindly, ignorantly attached

to the sense of self is presented as the

cause of suffering (Dukka)

, particularly the suffering referred to in the First Noble Truth.

It creates a

sense of separateness and isolation

It limits one to conventional realities and prevents seeing beyond conditions.

It leads to getting

caught up in one's own fears, anxieties, desires, opinions, views, ideals, and assumptions

Analyzing oneself from this position only creates a more complicated and stuck sense of self. Trying to figure out

the personality is a certain way reinforces the delusion.

It can lead to clinging to views, even views about Buddhism or the teachings themselves.

The "suffering of being somebody," of being a person, of having an identity with conditions is emphasized.

Believing what the ego tells you, believing your feelings, opinions, and views unquestionably, is being "born into the realm of death" (Marowati).

#### Practice and Seeing Through Sakaya Ditti:

The practice is

not about denying self or trying to get rid of it

. It's not an attack, annihilation, suppression, or rejection. Trying to destroy the self can even be another form of Sakaya Ditti ("I'm somebody who's trying to destroy the self").

recognition and observation

of Sakaya Ditti.

This is done through

awareness, mindfulness (Sati), awakened consciousness (Buddha/Puto)

Awareness is the

knower (Puto)

, the witness, rather than the person trying to know or observe. It is the

natural state

Being the observer allows one to

see the personality or the sense of me and mine as a mental object

recognize it without criticizing or adding to it

Practice involves

contemplating the present moment

and observing the conditions that constitute the apparent self: feelings, thoughts, physical sensations, assumptions, attitudes, ideals, identities, etc..

The aim is to

discern the difference

between the conventional reality of the self/personality and the ultimate reality of Anatta (not-self).

Reflecting on the first three fetters

(Sakaya Ditti, doubt, attachment to rites/rituals) is presented as a very helpful tool for observing oneself and breaking through delusion.

Buddhist teachings and forms like the Four Noble Truths, the Three Characteristics (especially Anatta), the Five Aggregates, and the Ten Fetters are

tools and pointers

to be used for this direct investigation, not objects for intellectual grasping or belief from the ego level.

Taking Refuge in Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha

is encouraged as a way to internalize the principles and trust in the non-personal awareness rather than identifying with external forms, teachers, or one's own personality. It's a refuge in awakened consciousness, truth, and the practice of goodness, transcending personal identity.

monastic life (Vinaya)

and its forms are described as

skillful means and conventions

intended to support the development of awareness and loosen attachment to conditioned phenomena, including the personality view. They are not meant to be identities themselves.

By observing and recognizing Sakaya Ditti, one learns to

no longer believe in it

or operate from the position of the ego.

This leads to a shift from the "highly emotive, painful, personal position" to the perspective of non-personal awareness.

The ultimate goal is the realization of

liberation from ignorance, conditioned phenomena, and the suffering that comes from being blindly attached to the sense of self as a person

. It's about realizing

Anatta (not-self)

– seeing that there is no permanent, separate self – which is peace. This is the

Deathless reality

In essence, Ajahn Sumedho emphasizes that the personality view is a deeply ingrained illusion causing suffering. His teachings provide practical methods, rooted in cultivating present moment awareness, to directly observe and understand this created self, thereby lessening and eventually relinquishing the blind attachment to it, leading to liberation.
