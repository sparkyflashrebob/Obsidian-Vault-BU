# Sakkaya Ditthi: The Wrong View of Self

In Buddhist philosophy, Sakkaya Ditthi, often translated as "personality view" or "self-identity view," refers to the belief that there is a permanent, unchanging self or ego. It's a misconstrual of the five aggregates (rupa, vedana, samjna, samskara, and vijnana) as a single, abiding self. Overcoming this view is considered a crucial step towards enlightenment and liberation.

#### Elaboration:

#### The Five Aggregates:

The five aggregates are the five basic components of experience: form (physical body), feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness. Sakkaya Ditthi mistakenly views these as a single, permanent entity – the self.

#### Wrong View:

Sakkaya Ditthi is considered a "wrong view" (Miccha Ditthi) because it misinterprets the impermanent and interdependent nature of the five aggregates.

#### Impact on Suffering:

This mistaken belief is a root cause of suffering and attachment. It leads to the clinging to a false sense of self, which in turn fuels greed, aversion, and fear.

#### Breaking the Fetter:

Sakkaya Ditthi is the first of the three fetters that must be overcome to attain the state of "stream-winning" or "arahatship," which marks the first stage of liberation in Theravada Buddhism.

#### Overcoming Sakkaya Ditthi:

It's achieved through understanding the nature of the five aggregates, practicing mindfulness and awareness (sati-sampajañña), and cultivating right view (Samma Ditthi).

#### Related Terms:

Sakkaya Ditthi is closely related to other concepts like Anatta (no-self) and the three characteristics of existence (impermanence, suffering, and no-self).
