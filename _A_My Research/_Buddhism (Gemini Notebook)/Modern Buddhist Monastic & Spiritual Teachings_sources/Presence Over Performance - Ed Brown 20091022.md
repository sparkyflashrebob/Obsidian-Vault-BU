#### AI Summary:

The talk examines the importance of authentic perception and observation in Zen practice, contrasting this with the common desire to conform to societal expectations or to dissociate from uncomfortable experiences. It emphasizes meditation as a practice of presence and acceptance, where one learns to face both pleasant and unpleasant experiences without seeking to control them. This shift from performance (seeking approval) to presence (accepting reality) underlies the speaker's approach to spiritual maturity and the essence of Zen Buddhism.

#### References:

"Zazen meditation is beyond human agency," translated by Masao Abe: This suggests that Zazen meditation transcends personal control, encouraging practitioners to engage fully with their experiences without trying to manipulate them.

Mention of Dogen: Refers to teachings that advocate undergoing meditation beyond one's control, highlighting the importance of engaging fully with each moment.

Reference to "The Old Testament": Used to illustrate the contrast between conventional, punitive conceptions of divine or parental authority and Buddhist notions of presence and personal experience.
