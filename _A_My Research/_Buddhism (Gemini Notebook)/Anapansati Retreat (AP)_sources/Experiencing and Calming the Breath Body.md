# Experiencing and Calming the Breath Body

Drawing on the sources and our conversation,

Experiencing the Breath (Kaya Sankhara)

is presented as a crucial stage within the larger context of Mindfulness of Breathing (Anapanasati), marking a significant shift in the practice.

#### Here's what the sources say:

#### Anapanasati as the Foundation and Training Ground:

Mindfulness of Breathing (

Anapanasati

) is described as the

very stable and concentrated foundation

of practice \[1, 2\]. It is essential, important, and necessary for cultivating the mind \[1, 2\]. While the initial stage involves simply knowing the short or long breath clearly (

) as it is \[3, 4\], the sources emphasize that from the

second stage onwards

, the Buddha uses the verb

"to train oneself"

\[4\]. This signifies that Anapanasati becomes a foundation for active training and studying, making things known within oneself through experience \[4\].

#### Kaya Sankhara: Experiencing the Breath and Body:

The second stage explicitly involves

experiencing (

) the bodily formations (

kaya sankhara

\[4\]. This stage is contrasted with merely "watching" the breath from a distance, emphasizing the importance of

experiencing fully

being really present for

what the body is doing while breathing \[5\]. It involves

entering into and abiding in the experience

\[5\], attending to sensations like tension, balance, relaxation, or knotting within the body as one breathes \[5\].

Kaya sankhara

can also be understood as the "breath body," focusing on the sensation of the breath itself as it moves in and out \[6\]. This can be experienced at a single point, like the tip of the nose, or as the entire feeling of the breath moving through the body, such as the abdomen rising and falling \[6\]. The sources note that the experience of the breath might not always align with a rational understanding of the physical body, and one should attend to the experience itself without getting distracted \[6, 7\].

#### Calming Kaya Sankhara as Part of the Training:

Integral to this stage is

calming the bodily formations (

kaya sankhara

\[4\]. The training involves using the breath and body to cultivate balance and clarity \[6\]. The purpose of calming the body is to make it

ready for work

, specifically ready for the work of being a meditator and

ready for the Dharma

\[8, 9\]. This calming and tranquilizing prepares the mind and body to be receptive to truth and conducive to liberation \[9, 10\].
