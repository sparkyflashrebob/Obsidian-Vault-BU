#### Here's what the sources say about Gladdening the Mind in this context:

#### Position and Introduction:

Gladdening the Mind is stated as the next step after Experiencing the Mind in the Anapanasati Sutta \[1, 2\]. The formula is "Gladdening the mind, I breathe in; Gladdening the mind, I breathe out" \[1\]. This stage is situated in the later part of the practice sequence, building upon the foundation of attending to the body, feelings, and the nature of the mind itself \[4, 5\].

What is "Gladdening the Mind"?

It involves a sense of

bringing delight to the mind

brightening it

with qualities of well-being \[1, 6-8\]. It's described as allowing the mind to truly

delight in the practice

, in the Dharma, and in stillness \[1\]. This is contrasted with the usual tendency to seek happiness, pleasure, and delight from the external world or from mental proliferation and fantasy \[1\].

#### Purpose and Significance:

#### Necessary for Mental Development:

Gladdening the mind is considered a very necessary part of the development of the mind \[1\].

#### Balances Investigation/Analysis:

It helps to

brighten the mind and bring it back to a point of balance

when one might get too absorbed into investigative or analytical aspects of meditation \[3, 9\]. While earlier stages might involve investigating

citta sankhara

(mind conditioners) \[3\], there's always a need to brighten the mind \[3\].

#### Fine-tuning Tranquility and Insight:

Gladdening the mind is part of the continuous process of

#### Supports Concentration:

It is explicitly stated that gladdening the mind

helps the mind become very pliant and malleable

, contributing to the ability to concentrate \[2\]. A mind that is gladdened settles into concentration naturally from happiness, which follows tranquility, joy, and gladness in a natural sequence \[7, 11\].

#### Prepares Mind for Dharma:

The sources suggest that cultivating qualities like brightness and a certain gentleness or softness in the mind, which gladdening contributes to, is necessary for the mind to be

ready to receive the Dharma

#### Cuts Through Desire:

Contemplations that lead to gladdening can help in

cutting through the "desire mind"

that is constantly seeking gratification and satisfaction \[13\].

#### How it is Cultivated:

In essence, Gladdening the Mind in Anapanasati is an active, yet natural, stage of cultivating positive mental states, particularly brightness and delight, which emerges from the skillful management of hindrances and the settling of the mind through earlier steps. It serves as a vital balance to investigative practices and is foundational for achieving deeper concentration and preparing the mind to receive the liberating truth of the Dharma \[8, 10-12, 17\]. It represents a subtle but necessary cultivation of an internal sense of well-being and composure \[2, 3, 10\].
