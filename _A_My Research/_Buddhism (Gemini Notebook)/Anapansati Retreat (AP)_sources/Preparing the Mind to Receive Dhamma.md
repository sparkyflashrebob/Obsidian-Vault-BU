# Preparing the Mind to Receive Dhamma

Based on the sources and our conversation, the concept of "Preparing the Mind to Receive Dhamma" is presented as a fundamental purpose and result of cultivating Mindfulness of Breathing (Anapanasati). Anapanasati serves as the primary method for this preparation.

#### Here's a discussion of what the sources say about this preparation:

#### Anapanasati as the Essential Foundation and Training:

Mindfulness of Breathing is repeatedly described as the

very stable and concentrated foundation

of practice \[1\]. It is presented as essential and important \[1\]. From the second stage onwards, the Buddha uses the verb

"to train oneself"

\[2\]. This highlights that Anapanasati is not just observation but an active process of

training, studying, and making known within oneself through one's experience

the results of the practice \[2, 3\]. This training is explicitly linked to the purpose of preparing the mind.

#### The Purpose of the Whole Practice:

The overarching purpose of the entire practice, including Anapanasati, is to

create balance and clarity of mind

\[4\]. This state of balance and clarity is crucial because it

allows the mind to be able to see and understand things clearly, see and understand things as they truly are

\[2, 4\]. This clear seeing is what ultimately liberates the mind \[2\]. The sources state that if the mind is not clear and balanced, it

never has that opportunity to see in that way

\[4\]. Therefore, preparing the mind means bringing it to this state of clarity and balance.

#### Making the Mind "Ready for Dhamma":

The sources specifically state that calming the body (part of the

kaya sankhara

stage in Anapanasati) and calming the mind make the mind

ready for work

ready for the Dhamma

\[5, 6\]. This readiness involves cultivating specific qualities:

The mind needs to be prepared to

receive the Dharma graciously, gratefully, appreciatively, delightfully

awareness and a kind of brightness

It requires

a kind of softness or gentleness

#### How Anapanasati Prepares the Mind:

The sequential training described in the Anapana Sutta lays the groundwork for these qualities:

Knowing the Breath Clearly (

: The initial stage establishes basic attention on the breath sensation \[7\].

Experiencing and Calming Bodily Formations (

Kaya Sankhara

: This involves entering into the full bodily experience of the breath, noticing sensations, and calming them \[2, 5, 8\]. This physical settling

calms the body

primary condition for alleviating restlessness

, which is a hindrance to clear seeing \[9\]. Calming the body and mind makes them

ready to receive the Dharma

Experiencing and Calming Feelings and Mind Formations

: This stage involves cultivating mindfulness of feelings (

) and mind states (

), understanding their arising and passing \[10-12\]. Learning to relate skillfully to feelings and perceptions, even unpleasant ones, helps prepare the mind to use them as opportunities for growth and cultivating wholesome qualities \[13, 14\]. It involves discerning the mind itself from its passing moods \[15, 16\], which contributes to clarity.

Gladdening the Mind (

: This is a necessary part of mental development, involving bringing

delight and brightness

to the mind through the practice itself \[17, 18\]. This is not seeking external pleasure, but finding contentment in stillness and relinquishment \[17\]. This gladdening is essential for further reflections and discriminations needed for insight \[18\], creating a "place of stillness and brightness" \[18\]. It's part of a natural sequence leading to joy, tranquility, happiness, and concentration \[19\].

Concentrating the Mind (

: Establishing firm concentration settles the mind \[20, 21\], pulls it together, focuses, and

purifies it of coarser elements

that agitate and stain it \[20\]. This purification is likened to cleaning windows to see through them \[20\]. Concentration makes the mind pliable and malleable \[20\], leading to a

pleasant abiding here and now

\[21\] and developing the

capacity for knowledge and vision

sharp mindfulness and alertness

to feelings, perceptions, and thoughts \[23\]. This settled, purified, and concentrated state is necessary for seeing things clearly and understanding their implications \[4, 23, 24\].

Contemplating Insight (

Dhammanupassana

: The final stage builds directly on the cultivated state, contemplating impermanence \[25\], dispassion \[26\], cessation \[27\], and relinquishment \[28\]. These insights into the true nature of reality are the direct "reception" of Dhamma's core truths, made possible by the prepared mind \[25, 29\].

#### Overcoming Hindrances:

The training inherent in Anapanasati is crucial for overcoming the Five Hindrances (sensual desire, ill will, sloth/torpor, restlessness/worry, skeptical doubt) \[30-32\]. These hindrances obstruct clarity and peace \[31\], making the mind impure and unable to see clearly \[24\]. Learning to recognize and relinquish them \[30, 33\] is vital for preparing the mind to be like

clear, still water

where things can be seen as they are \[24\]. Using mindfulness of breathing to calm and settle is the primary condition for alleviating restlessness \[9\], and developing awareness and attention helps dispel sloth and torpor \[31, 34\]. Guarding the mind from unskillful states is a key function of mindfulness \[35, 36\].

#### Beyond Mechanics:

The sources caution against simply going through the mechanics of the practice \[37\]. The preparation involves

learning how to attend, how to train, how to experience things fully, and how to know things clearly

\[3\]. It's about cultivating the right qualities and intending to make the mind ready to

experience the Dhamma, experience truth, and realize the fruits of the Buddha's teaching leading to liberation

\[6, 37\]. This readiness is the crucial point \[6\].

In essence, Mindfulness of Breathing, through its progressive stages of training (

) – from establishing clear attention on the breath, to experiencing and calming the body/breath, working with feelings and mind states, cultivating positive states like gladness and concentration, overcoming hindrances, and finally contemplating fundamental truths – systematically prepares the mind by developing the necessary qualities of balance, clarity, stability, and receptivity required to truly receive the profound insights of the Dhamma and realize liberation \[2-6, 37\].
