# Understanding the Roots of Disputes

Based on the sources, \*\*disputes\*\*, along with terms like \*\*quarrels\*\*, \*\*brawling\*\*, \*\*wrangling\*\*, and \*\*contention\*\*, are discussed as phenomena that arise from specific conditions and are generally seen as obstructive or undesirable states \[1-7\]. They are contrasted with harmonious states like living in concord with mutual appreciation \[7, 8\].

The sources provide several insights into what disputes are about and where they originate:

1.  \*\*Harboring ill will and thoughts of being wronged:\*\* Disputes can arise from clinging to thoughts such as "He abused me. He struck me. He defeated me. He robbed me" \[2\]. When such thoughts are harbored, hatred will never be allayed by further acts of hate; rather, it is allayed by non-hatred \[2\]. This suggests that at their root, disputes can stem from past perceived offenses and the failure to let go of ill will.
2.  \*\*Unwillingness to approve of others' well-stated views:\*\* When the Dhamma is taught through different methods of exposition, those who "will not concede, allow and approve of what is wellstated and well spoken by others" may become "contentious and quarrelsome and engage in dispute" and "dwell stabbing each other with verbal daggers" \[3\]. This indicates disputes can be about disagreements over teachings or explanations, particularly when there is a lack of openness to others' perspectives \[3\].
3.  \*\*Engaging in argumentative talk:\*\* Disputes manifest as engaging in talk aimed at refuting others, arguing about who understands the Dhamma and discipline correctly, who is practicing rightly or wrongly, and finding fault with the structure or consistency of others' arguments \[4\]. To not engage in dispute is to abstain from such talk \[4\].
4.  \*\*Attachment to views and being affected by defilements:\*\* Disputes are closely linked to \*\*views\*\* \[5, 9, 10\]. Specifically, relying on, adopting, and accepting specific \*\*dogmatic views\*\* (such as the view of being or the view of non-being) and being opposed to other views is a source of contention \[5, 9\]. This happens when individuals do not understand the origin, disappearance, gratification, danger, and escape in the case of these views as they actually are \[9\]. Those caught in such disputes are described as being \*\*affected by lust, hate, delusion, craving, and clinging\*\* \[9\]. They are also characterized by a lack of vision, being given to favoring and opposing, and delighting in proliferation \[9\].
5.  \*\*Holding a dogmatic view as supreme and disparaging others:\*\* A person who abides by a certain dogmatic view, considering it the highest, and disparages other views, is not free from disputes \[5\]. Such a person clings passionately to what they have seen, heard, recognized, or to rules and rites, seeing everything else as inferior \[5\]. This clinging to one's own view is described as a \*\*bond\*\* \[5\]. A sage, being free from the notion of self and clinging, does not take sides in controversy and has no dogmatic views \[5\].
6.  \*\*Having preferences and holding things dear:\*\* A direct cause of quarrels, disputes, lamentation, sorrows, selfishness, conceit, pride, and divisiveness is having \*\*preferences, from holding things precious and dear\*\* \[6, 11\].
7.  \*\*The chain of conditions leading to disputes:\*\* The impulse of \*\*desire\*\* is the source of preferences and things held dear \[11\]. Desire arises when people think of one thing as \*\*pleasant and another as unpleasant\*\* \[12\]. This perception of pleasant and unpleasant comes from \*\*contact (mental impression)\*\* \[12\]. Contact exists because the compound of mind and matter exists \[12\]. The \*\*habit of grasping\*\* is based on \*\*wanting things\*\* \[12\]. This detailed explanation traces disputes back through a chain of conditions rooted in contact and desire \[6, 11, 12\].
8.  \*\*Anger and bitterness:\*\* While also contributing to ill will, anger and bitterness towards others (especially companions) arising from perceived slights regarding offenses or admonishment are identified as a "blemish" \[13\]. Such states contribute to the mind's defilement and can fuel disputes \[13\].

In essence, the sources portray disputes as stemming from various unwholesome mental states and behaviors, particularly those related to clinging, desire, aversion, delusion, the attachment to views, and the inability or unwillingness to see reality clearly and accept different perspectives \[2, 3, 5, 6, 9-13\]. The absence of these defilements and the cultivation of qualities like non-hatred, wise understanding, and non-clinging lead to harmony and freedom from dispute \[2, 3, 5, 7-9\].
