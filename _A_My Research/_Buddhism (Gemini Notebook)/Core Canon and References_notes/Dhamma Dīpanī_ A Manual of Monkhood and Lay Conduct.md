# Dhamma Dīpanī: A Manual of Monkhood and Lay Conduct

\*A Manual of the Dhamma\*, also known as \*Dhamma Dīpanī\*, by Venerable Ledi Sayādaw, is structured around thirteen questions posed by seven laymen from Okkan village in July 1901 \[1, 2\]. These questions primarily concern the "problems of monkhood and its relationship with the laity" \[2\].

The thirteen questions are as follows:
1.  \*\*Defining Monk Types:\*\* What are the factors or characteristics of the three types of monks—\*lajjī\* (scrupulous monk with a moral conscience), \*alajjī\* (shameless monk with no moral conscience), and \*dussīlo\* (immoral, bad, fallen monk)—as mentioned in Pāḷi texts, Commentaries, and Subcommentaries? \[3, 4\]
2.  \*\*Associating with Shameless/Immoral Monks:\*\* Should those who know the truth about shameless and immoral monks refrain from associating with and paying respect to them? Does this align with the \*Maṅgala Sutta\*'s advice to avoid the foolish (\*asevanā ca bālānaṃ\*)? Is a lay person who disregards bad monks following the injunction of the \*Maṅgala Dhamma\*? The question also asks for scriptural evidence and examples of the results of such actions \[3\].
3.  \*\*Paying Respect to Bad Monks:\*\* If a person pays respect and shows deference (e.g., greeting, bowing) to a monk known to be shameless or immoral, does this fulfill the \*Maṅgala Dhamma\*'s directive to pay respect to the worthy or honorable ones (\*gāravo\*)? Does this behavior align with the text that advises respecting only those with good conduct (from the \*Kosala Saṃyutta\*)? Does worshipping bad monks constitute a reliable refuge, and what evidence or examples demonstrate the correct approach? \[5\]
4.  \*\*Speaking Ill of Shameless/Immoral Monks:\*\* If one speaks ill of or condemns a monk, directly or indirectly, knowing him to be shameless or immoral, does one incur ten evil punishments, or is one free from evil with this act? \[6\]
5.  \*\*Shameless Monk's Path to Scrupulousness:\*\* If a shameless monk becomes afraid of suffering in saṃsāra or acquires moral dread, how can he become a scrupulous monk? Is such a transformation possible? \[6\]
6.  \*\*Lay Persons Learning Vinaya:\*\* Should lay persons learn the Vinaya (monastic discipline)? Does this type of learning agree with the \*Maṅgala Dhamma\*'s advice to be well-trained in discipline (\*vinayo ca susikkhito\*)? What are the good or bad results of this act, with supporting evidence or examples? \[6\]
7.  \*\*Monk Teaching Vinaya to Lay Persons:\*\* Should a monk teach monastic discipline to a lay person? What are the good or bad results of this, with supporting evidence? \[7\]
8.  \*\*Four Purifying Moralities:\*\* What are the detailed factors or characteristics of each of the four purifying moralities (\*pārisuddhi sīla\*)? This includes their characteristic, function, manifestation, and proximate cause \[7\].
9.  \*\*Effects of Transgressing/Observing Monastic Restraint:\*\* Among the four purifying moralities, what are the bad effects if a monk transgresses basic monastic restraint (\*Pāṭimokkha saṃvara sīla\*)? What are the good effects if a monk observes it? The question also seeks an explanation of the good or bad effects of observing or not observing the remaining three types of purifying morality \[7\].
10. \*\*Factors of Offerings to the Saṅgha:\*\* What are the factors of offerings made to the entire Saṅgha (\*saṅghikadāna\*)? How can this type of donation be performed? \[4\]
11. \*\*Merit of Donations:\*\* Of the two types of donation—offerings to the Saṅgha and offerings to the Enlightened One—which has greater merit? \[4\]
