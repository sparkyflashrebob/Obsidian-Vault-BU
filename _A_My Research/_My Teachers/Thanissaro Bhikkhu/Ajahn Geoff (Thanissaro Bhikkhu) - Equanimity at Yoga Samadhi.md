# 🎙️ Ajahn Geoff (Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu) - Equanimity at Yoga Samadhi
### *Ven. Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu (Ajahn Geoff) — White Salmon, WA (2018)*

> *"Many modern teachers present equanimity as the ultimate goal—just passively accepting whatever happens, not taking sides. But the Buddha never taught passive indifference! Equanimity (upekkhā) is not the goal; it is a tool. We fabricate our experience for a purpose: to put an end to suffering. When a problem can be fixed, you exert effort; when a condition cannot be changed right now, you use equanimity to keep the mind steady so you don't waste vital energy."* — **Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu [00:00 / 09:33 / 18:53]**

## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Venerable Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu (Ajahn Geoff).

* **Theme**: The authentic role of equanimity (*Upekkhā*) in Buddhist practice; refuting the misconception that equanimity is passive resignation or non-judgmental indifference; intentional fabrication (*saṅkhāra*); equanimity as a strategic tool to end suffering.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered during an evening teaching at the Yoga Samadhi studio in White Salmon, Washington, clarifying the teleological (goal-oriented) nature of the Buddha's path.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Geoff opens with a critique of contemporary Western spirituality that reduces Buddhist wisdom to passive acceptance of whatever is happening. Drawing on the Pāli suttas, he demonstrates that the Buddha was actively goal-oriented: the entire Dhamma exists for a single purpose—to end suffering (*dukkhassa anta*). Experience is not a fixed reality we passively endure; it is an active fabrication (*saṅkhāra*) assembled moment by moment from raw past karma and present intention. Equanimity is not an aloof end-state, but one tool in the meditator's tool kit. In the *Devadaha Sutta* (MN 101), the Buddha explains that when a cause of suffering can be eliminated through energetic striving, one strives; only when a condition cannot be altered do we deploy equanimity. Equanimity protects the mind from despair while wisdom seeks skillful means.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

When facing difficulty, do not immediately collapse into apathetic surrender under the banner of 'equanimity.' First ask: *'Can this problem be skillfully solved? Can I make this situation better?'* If yes, act with energy. If the condition is completely beyond our control (an unalterable loss, physical aging, or weather), then deploy equanimity. If our stomach is tight or knotted up, invite it to soften, resting in steady patience.
