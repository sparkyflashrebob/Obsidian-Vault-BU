Aggregate of Feeling ([[vedanakkhandha]])
- arises thru contact***
	- encounter of consciousness with an object thru a sense base
		- sight consciousness of a ship thru the eye

		- pleasant

Aggregate of Perception ([[sannakkhanda]])

Aggregate of volitional activities ([[sankharakkhanda]])
- six kinds of volition
	- volition regarding visible forms
	- volition regarding sounds
	- volition regarding odors
	- volition regarding tastes
	- volition regarding tactile objects

Later this aggregate was expanded  to serve a broad range of categories comprising all the varied mental functions in the suttas that do not fit the other aggregates.
- thought ([[Vitakka]])
- examination ([[vicara]])
- unwholesome states as greed, hatred & delusion
- wholesome states such as mindfulness, kindness, compassion, & wisdom

Aggregate of consciousness 
- six kinds
	- eye-consciousness
	- ear-consciousness
	- nose-consciousness
	- tongue-consciousness
	- body-consciousness
	- mind-consciousness
- distinction between consciousness and perception is hard to draw
- linked
- teachings do not differentiate them other than
	- perception by way of its objects
	- consciousness by way of it internal sense base
- Bhikkhu Bodhi figures that:
	-  consciousness is the factor that "illuminates" an entire sensory sphere making it accessible thru a sense faculty, while
	- perception is the factor that homes in on the sense object illuminated by consciousness

Buddha casts the aggregates in a major role in his teaching because, <u>in the view of ordinary people</u>,  the aggregates serve as the primary basis for clinging.
This occurs in two ways:
- by means of appropriation
- by means of identification
- which are  two complementary sides of distorted cognition rooted in fundamental ignorance

Via Appropriation:
- 5 aggregates comprise all of the things we most intimately take to be "mine"
	- how they are a basis for appropriation
- also grounds for identification
	- gain a sense of our personal identity
	- act as objects that we attribute to this innate sense of "I" and reflectively define as our "self"

Via Identification:
The Buddha designated the aggregates as "clinging-aggregates" because they serve as objects of clinging
- clinging can occur in many ways
- but most insidious is the attachment to the aggregates as being "mine", "I", & "my self"
- seems like a debate about that for most folks
- spontaneously take body and mind to be "I", "me" & "mine"

[[Sakkayaditthi]]
-  view of the personal-assemblage
- sakkaya 
	- the 5 aggregates themselves
- ditthi
	- the view that arises as a result

[[four views of self]] 
1. being identical to one or more of the aggregates or 
2. to possess them, or 
3. to be contained within them, or 
4. to contain them within self.

Twenty possible formulations (5 time 4)
- one with each aggregate

One having adopted a view of self,  the worlding keeps revolving around the aggregates, unable to find release

This view is compounded due to the aggregates serving as a basis for pleasure and enjoyment

Each aggregate can be regarded from three perspectives
1. by way of the enjoyment it yields (lust)
2. by the danger inherent in it (lust)
3. and by the way of one escapes and is release from it

Enjoyment
-  pleasure and joy that arises in dependence on the aggregates
- cling to our bodies as essential instrument for finding happiness and pleasure
- crave pleasant feelings
- seek out agreeable objects of perception
- launch ambitious project or engage in enjoyable activities
- hold to consciousness as a precondition for all experiences of pleasure

Hidden - out of site - dangers
- impermanent
- unable to give enduring satisfaction
- subject to change

SEE SAMANUPASSANA SUTTA - ASSUMPTIONS THAT ULDERLIE SELF VIEW

INCORPORATION INTO THE 4 NOBLE TRUTHS
- To identify with them as "I" or to appropriate them as "mine" exposes oneself to suffering when the aggregates change and fail to meet our expectations
- To cling to them is to cling to dukkha
- once we recognize the suffering inherent in the five aggregates we can then seek to escape* or release* from them 
- this is won by "the removal and abandonment of desire-and-lust" in regard to each of the aggregates 
- the Buddha's achievement on the night of his enlightenment was to penetrate the true nature of the aggregates
- from then on out his teaching was to point them out, to make them known, analyze and explain them
- realized with direct experience
- those who follow the path of his teachings and practice become liberated by their own wisdom - also winning release from the aggregates
 
BREAKING FALSE IDENTIFICATION WITH THE AGGREGATES
- Proclaims that these constituents of our being, which we cling to and identify with, are really non-self, not our true identity.
- He does not explicitly state "there is no self"
- Take pragmatic approach of examining the things assumed to be self and showing, thru reasoned arguement, that they fail to measure up to the criteria of true selfhood.
- not a blanket denial of self but a negation of the claims made about the things we take as self.
- these same 5 aggregates are not the self they are assumed to be
- we can examine these for ourselves to reach this same conclusion

VARIOUS APPROACHES USED TO DEMONTRATE THE NOT-SELF NATURE OF THE AGGREGATES
[[Anattalakkhanasutta]]
- best know source
- 2nd formal discourse after the [[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Suttas 1/Dhammacakkapavattana Sutta]]
- proposes two arguments undermining the identification of the aggregates as a self
	-  strike at our innate conviction that there is an autonomous subject of experience residing at the core of our being
	- even beneath the threshold of clear awareness, in our subconscioous, we retain the intuition that our life revolves around a solid, unchanging "I".
	- center of thought and emotion, source of volitional (willful) activity
	- giving us the concrete sense of "being somebody"
	- this is the target of the arguments regarding anatta

ARGUMENTs
- a true self should be invulnerable to pain and suffering
	- self should be autonomous
	- able to exert control over itself and over things that come under its influence
	- intrinsically permanent
		- everything might change or vanish but the self would persist
		- retaining its intrinsic identity

1. INVULNERABILITY AND AUTONOMY (AFFLICTION)
- recognize that each aggregate is a source of affliction
	- body falls ill and decays
	- painful feelings harass us thru the day
	- disagreeable perceptions assail us
	- our volitional wishes fail to heed our wishes
	- consciousness flickers and falters
	- throws us thoughts, emotions & impulses that cause conflict and distress
	- they refuse to conform to our desires which they would if they were truely ours
		- let my form be this way!!!
		- let my form not be this way!!!
		- same with other aggregates
- hence they are not self

2.THE SELF BEING PERMANENT AND A SOURCE OF LASTING HAPINESS
- aggregates are impermanent
	- each arises and passes away
		- is form permanent? everything - direct experience or belief? 
		- are feelings permanent? (pleasant, unpleasant, neither?)
		- is perception permanent? (not able to recognize things thru the senses, including recognize what is going on in the mind)
		- is our volition/will permanent (or emotions, thoughts, moods, etc...)
		- is consciousness permanent (brain damage, sleep, death, psychological disorder, brain disorders, & drugs & alcohol, etc)
	- meditation practice allows us to see this ever clearer at subtler and subtler levels
- cannot be a source of happiness
- dukkha, defective & unsatisfactory
- as a result cannot be taken as "me" and "mine" or as "I"

3.IMPERMANENCE - DUKKHA - NON-SELF
- known as the [[three characteristics]]
- sign-posts to be penetrated with insight
	- remove the delusion of permanence, pleasure & self
	- insight progresses from impermanence to dukkha
	- from impermanence & dukkha to non-self
		- subtlest of the three
- 3 step progression
	- (2.1)but also from impermanence to detachment & liberation (destruction of the defilements)
	- (2.2)some from impermanence of conditions 
	-  some teachings maintain that the perception of impermanence eliminates sensual lust* 
	- others say we can directly contemplate the five aggregates as non-self
	- (2.15) Aggregate similes - same as when exploring "[emptyness]"*
		- lump of foam
		- bubbles on the surface of the water
		- a mirage
		- plantain trunk
		- magical illusion
	- appear solid but upon inspection they turn out to be nothing, hollow, and insubstantial

Come to the same conclusion
- thereby becoming disenchanted, losing one's fascination with them
- "being disenchanted, one becomes dispassionate.  Through dispassion one is liberated"

VARIOUS SUTTAS FROM HERE

1. [[Aniccasutta (SN 22:12)]] aggregates impermanent. disenchantment, dispassion, liberation. 
2. [[Sahetu-aniccasutta (SN 22:18)]] causes and conditions also impermanent
3. [[Parinnasutta (SN 22:23)]] aggregates to be understood
4. [[Abhijanasutta (SN 22:34)]] one not directly knowing the aggregates cannot destroy suffering
5. [[Assadasutta (SN 22:26)]] Enjoyment, Danger, Escape - aggregates a form of enjoyment, impermanence the danger, remove and abandonment of form is the escape.
6. [[Assadasutta 2 ((SN 22:28))]] because of enjoyment, there is attachment; if there was no danger there would be no disenchantment; if there was no escape, beings would not escape. know the enjoyment, the danger as danger and the escape as the escape.
7. [[Natumhakamsutta (SN 22:33)]] "Whatever is not yours, abandon it. This will be for your own welfare and happiness." Non-clinging. Liberation.
8. [[Upadiyamanasutta (SN 22:63)]] One -Clinging to the aggregates is bound by Mara, one not clinging is free from the Evil One.
9. [[Radhasutta (SN 22:71)]] How does one know and see so that I-making, and mine-making tendencies to conceit do not occur. (Seeing the aggregates)
10. [[Punnamasutta (SN 22:82)]] Aggregates - rooted in desire, desire and lust for them, diversity in that (control). Condition for the aggregates (contact & dependent origination). Self View as a result of the aggregates. 
11. [[Pupphasutta (SN 22:94)]] Flowers. Not Disputing the world. The Aggregates. Impermanent, subject to change, a source of suffering. Flower rising from the water is not tainted by the water. (?)
12. [[Phenapindupamasutta (SN 22:95)]] Lump of Foam. Form = foam, rain = bubbles, mirage in heat = perception, peeling a plantain = volition, magic trick = consciousness. Seeing all for what they are = Liberation.
13. [[Aniccasutta (SN 22:12)]] Perception of the Impermanent. Eliminates sensual lust for form, lust for existence, eliminates ignorance, demolishes all conceit 'I am'.
