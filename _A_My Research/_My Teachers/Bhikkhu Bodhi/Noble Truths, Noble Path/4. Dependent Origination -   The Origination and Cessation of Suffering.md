Terminology
- typical to say "interdependent co-arising"
- inaccurate
- dependence on one factor upon another
- not simultaneous arising either
- some factors such as contact and feeling may arise simultaneously
- others such as feeling and craving may be separate
- birth, old-age, and death have temporal (time gap)

Things arise due to conditions
> - “When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises. When this does not exist, that does not come to be; with the cessation of this, that ceases.”
   
   Bodhi. Noble Truths, Noble Path: The Heart Essence of the Buddha's Original Teachings (p. 114). Wisdom Publications. Kindle Edition. 

Principle applied in a variety of ways in the Suttas
- Main application is sequence of 12 factors
- dependent on its predecessor
- can be seen as an expansion of the 2nd and 3rd Noble Truths
- finer detail of origination and cessation (ending)
- reveals a more fundamental condition than craving - [[avijja]] [[ignorance]]
- narrowly defined as "not knowing suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path"
- lack of awareness of the principles that shine the light on the true nature of phenomena (experience)
- 4 Nobel Truths
- 3 Characteristics
- Dependent Origination
- Ignorance sustains the round of dukkha
- When Ignorance comes to the end, the entire network of conditions also ends

Individual Factors are formally defined
- suttas only leave definitions but not the how
- rise of different competing interpretations of the formula
- all versions show the sequence of causal factors over a series of lives

Modern Interpretation
- sequence pertaining to a single life
- [[Vibhaṅga (of the Abhidhamma Pitaka)]] shows how DO operates at the level of individual mind-moments (see Tricycle Article on "How Long Is the Moment")
- alter definitions of existence, birth, old age, & death
- the twelve terms of the formula are spread out over multiple lives though

TRADITIONAL EXPLANATION:
- because of [fundamental [[ignorance]]]]], one engages in [[volitional activities]]
- [[wholesome & unwholesome]]
	- [[bodily, speech and mind]]
	- generate [[Google Keep/Karma]] (plant seeds)
	- potential new existence (repercusions)
	- at death this karma propels consciousness into a new existence (result)
- new existence [[birth]] begins when consciousness arrives at a new embodiment
	- new assemblage of bodily & mental phenomena (name & form/aggregates)
- with [[name & form]] the [[six sense bases]] take shape and begin to function
- When the Six sense bases encounter objects there is [[contact]]
- with contact this gives rise to [[feeling]]
	- pleasant, painful, & neutral
	- which trigger reactions/responses
	- with an untrained person, feelings arouses [[craving]]
		- the desire for pleasant experience & avoiding painful ones
	- tendency when one gets what one wants they delight in them & hold on tight to them
		- this is [[clinging]]
			- which is a more intense craving
		-  in fact, it can be used to justify craving for pleasure and [[becoming]]*
- with this clinging, there is a fresh round of volitional activities that occur that create the potential for new existence
- this becoming may occur in the 3 realms recognized by Buddhist cosmology
	- desire realm
	- realm of subtle form
	- formless realm (like a spirit, or an angel)
- birth > old-age-and-death > other manifestations of dukkha

[[Three-life interpretation of dependent origination]] "THE COMMENTARIAL INVENTION"
- Past, Present & Future
- SN 12:19 
	- Unwise person does not eliminate ignorance and craving of the past
	- moves on to new embodied existence
	- Wise person does eliminate ignorance and craving and is freed from any future embodied existence
		- no longer bound to birth, old age & death

[[Unsorted Notes/Dependent Origination]] dynamic perspective on [[non-self]]
- complements analytical perspective of the [[the five aggregates]]
- process of rebirth & karmic causation occur <u>without an underlying subject</u>, a substantial self, one existence to the next.

Buddhas Time Two Camps: Eternalists & Annihilationist's
- Eternalists - core of every person there is an immortal self - substantial & autonomous - resists thru rebirths and attains liberation - preserving its unchanging status
- Annhilationists - denied existence of a permanent self that survives after death - at breakup of the body, personal existence comes to an absolute end - at death the living being is annihilated.
- <u>Dependent Origination - "teaching by the middle" - avoids two extremes - </u>
	- avoids that "all exists" by showing how personal continuity is possible with a self that persists thru the process
	- avoids that "all does not exist" by showing that as long as conditions drive the process of [[becoming]] remain intact, the conditions will continue to operate
