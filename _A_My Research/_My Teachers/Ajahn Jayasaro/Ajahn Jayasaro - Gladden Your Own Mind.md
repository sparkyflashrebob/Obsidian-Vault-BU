## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Jayasaro.

* **Theme**: The essential role of spiritual joy (*Pāmojja* and *Pīti*) in meditation; Step 10 of Ānāpānasati (*Abhippamodayaṃ Cittaṃ* - Gladdening the mind); Right Effort (*Sammā-Vāyāma*); rejoicing in small victories; reframing difficult sits as training in resilience.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered during a milestone visit to Abhayagiri in 2013—the exact day marking Ajahn Jayasaro's 35th year as a Buddhist monk.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Jayasaro highlights a common distortion among meditators: approaching practice with stern, self-critical severity. Many practitioners obsess over their failings—lamenting restlessness, sleepiness, or wandering thoughts—and completely overlook their wholesome cultivation. In the *Ānāpānasati Sutta*, the Buddha explicitly instructs the meditator to 'gladden the mind.' Wholesome joy is not a frivolous distraction; it is the vital nutrient that softens mental resistance and prepares the mind for deep samādhi. Right Effort involves not only abandoning unwholesome states, but actively nurturing and rejoicing in wholesome states (*muditā* towards oneself). Sitting through half an hour of intense agitation without quitting is a tremendous triumph of *khanti* (endurance). By learning to gladden the heart, meditation transforms from an exhausting battle into a joyful refuge.
