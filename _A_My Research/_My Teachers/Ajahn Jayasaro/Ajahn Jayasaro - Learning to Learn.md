# 🎙️ Ajahn Jayasaro - Learning to Learn
### *Ajahn Jayasaro — Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery (Saturday Night Pūjā Discourse)*

> *"Meditation is fundamentally learning how to learn. In the presence of unbroken mindfulness, the mind attains its optimum learning capacity. Samādhi stills our knee-jerk emotional reactions, creating stability, brightness, and clarity. Then yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana naturally arises: you see thinking arise and pass away, feeling arise and pass away. ## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Jayasaro.

* **Theme**: Meditation as the ultimate pedagogical methodology; deconstructing language, cognitive assumptions, and self-identity; the necessary role of *Samādhi* in overcoming subconscious knee-jerk reactivity; *Yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana* (knowing things as they actually are); recognizing thoughts as unowned phenomena (*anattā*).

* **Circumstances**: Delivered on a Saturday night following the communal chanting service at Abhayagiri to monastics and visitors.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Jayasaro presents a sophisticated epistemological examination of contemplative training. Human education focuses almost entirely on *what* to learn (acquiring facts, technical data, concepts), while ignoring *how* the mind learns. Meditation is learning how to learn from direct experiential reality. Our everyday perception is distorted by language, unexamined assumptions, and automatic knee-jerk reactions (liking and disliking). When we sit in meditation, we create an environment that minimizes sensory noise. Unbroken mindfulness (*sati*) stabilizes attention into *samādhi*, which generates internal brightness, clarity, and unshakeable stillness. In that stillness, we observe mental formations without reacting. We discover that thoughts arise, linger, and vanish completely without an internal owner. Insight into *anattā* is not an abstract theory—it is what the mind learns when it finally sees clearly.

## 💬 Impactful Quotes with Exact Timestamps

> *"We learn most easily and most reliably in the presence of mindfulness. Where mindfulness is unbroken and continuous, a sense of stability, brightness, and clarity arises—the mind is at its optimum learning capacity."* — **[71:32]**
