# 🎙️ Ajahn Jayasaro - Faith (Saddhā)
### *Ajahn Jayasaro — Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery (2004)*

> *"In the West, people often have a prejudice against the word 'faith,' equating it with blind belief in the improbable without evidence. But in Buddhism, saddhā is completely different: it is seeing the value in goodness, trusting in our own capacity to awaken, and recognizing that our own actions of body, speech, and mind determine our peace. When you have faith, difficult endurance (khanti) is no longer bitter—it becomes the joyful step toward your own true refuge."* — **Ajahn Jayasaro [28:58 / 53:06–54:15]**

## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Jayasaro.

* **Theme**: The role of *Saddhā* (Faith / Trust / Conviction) in Theravāda Buddhism; dispelling Western intellectual skepticism; the balance of faith and wisdom; self-efficacy and karma (*kammasakatā-saddhā*); building the Dhamma refuge in the heart.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered at Abhayagiri in 2004 upon the invitation of Ajahn Pasanno, addressing Western practitioners who struggle with skepticism and cynicism.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Jayasaro unpacks the faculty of *Saddhā*. In English, 'faith' often carries theological baggage—implying unquestioning allegiance to dogmas or miraculous claims. In Pāli, *saddhā* literally means 'placing the heart upon' truth. It is experiential confidence born of seeing the value in virtue, meditation, and peace. Without faith, practice collapses at the first obstacle: when bodily pain or emotional dryness occurs, skepticism whispers *'What's the use?'*, instantly undermining patience (*khanti*). Faith in the Triple Gem is not worshipping an external deity; it is faith in our own biological and mental capacity to awaken, and faith that intentional actions (*kamma*) genuinely bear fruit. Faith provides the emotional fuel that enables wisdom to penetrate suffering.
