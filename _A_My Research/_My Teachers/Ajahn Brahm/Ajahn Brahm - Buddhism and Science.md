## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ven. Ajahn Brahm.

* **Theme**: The convergence and divergence of Western science and Buddhist Dhamma; theoretical physics and meditation; empiricism (*ehipassiko*); the hard problem of consciousness; challenging materialist reductionism through direct contemplative evidence.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Drawing upon his academic background studying theoretical physics at Cambridge University under eminent scientists, Ajahn Brahm investigates the deep harmony between the scientific method and Buddhist contemplative inquiry. The Buddha proclaimed *ehipassiko*—'come and see for yourself'—an empirical approach codified in the *Kālāma Sutta* that rejects blind faith in favor of direct verification. However, mainstream science suffers from a fatal blind spot: materialism assumes that consciousness is merely an emergent epiphenomenon of brain chemistry. Ajahn Brahm presents clinical near-death experiences (NDEs), out-of-body perceptions during flatline cardiac arrest, and the deep stillness of jhāna (where physical senses shut down completely while consciousness remains luminous) as empirical refutations of physicalism. Meditation is the inner empirical laboratory.
