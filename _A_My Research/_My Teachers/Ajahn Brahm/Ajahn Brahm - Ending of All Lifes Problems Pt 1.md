## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ven. Ajahn Brahm.

* **Theme**: The authentic Buddhist understanding of Kamma; dispelling fatalism and guilt; the Buddha's Salt Crystal simile (*Loṇakapalla Sutta*); breaking cycles of retribution and tyranny; putting an end to all life's problems through present wholesome intention.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: In this celebrated 'Karmageddon' discourse, Ajahn Brahm dispels popular myths and fatalistic misconceptions about karma. Karma is not a cosmic bookkeeping system or divine vengeance; it is the natural law of volitional intention (*cetanā*). Fatalism ('Whatever happens is my karma, I can't do anything about it') is a severe misinterpretation explicitly refuted by the Buddha. Present actions (*diṭṭhadhammika*) have the power to transform the ripening of past deeds. Drawing upon the *Loṇakapalla Sutta*, Ajahn Brahm explains that an individual who cultivates boundless goodness, virtue, and mettā dilutes past mistakes just as the great river Ganges dilutes a lump of salt. Retaliation only perpetuates the cycle: *if you kill the tyrant out of vengeance, you become the tyrant.* By forgiving, generating generosity, and purifying intention now, we end all karmic debt.
