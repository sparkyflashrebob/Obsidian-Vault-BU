## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Amaro.

* **Theme**: The seamless integration of wisdom (*paññā*) and compassion (*karuṇā*); dissolving the internal dialogue through self-inquiry (*'What is it that knows this?'*); resting in formless awareness.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered to a mixed gathering of experienced meditators and newcomers exploring how contemplative insight harmonizes with emotional warmth and daily responsiveness.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: In spiritual life, wisdom and compassion are often treated as separate practices, or even as conflicting temperaments—the cool, detached sword of insight versus the soft, emotional warmth of love. But as the Buddha pointed out, if we are suffering while practicing compassion, we are missing the mark. Compassion without wisdom collapses into emotional enmeshment and grief; wisdom without compassion hardens into cynical detachment. Genuine practice weaves them into an indivisible whole: compassion is the spacious, non-judgmental capacity that allows every thought, feeling, and creature to enter awareness; wisdom is the clear seeing that penetrates their empty, ungraspable nature (*anicca, dukkha, anattā*). Together, they return the heart to the unshakeable Middle Way.

* **Expanded Version of the Teachings**: Drawing upon Mahāyāna iconography (Guanyin / Avalokiteśvara) and the Chan poem *Xin Xin Ming* (*Faith in Mind*), Ajahn Amaro illustrates the dynamic relationship between receptive listening and clear discernment. Meditators often feel tormented by an internal multi-log—the inner critic, the planner, the judge, the scared child. Rather than taking sides or attempting to violently suppress these voices, compassion simply listens to them without belief. To prevent identification, wisdom introduces the self-inquiry question: *'What is it that knows this?'* This question is not meant to produce a verbal answer, but to dismantle the habit of personal ownership. When the question lands in open awareness, the mind drops its stories and rests directly in the formless nature of knowing itself.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

In cultivating the balance of wisdom and compassion in meditation and daily interactions, we can practice:
1. **Observing Physical Reactivity**: When difficult news or criticism enters our senses, notice the physical contraction—often our stomach is tight or knotted up, our shoulders rise, and the mind prepares to defend. There is no need to fight this reaction. Compassion holds the physical tension gently; wisdom recognizes that the sensation is impermanent.
2. **Inquiring into the Knower**: When a painful mood or self-judgment grips the mind, drop the analytical debate and simply ask: *'What is aware of this feeling right now?'* Notice the sudden opening into spaciousness where the feeling exists without overwhelming the container.
3. **Resting in the Middle**: Throughout the day, let compassion say *'yes'* to whatever appears (welcoming the moment without resistance), and let wisdom say *'not mine'* (relinquishing possession and clinging).
