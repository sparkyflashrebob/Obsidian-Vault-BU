# 🎙️ What is Truly Miraculous? Vesākha Pūjā Reflection
### *Ajahn Amaro — Vesākha Pūjā Night (May 2010 / B.E. 2553)*

> *"In the Kevaddha Sutta, the Buddha explains that psychic feats and mind-reading are not the real wonder. No amount of celestial flowers raining down from the heavens is actually going to free the human heart from suffering. The only true miracle—the supreme wonder of the Dhamma—is the miracle of instruction (anusāsanī-pāṭihāriya): when a person caught in anger, fear, or greed hears the Dhamma, understands the nature of mind, and wakes up into peace."* — **Ajahn Amaro [15:47 / 40:54]**

## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Amaro.

* **Theme**: Demythologizing the miraculous; the threefold events of the Buddha's life (Birth, Awakening, Parinibbāna); the *Acchariya-abbhuta Sutta* (MN 123); why the miracle of education (*anusāsanī-pāṭihāriya*) surpasses all psychic phenomena.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered on the full moon night of Vesak at Abhayagiri Monastery to a packed Dhamma hall of monastics and lay visitors observing the all-night circumambulation and meditation vigil.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Vesākha Pūjā commemorates the three pivotal milestones of the historical Buddha: his birth beneath the sal trees in Lumbinī, his supreme awakening at Bodh Gayā, and his final passing (*Parinibbāna*) in Kusinārā. Ancient scriptures often surround these events with flamboyant cosmological miracles: gods singing, earth trembling, hot and cold water pouring from the sky, and blossoms raining down out of season. Ajahn Amaro recounts how Ajahn Chah radically reframed these legendary stories: the true miracle of the Buddha was not walking on water or reading minds, but showing human beings how to live without greed, hatred, and delusion. In meditation, the lifetime of the Buddha is reenacted in every passing thought and sensation: a memory or physical ache arises (birth), we recognize its nature without clinging (awakened life), and it dissolves naturally into stillness (*parinibbāna*).

* **Expanded Version of the Teachings**: In the *Kevaddha Sutta* (DN 11), a householder urges the Buddha to order a monk to perform supernatural displays to impress the townspeople. The Buddha firmly refuses, explaining that psychic powers (*iddhi*) and telepathy (*ādesanā*) can easily be dismissed as magic tricks or charms, creating attachment to spectacle. The only miracle that cannot be faked or corrupted is *anusāsanī-pāṭihāriya*—the miracle of wise instruction: *'Direct your mind thus; discard this unwholesome quality; dwell in this wholesome peace.'* Ajahn Amaro bridges this teaching with *The Island* Chapter 4 and 12: the Unconditioned (*asaṅkhata*) is not somewhere else in the cosmos; it is the natural space of knowing that is present right in the midst of worldly conditions.

## 💬 Impactful Quotes with Exact Timestamps

> *"When Venerable Ānanda recites the wonderful and marvellous qualities of the Buddha, people lean forward expecting magic. But the greatest marvel is that the Buddha watched feelings arise, persist, and vanish without a single ripple of craving or aversion. That is what leads to liberation for us."* — **[15:47]**

> *"Ajahn Chah used to say: 'People want to see someone fly through the air. But birds fly through the air all day, and they are still full of greed and fear. What is truly miraculous is an angry person becoming gentle, an anxious person finding peace, and a selfish person learning to give.'"* — **[12:34]**

> *"No amount of flowers raining down from the sky is actually going to help us when our stomach is tight or we are facing physical death. What helps us is the miracle of Dhamma—knowing how to let go."* — **[40:54]**

> *"When an ache in the knee or an embarrassing memory arises in meditation, that is its birth. Awakening doesn't mean it instantly vanishes; the awakened life is staying with that memory, observing it with compassion, having no entanglement with it. And then finally, it breaks up and dissolves—that is its Parinibbāna. Leave it alone."* — **[48:07]**

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

In reflecting on what is truly miraculous in our own meditation and daily living:
1. **Noticing the Micro-Lifetimes of Experience**: During sitting meditation, notice how a mood or thought takes birth. When irritation or anticipation arises, we can witness its full life cycle without panic or urgency. When it ends, we allow its *parinibbāna* without dragging it back.
2. **Somatic Calming Amidst Disappointment**: When external life doesn't match our spiritual fantasies, we can notice physical constriction—tightness in the throat, knots in the belly. There is no need to create a miracle to escape it. Holding the raw physical feeling with gentle presence transforms the reaction into peace.
3. **Honoring the Miracle of Practice**: Every time we refrain from an angry retort, choose generosity over self-clinging, or sit peacefully with bodily discomfort, we participate in the Buddha's true miracle.
