## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Trap of Meditative Performance Anxiety
Beginners and experienced meditators alike arrive at retreat carrying an invisible ledger of spiritual ambitions: *'This year I will achieve deep absorption; this year I won't get angry.'* Ajahn Amaro warns that bringing an ambitious consumer mindset into retreat creates immediate tension and inevitable disappointment.

### 2. The Right Attitude: Willingness to Not Know
Contemplative practice begins with humility: the willingness to set aside our self-made identities and simply be present. We do not meditate to become a spiritual hero; we meditate to understand the nature of things as they are (*yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana*).

### 3. Creating a Field of Non-Harm
By softening our internal expectations, we naturally soften our external interactions. Even in silent retreat, an attitude of gentleness, patience, and non-judgment radiates outwardly, creating an atmosphere of total safety for the entire assembly.
