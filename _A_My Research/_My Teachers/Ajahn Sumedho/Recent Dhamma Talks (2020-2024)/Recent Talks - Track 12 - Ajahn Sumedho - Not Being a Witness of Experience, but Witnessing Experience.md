### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic disciples, resident Sangha, visiting monastics, and lay community.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The subtle distinction between creating an egoic 'witness' identity versus the natural activity of witnessing.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho illuminates one of the most refined spiritual traps: subtly turning 'the witness' into a new spiritual persona. Meditators proudly claim: 'I am the detached witness observing my life.' But that 'witness' is just another identity (*sakkāya-diṭṭhi*). The Buddha taught Bāhiya: in the seen, just the seen; in the heard, just the heard. There is no entity doing the witnessing—there is only the luminous presence of knowing.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Pitfall of the Spiritual Observer**: Seeing how the ego sneaks in through the back door as 'the witness'.
- **The Bāhiya Sutta Instruction**: Direct realization of no perceiver behind perception.
- **Pure Witnessing as Anattā**: Resting in selflessness without claiming ownership of consciousness.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Identity | 'I am the spiritual observer standing apart from the world.' | Awareness is unowned; consciousness effortlessly reflects phenomena. |
| Subtle Defilement | Subtle conceit (*asmimāna*) and emotional aloofness from life | Total intimacy with life combined with boundless freedom from grasping. |
| Sutta Benchmark | Sakkāya-diṭṭhi clinging to viññāṇa as self | Bāhiya Sutta: 'When there is no you there, you will be in neither here nor beyond.' |

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por recalls sitting in meditation feeling pleased with himself for being such a calm, detached witness, until the question arose: 'Who is the one enjoying being the witness?'—and the entire identity collapsed.
