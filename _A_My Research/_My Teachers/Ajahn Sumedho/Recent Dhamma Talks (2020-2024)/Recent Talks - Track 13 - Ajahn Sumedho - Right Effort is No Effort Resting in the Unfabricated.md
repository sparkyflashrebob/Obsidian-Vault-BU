### Circumstances
Late-stage contemplative reflections and public discourses delivered by Venerable Ajahn Sumedho during his late 80s and 90th year, offering radical, direct pointers to awareness, the cessation of grasping, and living from the Deathless.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic disciples, resident Sangha, visiting monastics, and lay community.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Redefining Right Effort (sammā-vāyāma) not as aggressive striving, but as the effortless cessation of self-fabrication.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this paradoxically titled discourse, Luang Por Sumedho explores the true meaning of Right Effort (*sammā-vāyāma*). In worldly life, effort means strain, struggle, and willpower. But in Dhamma, striving to become enlightened creates the very obstacles we seek to overcome. True Right Effort is 'no effort'—the effortless resting in awareness that allows defilements to cease without a fight.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Exhaustion of Willpower**: Why aggressive striving leads to burnout and cynicism.
- **The Four Aspects of Sammā-Vāyāma**: Preventing, abandoning, cultivating, and maintaining—framed as relaxed vigilance.
- **The Art of Non-Striving**: Discovering that the cessation of desire is completely effortless.

| Paradigm | Egoic Striving (Wrong Effort) | Right Effort (*Sammā-Vāyāma*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Dynamic | Gritting teeth, fighting wandering thoughts with anger, pushing for states | Noticing the arising of craving and effortlessly releasing it. |
| Underlying Desire | Taṇhā for becoming (*bhava-taṇhā*) an enlightened master | Relinquishing all becoming; resting in the unfabricated (*asaṅkhata*). |
| Energetic Fruit | Headaches, irritability, tension, spiritual depression | Spacious joy, physical ease, clarity, and boundless calm. |
