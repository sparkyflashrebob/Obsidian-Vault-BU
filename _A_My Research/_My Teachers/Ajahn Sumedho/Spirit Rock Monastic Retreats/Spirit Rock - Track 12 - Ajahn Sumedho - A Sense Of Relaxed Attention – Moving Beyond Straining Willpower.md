### People Involved

- **Assembly**: Spirit Rock resident teachers, visiting monastics from Abhayagiri and Amaravati, and lay retreatants.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The art of non-effortful mindfulness; abandoning the aggressive, achieving mindset in favor of receptive conscious presence.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho addresses a universal hazard among Western practitioners: meditating with hyper-tense willpower. He introduces 'A Sense of Relaxed Attention'—the counter-intuitive realization that true mindfulness (*sati*) requires relaxing rather than straining. Drawing on the classic simile of tuning a lute string from the Soṇa Sutta, Luang Por demonstrates that when effort is too tight, restlessness arises; when too loose, drowsiness takes over. Relaxed attention is the peaceful balance where awakening naturally happens.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
**The Violence of Spiritual Ambition**: How trying to force the mind into silence creates an iron cage of tension.

**The Art of Receptive Awareness**: Allowing conditions to arise, play out, and cease without interfering or policing thoughts.

**Tuning the Instrument of Mind**: Balancing energy (*vīriya*) and tranquility (*passaddhi*) in everyday postures.

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por shares how during his first year under Ajahn Chah, he meditated with such ferocious ambition that his head felt like it would explode, until Ajahn Chah poked his belly and said: 'Sumedho, why are you fighting with yourself? Just relax and watch.'
