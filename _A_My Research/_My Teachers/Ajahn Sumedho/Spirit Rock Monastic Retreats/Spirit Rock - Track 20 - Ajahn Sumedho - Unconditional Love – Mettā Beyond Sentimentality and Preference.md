### People Involved

- **Assembly**: Spirit Rock resident teachers, visiting monastics from Abhayagiri and Amaravati, and lay retreatants.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The true meaning of mettā: not romantic emotion or conditional liking, but unconditional goodwill rooted in non-self (anattā).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this deeply touching evening discourse, Luang Por Sumedho clarifies the profound Theravāda understanding of 'Unconditional Love' (*mettā*). In Western culture, love is often confused with romantic passion, emotional codependency, or liking someone because they treat us well. True mettā has no conditions; it asks for nothing in return. It is the natural fragrance of a mind that has let go of personal grasping, radiating boundless benevolence toward all living beings without distinction.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
**The Illusion of Conditional Love**: Why loving only what pleases us leads inevitably to heartbreak and resentment.

**The Simile of the Saw**: Meditating on the Buddha's instruction to maintain love even toward those who saw us limb from limb.

**Mettā as Selfless Presence**: Healing self-hatred by holding our own wounded humanity in boundless gentleness.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Condition** | Requires reciprocity, beauty, good behavior, agreement | Unconditional; radiates equally on friends, strangers, and enemies. |
| **Underlying Driver** | Desire to possess, fear of abandonment, ego gratification | Pure non-attachment, profound empathy, selfless benevolence. |
| **Vulnerability** | Easily transforms into jealousy, bitterness, and rage | Invulnerable; indestructible shelter like the open sky. |
