### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Survey of contemplative techniques—mantra, breath, reflection, sweeping, and silence—as practical instruments of awakening.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho reviews the various 'tools of the trade' available to Buddhist meditators. Whether using the mental repetition of the mantra 'Buddho', watching the rise and fall of the breath, practicing standing or walking meditation, or listening to the inner Sound of Silence, these techniques are not holy goals in themselves. They are simply skillful instruments used to dismantle delusions and anchor the heart in peace.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Techniques as Tools, Not Masters**: How to avoid turning meditation methods into rigid dogmas.
- **The Buddho Mantra**: Using a simple mental rhythm to sweep away obsessive compulsive thought streams.
- **Walking Meditation**: Grounding attention through the soles of the feet to counter lethargy and mental agitation.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Contemplative Tool | Specific Application | Common Pitfall to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mantra (*Buddho*) | Clearing racing thoughts; stabilizing erratic attention | Turning the mantra into mechanical rote repetition without awareness. |
| Breath Awareness (*Ānāpāna*) | Calming physical arousal; grounding somatic presence | Straining to control respiratory rate or hyperventilating. |
| Sound of Silence (*Nāda*) | Direct entrance into mental spaciousness; clearing chatter | Straining physically with the ears rather than listening with the mind. |
| Walking Meditation | Overcoming sleepiness (*thīna-middha*); integrating movement | Rushing up and down the path or daydreaming during movement. |
