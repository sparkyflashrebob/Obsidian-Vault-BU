# Facing Fear and Doubt with Awareness

Based on the provided sources, here is the gist of Ajahn Sumedho's talks on fear and doubt:

Ajahn Sumedho frequently addresses fear and doubt as common human experiences and obstacles on the path to liberation \[1\].

1.  \*\*Nature of Fear and Doubt:\*\*
    \*   Fear and doubt are described as states of mind \[1, 2\].
    \*   They are considered conditions that arise and pass away \[1, 3, 4\].
    \*   Doubt, specifically, is often seen as a result of thinking \[5\] and not knowing for sure \[1\]. It can involve uncertainty about oneself, one's practice, meditation, Buddhism, teachers, or techniques \[1\].
    \*   For well-educated people who tend to think a lot, doubt can be one of the biggest sources of suffering \[6\].
    \*   Fear is a very powerful emotional state \[4\].

2.  \*\*The Problem with Reacting:\*\*
    \*   We give power to fear when we remain ignorant and react blindly to it, creating and adding to it with more thoughts, views, and opinions \[1\].
    \*   The immediate reaction when feeling frightened is often to run away or try to get rid of it \[4\].
    \*   Analyzing fear or doubt, trying to figure out why they are there, or engaging in endless analysis perpetuates these states \[7-9\].
    \*   Clinging to the idea "I am afraid" or "I am doubtful" is an interpretation through the ego and reinforces the self-view (sakyaditthi) problem \[5, 10, 11\].
    \*   Wanting to get rid of fear or other emotional problems is described as a mental creation, a "self scenario" \[12\]. Attaching to this aspiration is still suffering \[12\].

3.  \*\*The Path of Awareness and Acceptance:\*\*
    \*   Ajahn Sumedho encourages facing, looking at, welcoming, and accepting things as they are in the present moment, including fear and doubt \[1, 4, 13\].
    \*   Instead of analyzing, one should just note the feeling, mood, or emotion of fear or doubt \[8\].
    \*   Allowing confusion or whatever is present to be what it is, as a feeling or energy, allows it to be embraced rather than perpetuated \[9\]. This is not suppressing or indulging \[9\].
    \*   Acceptance of fear takes away its power \[1\].

4.  \*\*Learning from Hindrances and Fetters:\*\*
    \*   Doubt is identified as one of the five hindrances \[14\].
    \*   The hindrances (including doubt) are presented not as things to get rid of, but as teachers to learn from \[14\]. They are like tests or ordeals that bring wisdom and knowledge \[14\].
    \*   The first three fetters (which bind one to samsara) are seeing through self-view (sakyaditthi), attachment to forms and conventions for purification (silabbataparāmāsa), and \*\*doubting (vicikicchā)\*\* \[5, 6\]. Doubting the practice, path, Buddhism, teachers, or oneself is seeing through the doubting function of the mind \[6\]. These fetters are seen as artificial things that humans create \[5\].

5.  \*\*Trusting Awareness as Refuge:\*\*
    \*   The recommended approach is to trust in \*\*awareness (sati)\*\*, the knowing that is not personal \[10, 15, 16\]. Awareness is the "gate to the deathless" and the way to liberation \[13, 17\].
    \*   Awareness provides the stability \[13\] and courage to look at things like fear \[8, 18\].
    \*   By trusting awareness, one can be receptive to emotional energies like fear, seeing them as they are, rather than personal issues ("I am angry," "I am afraid") \[4, 7\].
    \*   The refuge is in the Buddha (awakened consciousness/awareness), Dhamma (the way things are), and Sangha (those who practice rightly) \[8, 10, 18-21\]. This is a direct, intuitive knowing \[15, 16, 22\].
    \*   If one believes they are a hopeless case due to fear or doubt, this is an attachment to a perception \[23\]. Refusing to be aware of this belief as a condition perpetuates suffering \[23\]. Waking up to this belief as a condition is possible through awareness and wisdom \[23\].
    \*   Being awake and aware prevents one from being a helpless victim of emotions and habits like fear \[24\].

In essence, Ajahn Sumedho teaches that fear and doubt are conditions to be observed and accepted with awareness, rather than being identified with, analyzed, suppressed, or fled from. They are opportunities to learn and apply mindfulness, trusting in the non-personal knowing that is always present, which is the real refuge \[1, 8-10, 13, 14\].
