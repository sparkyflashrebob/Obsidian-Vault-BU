## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Dtao Dam Monastery / Wat Pah Nanachat, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: AN 5.193 (Saṅgārava Sutta - The Five Hindrances), SN 46.51.

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Skillful means for cutting through the two pervasive hindrances of sloth/torpor (thīna-middha) and ill-will/anger (byāpāda).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho offers targeted, practical advice for dealing with two chronic obstacles in monastic practice: dullness (*thīna-middha*) and anger (*byāpāda*). Dullness puts the mind to sleep through a foggy, heavy stupor, while anger agitates the mind with burning indignation. Luang Por shows how to energize the posture against dullness and how to cool the fever of anger through somatic mindfulness and compassion.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Nature of Dullness**: Recognizing sloth as an unwholesome escape mechanism from boredom.
- **The Fever of Anger**: Seeing through the moral righteousness that anger uses to justify itself.
- **Energetic Transmutation**: Using the breath, walking meditation, and posture awareness to cut through both extremes.
