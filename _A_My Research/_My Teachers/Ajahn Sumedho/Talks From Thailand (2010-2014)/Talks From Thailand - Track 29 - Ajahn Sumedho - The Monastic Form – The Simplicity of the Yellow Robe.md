## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 8 (Sallekha Sutta - Effacement), AN 4.28.

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The timeless genius of the Theravāda mendicant lifestyle; effacement (sallekha) through wearing the robes.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this intimate 22-minute talk, Luang Por Sumedho reflects on 'The Monastic Form.' The shaven head, the ochre robes, the alms bowl, and the mendicant lifestyle are not mere quaint Asian relics; they are an ingenious, time-tested technology for effacing ego (*sallekha*). By reducing material life to the absolute minimum, the mendicant lives as a free bird in the sky, owning nothing, needing little, and pointing the world toward the Deathless.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Sallekha (Effacement)**: The deliberate polishing away of self-centered demands through simplicity.
- **The Shaven Head and Robe**: How shedding hair and fashion instantly removes 90% of worldly vanity.
- **The Mendicant as Spiritual Mirror**: Standing as a visible reminder of peace in a greedy world.
