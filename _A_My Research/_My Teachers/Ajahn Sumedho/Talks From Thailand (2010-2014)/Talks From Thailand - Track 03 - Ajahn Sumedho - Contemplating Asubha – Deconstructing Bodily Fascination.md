## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Pong / Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 119 (Kāyagatāsati Sutta - 32 Parts of the Body), AN 10.60 (Girimānanda Sutta).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The classical Forest contemplation of bodily unattractiveness (asubha) to cure lust, vanity, and obsessive physical attachment.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In his first month following formal retirement from Amaravati, Luang Por Sumedho addresses the monastic community in Thailand on the deep practice of *asubha-bhāvanā* (contemplating the unattractiveness of the flesh). Society bombards the senses with hyper-sexualized glamour, perfume, and fashion to conceal physical decay. By dissecting the 32 parts of the body—hair, nails, teeth, skin, bones, organs, and excrement—the mind punctures the fantasy of lust and realizes genuine dispassion (*virāga*).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Illusion of Glamour**: How sensual desire projects beauty onto a thin layer of outer skin.
- **The 32 Parts of the Body**: Systematically meditating on the viscera, bones, and bodily fluids to dissolve lust.
- **Dispassion (*Virāga*) vs. Repulsion**: Recognizing that true asubha practice produces luminous lightness and freedom, not morbid aversion.
