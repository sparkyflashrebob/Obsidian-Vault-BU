## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: SN 22.89 (Khemaka Sutta - The Residual Scent of 'I Am').

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating the subtle residual conceit of identity (asmimāna); transcending the instinctual sense of 'I am' into pure unconditioned knowing.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho explores the subtle fetter of *asmimāna*—the lingering reflex of 'I am'. Even after intellectual views of self are abandoned, a visceral, emotional scent of 'I am' persists in consciousness like the scent of soap clinging to a freshly washed cloth. Luang Por directs practitioners to observe this feeling of 'I am' without judgment, realizing that the one who is aware of 'I am' is not 'I am'.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Scent of the Cloth**: Contemplating the Khemaka Sutta's simile of residual self-conceit.
- **Visceral Identity vs. Cognitive View**: Differentiating gross views (*sakkāya-diṭṭhi*) from the subtle feeling of existence (*asmimāna*).
- **The Final Release**: Allowing the sense of 'I am' to dissolve into the unconditioned space of awareness.
