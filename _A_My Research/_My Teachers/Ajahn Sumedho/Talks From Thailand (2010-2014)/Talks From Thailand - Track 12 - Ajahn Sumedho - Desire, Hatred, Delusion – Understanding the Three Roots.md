## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: AN 3.68 (Mūla Sutta - Roots), MN 9 (Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating the Three Unwholesome Roots (akusala-mūla): lobha (greed), dosa (hatred), and moha (delusion) without moralistic condemnation.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho delivers a penetrating psychological analysis of the Three Roots of suffering: *Lobha* (greed/desire), *Dosa* (aversion/hatred), and *Moha* (delusion/confusion). Meditators frequently react to their own defilements with guilt and self-hatred, which is simply more *dosa*! Luang Por demonstrates how to investigate greed, anger, and delusion with objective compassion, seeing them as impersonal energetic weather patterns that lose their power when clearly known.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Mechanism of the Three Roots**: Greed pulls toward; hatred pushes away; delusion spins in confusion.
- **The Trap of Spiritual Guilt**: Why hating our defilements fuels the second arrow of suffering.
- **The Transmutation of Defilements**: Using the light of pure awareness to dissolve greed and anger into wisdom.
