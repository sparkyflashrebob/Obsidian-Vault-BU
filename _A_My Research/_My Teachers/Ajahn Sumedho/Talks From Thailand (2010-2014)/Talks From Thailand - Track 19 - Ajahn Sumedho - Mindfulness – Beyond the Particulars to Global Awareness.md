## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 10 (Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta), AN 10.60.

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Expanding mindfulness from narrow micro-focus on isolated objects to open, panoramic, global awareness.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho clarifies a common misunderstanding about mindfulness (*sati*). Many practitioners believe mindfulness means squinting at tiny details—narrowly tracking the tip of the nose or individual foot movements—which often causes tension and tunnel vision. Luang Por emphasizes that true sati is open, spacious, and panoramic: it is the broad, unentangled awareness of the entire field of experience, holding both the particular sensations and the vast conscious space in which they appear.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Narrow Focus vs. Panoramic Awareness**: Moving from rigid micro-concentration to relaxed global presence.
- **The Field of Experience**: Including sounds, physical posture, feelings, and space simultaneously.
- **Natural Ease**: Discovering that spacious awareness requires no muscular strain.

| **Scope** | Obsessed with a single pinpoint (e.g. nostril sensation) | Spacious awareness of the whole body, mind, and ambient room. |
| **Physical Tension** | Eyestrain, clenched jaw, furrowed brow, rigidity | Soft face, relaxed belly, buoyant upright spine, natural ease. |
| **Integration** | Easily shattered when leaving the meditation cushion | Seamlessly maintained while walking, speaking, working, eating. |

---

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por compares narrow focus to walking through a dense jungle looking through a paper towel tube—you see a leaf clearly, but you step on a snake. Panoramic awareness sees the entire jungle at once.
