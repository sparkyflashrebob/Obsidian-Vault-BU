## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: DN 16 (Mahāparinibbāna Sutta), MN 140 (Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
A profound, compassionate 75-minute reflection on the visceral, embodied reality of human existence; aging, vulnerability, and resting in the Deathless.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this monumental 75-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho reflects from the vantage point of his 79th year on 'What It Is Like to Be a Human Being.' To have a human body means to be sensitive: to experience backaches, weariness, cold, heat, and eventual death. Worldly culture constantly sells us fantasies of perpetual youth, power, and perfection. But awakening is accepting our frail, vulnerable human embodiment with immense kindness, while resting in the unborn conscious space that transcends mortality.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Reality of Vulnerability**: Embracing physical sensitivity without resentment or denial.
- **Aging as a Noble Teacher**: How gray hair, stiff joints, and fatigue point relentlessly to Dhamma.
- **The Human Sanctuary**: Cultivating boundless compassion for ourselves and all living beings who share this mortal state.
