## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: Dhp 1–2 (Yamakavagga - Mind Precedes All Things), SN 35.23 (Sabba Sutta - The All).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating how habitual thought and perception construct our subjective reality; awakening from the self-created world into the Dhamma.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Reflecting on the opening verses of the Dhammapada ('Mind precedes all mental states; mind is their chief; they are mind-made'), Luang Por Sumedho unpacks how 'We Create the World.' We believe we are objective observers viewing an external world, but our experience is heavily filtered through acquired biases, grievances, and desires. When the mind is purified of clinging, the world of suffering ceases, and reality is experienced with luminous clarity.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Architecture of Perception**: How our concepts and memories project meaning onto raw sensory data.
- **The Prison of Self-Creation**: Recognizing that the enemies, burdens, and grievances we suffer are largely self-fabricated.
- **Stepping Outside the Projection**: Resting as pure knowing prior to conceptual projection.

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por describes how two people can attend the exact same monastic meal: one complains bitterly about the spicy soup and hard floor, while the other is overwhelmed with joy and gratitude, proving that experience is created in the mind.
