## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 4 (Bhayabherava Sutta - Fear & Dread), AN 4.184 (Abhaya Sutta).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating existential fear, panic, and worldly stress; finding the Fearless (Abhaya) in unconditioned presence.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho addresses fear and stress—the pervasive companions of worldly existence. Fear of death, fear of failure, fear of rejection, and physical stress tighten the body and cloud the mind. Luang Por reminds the Sangha that the Buddha practiced deliberately going into spooky wilderness charnel grounds to face fear and dread (*Bhayabherava Sutta*). By meeting fear with welcoming awareness rather than running away, we discover that awareness itself has never experienced a single second of fear.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Biological Reflex of Fear**: Understanding that fright is animal survival programming, not personal weakness.
- **Facing Dread (*Bhaya*)**: How retreating monastics confront snakes, sickness, and isolation in the jungle.
- **The Realm of Abhaya (Fearlessness)**: Taking refuge in the Deathless, where no threat can reach.

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por describes his terrifying encounters with deadly green pit vipers and king cobras in his kuti at Wat Pah Pong, and how forcing himself to sit perfectly still and radiate mettā dissolved the terror into immense calm.
