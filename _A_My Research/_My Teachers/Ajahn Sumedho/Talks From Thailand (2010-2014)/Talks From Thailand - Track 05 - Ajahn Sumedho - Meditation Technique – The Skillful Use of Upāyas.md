## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Pong, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 22 (Alagaddūpama Sutta - The Raft Simile), AN 4.49 (Vipallāsa Sutta).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Understanding meditation techniques as rafts and skillful means (upāyas) rather than holy ends in themselves.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this concise 17-minute exhortation, Luang Por Sumedho addresses meditation techniques. Techniques—whether reciting 'Buddho', sweeping the body, counting breaths, or noting—are temporary tools (*upāyas*). Their sole purpose is to steady the wandering mind. Clinging to a technique creates sectarian dogmatism; letting go of the tool when it has done its work reveals the unconditioned.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Technique as a Skillful Device**: Recalling the Buddha's simile of the raft: tools are for crossing over, not carrying on one's back.
- **Beyond Mechanical Practice**: Why repeating a mantra without awareness becomes dull hypnosis.
- **Resting in the Open Ground**: Transitioning from guided effort to unconditioned presence.
