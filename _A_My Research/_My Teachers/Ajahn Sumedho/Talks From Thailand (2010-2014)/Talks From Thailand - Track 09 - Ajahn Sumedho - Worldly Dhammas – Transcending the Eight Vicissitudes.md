## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Pong, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: AN 8.5 & AN 8.6 (Lokadhamma Suttas - The Worldly Conditions).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Unshakable equanimity amidst the Eight Worldly Dhammas: gain and loss, praise and blame, fame and disrepute, pleasure and pain.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho addresses the classic teaching of the *Aṭṭha Lokadhamma* (The Eight Worldly Winds). The unenlightened mind is tossed endlessly like a leaf in a gale: elated by praise and gain, devastated by blame and loss. Luang Por reminds monastics that liberation is not arranging a life where only pleasure and praise occur, but developing the majestic equanimity (*upekkhā*) that remains unshakeable through all eight conditions.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Eight Worldly Winds**: Pleasure/Pain, Gain/Loss, Praise/Blame, Fame/Disrepute.
- **The Suffering of Attachment**: How craving for praise and gain turns practitioners into emotional beggars.
- **The Mountain of Equanimity**: Remaining solid like granite whether praise or blame blows across the heart.

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por describes how Ajahn Chah would intentionally praise a monk in the morning to see if his head swelled, and harshly criticize him in the evening to see if he sulked, teaching them to stand free of both.
