## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat / Wat Pah Pong, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: AN 4.28 (Ariyavaṁsa Sutta - Traditions of the Noble Ones), AN 5.77.

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The foundational ascetic practices (dhutaṅgas) of the Thai Forest Tradition: enduring hardship, eating little, speaking little, sleeping little, and community work as contemplative inquiry.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho reflects on the austere foundational training under Venerable Ajahn Chah. Modern culture obsesses over comfort, sleep, rich food, and endless socializing. But the Forest Tradition intentionally restricts these four physical outlets: eating little, speaking little, sleeping little, and bearing patiently with weather and sickness. Restraint is not self-mortification; it is a skillful strategy to reveal the underlying restlessness and clinging of the ego.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Four Forest Restraints**: How limiting food, sleep, speech, and physical comfort disarms habitual seeking.
- **Khanti (Patient Endurance)**: Cultivating the unshakeable capacity to bear heat, cold, insects, and fatigue without mental complaint.
- **Work as Meditation**: Sweeping pathways, washing alms bowls, and maintenance chores as immediate exercises in selfless presence.
