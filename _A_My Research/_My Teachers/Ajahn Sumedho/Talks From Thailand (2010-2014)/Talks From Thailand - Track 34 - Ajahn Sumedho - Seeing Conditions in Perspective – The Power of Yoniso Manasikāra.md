## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Pong, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 2 (Sabbāsava Sutta - Wise Attention), AN 10.61.

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Developing wise perspective (yoniso manasikāra) over bodily illness, difficult companions, and changing worldly fortunes.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho explores *Yoniso Manasikāra*—wise, appropriate attention. When we lose perspective, small problems become overwhelming catastrophes: a cold draft, a sore tooth, or a rude word feels like the end of the world. By putting conditions into perspective—seeing them against the vast backdrop of time, impermanence, and universal human suffering—the mind immediately regains equilibrium and calmness.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Microscope of the Ego**: How self-view magnifies trivial annoyances into major suffering.
- **Yoniso Manasikāra (Wise Reflection)**: Stepping back to view events in their proper cosmic and Dhamma context.
- **The Gift of Perspective**: Meeting life's challenges with lightness of heart and good humor.

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por recalls getting malaria in a remote forest hut in Thailand with a 104-degree fever, feeling like he was dying, until he realized: 'Well, either I will live or I will die; worrying about it won't cure malaria,' which brought instant emotional peace.
