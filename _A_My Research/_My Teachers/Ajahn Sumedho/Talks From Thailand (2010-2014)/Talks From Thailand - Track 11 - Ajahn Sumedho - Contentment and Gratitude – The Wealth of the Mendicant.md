## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Pong, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: AN 2.32 (Kataññū Suttas - Gratitude), Sn 2.4 (Maṅgala Sutta - Contentment & Gratitude).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Santosa (contentment) and Kataññū-katavedī (gratitude) as supreme spiritual guardians of monastic harmony and personal serenity.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho speaks on the twin monastic jewels of *Santosa* (contentment with little) and *Kataññū* (profound gratitude). Greed and discontentment are chronic sicknesses of worldly society that poison the mind with envy. A true practitioner trains to be content with whatever requisites are offered—food, shelter, robes, medicine—and radiates unbounded gratitude toward teachers, ancestors, and all living beings.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Power of Santosa**: Finding complete satisfaction in simple monastic requisites without seeking upgrades.
- **Gratitude as a Spiritual Foundation**: Recognizing the immense sacrifice of parents, teachers, and supporters.
- **Curing Discontent**: How mindfulness of gratitude instantly dispels bitterness and entitlement.
