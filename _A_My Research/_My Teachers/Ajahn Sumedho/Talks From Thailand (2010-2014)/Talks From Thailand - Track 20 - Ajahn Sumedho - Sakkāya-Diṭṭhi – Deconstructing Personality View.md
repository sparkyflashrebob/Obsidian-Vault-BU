## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: MN 44 (Cūḷavedalla Sutta), SN 22.89 (Khemaka Sutta), SN 22.1 (Nakulapita Sutta).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The foundational fetter: thoroughly deconstructing sakkāya-diṭṭhi (personality view / identity view) to realize stream-entry (sotāpatti).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered to senior and junior monks at Wat Pah Nanachat in late 2012, Luang Por Sumedho focuses on the first of the Ten Fetters: *Sakkāya-diṭṭhi* (personality view). Identifying with the five aggregates as 'me' or 'mine' is an acquired habit learned after birth. When we observe thoughts of self-worth, comparison, status, and physical aging as transient conditions, the delusion of a permanent personal core collapses, opening the heart to stream-entry.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Illusion of the Separate Self**: How society instills personality view through names, status, and comparisons.
- **The 20 Modes of Sakkāya-Diṭṭhi**: Identifying form, feeling, perception, choices, or consciousness as the self.
- **Stream-Entry (*Sotāpatti*)**: The unshakeable confidence that arises when identity-view is forever dismantled.
