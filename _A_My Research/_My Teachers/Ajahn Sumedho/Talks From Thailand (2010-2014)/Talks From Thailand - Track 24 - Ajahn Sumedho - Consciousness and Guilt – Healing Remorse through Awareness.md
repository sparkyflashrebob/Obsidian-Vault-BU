## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Wat Pah Nanachat, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand, recorded during Luang Por Sumedho's historic post-retirement residency in Thailand following his step-down as Abbot of Amaravati in 2010.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: AN 3.65 (Kesamutti / Kālāmā Sutta), AN 10.1 (Kimatthiya Sutta - Freedom from Remorse).

### Circumstances
Intimate, advanced monastic instructions given to junior and senior forest monks at Wat Pah Nanachat, Wat Pah Pong, and Dtao Dam, focusing on pure unconditioned awareness, ascetic discipline, and the living legacy of Venerable Ajahn Chah.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha of Wat Pah Nanachat and Wat Pah Pong, international monks, and visiting lay practitioners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Dissolving the toxic psychological habit of guilt and remorse (kukkucca) using the luminous power of pure consciousness.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho diagnoses a widespread disease that afflicts Western practitioners: spiritual guilt and chronic remorse (*kukkucca*). Raised in cultures that equate righteousness with guilt and self-punishment, meditators weaponize Dhamma teachings to beat themselves up for past mistakes. Luang Por shows how guilt is simply another unwholesome condition of aversion (*dosa*). By bringing guilt into conscious awareness without defending or believing it, the heart is cleansed by the cool water of forgiveness and moral clarity (*avippatisāra*).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Poison of Guilt**: Recognizing guilt as subtle self-hatred disguised as moral sensitivity.
- **Wise Conscience (*Hiri-Ottappa*) vs. Neurotic Guilt**: Differentiating healthy moral shame from destructive self-punishment.
- **Freedom from Remorse (*Avippatisāra*)**: How present-moment mindfulness wipes the slate clean.
