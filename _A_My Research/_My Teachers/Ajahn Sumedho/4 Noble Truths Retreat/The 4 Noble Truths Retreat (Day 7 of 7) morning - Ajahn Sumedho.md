## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance
Delivered at Kong Meng San Phor Kark See Monastery, Bright Hill Road, Singapore, hosted by the Buddha Dhamma Foundation and Venerable Sik Kwang Sheng.

### Canonical Setting
Canonical reference: DN 16 (Mahāparinibbāna Sutta), AN 5.179 (Gihi Sutta - The Lay Follower's Practice).

### Circumstances
Morning discourse from the intensive 7-day Four Noble Truths retreat held in Singapore in 2006, guiding international and Singaporean lay practitioners into the experiential realization of non-attachment and unconditioned awareness.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Host**: Venerable Sik Kwang Sheng (Abbot of Kong Meng San Phor Kark See Monastery) & The Buddha Dhamma Foundation.
- **Assembly**: Hundreds of Singaporean and international retreatants observing the Eight Precepts and noble silence.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Closing ceremony, precept renewal and final blessings; transitioning from the Eight Precepts back to the Five Precepts; carrying unshakeable refuge into modern daily life.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
On the final morning of the retreat, Luang Por Sumedho leads the closing ceremony and delivers his parting exhortation to the Singapore assembly. As participants transition from the monastic austerity of the Eight Precepts back to the Five Precepts for lay life, Luang Por reminds them that awareness does not end when retreat bows finish. Anger, fear, jealousy, and worldly stress will arise in daily family life, but they are not personal—they are simply worldly conditions. Luang Por offers heartfelt gratitude to Venerable Kwang Sheng, the Buddha Dhamma Foundation, and Kong Meng San Phor Kark See Monastery, leaving practitioners anchored in the living refuge of Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Transition to Daily Life**: Returning to family, career, and society with the Five Precepts as an ethical compass.
- **Awareness Beyond the Retreat**: Remembering that emotions are impersonal worldly phenomena, not personal failings.
- **Gratitude & Community**: Acknowledging the monastic hosts and volunteers who created the conditions for Dhamma practice.
