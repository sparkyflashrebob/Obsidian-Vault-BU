### Circumstances
Morning discourse from the intensive 7-day Four Noble Truths retreat held in Singapore in 2006, guiding international and Singaporean lay practitioners into the experiential realization of non-attachment and unconditioned awareness.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].

- **Assembly**: Hundreds of Singaporean and international retreatants observing the Eight Precepts and noble silence.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Deep somatic contemplation of the physical body (rūpa); deconstructing the first fetter of personality view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi) by shifting from 'I am hurting' to 'There is bodily feeling'.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
As retreatants reach the third day—often the peak of somatic restlessness and physical aches—Luang Por Sumedho addresses the root illusion of identity view (*sakkāya-diṭṭhi*). Society conditions us from childhood to equate our self-worth with the appearance, health, youth, and agility of the flesh. But the body is an aggregate of nature (*khandha*), composed of the four elements (*earth, water, fire, wind*), subject to sickness, decay, and death. Through Vipassanā, we drop the habit of taking the body personally. By shifting from the panicked thought 'I am suffering' to the objective observation 'There is painful feeling', the heart steps free from bondage.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Somatic Vessel**: Viewing the body as biological nature rather than personal property.
- **The Deconstruction of Sakkāya-Diṭṭhi**: Exposing the mental reflex that manufactures an 'I' around raw sensory signals.
- **Taking the Person Out of Experience**: Practicing the language of Dhamma: observing sensations as impersonal conditions.
