### Circumstances
Morning discourse from the intensive 7-day Four Noble Truths retreat held in Singapore in 2006, guiding international and Singaporean lay practitioners into the experiential realization of non-attachment and unconditioned awareness.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].

- **Assembly**: Hundreds of Singaporean and international retreatants observing the Eight Precepts and noble silence.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Establishing the container of retreat practice: taking refuge in the Triple Gem as present-moment conscious awareness (Buddho); the Eight Precepts and noble silence; understanding suffering (dukkha).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho opens the 7-day Singapore retreat by radically reframing the Three Refuges and the Eight Precepts. Refuge in Buddha is not devotion to a historic statue or bronze idol; it is taking refuge in present-moment conscious awareness (*Buddho*). Refuge in Dhamma is trusting the ultimate, unconditioned reality (*paramattha dhamma*) here and now. The Eight Precepts provide the necessary somatic boundary and noble silence to restrain habitual distraction, setting the stage for investigating the First Noble Truth: suffering is not a personal failure, but a universal condition to be deeply understood (*pariññeyya*).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The True Meaning of Refuge**: Moving beyond ethnic and ritualistic Buddhism to locate Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha directly within mindful awareness.
- **The Power of the Eight Precepts**: How ethical restraint and noble silence create an unshakeable boundary that starves sensory proliferation.
- **The Task of the First Noble Truth**: Why dukkha must be understood rather than avoided, fought, or resented.
