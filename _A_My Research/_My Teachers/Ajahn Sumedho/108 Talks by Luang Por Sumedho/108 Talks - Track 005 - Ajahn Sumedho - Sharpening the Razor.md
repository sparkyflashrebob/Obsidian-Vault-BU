### People Involved

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Cultivating razor-sharp discernment (paññā) to cut through the subtlest forms of conceit, spiritual ambition, and unconscious self-justification.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered in 1980 at Chithurst, Luang Por Sumedho employs the metaphor of 'sharpening the razor' of wisdom. In spiritual practice, blunt awareness merely floats on top of conditioning. True insight requires an unsparing, keen honesty that inspects even the most virtuous thoughts and spiritual identities. By cutting through identification with the refined states of meditation, the mind realizes genuine detachment.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Dull vs. Keen Mind**: Dull mindfulness tolerates habitual daydreaming; sharpened mindfulness cuts immediately upon the arising of conceit.
- **The Subtlety of Spiritual Conceit**: How practitioners use Dhamma language to justify subtle arrogance, judgment of others, or spiritual complacency.
- **Slicing Through the Five Aggregates**: Using the razor of impermanence (*anicca*) to dissect form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness.
- **The Simplicity of Non-Attachment**: Leaving things exactly as they are without adding conceptual commentary.
