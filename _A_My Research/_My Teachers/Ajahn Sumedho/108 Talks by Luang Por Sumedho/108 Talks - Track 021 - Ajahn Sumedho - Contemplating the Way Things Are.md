### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Developing Yathābhūtañāṇadassana—direct seeing of the way things are—by stepping outside the projections of memory and expectation.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered in 1987 at Amaravati, Luang Por Sumedho explores the central aim of Buddhist meditation: knowing the way things are. We spend our lives trapped in conceptual ideals of how we want ourselves and others to be. By anchoring attention in the present moment through mindfulness, we directly behold the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of all phenomena.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Reality vs. Idealism**: Western culture conditions us to measure reality against abstract perfection, creating chronic disappointment.
- **Seeing the Arising and Ceasing**: Direct perception of the birth and death of thoughts, sensations, and feelings.
- **The Peace of Non-Contention**: When we no longer argue with the present moment, suffering ceases.
