### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Cultivating the Four Sublime Abidings (Brahma-Vihāras)—Loving-Kindness (Mettā), Compassion (Karuṇā), Sympathetic Joy (Muditā), and Equanimity (Upekkhā)—as boundless spiritual attitudes of harmlessness and emotional maturity.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered during the early Hampstead Vihara days in 1978, Luang Por Sumedho introduces the Four Sublime Abidings (*Brahma-Vihāras*). Mettā is not romantic affection or sentimental warmth; it is the deliberate refusal to harbor aversion toward any condition, including one's own perceived imperfections. When the heart rests in non-ill-will, compassion, sympathetic joy, and unshakable equanimity follow naturally.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Mettā as Harmlessness**: Loving-kindness is the unshakeable determination not to add negativity or aversion to whatever arises in the mind or environment.
- **Karuṇā Meeting Suffering**: Compassion does not pity or wallow in sadness; it holds the pain of living beings in tender, spacious presence without panic.
- **Muditā Overcoming Jealousy**: Celebrating the good fortune, virtue, and success of others dissolves the subtle competitive comparisons of the ego.
- **Upekkhā as the Anchor**: Equanimity is the discerning wisdom that understands kamma, knowing that all beings inherit the fruits of their intentional actions.
