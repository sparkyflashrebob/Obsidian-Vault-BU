### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating the self-constructed psychological cages of conceit, expectations, and habitual roles; the keys to effortless liberation.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho examines the subtle psychological traps we construct: 'Traps We Bind Ourselves Into.' We complain about feeling constrained, trapped by work, trapped by family obligations, trapped by aging, or trapped by religious vows. Yet upon close investigation, nobody locked us in a cage from the outside; we weave our own prisons out of attachment to roles, status, desires, and grievances. Realizing that the cage door was never locked allows us to step out into boundless freedom.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Architecture of the Cage**: How identity-view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi) builds invisible bars of obligation and resentment.
- **The Illusions of External Blame**: Seeing through the reflex to blame parents, society, or organizations for our inner bondage.
- **The Open Door**: Discovering that non-clinging (anupādāna) instantly dissolves the prison.

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por tells the famous story of the monkey trap in India: a hollowed gourd containing fruit with a small hole. The monkey reaches in, grabs the fruit, balls his fist, and finds he cannot pull his hand out. The hunter easily captures him—not because the trap is strong, but because the monkey refuses to let go of the fruit.
