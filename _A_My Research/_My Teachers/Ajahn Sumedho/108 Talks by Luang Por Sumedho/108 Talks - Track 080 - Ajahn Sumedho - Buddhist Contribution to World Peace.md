### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Non-violence begins internally; why outer political treaties fail without the inner disarmament of anger and aversion.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 2006 discourse, Luang Por Sumedho addresses 'The Buddhist Contribution to World Peace.' Outer wars, terrorism, and political conflicts are the magnified external expressions of unacknowledged greed, hatred, and fear inside individual human hearts. True world peace cannot be legislated by politicians; it requires personal inner disarmament—learning to bear with our own aversion without weaponizing it.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Outer War as Inner Reflection**: Seeing that planetary violence is the collective projection of unhealed human anger.
- **The Discipline of Non-Harming (*Ahiṁsā*)**: Refusing to act out malice toward any living being, no matter the provocation.
- **The Sovereign Refuge**: Offering an unshakeable sanctuary of peace and kindness to a frightened, polarized world.
