### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Practical troubleshooting and experiential inquiry into working with the Sound of Silence in sitting meditation, walking, and daily activity.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Recorded in 1989 at Amaravati, this question-and-answer session addresses retreatants' direct experiences with the Sound of Silence. Luang Por Sumedho answers nuanced questions about distinguishing the sound from physical ear damage, working with volume variations, integrating the sound with breath awareness, and utilizing it to dissolve acute fear and mental agitation.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Distinguishing Nāda from Tinnitus**: Tinnitus is typically irritating and accompanied by ear fullness or hearing loss; the sound of silence brings deep calm and spaciousness.
- **Handling Volume Fluctuations**: Noticing how the sound seems louder when the mind is quiet, and softer when agitated.
- **Integrating Sound with Breath**: Using the sound as the background space within which the breathing body moves.
- **Dissolving Panic**: Tuning directly into the sound when overwhelming emotional storms arise.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 'Is this sound just tinnitus?' | Worrying about medical pathology or hearing loss | Listen without grasping; notice if it induces peace. If peaceful and steady, use it as a portal to stillness. |
| 'What if the sound disappears?' | Straining to hear it; fear of losing the meditation object | Notice the silence itself; do not create anxiety over perceived absence. |
| 'Can I listen to it while walking or working?' | Thinking meditation can only occur with closed eyes | Yes, use it as a continuous background anchor while interacting in daily life. |

Luang Por Sumedho answers a meditator who was terrified that listening to the sound would drive them crazy, reassuring them that listening to the sound leads to sanity, not insanity.
