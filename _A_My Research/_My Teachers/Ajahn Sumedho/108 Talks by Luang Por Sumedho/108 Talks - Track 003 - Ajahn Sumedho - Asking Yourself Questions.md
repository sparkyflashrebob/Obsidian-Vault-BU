### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Using deliberate, contemplative self-inquiry (ko'ham / 'Who am I? What is this?') to puncture identity view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi) and cut the momentum of conceptual narrative.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered in 1978, Luang Por Sumedho introduces the powerful contemplative tool of asking internal questions. By consciously asking 'Who am I?', 'What is this?', or 'Why was I born?', the thinking mind pauses in a gap of non-knowing. In that silence between the question and any conceptual answer, the unconditioned nature of awareness reveals itself.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Puncturing the Story of Self**: Questions stop the unconscious momentum of self-centered narratives.
- **The Silence Following the Question**: The value is not in formulating an intellectual answer, but in noticing the silent presence that receives the question.
- **Investigating the Core Questions of Existence**: Mortality, origin, purpose, and consciousness examined not through philosophy, but through direct observation.
- **The Shift from Thought to Knower**: Moving from the content of mind (*dhamma*) to the knowing of mind (*citta*).

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Contemplative Inquiry | Intellectual / Conceptual Trap | Direct Contemplative Realization |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 'Who am I?' | Formulating a psychological, biographical, or spiritual identity | The pause before thought arises; resting in unnamable awareness (*Buddho*). |
| 'What is this feeling?' | Analyzing childhood causes or rationalizing complaints | Direct somatic attunement to raw sensation without narrative. |
| 'What happens when I die?' | Clinging to rebirth theories or dreading annihilation | Recognizing that that which knows birth and death was never born and never dies. |
