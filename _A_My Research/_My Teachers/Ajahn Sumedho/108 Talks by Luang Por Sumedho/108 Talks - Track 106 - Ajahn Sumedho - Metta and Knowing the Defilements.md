## 2. Structured Contextual Metadata

### Location & Provenance

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha and lay community gathered at Amaravati Buddhist Monastery, Great Gaddesden, Hertfordshire, UK.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Cultivating unconditional loving-kindness (mettā) specifically toward our own mental blemishes and defilements (kilesas) rather than waging war against them.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this deeply compassionate late-career discourse, Luang Por Sumedho reframes the relationship between loving-kindness and mental defilements: 'Mettā and Knowing the Defilements.' Monastics and meditators frequently fall into aversion toward their own anger, greed, jealousy, and dullness, treating the defilements as enemies to be violently stamped out. Luang Por teaches that true mettā must begin right here: holding our imperfections, neurotic reactions, and unskilful habits in vast, patient, non-judgmental kindness, allowing them to dissolve naturally in awareness.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Aversion to Defilements**: How spiritual guilt and self-hatred masquerade as righteous discipline.
- **Mettā as Contemplative Holding**: Bringing gentle warmth to the knotted, aching places in the mind and heart.
- **The Alchemy of Acceptance**: Watching defilements lose their destructive momentum when met with compassion rather than resistance.
