### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating sensual pleasure (kāmasukha); understanding its allure (assāda), drawback (ādīnava), and the superior, enduring peace of renunciation (nekkhamma).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered in 1986, Luang Por Sumedho examines humanity's obsession with sensual gratification. Sensual pleasure is not evil, but it is inherently limited, ephemeral, and incapable of providing lasting fulfillment. By recognizing the brief spark of sensory pleasure and the inevitable fatigue that follows, the heart naturally inclines toward the unconditioned joy of inner stillness.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Allure and the Trap**: Sights, sounds, tastes, and touches provide brief satisfaction followed by renewed craving.
- **Similes of the Buddha**: Sensual pleasures likened to a dry bone coated with blood, or a torch carried against the wind.
- **Nekkhamma-Sukha (The Joy of Letting Go)**: Experiencing the physical and psychological lightness of renunciation.
