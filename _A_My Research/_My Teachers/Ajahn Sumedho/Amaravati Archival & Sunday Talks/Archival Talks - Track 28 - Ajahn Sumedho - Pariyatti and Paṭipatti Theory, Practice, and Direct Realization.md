### People Involved

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Harmonizing scriptural study (pariyatti) with direct meditative cultivation (paṭipatti) to achieve realization (paṭivedha).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Speaking in Thailand, Luang Por Sumedho examines the classical Theravāda triad: *pariyatti* (scriptural learning), *paṭipatti* (meditative practice), and *paṭivedha* (direct realization). He warns of two common extremes: scholars who master the Pāli Canon but remain slaves to irritability and pride, and meditators who despise learning and invent their own eccentric delusions. True practice uses scripture as a road map and direct meditation as the journey.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Water-Snake Parable**: How grasping scriptural theory without practice leads to arrogance.
- **Paṭipatti as the Test of Fire**: Putting scholastic theories into the crucible of ascetic monastic life.
- **Paṭivedha as the Fruit**: When theoretical knowledge dissolves into direct, verified awakening.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Stage | Essential Function | Pitfall of One-Sided Clinging |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pariyatti (Study) | Provides the doctrinal road map, ethical guidelines, and sutta compass | Scholastic arrogance, debating pride, collecting intellectual concepts without peace. |
| Paṭipatti (Practice) | Directly confronting hindrances, bodily pain, and psychological shadow | Blind wandering, spiritual eccentricity, inventing personal techniques without a guide. |
| Paṭivedha (Realization) | Direct, irrevocable insight into the Four Noble Truths and Anattā | Mistaking temporary meditative bliss or energetic highs for final enlightenment. |
