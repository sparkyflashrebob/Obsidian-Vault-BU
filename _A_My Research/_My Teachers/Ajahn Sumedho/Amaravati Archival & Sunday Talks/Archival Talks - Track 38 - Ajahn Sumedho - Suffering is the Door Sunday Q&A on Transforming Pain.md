Archival monastic discourse delivered by Venerable Ajahn Sumedho, addressing resident monastics, visiting teachers, and the lay assembly on core principles of Theravāda Forest Tradition contemplation.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha, resident community, visiting practitioners, and lay disciples.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The Upanisa Sutta doctrine: how suffering (dukkha) acts as the proximate cause for faith (saddhā) and liberation.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this penetrating Sunday Q&A session, Luang Por Sumedho reframes suffering from a curse into a sacred doorway. Drawing on the Upanisa Sutta (Transcendental Dependent Arising), he shows that suffering is the indispensable catalyst for spiritual awakening. As long as life is mildly pleasant, we sleepwalk in comfort. It is the acute rub of dukkha that forces us to question delusion and seek the Deathless.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Dukkha as the Sacred Catalyst**: Why suffering is the first Noble Truth, not the last.
- **Transcendental Dependent Arising**: Tracing the chain from dukkha to faith, joy, tranquility, and liberation.
- **Embracing the Unpleasant**: Welcoming pain as our most reliable spiritual teacher.

| Spiritual Fruit | Chronic despair, cynicism, recurring cyclic misery | Deep faith (*saddhā*), profound peace, and irrevocable liberation. |
