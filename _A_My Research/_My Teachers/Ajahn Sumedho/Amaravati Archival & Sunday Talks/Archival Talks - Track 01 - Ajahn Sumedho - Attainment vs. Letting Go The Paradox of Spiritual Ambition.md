### People Involved

- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha, resident community, visiting practitioners, and lay disciples.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Resolving the tension between the desire to attain enlightenment and the radical practice of letting go.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho addresses the fundamental paradox of Buddhist practice: we begin with the desire to attain enlightenment, but the very desire to attain something becomes the main obstacle. Enlightenment is not an acquisition added onto the self; it is the total cessation of grasping (*upādāna*). When we stop trying to attain special states and let go of all conditions, the deathless reality reveals itself.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Ambition to Attain**: Exposing the subtle greed hidden within spiritual aspirations.
- **The Raft Metaphor**: Understanding that Buddhist teachings are vehicles for crossing, not objects to own.
- **The Freedom of Non-Attainment**: Discovering peace in what is already unconditioned.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Goal | Acquiring samādhi, jhānas, wisdom, or status as an enlightened person | Relinquishing all grasping; resting in the natural cessation of craving. |
| Attitude to Present | Treating the present moment as inadequate or an obstacle to future peace | Seeing the present moment as the only doorway to the Deathless. |
| Result | Frustration, spiritual exhaustion, subtle conceit (*māna*) | Immediate spaciousness, unshakeable ease, and selfless humility. |
