Archival monastic discourse delivered by Venerable Ajahn Sumedho, addressing resident monastics, visiting teachers, and the lay assembly on core principles of Theravāda Forest Tradition contemplation.

### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha, resident community, visiting practitioners, and lay disciples.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The Buddha's declaration: 'Whoever sees the Dhamma sees me; whoever sees me sees the Dhamma.'

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho reflects on the living relationship between Buddha and Dhamma. In the Vakkali Sutta, the Buddha famously rebuked a disciple who spent all day gazing at his physical form: 'What is there in looking at this foul body, Vakkali? He who sees the Dhamma sees me.' Luang Por demonstrates that Buddha is not a historical corpse buried in India; Buddha is the living, real-time knowing of reality as it is.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Historical vs. The Living Buddha**: Transcending idol worship to realize Buddho.
- **Seeing the Dhamma in All Things**: Recognizing impermanence in every passing thought and breath.
- **The Supreme Refuge**: How taking refuge in Buddha-Dhamma gives unshakeable stability.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nature | A physical body born in Lumbini, aged in Magadha, died at Kusinara | The unborn, unconditioned quality of wakefulness present in all hearts. |
| Accessibility | Vanished 2,500 years ago into final Nibbāna | Directly accessible this very second by paying attention to the present. |
| Refuge | A memory and historical inspiration | The living, immediate sanctuary from all suffering (*Sandīṭṭhiko*). |
