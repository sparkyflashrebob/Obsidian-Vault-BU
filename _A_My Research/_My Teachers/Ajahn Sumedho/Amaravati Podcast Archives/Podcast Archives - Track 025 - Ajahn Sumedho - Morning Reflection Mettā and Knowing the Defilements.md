### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Meeting mental defilements (kilesas) with unconditional loving-kindness (mettā) rather than self-hatred.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho addresses the common spiritual trap of waging war against defilements with anger and self-hatred. When greed, jealousy, lust, or pettiness arise, meditators often beat themselves up: 'I should be enlightened by now; I hate my mind!' But hating defilements is just another defilement (aversion). Luang Por shows that defilements must be met with *mettā*—embraced in spacious, loving awareness until they naturally dissolve.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Trap of Hating Defilements**: Why self-hatred cannot produce spiritual purity.
- **Mettā as the Universal Solvent**: Holding petty grievances in boundless goodwill.
- **The Karaṇīya Mettā Sutta Standard**: Like a mother protecting her only child with her life, so radiate boundless love.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Approach to Defilement | Waging War (Aversion / Dosa) | Embracing in Awareness (*Mettā-Bhāvanā*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Reaction to Greed / Lust | Panic, harsh self-flagellation, suppression, moralistic disgust | Noticing the energetic pull in the body; holding it with gentle, spacious metta. |
| Reaction to Petty Jealousy | Shame, hiding, pretending to be generous while boiling inside | Admitting: 'Here is jealousy; it is an impersonal condition; may this heart be at peace.' |
| Fruit | Emotional brittleness, depression, eventual explosive relapse | Natural purification, deep emotional healing, and unshakeable peace. |

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por shares how as a young monk he tried to blast away his sexual desire and food cravings by starving himself and sitting on sharp gravel, until Ajahn Chah laughed and told him: 'You don't cure greed by torturing the body; you cure greed by seeing that it has no owner.'
