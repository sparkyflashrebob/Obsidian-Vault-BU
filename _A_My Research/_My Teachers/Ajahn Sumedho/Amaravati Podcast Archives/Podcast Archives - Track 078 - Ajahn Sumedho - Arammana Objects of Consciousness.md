### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The nature of mind-objects (ārammaṇa) in Buddhist psychology: distinguishing between sensory objects and the knowing consciousness.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
A concise, technical, and practical 23-minute exposition on the Pali term 'ārammaṇa' (mind-object). Luang Por explains that everything perceived through the six sense doors—sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and thoughts—is merely an ārammaṇa. Consciousness is not the object; consciousness is that which knows the object.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Defining ārammaṇa as the objective support or mental object required for sensory consciousness to arise.
- How human suffering begins when consciousness becomes entangled with and possessed by its objects.
- Practicing the shift from being lost in the object to abiding as the knowing space (Buddho).
- The realization of cessation (nirodha) when the mind ceases to grasp at mental objects.
