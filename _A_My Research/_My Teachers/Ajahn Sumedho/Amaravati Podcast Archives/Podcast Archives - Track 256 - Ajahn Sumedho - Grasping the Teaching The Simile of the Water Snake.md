### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The famous Water Snake Simile: why holding Buddhist teachings incorrectly turns the Dhamma into a toxic instrument of conceit.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho delivers a 35-minute warning on spiritual dogmatism based on the Alagaddūpama Sutta. If a man grasps a water snake by its tail, the snake will turn around and bite his hand, causing death or agony. In the same way, if one grasps the Dhamma by the wrong end—using teachings to criticize others, debate philosophy, or feed spiritual pride—the Dhamma itself becomes a source of suffering. We must hold the Dhamma gently, as a medicine for our own defilements.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Water Snake Simile (Alagaddūpama Sutta): grasping Dhamma correctly by the neck.
- Using teachings to judge other people: the ultimate symptom of holding the snake by the tail.
- The difference between defensive religious dogma and transformative contemplative practice.
- Treating the teachings as a temporary raft for crossing, not a permanent monument.
