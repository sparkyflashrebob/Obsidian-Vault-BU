### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Morning reflection on the history and purpose of cross-legged sitting posture: freeing the body from dogmatic rigidity while maintaining alert presence.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 48-minute morning reflection, Luang Por Sumedho explores the historical evolution of meditation posture. In modern spiritual circles, sitting in the full lotus is often idolized as a magical guarantee of enlightenment, causing practitioners with stiff joints to suffer immense guilt. Luang Por reminds monastics that posture is merely an external frame for wakefulness. Whether on a cushion, a wooden bench, or a chair, the essence of meditation is an upright, relaxed spine and an awake heart.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The historical context of the lotus posture in ancient India.
- The Four Postures in the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta: sitting, standing, walking, lying down.
- Overcoming the guilt and spiritual vanity associated with lotus postures.
- Harmonizing physical alignment with effortless mental stillness.
