### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Ajahn Chah's famous folk teaching on Upāya (skillful means): using gentle ingenuity rather than blunt force to train the stubborn mind.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this delightful, humorous 53-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho shares one of Ajahn Chah's classic village metaphors: 'If you want to get your pig across the bridge, don't push it!' If you push a pig forward, it digs its hooves in and screams in stubborn refusal. But if you walk ahead and pull its tail backward, the pig will eagerly surge forward across the bridge! In the same way, blunt force and rigid willpower only make the mind rebellious; we need wise skillful means (*upāya*) to outsmart our defilements.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The failure of brute willpower: why forcing the mind to concentrate creates rebellion.
- Ajahn Chah's folk wisdom: using humor, paradox, and gentle cunning in meditation.
- Skillful means (Upāya): matching the remedy to the specific temperament of the obstacle.
- Transforming stubborn resistance into willing, joyful contemplative cooperation.
