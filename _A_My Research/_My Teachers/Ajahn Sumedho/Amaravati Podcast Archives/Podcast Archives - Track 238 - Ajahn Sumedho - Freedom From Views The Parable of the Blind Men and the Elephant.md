### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The famous Udāna parable of the blind men and the elephant: transcending dogmatic views, political polarization, and religious bigotry.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 62-minute masterwork, Luang Por Sumedho unpacks the Buddha's timeless parable of the blind men and the elephant from the Udāna. Each blind man feels one part of the beast—the trunk, the ear, the tusk, the leg—and angrily insists that an elephant is a snake, a fan, a plow, or a pillar, coming to blows over their partial views. Luang Por shows how all human wars, internet debates, and religious fanaticism stem from clinging to partial views as absolute truth.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Tittha Sutta: sectarian philosophers quarreling like blind men fighting over an elephant.
- How views (diṭṭhi) become identity armor: 'If you attack my opinion, you attack *me*.'
- The freedom of holding opinions lightly: seeing views as temporary perspectives.
- Awakening to the whole: resting in the unconditioned awareness that encompasses all views.
