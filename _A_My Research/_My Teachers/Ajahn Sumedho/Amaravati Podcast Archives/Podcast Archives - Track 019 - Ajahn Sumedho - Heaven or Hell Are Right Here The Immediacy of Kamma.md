### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Demystifying heaven and hell from cosmological afterlife realms into real-time psychological states.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho explores the realms of heaven (*sagga*) and hell (*niraya*). People often treat heaven and hell as physical places you travel to after death. Luang Por shows that heaven and hell are experienced right here through the six senses. Hell is the burning fire of rage, jealousy, and guilt; heaven is the cool delight of generosity and contentment. Awakening is transcending both into unconditioned peace.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Hell as Real-Time Contraction**: Experiencing the torture of resentment and self-hatred.
- **Heaven as Temporary Relief**: Why even celestial bliss is impermanent and cannot satisfy.
- **Beyond Heaven and Hell**: The supreme transcendence of Nibbāna.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Realm | Cosmological Fantasy | Immediate Psychological Reality (*Sandīṭṭhiko*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hell (*Niraya*) | Underground caverns of fire and pitchfork demons | The agonizing heat of rage, paranoia, chronic guilt, and suicidal despair. |
| Heaven (*Sagga*) | Fluffy clouds, golden harps, and eternal sensory pleasure | The refined joy of generosity, clean conscience (*avippaṭisāra*), and samādhi. |
| Nibbāna | A blank extinction or remote philosophical abstraction | The unconditioned peace of awareness free from all grasping right now. |

## 4. Stories & Anecdotes Offered

Luang Por tells the famous story of the samurai warrior who demanded a Zen master explain heaven and hell; when the master insulted him, the samurai drew his sword in fury, and the master said: 'Here open the gates of hell'; the samurai sheared his sword and bowed in gratitude, and the master said: 'Here open the gates of heaven.'
