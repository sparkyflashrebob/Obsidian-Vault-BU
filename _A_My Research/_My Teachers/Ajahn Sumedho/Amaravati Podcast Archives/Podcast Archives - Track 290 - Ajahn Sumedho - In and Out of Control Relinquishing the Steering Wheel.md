### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The core proof of non-self (Anattā): realizing that the body, emotions, and thoughts cannot be permanently controlled by personal will.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho unpacks the central argument of the Buddha's second discourse, the Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta. The Buddha asked the five monks: 'If form were self, could you say: *Let my body be thus; let my body not be thus*?' Obviously not! Bodies get sick, grow old, and die regardless of our wishes. Thoughts arise unbidden; feelings surge without permission. Luang Por shows that trying to control the uncontrollable is the definition of madness. Peace is relinquishing the steering wheel.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta argument: lack of control as the decisive empirical proof of Anattā.
- The exhausting illusion of the 'personal manager' running the mind.
- Surrendering control vs. chaotic recklessness: the distinction between non-clinging and negligence.
- Resting as the peaceful space that witnesses the flow of nature without interference.
