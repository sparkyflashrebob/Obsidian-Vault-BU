### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Mindfulness of the mind (cittānupassanā): witnessing moods, defilements, and states of consciousness without judgment.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 46-minute foundational exposition on the third satipaṭṭhāna, Luang Por Sumedho illuminates Cittānupassanā (mindfulness of the mind). When the mind is lustful, angry, dull, restless, or serene, we do not praise or condemn it; we simply know it as it is: 'Mind with lust, mind without lust, contracted mind, expanded mind.' This objective witnessing breaks the illusion of personal ownership.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The classical Satipaṭṭhāna formula for observing the mind: bare objective recognition.
- Deconstructing the habit of taking moods personally: 'I am not an angry person; anger is present in the mind.'
- Working skillfully with the shadow side: shame, jealousy, depression, and obsessive worry.
- Discovering the pure, unconditioned awareness that knows all mental states without being stained by them.

| Mental State (Citta) | Deluded Ego Reaction | Cittānupassanā Observation | Liberating Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sarāga Citta (Lust / Craving) | Indulgence or harsh puritanical guilt | 'Mind with lust is like this; it is anicca' | Craving subsides naturally without guilt |
| Sadosa Citta (Anger / Irritation) | Righteous fury, planning retaliation | 'Mind with anger is like this; it is heat' | Anger is an impersonal fire |
| Samāhita Citta (Concentrated / Calm) | Pride, spiritual conceit, grasping at calm | 'Mind in stillness is like this; it is pleasant' | No clinging to meditation highs |
