Mid-point reflection during the 3-month monastic winter retreat: renewing spiritual dedication and confronting complacency.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered exactly at the halfway mark of the three-month monastic winter retreat, Luang Por Sumedho gathers the community to take stock. The initial honeymoon of retreat silence has faded; the mind has settled into a comfortable routine, and subtle sloth, daydreaming, or restless counting of days begins to creep in. Luang Por reminds the Sangha: 'Half is over! Time waits for no one.' He calls for a fresh surge of Appamāda (heedfulness).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The psychology of the retreat midpoint: navigating boredom, fatigue, and complacency.
- The danger of 'marking time': waiting for retreat to end rather than practicing now.
- Appamāda (heedfulness) as the supreme antidote to spiritual stagnation.
- Reflecting on mortality: this breath may be the last opportunity to awaken.
