### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Somatic and psychological inquiry into the stubborn visceral feeling of 'I am' (asmimāna) that persists even after coarse identity view is abandoned.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this nuanced 60-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho explores the subtle feeling of self. He unpacks the famous Khemaka Sutta, where the elder Khemaka explains that although he has abandoned the five lower fetters, there still lingers a residual scent of 'I am'—like the smell of soap remaining on a freshly washed cloth. Luang Por guides meditators into feeling this subtle contraction in the body and letting it dissolve into space.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Khemaka Sutta: the lingering scent of 'I am' (asmimāna) prior to full Arahantship.
- The difference between coarse intellectual identity view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi) and subtle emotional selfhood.
- Locating the feeling of self as a physical knot or armoring in the solar plexus and throat.
- Dissolving the knot: allowing the feeling of 'I am' to evaporate into unconditioned silence.
