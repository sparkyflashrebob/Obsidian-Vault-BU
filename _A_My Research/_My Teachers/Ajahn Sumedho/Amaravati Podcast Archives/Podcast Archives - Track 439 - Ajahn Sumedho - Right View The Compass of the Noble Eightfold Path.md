### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The foundational factor of the Eightfold Path: Sammā-diṭṭhi as direct experiential seeing of the Four Noble Truths rather than ideological dogma.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 56-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho explores the alpha and omega of Buddhist practice: Right View (*Sammā-diṭṭhi*). Unpacking the Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta taught by Venerable Sāriputta, Luang Por clarifies that Right View is not merely believing a set of Buddhist propositions. Mundane Right View understands kamma (cause and effect); Transcendent Right View is the direct, experiential seeing that grasping causes suffering, and letting go brings peace.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Mundane Right View (kammassakatā) versus Transcendent Noble Right View (lokuttara).
- Right View as a raft (Alagaddūpama Sutta): a tool for crossing over, not a dogma to carry.
- How Right View guides the remaining seven factors of the Eightfold Path.
- Dropping all views: the ultimate fulfillment of Right View in pure awareness.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Level of View | Wrong View (Micchā-diṭṭhi) | Mundane Right View (Lokiya) | Transcendent Right View (Lokuttara) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Core Belief | Actions have no consequences; no rebirth | Actions have consequences; good kamma brings good fruit | Direct seeing of the Four Noble Truths |
| Relationship to Ego | Selfish, reckless, nihilistic license | Cultivating good ego; accumulating merits | Shattering of identity view (Anattā) |
| Ultimate Fruit | Severe suffering and rebirth in lower realms | Pleasant rebirth in human or heavenly realms | Complete liberation; Nibbāna |
