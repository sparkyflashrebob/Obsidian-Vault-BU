### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The paradox of monastic discipline: how the strict boundaries of the Vinaya dismantle self-will and open absolute inner freedom.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho explores the genius of the Buddhist monastic code (*Vinaya*). Western individualists look at the 227 rules of the Pātimokkha—eating before noon, not handling money, celibacy, communal permissions—and see a rigid prison. Luang Por explains that Vinaya is a voluntary container designed to frustrate the ego's addiction to doing whatever it wants. When you surrender personal preference to the rule, you discover that the boundaries set you free.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Vinaya as a communal training vessel, not a moralistic punishment.
- How voluntary limitations eliminate thousands of unnecessary worldly decisions.
- Frustrating the spoiled child of self-will: learning to say 'no' to impulsive desires.
- The joy of blamelessness (anavajja-sukha) that blossoms from impeccable ethical restraint.
