### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Exposing the chronic habit of mental grumbling, irritation, and fault-finding as the foundational anatomy of Dukkha.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this humorous, piercing retreat talk, Luang Por Sumedho spotlights the 'grumble-grumble' mind. Even when living in a comfortable monastery with abundant food and clean shelter, the mind easily finds something to grumble about: the weather is too damp, the tea is too cold, the chanting is too slow. Luang Por shows that complaining is an unconscious addiction to discontent. When we observe grumbling without believing it, peace replaces irritation.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The pervasive undercurrent of dis-ease: the habit of seeking what is wrong.
- Observing the inner complainer with humor and affectionate disidentification.
- The Second Arrow: adding psychological resentment on top of ordinary physical inconveniences.
- Cultivating contentedness (santuṭṭhi) as the supreme spiritual wealth.
