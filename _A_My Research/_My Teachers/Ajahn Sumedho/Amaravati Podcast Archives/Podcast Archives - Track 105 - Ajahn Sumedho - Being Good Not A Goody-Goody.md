### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Authentic moral integrity and kindness versus priggish, self-righteous perfectionism.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho draws a sharp and hilarious distinction between genuine moral goodness and being an annoying 'goody-goody'. Goodness is natural, warm, humble, and compassionate; being a goody-goody is self-conscious, priggish, judgmental of others, and desperately seeking social approval. The Buddha's ethics (sīla) are designed to free the heart from remorse, not to build a smug spiritual ego.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Deconstructing the psychology of the 'good boy / good girl' syndrome: seeking love through neurotic obedience.
- How self-righteousness turns Buddhist precepts into weapons for judging non-vegetarians or non-meditators.
- Authentic virtue (sīla) as natural non-harming, gentle presence, and uncalculated generosity.
- The courage to be authentic and flawed rather than wearing a plastic mask of holiness.
