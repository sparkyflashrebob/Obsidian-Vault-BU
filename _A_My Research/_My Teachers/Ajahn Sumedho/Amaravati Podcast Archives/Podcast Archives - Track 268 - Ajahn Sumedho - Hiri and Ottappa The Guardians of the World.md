### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The twin moral guardians of the world (Lokapāla Dhammā): Hiri (moral self-respect) and Ottappa (wise dread of harmful consequences).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho examines the classical Buddhist teaching on *Hiri* and *Ottappa*, known as the *Lokapāla Dhammā*—the guardians of the world. In the suttas, the Buddha states that without Hiri and Ottappa, human society would degenerate into bestial chaos. Luang Por defines Hiri as noble self-respect (not wanting to soil your dignity with cruelty) and Ottappa as intelligent fear of karma (respecting the painful consequences of harmful action).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Lokapāla Sutta: Hiri and Ottappa as the protective shield preserving human civilization.
- Hiri: intrinsic self-respect and conscience, independent of whether anyone is watching.
- Ottappa: healthy moral prudence; understanding the inexorable law of karma and vipāka.
- How Hiri and Ottappa protect meditation from the agitation of remorse and guilt.

| Ottappa (Moral Dread) | Wise foresight; 'This action will produce bitter, painful karma' | Cruelty, violence, exploitation, slander | Freedom from legal, social, and karmic remorse |
| World Without Guardians | Sociopathic disregard for conscience and consequences | Unrestrained greed, violence, exploitation | Self-destruction of society |
