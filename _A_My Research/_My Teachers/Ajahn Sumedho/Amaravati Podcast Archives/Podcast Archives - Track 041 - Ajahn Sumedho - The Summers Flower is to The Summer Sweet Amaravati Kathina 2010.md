### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Contemplation of Shakespeare's 94th Sonnet on selfless elegance, offered during Luang Por's final Kathina as Abbot.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered on the occasion of Luang Por's final Kathina festival as Abbot of Amaravati, this extraordinary discourse weaves Shakespeare's Sonnet 94 ('They that have pow'r to hurt and will do none...') into deep Buddhist teachings on non-harming, royal spiritual dignity, and the beauty of selfless virtue.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Reciting and interpreting Shakespeare's famous lines: 'The summer's flower is to the summer sweet, though to itself it only live and die.'
- Contemplating virtue (sīla) and restraint as true nobility of heart, rather than displays of worldly arrogance.
- Celebrating the vibrant international lay community whose generosity (dāna) has supported Amaravati for over 25 years.
- A poignant farewell reflection thanking the Sangha and lay supporters as Luang Por prepares for retirement.
