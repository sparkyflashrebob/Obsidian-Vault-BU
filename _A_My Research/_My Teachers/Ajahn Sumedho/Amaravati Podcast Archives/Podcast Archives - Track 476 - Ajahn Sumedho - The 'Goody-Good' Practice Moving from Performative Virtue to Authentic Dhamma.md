### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Dismantling performative spiritual perfectionism: moving beyond the neurotic desire to be seen as a 'good boy or good girl' into authentic virtue.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 49-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho exposes a common Western spiritual neurosis: the 'goody-good' practice. Conditioned by childhood demands to please parents and teachers, many practitioners turn Buddhism into a performance of being exceptionally well-behaved, solemn, and saintly. Luang Por explains that performative virtue is rooted in fear and identity view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi). True Sīla is not about looking good; it is about living with fearless honesty and harmlessness.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The psychology of the 'good boy/good girl' syndrome in spiritual seekers.
- Sīlabbata-parāmāsa: attachment to rules, rituals, and outward appearances.
- The hidden rage and resentment generated by trying to be artificially saintly.
- Authentic virtue (Sīla) as spontaneous, natural integrity born of wisdom.
