### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Cultivating unshakeable loving-kindness amidst international wars, social hostility, and political hatred.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this potent 61-minute talk delivered during a period of intense global conflict, Luang Por Sumedho discusses how to embody love in a world consumed by hatred. The Buddha taught the famous Simile of the Saw: even if bandits were to sever our limbs with a two-handled saw, anyone who harbored hatred would not be practicing his teaching. Luang Por emphasizes that Mettā is not sentimental weakness; it is the ultimate spiritual courage to refuse to add hatred to a burning world.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Buddha's Simile of the Saw (Kakacūpama Sutta): radical non-hatred under extreme provocation.
- Refusing to participate in collective hysteria and political demonization.
- How hatred burns the container that holds it long before harming the enemy.
- Embodying immeasurable goodwill (mettā-bhāvanā) as active spiritual resistance.
