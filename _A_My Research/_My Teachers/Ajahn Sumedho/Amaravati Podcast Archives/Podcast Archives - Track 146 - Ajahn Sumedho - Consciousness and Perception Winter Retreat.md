### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Disentangling pure consciousness (viññāṇa) from conditioned perceptual memory and labeling (saññā).

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered during the silent winter retreat of January 2006, Luang Por Sumedho examines the critical interface between consciousness and perception. Consciousness knows bare sensory contact; perception (saññā) instantly slaps a label, a name, a memory, and a value judgment onto it ('good', 'bad', 'enemy', 'ugly'). By observing the gap between knowing and labeling, the tyranny of conditioned perception collapses.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Deconstructing the five aggregates: the distinct functions of viññāṇa (knowing) and saññā (perceiving).
- How distorted perception (saññā-vipallāsa) creates illusions of permanence, beauty, and self.
- The practice of looking at visual forms and hearing sounds before perceptual labels arise.
- Resting in the pristine clarity of bare sensation held in spacious awareness.
