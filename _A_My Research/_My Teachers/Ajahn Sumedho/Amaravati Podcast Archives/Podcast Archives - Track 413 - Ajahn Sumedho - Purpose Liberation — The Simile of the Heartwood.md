### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Remembering the supreme goal of practice: not falling in love with the twigs, bark, or sapwood, but attaining the heartwood of unshakeable liberation.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this uncompromising 50-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho unpacks the Cūḷasāropama Sutta. A man seeking valuable heartwood walks into a forest, but gets enchanted by twigs and leaves, then by outer bark, then by sapwood, taking them home thinking he has found the core. The Buddha warns that practitioners do the same: they get enchanted by material gain, by moral purity, or by meditative absorption, mistaking them for the goal. Luang Por reminds us: the sole purpose of the holy life is unshakeable liberation of the heart (*akuppā ceto-vimutti*).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The five layers of the spiritual tree in the Cūḷasāropama Sutta.
- The trap of stopping at the leaves (material requisites and donations).
- The trap of stopping at the bark (moral rectitude and religious pride).
- The trap of stopping at the sapwood (deep meditative jhānas and psychic powers).
- The heartwood: unshakeable release of mind through wisdom.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Layer of Tree (MN 29) | Spiritual Achievement | Inherent Danger / Trap | Contemplative Discernment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Twigs & Leaves (Sākhā) | Material offerings, wealth, monastery buildings | Becoming a greedy, complacent monastic manager | Leaves are merely external shelter; keep moving |
| Outer Bark (Taca) | Flawless moral discipline, austerity (dhutaṅga) | Spiritual conceit; looking down on others | Sīla is a tool for peace, not an ego badge |
| Sapwood (Pheggu) | Blissful meditative jhānas, samādhi trances | Addiction to altered states; fear of everyday life | Samādhi is a means to see clearly, not the goal |
| Heartwood (Sāra) | Akuppā ceto-vimutti (Unshakeable Liberation) | None; unconditioned and beyond all corruption | The ultimate fruit: complete end of Dukkha |

Luang Por recalls a senior monk who sat in perfect samādhi for 14 hours every day but flew into a rage when someone moved his cushion, illustrating how easy it is to possess beautiful sapwood while lacking the heartwood of liberation.
