### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The foundational Theravāda triad: Dāna (giving), Sīla (ethical restraint), and Bhāvanā (mental cultivation) as an indivisible vehicle.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho articulates the foundational matrix of Buddhist life: Dāna, Sīla, and Bhāvanā. Western meditators often jump straight to intensive meditation while neglecting generosity and ethical restraint, wondering why their minds remain agitated. Luang Por demonstrates that giving loosens the gripping fist of greed, morality establishes psychological safety and blamelessness, and meditation blossoms naturally upon this clean foundation.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Dāna as the primary practice of releasing attachment and softening self-cherishing.
- Sīla as intentional harmlessness (ahiṁsā) that eliminates moral guilt and remorse.
- Bhāvanā as the direct investigation of reality supported by an unburdened conscience.
- The inter-dependence of heart cultivation: generosity feeding virtue, virtue stabilizing stillness.
