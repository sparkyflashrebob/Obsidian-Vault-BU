### Theme of the Dharma Talk
A sharp, witty deconstruction of Descartes' 'Cogito ergo sum' from the perspective of Buddhist non-self (Anattā) and the paralysis of doubt.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this brilliant 71-minute discourse from 2021, Luang Por Sumedho takes on Western philosophy's famous maxim: Descartes' 'I think, therefore I am' (*Cogito ergo sum*). Luang Por points out that Descartes made a fatal error: he assumed that because there is thinking, there must be an enduring 'I' doing the thinking! In Buddhist reality: 'There is thinking, therefore there is doubt!' When we step back from thoughts, we discover the pristine knowing that exists prior to thought.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Critiquing Western Cartesian dualism from the standpoint of Buddhist Dependent Origination.
- How thinking inherently breeds skepticism, doubt (vicikicchā), and existential alienation.
- Anattā: seeing thoughts as impersonal phenomena that arise and cease without a thinker.
- Resting in the unconditioned knowing that was never manufactured by intellect.
