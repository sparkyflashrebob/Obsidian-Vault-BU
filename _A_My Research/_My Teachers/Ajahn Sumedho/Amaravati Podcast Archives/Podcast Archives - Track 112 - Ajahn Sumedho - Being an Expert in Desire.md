### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Deep investigation of craving (taṇhā)—sensual desire, desire for becoming, and desire for annihilation.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho humorously suggests that every Buddhist practitioner should become a certified 'expert in desire'. Instead of running away from desire or moralizing against it, we must study its anatomy: how desire begins with a subtle itch, creates a compelling mental fantasy, promises eternal satisfaction, and ends in disillusionment. By understanding desire completely, we transcend it.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The three kinds of craving: kāma-taṇhā (sensual), bhava-taṇhā (becoming), and vibhava-taṇhā (annihilation).
- Why suppressing desire only drives it into the unconscious shadow.
- Observing the somatic pull and physical hunger of desire without acting upon it.
- The supreme joy of non-desire (nekkhamma): tasting the unhooked, contented heart.
