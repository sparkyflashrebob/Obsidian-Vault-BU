### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Contemplating the universal law of origination and cessation: 'Whatever is subject to origination is subject to cessation.'

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho unpacks the famous insight of the Venerable Koṇḍañña upon hearing the Buddha's First Discourse: 'Yaṁ kiñci samudayadhammaṁ sabbaṁ taṁ nirodhadhamman' (Whatever is subject to origination is subject to cessation). By resting in cessation (nirodha), the mind experiences the unshakeable peace that was never born.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The profound simplicity of Koṇḍañña's insight that gained him the Dhamma Eye (dhamma-cakkhu).
- How meditators fixate obsessively on the arising of thoughts while ignoring their natural cessation.
- Practicing watching thoughts vanish into silence, and resting in the stillness that follows.
- The realization of Nirodha (Third Noble Truth) as the living taste of Nibbāna.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Phase of Condition | Habitual Focus | Vipassanā Contemplation | Liberating Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Samudaya (Arising) | Fascinated or terrified by new thoughts | Noting that a thought has arisen by cause | Detachment from content |
| Ṭhiti (Sustaining) | Feeding the thought with mental commentary | Allowing the thought to exist without refueling it | Seeing its energy ebb |
| Nirodha (Cessation) | Quickly searching for another distraction | Resting in the empty stillness after the thought ends | Direct recognition of the Deathless |
