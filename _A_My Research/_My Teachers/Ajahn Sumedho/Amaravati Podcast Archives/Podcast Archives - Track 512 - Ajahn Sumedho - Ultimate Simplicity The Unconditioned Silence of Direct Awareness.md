### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Resting in ultimate simplicity: recognizing the unformed, unconditioned nature of awareness beneath all worldly and mental complexity.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 43-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho points directly to the ultimate simplicity of the Dhamma. Human suffering is intensely complicated, fueled by millions of thoughts, past regrets, future anxieties, and complex identities. Yet liberation is utterly simple: it is the stillness that knows. Quoting the famous Udāna passage on the 'unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned,' Luang Por reminds monastics that Nibbāna is not something to be constructed through effort, but the unconditioned peace revealed when grasping ceases.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Udāna 8.1 discourse on the unborn, unbecome, and unconditioned (asaṅkhata).
- The difference between fabricating meditative states and realizing unconditioned peace.
- The Nada sound (sound of silence) as an effortless pointer to ultimate simplicity.
- Releasing the burden of spiritual ambition and personal attainment.
