### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The natural, organic awakening of the Seven Factors of Enlightenment (Bojjhaṅga) through simple receptive presence.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Luang Por Sumedho explores the Seven Factors of Enlightenment (*Bojjhaṅga*). Meditators often believe awakening is an explosive lightning bolt that requires violent mental force. Luang Por reveals that awakening is an organic unfolding: when mindfulness (*sati*) is established, investigation (*dhamma-vicaya*) naturally sparks, energy (*viriya*) flows without strain, joy (*pīti*) and tranquility (*passaddhi*) settle the nervous system, leading naturally to unwavering samādhi and equanimity (*upekkhā*).

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The Seven Factors of Enlightenment as an integrated ecological system.
- The balance between energizing factors (investigation, energy, joy) and calming factors (tranquility, concentration, equanimity).
- Why awakening cannot be forced by willpower, but must be allowed to mature.
- Resting as the unconditioned ground in which all seven factors blossom.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Awakening Factor (Bojjhaṅga) | Somatic & Mental Manifestation | Defilement Neutralized | Awakened Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Sati (Mindfulness) | Receptive, open awareness of the present | Heedlessness, distraction | The master foundation |
| 2. Dhamma-vicaya (Investigation) | Curious inquiry into conditionality | Delusion, mental stupor | Ignites wisdom |
| 3. Viriya (Energy) | Tireless vitality; cheerful perseverance | Sloth and torpor (Thīna-middha) | Sustains practice |
| 4. Pīti (Joy / Rapture) | Lightness of heart; physical goosebumps | Dryness, cynical depression | Nourishes the spirit |
| 5. Passaddhi (Tranquility) | Cool, deeply settled nervous system | Restlessness and worry (Uddhacca) | Calms the body |
| 6. Samādhi (Stillness) | Unwavering absorption in the moment | Scattered wandering attention | Gathers the mind |
| 7. Upekkhā (Equanimity) | Unshakeable balance; neither liking nor disliking | Reactivity, partiality | Crown of liberation |
