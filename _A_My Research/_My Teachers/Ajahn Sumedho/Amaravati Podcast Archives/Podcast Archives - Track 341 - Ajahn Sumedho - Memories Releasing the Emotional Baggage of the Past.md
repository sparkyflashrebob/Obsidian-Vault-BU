### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating the phantom nature of autobiographical memories: dissolving old grievances, nostalgic yearnings, and traumatic loops.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this extensive 84-minute discourse, Luang Por Sumedho explores the nature of memory (*saññā*). Most human suffering consists of replaying past memories—reliving childhood grievances, failed marriages, perceived insults, or longing for lost youth. Luang Por demonstrates that a memory is not the past; a memory is a fleeting thought occurring right now in the present moment. Recognizing memory as empty mental fabrications instantly liberates the heart.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The psychology of memory (saññā): how the brain categorizes and replays conditioned files.
- Trauma and grievance: how the ego builds its identity out of past suffering.
- The Bhaddekaratta Sutta: 'Do not pursue the past; do not build hopes upon the future.'
- Resting in the unconditioned present where memories are recognized as harmless phantoms.
