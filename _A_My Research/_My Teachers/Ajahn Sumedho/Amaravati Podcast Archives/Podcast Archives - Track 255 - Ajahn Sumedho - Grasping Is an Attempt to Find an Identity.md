### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The deep psychology of clinging (upādāna): why we cling to opinions, possessions, and relationships to sustain a phantom self.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this penetrating 2021 reflection, Venerable Ajahn Sumedho unpacks the core mechanics of Upādāna (grasping). We usually think we grasp things because they are enjoyable. Luang Por shows the deeper truth: we grasp things because without grasping, the ego feels terrified of non-existence! Grasping to an opinion, an enemy, a political cause, or a possession is a desperate attempt to say 'I exist'. When we discover the fullness of unconditioned knowing, the need to grasp vanishes.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The four types of clinging (upādāna): sensual pleasures, views, rites/rituals, and self-doctrine.
- How clinging functions as the existential glue holding together the illusion of an ego.
- Why arguing about politics or religion feels like life-or-death self-defense.
- Resting in the unconditioned space where nothing needs to be possessed or defended.
