### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Contemplating nekkhamma (renunciation): tasting the profound joy and lightness of living with few possessions and zero worldly ambition.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
In this 39-minute morning discourse, Luang Por Sumedho explores the second factor of the Noble Eightfold Path: Right Intention, specifically the intention of renunciation (*nekkhamma-saṅkappa*). Worldly culture equates happiness with accumulation—more possessions, more followers, more sensual indulgence. Luang Por shows how monastic life proves the exact opposite: true happiness (sukha) is the joy of renunciation, of laying down burdens.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- Nekkhamma (renunciation) as one of the Ten Pāramīs and Right Intention.
- The psychological exhaustion of maintaining material wealth and social status.
- The monastic joy of owning three robes, an almsbowl, and a quiet room.
- Renunciation of opinions, grievances, and the compulsion to be right.
