### Theme of the Dharma Talk
William Blake's famous verse on non-clinging applied to Buddhist contemplative practice: enjoying joy without attempting to possess it.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered during winter retreat, Luang Por Sumedho weaves William Blake's immortal lines into the heart of Dhamma: *'He who binds to himself a joy, does the winged life destroy; but he who kisses the joy as it flies, lives in eternity's sunrise.'* When pleasant feelings, meditative bliss, or deep insights arise, our instinctual habit is to grab them, cage them, and demand they stay. Luang Por teaches the noble art of 'kissing joy as it flies': savoring goodness with an open palm.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- The destructive nature of possessive clinging: crushing the butterfly of joy in a fist.
- Blake's poem as a flawless expression of Buddhist non-attachment (anupādāna).
- Why attempting to repeat yesterday's great meditation sit always ends in frustration.
- Abiding in 'eternity's sunrise': resting in the deathless present moment.
