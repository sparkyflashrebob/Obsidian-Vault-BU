### People Involved
- **Speaker**: [[Ajahn Sumedho]].
- **Assembly**: Monastic Sangha, resident community, retreatants, and podcast listeners.

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Commemorating the gathering of 1,250 arahants and the fundamental charter of Buddhist practice: the Ovāda-Pātimokkha.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
On the full moon of Māgha, Luang Por Sumedho delivers an extensive discourse on the Ovāda-Pātimokkha—the Buddha's fundamental charter of the spiritual life. He breaks down the classic verses: 'Not doing any evil, cultivating the good, purifying one's own mind—this is the teaching of the Buddhas.' Patient endurance is the supreme tapo; one who harms another is no monastic. Luang Por shows how this ancient summary provides the ultimate guide for daily life.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Spontaneous Gathering**: 1,250 arahants assembling without prior notice.
- **The Triad of Practice**: Ceasing harm (*sabba-pāpassa akaraṇaṁ*), cultivating good (*kusalassūpasampadā*), and purifying the mind (*sacitta-pariyodapanaṁ*).
- **Patience as the Supreme Fire (*Khantī Paramaṁ Tapo*)**: Why patient endurance burns defilements without causing violence.

## 3. Topic Breakdown & Practical Frameworks

| Core Verse | Literal Pāli Instruction | Living Contemplative Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Ceasing Evil | *Sabbapāpassa akaraṇaṁ* | Strict adherence to harmlessness; refraining from violence, theft, deceit, and malice. |
| 2. Cultivating Good | *Kusalassūpasampadā* | Actively generating loving-kindness, generosity, gratitude, and helpfulness. |
| 3. Purifying Mind | *Sacittapariyodapanaṁ* | Silent meditation; cleaning the heart of greed, hatred, and delusion through awareness. |
| 4. Patient Endurance | *Khantī paramaṁ tapo tītikkhā* | Holding discomfort, insults, and hardships with graceful, uncomplaining forbearance. |
