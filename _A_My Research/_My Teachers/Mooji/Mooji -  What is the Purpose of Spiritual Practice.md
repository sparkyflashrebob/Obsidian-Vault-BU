What is the Purpose of Spiritual Practice?
28 October 2020 (with subtitles)
Hello again. Namaste beloved ones, friends.
I want to share just a short pointer.
As far as spiritual practice is concerned,
the most important thing to remember is that,
practice, whatever it may be,
whether it is pranayama, or a sort of meditation,
or walking meditation,
contemplation, pujas, yoga, whatever it is,
the purpose of practice
is actually to come to the end of practice.
It is to shed spiritual and personal luggage, for good.
So practice is not something that is the goal.
Practice is a means to the goal.
And for the seekers of Truth,
practice is the means to return to our original nature,
our original status, the Self.
And it's very important,
because if you are practicing, don't become attached to practice.
Be earnest about the practice,
but the practice actually is to clarify,
to make more clear what is true from what is untrue.
Truth is not many. Truth is one.
Untruths are uncountable!
So, as you look into whatever practice you are doing,
remember that it is not sufficient
just for it to become that the practice is your daily practice,
but without a focused goal.
If we are ...
Whatever it is that your practice, your sadhana, is,
if it requires, at the end of the day,
that your peace, your inner composure,
or your realisation of the Truth depends upon a practice,
you have not really attained it.
The practice should be cutting away the false, permanently,
shaving it off.
And the True, you don't need to get,
because when the false is identified,
exposed, identified and flushed out,
what remains is the Truth.
The Truth did not go somewhere else
while we were engaged with lesser or relative truths.
It was always here.
In fact, nothing relative can appear
without the absoluteness of the Truth.
Please remember that. Love you, love you.
All the very best. And God bless you.
