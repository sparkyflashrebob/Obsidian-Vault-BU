## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker / Chanting Leader**: Ajahn Candasiri.

* **Theme**: The meditative practice of communal Pāli chanting; *Buddhānussati, Dhammānussati, Sanghānussati*; aligning breath and voice in harmonious unison; internalizing the sonic resonance of sacred text.

* **Circumstances**: Conducted as the opening devotional evening pūjā during the 2008 Spirit Rock Monastic Retreat co-led by the Forest Sangha monastics.

* **Condensed Version of the Practice**: Ajahn Candasiri leads the retreat assembly in the traditional Theravāda evening liturgy in the original Pāli language. The service begins with the Dedication of Offerings (*Yo so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho*), proceeding to the Homage to the Triple Gem (*Namo Tassa*), the Supreme Praise of the Buddha (*Buddhābhithuti*), the Supreme Praise of the Dhamma (*Dhammābhithuti*), and the Supreme Praise of the Sangha (*Sanghābhithuti*), concluding with the Salutation to the Triple Gem and Request for Forgiveness (*Kāyena vācāya va cetasā vā*). Rather than treating chanting as a passive spectator event, Ajahn Candasiri guides practitioners to let the vocal vibration resonate within the physical chest and abdomen, turning sacred sound into a somatic anchor that calms agitation and establishes deep presence.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

Chanting can be practiced individually or in community as a powerful remedy for mental dispersion. When reciting sacred reflections, feel the vibration in the throat, chest, and belly. If our stomach is tight or knotted up, allow each long out-breath of chanting to soften the abdominal wall, grounding attention in the present reality of refuge.
