## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Candasiri.

* **Theme**: Engaging ethically and mindfully with society; avoiding moral self-righteousness; the danger of ideological polarization; cultivating Right Thought (*Sammā-Saṅkappa*), Right Speech (*Sammā-Vācā*), and Right Action (*Sammā-Kammanta*) in the public arena.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered to a crowded hall of lay visitors and practitioners at Amaravati on a Sunday afternoon, addressing the tensions of modern social, environmental, and political engagement.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Candasiri addresses the deep challenge of living as a conscious human being in a troubled society. Many sincere people want to right wrongs—protesting war, injustice, environmental degradation, or poverty. However, activists frequently burn out because their fuel is anger, moral superiority, and indignation (*'We are the righteous ones, they are the evil ones'*). Drawing upon the Buddha's teachings on effacement (*Sallekha Sutta*) and Right Speech (*Vācā Sutta*), Ajahn Candasiri reminds us that means and ends cannot be separated. If our speech is harsh, sarcastic, or contemptuous, we perpetuate the very division we seek to heal. True social action begins with purifying the inner landscape: acting firmly from love, truthfulness, and non-harming (*ahiṃsā*).
