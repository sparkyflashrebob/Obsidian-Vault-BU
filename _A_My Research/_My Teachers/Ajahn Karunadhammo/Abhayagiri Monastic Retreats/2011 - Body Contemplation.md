## 📖 Executive Summary & Core Teachings

Ajahn Karunadhammo introduces the memorable simile of "walking on the top of a fence":
* On one side of the fence is the necessity of cultivating wholesome habits, concentration techniques, and ethical conduct. We cannot bypass effort; we need skillful means (*upāya*) to train the undisciplined mind.
* On the other side of the fence is the profound insight of the Three Characteristics (*tilakkhaṇa*): recognizing that even the most sublime meditative attainments and skillful habits are impermanent (*anicca*), conditioned (*saṅkhāra*), and not-self (*anattā*).

Contemplation of the body (*kāyagatāsati*) is the ultimate practice for maintaining this dynamic balance. The body is relentlessly honest. It keeps the practitioner grounded in sensory reality, preventing the mind from escaping into spiritual abstraction or conceptual pride.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Karunadhammo:**

Over the past few days, we have been exploring many different aspects of practice, but they generally fall along two primary tracks: 
First, developing skillful habits and cultivating wholesome qualities of mind. 
Second, viewing all conditioned phenomena through the lens of the Three Characteristics: impermanence (*anicca*), unsatisfactoriness (*dukkha*), and not-self (*anattā*).

It is very much like walking along the top of a narrow fence. 

We know that we must cultivate virtue, we must train the attention, and we must develop skillful tools like breath awareness and loving-kindness. We need these rafts to cross the river. But simultaneously, we must avoid the trap of spiritual materialism—clinging to our techniques and making an identity out of our attainments.

Mindfulness of the body (*kāyagatāsati*) is our greatest ally in walking this fence. 

The physical body doesn't engage in philosophical spin. You can convince yourself in your head that you are serene and detached, but the body shows the reality: an accelerated heart rate, clenched facial muscles, or a knotted belly. 

When we ground awareness in the body, we meet reality without delusion. We use the body as an anchor, cultivate stillness, and let insight arise naturally.
