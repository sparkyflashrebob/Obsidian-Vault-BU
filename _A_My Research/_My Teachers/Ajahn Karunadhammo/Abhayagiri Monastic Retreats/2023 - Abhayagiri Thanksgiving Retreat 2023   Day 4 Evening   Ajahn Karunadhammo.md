## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Core Universal Diagnosis
The Buddha's awakening did not produce a complicated speculative philosophy; it revealed three undeniable characteristics of all conditioned phenomena:
- *Anicca*: Everything in the universe is in constant flux; nothing stands still.
- *Dukkha*: Because things are in flux, they are incapable of providing permanent security or ultimate happiness.
- *Anattā*: Because things are in flux and unsatisfying, they cannot be claimed as an autonomous, enduring 'self.'

### 2. Conditionality (*Idappaccayatā*) in Practice
*When this is, that is; with the arising of this, that arises. When this is not, that is not; with the cessation of this, that ceases.* Ajahn Karuṇadhammo brings this cosmic law down to the meditation cushion: when the breath flows in, chest sensations arise; when breath flows out, they cease. There is no controller behind the curtain; there is only nature.

### 3. The Relief of Disenchantment (*Nibbidā*)
Seeing the Three Characteristics does not create depression; it sparks *nibbidā* (holy disenchantment). We stop expecting a rental car to be a permanent palace. Disillusionment with worldly grasping leads directly to dispassion (*virāga*) and unshakeable freedom (*vimutti*).

## 📜 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Broken Glass**: Ajahn Karuṇadhammo shares Luang Por Chah's famous simile of the drinking glass: 'You see this glass? For me, this glass is already broken. I enjoy using it, I drink from it, but when the wind knocks it over or someone drops it and it shatters, I say: "Of course." When you see that the glass is already broken, every moment with it is precious, and its breaking brings no suffering.'
