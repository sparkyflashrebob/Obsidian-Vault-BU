## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Art of Balances (*Indriya-Samatta*)
Meditation often falters because spiritual faculties pull in conflicting directions:
- **Faith vs. Wisdom**: Excessive faith without wisdom leads to naive credulity; excessive wisdom/analysis without faith produces cynical, hyper-critical intellectualism.
- **Energy vs. Concentration**: Excessive energy without stillness causes restless agitation (*uddhacca*); excessive concentration without energy causes sleepy, dull torpor (*thīna-middha*).
- **Mindfulness (*Sati*)**: The universal balancer that harmonizes both pairs.

### 2. Death Contemplation as the Great Clarifier
Ajahn Karuṇadhammo reflects on sitting at the bedsides of dying patients. When breath begins to fail, bank accounts, academic degrees, and grudges mean nothing. The only question that matters is: *'Have I cultivated peace, kindness, and understanding in this heart?'*

### 3. Turning Mortality into Liberation
Mindfulness of death (*maraṇānussati*) is not macabre or depressing; it is the ultimate wake-up call. It shatters the delusion that we have endless time, inspiring practitioners to treat every single breath as a priceless, irrecoverable gift.

## 📊 Topic Breakdown & Supporting Practices

| Meditative Imbalance | Symptoms in Practice | Skillful Balancing Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Faith > Wisdom** | Emotional devotion without critical discernment; vulnerable to blind belief. | Cultivate *Paññā*: question, investigate, inspect the Three Characteristics. |
| **Wisdom > Faith** | Over-intellectualizing; cynical doubt; dry theoretical critique. | Cultivate *Saddhā*: recollect virtues of the Buddha and the lineage. |
| **Energy > Samādhi** | Restlessness, jumping from technique to technique, somatic tension. | Cultivate *Samādhi*: soften body, lengthen exhalation, settle into anchor. |
| **Samādhi > Energy** | Sinking into pleasant blankness, sluggish nodding, dull stupor. | Cultivate *Viriya*: stand up, open eyes, sharpen attention on in-breath. |
