## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Myth of the Sacred Cushion
Many practitioners view the meditation cushion as the only holy ground, treating all activities between sits as meaningless interruptions. Ajahn Karuṇadhammo corrects this view: if we are mindful for 45 minutes of sitting but unconscious for 15 minutes of transition, the unmindful momentum undoes the stillness we just cultivated.

### 2. The Mechanics of the Transitions
- **Uncrossing the Legs**: Noticing the release of physical pressure without rushing.
- **Standing Up**: Feeling the shift of weight into the soles of the feet, the engagement of thigh muscles, the spine finding vertical balance.
- **Walking Meditation (*Caṅkama*)**: Knowing the lifting, moving, and placing of each foot, keeping attention rooted in somatic touch.

### 3. Creating an Unbroken Thread of Presence
Like rubbing two dry sticks together to make fire, if you rub for thirty seconds and then stop for ten, the heat dissipates and fire never catches. Continuous mindfulness builds the spiritual friction that ignites liberating insight.

## 📊 Topic Breakdown & Supporting Practices

| Meditative Approach | Broken / Fragmented Practice | Continuous Transitional Mindfulness (*Sampajañña*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Post-Bell Behavior** | Jumps up abruptly; mind immediately plans next task. | Pauses; notices sensations of moving; maintains gentle gaze. |
| **Walking Meditation** | Pacing back and forth while daydreaming. | Tangible contact with soles of feet; rhythmically linked to breath. |
| **Handling Tasks** | Rush to get chores done so one can 'meditate.' | Treating the chore itself as the meditation object. |
| **Samādhi Momentum** | Constantly starting over from zero on each sit. | Cumulative, snowballing tranquility that carries through the day. |

## 📜 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Fire Sticks Simile**: Ajahn Karuṇadhammo shares the ancient Forest Tradition simile: an impatient monk rubs fire sticks together, but every time his arms get tired, he stops to rest. After ten years, he still has no fire. The wise monk rubs steadily without stopping, and flame blazes forth.
