## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Classical Dignity of Caṅkama
In the suttas, walking meditation was practiced extensively by the Buddha and elder disciples. The rhythm of walking generates gentle physical warmth, promotes digestion, and provides an active somatic anchor that prevents the mind from sinking into dullness.

### 2. Establishing the Walking Track
- Choosing a straight path of 20 to 30 paces.
- Standing at one end, establishing posture, and feeling the body grounded in gravity.
- Walking at a natural, unhurried pace, feeling the lifting, moving, and placing of each step.
- Pausing mindfully at each end before turning around.

### 3. A Concentration That Endures
The Buddha pointed out that concentration developed while walking lasts longer than concentration developed solely while sitting, because it trains the mind to remain serene in the midst of movement.
