## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Onset of Physical Fatigue
By Day 3, the initial novelty of retreat has worn off, and the body registers accumulated fatigue. Sitting for long periods brings up intense somatic sensations. Ajahn Karuṇadhammo validates this as the natural biological curriculum of meditation.

### 2. Dissecting the Monolith of 'Pain'
'Pain' is an intimidating, aggressive conceptual label. When we peel back the label, what is actually there?
- Is it burning heat?
- Is it dull throbbing?
- Is it mechanical pressure or pulling?
Sensations constantly change in intensity and boundary. They are not solid or permanent.

### 3. Softening the Defensive Shell
Our habitual reaction to pain is to clench the jaw, hold the breath, and brace the stomach. Ajahn Karuṇadhammo instructs meditators to breathe into the area, softening the surrounding tissues. When the Second Arrow of aversion is abandoned, the First Arrow of physical sensation loses its power to torment the mind.

## 📜 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Tight Fist**: Ajahn Karuṇadhammo uses the simile of clenching a fist as hard as possible until knuckles turn white: the strain comes not from the hand itself, but from the continuous muscular contraction. When you open the hand, instant relief follows. Open the heart around physical pain.
