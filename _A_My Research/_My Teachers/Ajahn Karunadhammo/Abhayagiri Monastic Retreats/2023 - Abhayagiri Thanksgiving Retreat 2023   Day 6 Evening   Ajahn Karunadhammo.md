## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Anatomy of Kataññū-Katavedī
In the Pāli Canon, gratitude is not a passive emotion; it is an active moral orientation.
- *Kataññū*: Recognizing and acknowledging the goodness done for us.
- *Katavedī*: Taking action to reciprocate that goodness in the world.

### 2. Gratitude as the Antidote to Grievance
Modern worldly culture breeds grievance, cynicism, and entitlement: *'I am owed more; society is broken; life is unfair.'* While injustice exists, living in chronic grievance poisons the heart. Gratitude shifts attention to the miraculous fact that against all odds, we have food, shelter, air to breathe, and access to the liberating Dhamma.

### 3. Paying the Dhamma Forward
As silence prepares to break, Ajahn Karuṇadhammo reminds practitioners that the best way to honor this retreat is not by building a secluded shrine, but by becoming a pillar of patience, listening, and compassion for family, friends, and co-workers.
