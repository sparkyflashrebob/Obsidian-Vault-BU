## 1. Teacher Profile & Contemplative Scope

Ven. Ajahn Karuṇadhammo was born in the United States and ordained under Ven. Ajahn Pasanno and Ven. Ajahn Amaro at Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery in 1997. Serving for many years as a pillar of the community and currently as co-abbot of Abhayagiri, his teachings are celebrated for their meticulous integration of canonical early Buddhist suttas (particularly the Khandha and Sacca saṃyuttas, Majjhima Nikāya discourses, and the Vinaya Pitaka) with the direct, earthy wisdom of Luang Por Chah.

His presentations provide practical contemplative tools for working with spiritual plateaus, emotional contractions, the illusion of control, and uncovering the unconditioned peace of Nibbāna.

## 3. Contemplative Architecture & Doctrinal Synergy

### The Canonical Similes of Non-Self (*Anattā*)
In his Five Khandhas retreat talks, Ajahn Karuṇadhammo draws upon the most striking similes of the Pāli Canon to deconstruct self-view (*sakkāya-diṭṭhi*):
- **The Chariot (SN 5.10 / Milindapañha)**: Just as the word "chariot" exists only in dependence on pole, axle, wheels, and frame, so "a living being" exists only in dependence on the five aggregates.
- **The Lump of Cowdung (SN 22.96)**: Taking a lump of cowdung, the Buddha declares that there is not even that much permanent, stable selfhood to be found in any of the aggregates.
- **The Dog on the Leash (SN 22.99)**: The untrained mind, bound by craving, runs in circles around form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness, just as a leashed dog circles a post.
- **The Washed Cloth (SN 22.89 - Khemaka Sutta)**: Even after coarse defilements are removed, a subtle residual fragrance of "I am" (*asmimāna*) lingers, requiring continuous contemplation of impermanence to completely evaporate.

### Culmination in Nibbāna and The Island
The 2013 Winter Retreat study reaches its summit in Track 56 with readings from *The Island* (Chapter 20: "Ah, What Bliss! – The Blessings of Nibbāna") alongside the unconditioned declarations of Udāna 8.1 and 8.3: *"There is, monks, an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned..."* Here, Ajahn Karuṇadhammo weaves together the canonical architecture with monastic retreat practice, demonstrating that Nibbāna is the ultimate cessation of all grasping.
