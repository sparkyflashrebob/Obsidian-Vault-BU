## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Cunda.

* **Theme**: Sustaining motivation in long-term spiritual practice; the psychology of internal resistance; the Buddha's teaching on going against the stream (*Paṭisotagāmī*); navigating the friction between habitual worldly desire and renunciation.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered to the resident monastic community and retreatants at Abhayagiri, investigating why practitioners stall in their practice and how to re-ignite authentic spiritual vigor.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Cunda addresses a universal spiritual paradox: we understand that letting go brings peace, yet our minds stubbornly cling to grievances, views, and desires. Worldly conditioning flows in the direction of sensual gratification, self-aggrandizement, and defensive reactivity (*anusotagāmī*). Spiritual practice, by contrast, flows 'against the stream' (*paṭisotagāmī*). When we sit in meditation or engage in community life, going against the grain creates immediate psychological friction. If we mistake this friction for failure, we lose motivation. Ajahn Cunda encourages practitioners to recognize friction as proof that practice is working. By patiently observing resistance without self-criticism, awareness unhooks from desire, transforming struggle into profound peace.
