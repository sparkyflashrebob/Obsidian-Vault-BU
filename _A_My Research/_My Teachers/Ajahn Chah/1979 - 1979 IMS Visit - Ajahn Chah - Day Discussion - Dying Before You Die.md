# 🎙️ 1979 - 1979 IMS Visit - Ajahn Chah - Day Discussion - Dying Before You Die
### *Ven. Ajahn Chah (Translated by Jack Kornfield) — Insight Meditation Society, Barre, MA (June 16, 1979)*

> *"Someone asked: 'Is it best to die now and get it over with?' Ajahn Chah smiled and said: 'Yes, die now! Die to the false idea of self, die to your preferences, die to your grasping. When you die before you die, there is no death left to fear.' Contemplating mortality is not looking toward an end in the distant future—it is recognizing that every thought, every breath, every conditioned state is dying right now in front of our eyes."* — **Ajahn Chah & Jack Kornfield [00:00 / 27:40 / 50:50]**

## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Venerable Ajahn Chah (Luang Por Chah Subhaddo of Wat Nong Pah Pong).

* **Translator & Dialogue Facilitator**: Jack Kornfield (Senior American disciple, co-founder of IMS and Spirit Rock).

* **Theme**: 'Dying before you die'; daily reflection on mortality (*Maraṇānussati*); overcoming fear and existential dread; the nature of the unconditioned mind versus transient mental formations; working through doubt in meditation.

* **Circumstances**: Recorded during Ajahn Chah's historic first and only visit to North America in the summer of 1979. Leading a retreat at the newly established Insight Meditation Society in Barre, MA, Ajahn Chah engages in an open, humorous, and deeply penetrating dialogue with Western retreatants.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: In response to a retreatant's provocative question about whether it is better to 'die now and get it over with,' Ajahn Chah reframes death from physical termination into psychological liberation. Physical death is merely the eventual shedding of borrowed elements (earth, water, fire, air). The true death the Buddha praised is the death of ego-attachment—dying to the illusion of being a permanent controller. Ajahn Chah asks the assembly whether anyone in the room has honestly contemplated their own death today. When we ignore our inevitable mortality, we waste life squabbling over petty likes and dislikes. When we face death with open awareness, the compulsive grasp of desire relaxes, revealing the timeless, peaceful nature of the heart.
