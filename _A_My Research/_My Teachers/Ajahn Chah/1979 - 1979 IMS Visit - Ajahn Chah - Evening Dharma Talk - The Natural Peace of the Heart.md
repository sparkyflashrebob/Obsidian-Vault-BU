# 🎙️ 1979 - 1979 IMS Visit - Ajahn Chah - Evening Dharma Talk - The Natural Peace of the Heart
### *Ven. Ajahn Chah (Translated by Jack Kornfield) — Insight Meditation Society, Barre, MA (June 16, 1979)*

> *"We come on retreat thinking we are going to be peaceful, and in fact it goes just the opposite: we become totally chaotic! But don't be dismayed. The mind is naturally peaceful, just like clean, clear rainwater. When water is mixed with yellow dye, it becomes yellow; when mixed with red dye, it turns red. When the mind chases thoughts, it becomes agitated. But the water itself is not the dye. When you stop stirring the water, the mud settles, and the mind's pristine natural peace reveals itself."* — **Ajahn Chah & Jack Kornfield [21:27 / 35:10 / 42:47]**

## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Venerable Ajahn Chah.

* **Translator**: Jack Kornfield.

* **Theme**: The original, luminous peace of the heart (*citta pabhassara*); working with chaotic mental proliferation (*papañca*) during meditation retreat; the famous simile of clean rainwater and colored dyes; patient endurance (*khanti*); Ajahn Chah's formal blessing and forgiveness to the Western Sangha.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered on Saturday evening, June 16, 1979, during Ajahn Chah's landmark visit to IMS Barre. The hall was filled to capacity with early Western vipassanā practitioners, monastics, and seekers.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Chah begins with characteristic humility, thanking the assembly and playfully asking forgiveness for taking their time. He addresses the core frustration of meditators: expecting retreat to be an oasis of instant calm, only to find the mind swirling in turbulent chaos. Ajahn Chah reassures the practitioners that chaos is normal. The mind in its original nature is completely clean and still, like pure rainwater. When sensory sights, sounds, memories, and desires enter, the mind gets dyed by emotional flavors. The meditator's duty is not to wage war against the dye, but to cease stirring the pot. By observing with patient endurance (*khanti*), the sediments naturally settle to the bottom. He concludes by giving a formal Pāli blessing (*Yathā vārivahā pūrā...*), offering his loving presence to the American pioneers of practice.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

When emotional storms brew—irritation, sadness, or restlessness—notice the instinct to identify as the storm: *'I am angry, I am depressed.'* Remember Ajahn Chah's rainwater simile. Anger or sadness is just a temporary dye entering the senses. The knowing heart is like clear water, untouched by the color. Rest as the water, not the dye.
