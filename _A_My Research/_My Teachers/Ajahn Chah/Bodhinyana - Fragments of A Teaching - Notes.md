# Fragments of a Teaching
- hearing teachings
- sometimes broad and vague
- other times high language or special jargon
- not understand
- also in balanced way
	- neither vague nor too profound
	- neither broad or esoteric
	- just right for listener to understand and practice 
- In order to reach buddha-dhamma
	- has faith or confidence as a foundation

	- Buddha - one who knows - purity - radiance - peace in their heart
	- dhamma - characteristics of purity, radiance and peace
		- arise from morality, concentration & wisdom
- Reach Buddha-Dhamma
	- One who cultivates and developes morality, concentration and wisdom within themselves

Walking the Path
- those who want to go home are not those who merely sit and think of traveling
- must travel step by step in the right direction
- take wrong path they may run into difficulties, obstacles or get into difficult situations
- those who reach home can relax and sleep comfortably
- home is place of comfort for body and mind
- if a traveler only passed by the front or walked around it they would not receive the benefit of having travelled

In the same way one walking the path to reach buddha-dhamma
- one must do it alone
- no one can do it for us
- must trave along the proper path of morality, concentration and wisdom to find the blessings of purity, radiance and peacefulness of mind
- these are the fruits of travelling the path

Only knowing the books and teachings one only knows the maps or plans for the journey
- one will waste their efforts and never get the real benefits of practice
- Teachers only point out the direction of the path
- After listening to the teachers, whether or not we walk the path by practicing ourselves
	- where we reap the fruits of practice
	- is strictly us to us

Another way is to look at it is to compare practice to a bottle of medicine.
- on the bottle is written details for how to take the medicine
- if one only reads the directions one will not get well
- the medicine is worthless is one hasn't followed the advice of the doctor and taken the medicine
- but if one does, then they will recover
- if very ill, it will be necessary to take a lot of medicine
- if only mildly ill, only a little medicine will be required

Teachings of the Buddha are prescribed to cure disease of the mind
- bring back to healthy state
- He can be seen as a doctor who prescribes cure for the ills of the mind

Mental ills are found in each one of us, without exception
- it makes sense to look for the Dhamma for support
- as medicine to cure our ills
- Not done with the body
- To reach the benefits we must  travel with the mind

3 Levels:
1. Those who understand  they must practice themselves. Take Buddha, Dhamma & Sangha as a refuge. Resolve to practice diligently according to the teachings. They hae discarded merely following customs and traditions.  Use reason to examine for themselves the nature of the world. These are believers.
2. Those who have practiced until they have faith in the teachings of the Triple Gem. Have penetrated the understanding of the true nature of all compounded formations. They gradually reduce clinging and attachment. Do not hold onto things. Minds reach a deep understanding of the dhamma. Depending on their degree of non-attachment and wisdom they are progressively known as stream-enterers, once-returners, non-returners, or simply noble ones.
3. Highest level is the group whose practice has led them to the body, speech & mind of  a Buddha.  They are free of the world, free from all attachment and clinging. Known as arahants, or free ones, the highest of the noble ones.

How to Purify One's Morality
- Morality is restraint and discipline of body and speech.
- divided into classes of precepts for laypeople and for monks & nuns.
- In general terms there is one basic characteristic - that is [[intention]].
- When [[mindful]] or [[self-recollected]] we have [[right intention]]
- Practicing [[Readwise/Books/Mindfulness]] (sati) and [[self-recollection]] (sampajanna) will generate good morality
- when we put on dirty clothes and our bodies are dirty, our minds will feel [[uncomfortable]] and [[depressed]]
- if we keep our bodies [[clean]] and wear clean clothes then it makes our [[minds light and cheerful]]
- when our morality not kept - bodily actions and speech are dirty
- cause for making the mind unhappy, distressed and heavy
- when separated from right practice this prevents us from penetratinv the essense of dhamma in our minds
- wholesome body and speech themselves depend on mind, properly trained.
- since mind orders body and speech
- we must continually practice by training our minds.

# THE PRACTICE OF CONCENTRATION

[[Samadhi]]
- make mind firm and steady
- bring peacefulness of mind
- untrained moving and restless
- hard to control and manage
- mind follows sense distractions wildly
- water flowing this way and that
- seeking lowest level
- humans know how to channel water
- make more usable
- water stored a source of power and light
- likewise with mind
- mind that has been controlled bring true happiness
- training it well has highest benefits
- similary to trained animals being useful for work
- help us in all professions
- all situation
- diciplined mind keeps our lives balanced
- work easier
- develop and nurture reason
- govern our actions
- happiness will increase as we follow the proper mind training

MINDFULNESS AND BREATHING
- training the mind can be done in many ways
- method most useful is [[Readwise/Books/Mindfulness of Breathing]]
- developing mindfulness of [[in-breath and out-breath]]
- here [[tip of the nose]]
- [[Bud-dho]]
- another word or simply aware of air moving in and out is fine.
- adjust practice to suit yourself
- essential factor is noting or awareness of the breath kept up in the present moment so that one is mindful of each in-breath and out-breath just as it occurs
- with [[walking meditation]] constant mindfulness of the sensation of the feet touching the ground 
- practice must be pursued as continuously as possible to bear fruit
- consistency
- practice often
- practice diligently
- continuous practice of mental training
- quiet place free from distractions
- regardless, wherever we are we must practice
- if attention wanders bring it back
- try to put away thoughts and cares
- if mindful of thoughts as soon as they arise and keep returning to your object them the mind will become quieter and quieter
- when the mind is peaceful and concentrated - release it from the object of concentration
- examine the body and mind - [[the five khandas]]
- watch as they come and go
- you will clearly see they are impermanent
- this makes them unsatifactory and undesireable
- there is 'no-self' running things
- only nature moving according to cause and effect
- all things are instable
- seeing in this way, attachment and clinging to the khandas will be reduced
- this is the arising of wisdom

THE ARISING OF WISDOM
- [[PANNA]]
- Wisdom is to see the truth of the various manifestions of body and mind
- Five Khandas - body and mind are impermanent - unsatifactory and soul-less
- seeing all compounded things we do not cling or grasp them
- whatever we recieve, we receive mindfully
- no excessively happy
- when things break or disappear we are not unhapy and do not suffer painful feelings
- we see the impermanent nature of things
- illness or pain we are equanimous becasue our mind are well trained
- true refuge is in a trained mind
- the wisdom  thatknow the true characteristics of things as they arise
- wisdom arises from mindfulness and concentration
- concentration arises from a base of morality or virtue
- morality, concentration and wisdom are inter-related in a way that they cannot be separated
- practice can be seen as 1st dicipling the mind to be attentive of breathing
- this is the arising of morality
- when mindfulness of breathing practices continuously them the mind becomes quiet
- this is the arising of concentration
- the examination showing the breath as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and not-self
- then the non-attachment to it
- arising of wisdom
- all three come together
