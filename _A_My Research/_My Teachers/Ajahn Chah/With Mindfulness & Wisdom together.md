## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ven. Ajahn Chah.

* **Contemplative Domain**: Breath Awareness (*Ānāpānasati*) & Body Sweeping (*Kāyagatāsati*).

* **Structure**: Grounding scan from crown to toes and back, establishing easy natural breathing, and pairing mindfulness (*sati*) with clear comprehension (*sampajañña*).

* **Condensed Version of the Practice**: Ajahn Chah demystifies sitting meditation. Making the mind peaceful does not require complex conceptual gymnastics. First, pass awareness from the crown of the head down through the body to the tips of the toes, and back up again, establishing an honest baseline of how the physical body feels right now. Next, settle attention upon the natural flow of the breath—never forcing it to be longer, shorter, or deeper than normal. Ajahn Chah corrects the widespread misconception that meditation means eliminating thoughts: thoughts and feelings inevitably arise within awareness. When we stop trying to annihilate them and instead view them as visiting guests who arrive and depart by themselves, true equanimity is born.

## 🧘 Experiential Guided Meditation Script

### 1. Releasing Duties & Initial Body Scan [00:00–03:15]

Sitting in meditation and making the mind peaceful, you don't have to think about too much. Right now, just focus on the mind and nothing else. Don't let the mind shoot off to the left or to the right, to the front or behind, above or below. Our only duty right now is to practice mindfulness of breathing.


`[Pause 30s]`


First, fix your attention at the head and move it down through the body to the tips of the feet, and then back up to the crown of the head. Pass your awareness down through the body, observing with wisdom. We do this to gain an initial understanding of the way the body is right now. If our stomach is tight or knotted up, we invite it to soften.


`[Pause 45s]`


### 2. Natural, Unforced Breathing [03:15–07:20]

Then begin the meditation. Noting that at this time, your sole duty is to observe the inhalations and exhalations. Don't force the breath to be any longer or shorter than normal. Just allow it to continue easily. Don't put any pressure on the breath; rather let it flow evenly, letting go with each in-breath and out-breath.


`[Pause 60s]`


Maintain the resolve that at this time, you have no other duties, responsibilities, or thoughts about what will happen. What you will know or see during the meditation may arise from time to time, but once they arise, just let them cease by themselves. Don't be unduly concerned over them.


`[Silence 90s]`


### 3. Mindfulness and Wisdom Working Together [07:20–11:24]

Some people don't want to experience any thoughts or feelings at all, but this isn't right. Feelings arise within the state of awareness; observe them, know them, and let them go. When you notice the mind has wandered, gently bring it back. When mindfulness and wisdom work together, the heart remains untouched.


`[Long Bell]`
