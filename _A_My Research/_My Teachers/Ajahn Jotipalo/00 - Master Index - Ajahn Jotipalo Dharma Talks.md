## 1. Overview & Doctrinal Scope

Ven. Ajahn Jotipalo is a senior American disciple of the Thai Forest lineage of Ven. Ajahn Chah, ordained under Ven. Ajahn Pasanno at Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery. His teachings combine rigorous monastic discipline, profound canonical textual scholarship (especially the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, the Bojjhaṅgas, and early Buddhist phenomenology), and warm, practical guidance for daily life and silent retreat.

This master index catalogs all five archived audio talks and retreat teachings by Ajahn Jotipalo preserved in the monastic collection, systematically transcribed and analyzed according to the Gold Standard specifications.

## 3. Doctrinal Synthesis & Core Contemplative Themes

### The Architecture of the Awakening Factors (*Bojjhaṅga*)
Across the 2013 Winter Retreat study series, Ajahn Jotipalo unpacks the Seven Factors of Enlightenment not as isolated states, but as a dynamic, self-reinforcing ecosystem:
1. **Mindfulness (*Sati*)**: The anchor and guardian that maintains balanced awareness of what is present.
2. **Investigation (*Dhamma-vicaya*)**: Awakened through wise attention (*yoniso manasikāra*), exploring phenomena phenomenologically rather than ontologically.
3. **Energy (*Viriya*)**: Cultivated methodically across three progressive thresholds: initial effort (*ārambha*), persevering exertion (*nikkama*), and invincible momentum (*parakkama*).
4. **Joy (*Pīti*) & Tranquillity (*Passaddhi*)**: Wholesome somatic and affective settling that arises when mental friction subsides.
5. **Concentration (*Samādhi*) & Equanimity (*Upekkhā*)**: Collected, sovereign stillness capable of meeting the dissolution of the body in sickness and death without agitation.

### Somatic Grounding & Catching the Feeling Tone
In *Catching It at the Feeling*, Ajahn Jotipalo emphasizes that the pivotal battlefield of meditation is the juncture of sensory contact (*phassa*) and feeling tone (*vedanā*). When unpleasant feelings arise, the biological survival system often tightens the stomach, knots the gut, or quickens the breath. If mindfulness recognizes this somatic contraction immediately with gentleness, the mind is prevented from spinning off into the Second Arrow of narrative proliferation (*papañca*) and chronic stress.
