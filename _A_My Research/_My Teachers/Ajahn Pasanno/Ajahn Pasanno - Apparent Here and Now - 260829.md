In this Dhamma talk, _Ajahn Pasanno_ explores the practice of mindfulness and the nature of the mind through the lens of **repetition** and the **Middle Way**. Recorded at _Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery_, the talk emphasizes that while our daily lives are filled with cycles and repetition, these moments offer constant opportunities for **awakening** and **clarity** (0:00 - 1:43).

**Key themes include:**

- **Working with Dukkha:** _Ajahn Pasanno_ discusses how to attune our experience to recognize _Dukkha_ (suffering or stress) and find the path to its cessation or "non-arising" through non-clinging (2:57 - 5:00).
- **Reading the Mind:** A central practice is learning to "read one's own mind." This involves observing the mood or content of the mind—such as desire, aversion, or distraction—without getting lost in the "I, me, and mine" agenda (11:21 - 14:31).
- **The Quality of Knowing:** The talk highlights the importance of connecting with the "one who knows" (_phu-ru_), an inherent capacity for awareness that is **apparent here and now** and **timeless** (19:11 - 21:59).
- **Mind vs. Objects of Mind:** Drawing on teachings from _Ajahn Chah_ and _Ajahn Mun_, the talk explains the vital distinction between the mind (_citta_) and the objects of the mind (_arammaṇa_). By realizing this difference, practitioners can settle into a place of stillness and spaciousness (29:34 - 33:14).

_Ajahn Pasanno_ concludes by encouraging a sense of **ordinary awareness** and sharing a teaching poem by _Patrul Rinpoche_, reminding listeners to avoid ruminating on the past or anticipating the future, and instead, leave the mind just as it is in the present instant (34:18 - 36:20).

-----

The poem offered by _Ajahn Pasanno_ is attributed to the 18th-century Tibetan master _Patrul Rinpoche_ (34:44 - 35:58). He shares this verse to illustrate the importance of resting in the present moment without unnecessary mental fabrication:

> "Don't ruminate about the past. Don't anticipate the future. Don't cogitate about the present. Not tampering with it. Leave mind just as it is this very instant. Aware, relaxed. Beyond this, there's not a damn thing."

_Ajahn Pasanno_ notes that _Patrul Rinpoche_ was known for his "earthiness" and uses this poem to emphasize that the path to liberation is found by returning to this here-and-now, ordinary awareness (36:02 - 36:20).

### Summary of the Talk

In this talk titled **"Apparent here and Now"** ([00:00:02](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iu9DzWW9n6Y&utm_source=gemini)), Ajahn Pasanno explores how the repetitive routines of daily life and practice can serve as the direct doorway to awakening. ### Key Teachings & Outline

- **Repetition as an Opportunity for Awakening** [[00:16](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D16)]:
    
    - Daily and monastic life is built on repeating cycles—observance days, breathing, eating, and sitting.
        
    - While repetition can easily turn into mindless routine, each moment actually holds fresh potential for clarity, understanding, and release.
        
- **The Middle Way and the Cessation of Dukkha** [[01:50](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D110)]:
    
    - The middle way is not a rigid formula; it requires learning how much effort to apply versus how much to relax and relinquish [[02:18](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D138)].
        
    - _Dukkha-nirodha_ is often translated as the "cessation" of suffering, but it equally means the **non-arising** of dukkha [[04:18](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D258)]—not buying into the mental habits that construct stress in the first place.
        
- **Learning to "Read Your Own Mind"** [[11:47](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D707)]:
    
    - Echoing Ajahn Chah, Ajahn Pasanno notes that instead of constantly seeking knowledge and affirmation externally, practice is about reading the internal state and mood of one's own heart and mind.
        
    - This involves noticing craving, aversion, dullness, or the subtle grasping of "I-making" and "mine-making" (_ahaṅkāra_ and _mamiṅkāra_) [[13:43](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D823)].
        
- **The Mind (_Citta_) vs. the Objects of Mind (_Ārammaṇa_)** [[29:44](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D1784)]:
    
    - Drawing on the teachings of Ajahn Mun and Ajahn Chah, he highlights the critical distinction between the _citta_ (the knowing heart/mind) and the _ārammaṇa_ (the moods, preferences, thoughts, and objects passing through it).
        
    - Most suffering comes from identifying with passing mental objects rather than resting in the innate spaciousness of awareness.
        
- **The "One Who Knows" (_Buddho_) and Consciousness as an Element** [[19:27](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D1167)]:
    
    - Awakening touches the innate quality of _Buddho_—the one who knows, is awake, and is bright (_pabhassara_ / _boek-baan_) [[21:17](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D1277)].
        
    - Ajahn Chah's simple answer to why he repeatedly taught _Buddho_ was homage to the fundamental element of consciousness (_viññāṇa-dhātu_) [[25:35](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D1535)]—a natural, impersonal element free from identity.
        
- **Resting in the Ordinary Present Moment** [[33:44](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D2024)]:
    
    - The Dhamma is _sandiṭṭhiko_ (apparent here and now) and _akāliko_ (timeless), yet we often miss it because we look for something grand or exciting.
        
    - He closes by referencing a teaching poem from Tibetan master Patrul Rinpoche [[34:59](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Diu9DzWW9n6Y%26t%3D2099)]: not ruminating on the past, anticipating the future, or tampering with the present, but simply resting aware and relaxed in the immediate present.
