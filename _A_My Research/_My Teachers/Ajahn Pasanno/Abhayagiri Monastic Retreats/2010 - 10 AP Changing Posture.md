## 📖 Executive Summary & Core Teachings

Changing postures is not merely an interruption to relieve physical discomfort; it is a primary contemplative practice in its own right. The Buddha pointed out in the *Iriyāpathapabba* of the *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta* that dukkha is continually arising in the physical body, hidden by the frequent change of posture. By changing posture with deliberate mindfulness, we observe the body’s search for equilibrium, maintaining continuity of awareness without breaking the meditative thread.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:**

We can have a period of walking meditation until 3:30 PM. 

Use the change of posture not just as an intermission, but as a deliberate continuation of your practice. Observe the physical body as it stands up, transitions off the cushion, and begins to pace along the path. 

Maintain that steady, grounded presence as you move.
