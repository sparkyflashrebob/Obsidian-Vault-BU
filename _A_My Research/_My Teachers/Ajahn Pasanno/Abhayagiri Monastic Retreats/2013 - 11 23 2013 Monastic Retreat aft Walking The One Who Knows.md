## 🏛️ Structured Contextual Metadata

### Canonical & Contemporary Setting
* **Canonical Setting**: Reflects the Caṅkama Sutta (AN 5.29), where walking meditation builds endurance for traveling, physical health, easy digestion, and produces concentration that lasts for a long time.
* **Contemporary Recording Venue**: Angela Center sanctuary and grounds, Santa Rosa, California. Delivered right before the afternoon walking meditation period.
* **Circumstances & Occasion**: Transitioning from prolonged seated meditation to movement. Practitioners often view walking meditation as an informal break or stroll. Ajahn Pasanno grounds the physical mechanics of walking as an equal, highly rigorous meditative posture.
* **People Involved**: Ajahn Pasanno (speaker), monastic sangha, and retreat participants.

## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**Grounding Attention in the Soles of the Feet and Resting as 'The One Who Knows'**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Transitioning from sitting to walking meditation, Ajahn Pasanno encourages walking slightly slower than normal to allow the nervous system to settle. Focusing on the contact sensation at the feet—lifting, swinging, placing—draws vital energy down out of the head. When thoughts intrude, rather than reacting with frustration or aversion, we simply notice, let go, and re-establish awareness as 'the one who knows.'

## 📊 Wandering Stroll vs. Formal Caṅkama Meditation

| Dimension | Wandering Stroll (Aimless Movement) | Formal Caṅkama Meditation (Thai Forest Method) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spatial Field** | Meandering without boundaries, looking at scenery. | Prescribed path (15–25 paces) walked back and forth. |
| **Physical Demeanor** | Arms swinging, looking around, casual posture. | Composed: hands clasped in front, gaze cast 6 feet ahead. |
| **Turning Point** | Fluid, unconscious turning while lost in thought. | Deliberate physical and mental stop to re-establish presence. |
| **Mental Anchor** | Dispersed in daydreaming and sensory stimuli. | Anchored in touch sensations at the soles of the feet. |
| **Spiritual Fruit** | Restlessness (*uddhacca*) increases. | Sturdy samādhi that carries seamlessly into sitting. |

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:** So we can transition to walking meditation. We will come back for another sitting at 3:30.

Taking walking meditation: there are different ways of using walking meditation, in the sense that one can walk fairly quickly or walk very slowly. Generally, I encourage walking a little bit slower than one's normal pace. There is a benefit that tends to come with physical slowing down—letting things settle.

Bring attention to the sensation of touch at the feet: paying attention to the lifting of the foot, moving it, and setting it down. Bringing attention to the lower part of the body is a wonderful way of getting out of our thinking minds. Coming to that very simple sensation of touch tends to be very settling and soothing.

Again, use this as an opportunity for continuity of awareness. Whenever distraction arises, whenever thoughts impinge on the mind, do not get swept up in reactions of aversion. Sometimes we over-idealize what we think we should be doing, but the reality is simply what is happening right now.

Recognize it, pay attention to it, reflect on the experience, and re-establish attention and awareness. The continuity is in that re-establishing, being willing to return to that fundamental quality of awareness—being the one who knows.
