* **Canonical Setting**: Reflects the ancient monastic contemplation of the Four Great Elements (catu-dhātu): earth (pathavī), water (āpo), fire (tejo), and wind (vāyo), where biological nourishment is recognized as simply external physical elements sustaining internal physical elements.

## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**Contemplating the Body and Food as the Four Natural Elements**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Transitioning from formal meditation to the midday meal, Ajahn Pasanno introduces the classical contemplation of the four elements. By recognizing that the physical body is composed of earth, water, fire, and air, and that the food on the plate is composed of the exact same four elements, the mind establishes a cool, spacious equanimity. Food is no longer an object of sensual addiction or anxiety, but natural elements nourishing natural elements.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:** So transitioning to the meal: one of the contemplations that we do is contemplating the physical body as elements—the material earth element, the liquid water element, the element of heat, and the element of air.

Then we also contemplate the food in the exact same way. So we contemplate the body that we are feeding as elements, and we contemplate the food that we are using to nourish the body as elements. Using that establishes a deep equanimity both toward the body as well as toward food. There is a coolness and clarity that comes when we are able to look after ourselves in that way.
