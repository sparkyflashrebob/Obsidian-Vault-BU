### Canonical & Contemporary Setting
* **Canonical Setting**: Reflects the Upakkilesa Sutta (AN 5.23) and Paṃsukovāra Sutta (AN 3.100), where the Buddha compares the five hindrances to five impurities in gold (iron, copper, tin, lead, and silver). When stripped of these, the gold becomes refined, pliant, malleable, and radiantly bright (kammanīyo pabhassaro ca).

## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**Purifying the Mind's Gold: Starving the Five Hindrances and Cultivating Awakening Factors**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Meditators frequently feel discouraged when the mind is dull, agitated, or burdened by hindrances. Ajahn Pasanno reminds us that the innate nature of awareness is luminous and workable (*pabhassara citta*), obscured only by temporary visiting impurities (*upakkilesa*). Utilizing the Buddha's similes of the master goldsmith and the teaching on 'feeding and starving' mental states, he guides practitioners in starving defilements through wise reflection and cultivating a pliant, radiant heart.

## 📖 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Master Goldsmith**: Ajahn Pasanno describes the patient craft of the goldsmith: washing away the coarse grit, blowing gently on the embers, and removing slag. The smith doesn't curse the gold for having dirt; he methodically purifies it until it becomes pliant (*kammaniya*) and can be fashioned into any exquisite ornament. That same pliancy is our mind's natural birthright.
