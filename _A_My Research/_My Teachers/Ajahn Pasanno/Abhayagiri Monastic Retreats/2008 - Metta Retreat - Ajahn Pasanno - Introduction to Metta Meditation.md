### Theme of the Dharma Talk
The systematic methodology of mettā meditation: establishing phrases, working with categories of beings, and navigating emotional numbness.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Given on September 9, 2008, Ajahn Pasanno provides systematic meditation instructions. He explains how to use traditional phrases ('May you be well, may you be peaceful') as gentle drops of water rather than sledgehammers, progressing smoothly from oneself to benefactors, neutral persons, and eventually difficult individuals.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Using Phrases as Whispers**: Phrases are anchors for feeling; if the mind fixates on mechanical repetition, practice becomes dry. The phrase should evoke an authentic feeling of goodwill.
- **Self-Compassion First**: We cannot radiate warmth to the world if we are assaulting ourselves with harsh expectations. Softening self-critique is the prerequisite.
- **Navigating Emotional Numbness**: When the heart feels flat or dry, not panicking; recognizing that patience and non-ill-will are themselves authentic forms of mettā.

| **Benefactor (*Garubhāvanīya*)** | Recalling kindness of teachers and parents | Easy to cultivate; joy naturally arises. |
| **Neutral Person (*Majjhatta*)** | A stranger or casual acquaintance | Boredom / indifference; see their basic humanity. |
| **Difficult Person (*Verī*)** | Someone who provoked anger or hurt | Aversion; focus on their suffering, not their bad behavior. |
