## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Abbot's Burden and Luang Por Liem's Counsel
Ajahn Pasanno recounts the pivotal moment when Ajahn Sumedho asked Ajahn Amaro to leave Abhayagiri and step into the demanding role of abbot at Amaravati. Sensing the sheer weight of administrative responsibility, Luang Por Liem (abbot of Wat Nong Pah Pong) smiled and gave a single sentence of advice that completely transformed their approach: *'If you look upon it as work, it's going to be heavy and suffering; but if you look upon it as making an offering, it is light, joyful, and an opportunity for merit.'*

### 2. The Trap of the 'Getting' Mind
Modern society conditions human beings to relate to everything—careers, relationships, and even spiritual retreats—with a consumer mindset: *'What am I getting out of this? Am I getting my money's worth? Is this benefiting me?'* This grasping posture makes the mind petty, anxious, and fragile.

### 3. The Lightness of Making an Offering
When meditation, sitting, walking, chores, and dealing with difficult personalities are framed as offerings (*dāna*), the burden evaporates. We are no longer defending personal territory or demanding pleasant feelings. We give our best energy freely to the Dhamma, to our companions, and to the world.
