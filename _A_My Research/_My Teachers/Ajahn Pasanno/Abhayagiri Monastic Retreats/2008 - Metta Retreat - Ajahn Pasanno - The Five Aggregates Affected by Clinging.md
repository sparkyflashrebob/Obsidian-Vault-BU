### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Investigating how suffering arises when the mind clings to the five aggregates (*pañcupādānakkhandhā*), and using mettā to dissolve rigid ego-boundaries.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered on September 13, 2008, Ajahn Pasanno delves into the core Buddhist psychology of the five aggregates: form (*rūpa*), feeling (*vedanā*), perception (*saññā*), mental formations (*saṅkhāra*), and consciousness (*viññāṇa*). Suffering is not inherent in the aggregates; it arises when we claim them as 'me' or 'mine'. Mettā provides the emotional safety needed to let go of ownership.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Aggregates vs. Clinging-Aggregates**: An enlightened being still has the five aggregates, but no clinging (*upādāna*). The aggregates function cleanly like clear glass.
- **The Weight of Identification**: How claiming physical aging, emotional pain, or racing thoughts as personal failures creates intense suffering.
- **Mettā as the Softener**: Approaching the aggregates with gentleness allows the mind to release its desperate defensive grasp.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aggregate (*Khandha*)** | Clinging Mechanism (*Upādāna*) | Liberative Insight (*Anattā*) |
| **Form (*Rūpa*)** | Identifying with body appearance, health, vanity | Physical form is nature; mineral elements return to earth. |
| **Feeling (*Vedanā*)** | Demanding pleasure, rejecting pain | Feeling tones are transient ripples; not a self. |
| **Perception (*Saññā*)** | Clinging to memory, labels, judgments | Perceptions are colored lenses; constantly shifting. |
| **Formations (*Saṅkhāra*)** | Identifying with thoughts, opinions, moods | Mental states are conditioned visitors; awareness is unmoving. |
| **Consciousness (*Viññāṇa*)** | Believing consciousness is an eternal soul | Cognition arises dependent on sense doors; ungraspable. |
