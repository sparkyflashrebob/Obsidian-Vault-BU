## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Breath as a Comprehensive Vehicle
Many modern practitioners view breath awareness as merely step one: a preliminary concentration practice to be discarded when 'real insight' begins. Ajahn Pasanno demonstrates that in MN 118, the Buddha presents ānāpānasati as a complete path spanning from the first mindful breath all the way to final liberation (*vijjā-vimutti*).

### 2. The Four Tetrads Mapped to the Four Foundations
- **First Tetrad (Body / Kāya)**: Knowing long breath, knowing short breath, experiencing the whole body of breath (*sabbakāya*), and calming the bodily formation (*passambhayam kāyasaṅkhāram*).
- **Second Tetrad (Feelings / Vedanā)**: Experiencing rapture (*pīti*), experiencing joy/ease (*sukha*), experiencing mental formations, and calming mental formations.
- **Third Tetrad (Mind / Citta)**: Experiencing the mind, gladdening the mind (*abhippamodayaṁ cittaṁ*), concentrating the mind, and liberating the mind.
- **Fourth Tetrad (Dhammas)**: Contemplating impermanence (*aniccānupassī*), contemplating fading away (*virāgānupassī*), contemplating cessation (*nirodhānupassī*), and contemplating relinquishment (*paṭinissaggānupassī*).

### 3. Calming the Bodily and Mental Formations
Ajahn Pasanno focuses deeply on step 4 (*calming the breath*) and step 8 (*calming mental activity*). As bodily agitation subsides, the mind naturally settles into a calm, pliant luminosity (*pabhassara*), making insight effortless rather than forced.
