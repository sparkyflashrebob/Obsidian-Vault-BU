## 📖 Executive Summary & Core Teachings

Ajahn Pasanno emphasizes that meditation is not confined to seated immobility. The Buddha taught the cultivation of mindfulness across all four postures: sitting, walking, standing, and lying down. 

When taking up walking meditation, awareness rests in the physical sensations of each step, the rhythm of the breath, and the balance of the body. Rather than rushing to reach the end of the path, we observe the mental tendencies that arise in movement: the impulse to lean forward into the future, or the sluggish resistance to physical exertion. Walking meditation develops a resilient, dynamic clarity that carries effortlessly into everyday life.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:**

So changing postures, moving to a walking meditation. 

When the Buddha talked about meditation, it wasn't just sitting meditation. It encompasses all four postures that we utilize in our life as human beings: sitting, walking, standing, and lying down. We really want to give ourselves the opportunity to develop familiarity with applying mindfulness and attention in these different postures.

With the walking, it is similar to the instruction I just gave: experiencing the walking, experiencing each step, experiencing the breath as we walk, experiencing how we hold our body. Experiencing the balance or lack of balance, the compulsion to move forward, or the resistance that we have—whatever it is, just experiencing it directly.

As we take up walking meditation, we can be just as peaceful in walking meditation as we can be in sitting meditation. It is all in how we hold the mind and facilitate our clarity. 

So take this opportunity for a period of walking meditation, and we will gather back at 3:30 for another sit.
