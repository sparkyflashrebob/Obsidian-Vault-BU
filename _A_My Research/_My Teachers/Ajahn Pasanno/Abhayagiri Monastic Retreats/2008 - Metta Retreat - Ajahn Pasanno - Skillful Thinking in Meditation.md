### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Transforming our relationship with thoughts—learning to sort thoughts into wholesome and unwholesome streams rather than trying to suppress thinking.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered on September 11, 2008, Ajahn Pasanno addresses the meditator's dilemma of thinking. Practice does not require instantly eliminating all thought. Following the Bodhisatta's method in the Dvedhāvitakka Sutta, we divide thoughts into two categories: those imbued with craving, ill will, and cruelty versus those imbued with renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness. We encourage the wholesome stream and gently abandon the unwholesome.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Bodhisatta's Discovery (MN 19)**: Recognizing that whatever we think about repeatedly becomes the inclination of the mind (*yathā yathā hi vitakketi*).
- **Thinking Imbued with Mettā**: Using thought skillfully to reframe grievances, see others' suffering, and gladden the heart.
- **Subtle Thinking vs. Conceptual Proliferation**: Discerning *vitakka* (initial thought application) from *papañca* (obsessive psychological spinning).
