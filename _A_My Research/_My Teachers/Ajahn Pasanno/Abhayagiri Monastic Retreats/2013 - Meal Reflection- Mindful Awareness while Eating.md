## 🏛️ Structured Contextual Metadata

### Canonical & Contemporary Setting
* **Canonical Setting**: Reflects the clear comprehension section of the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10): in eating, drinking, chewing, and savoring, the practitioner acts with full awareness (asite pīte khāyite sāyite sampajānakārī hoti).
* **Contemporary Recording Venue**: Angela Center dining hall, Santa Rosa, California. Delivered right before the retreat midday meal.
* **Circumstances & Occasion**: Transitioning from silent seated meditation to the daily communal meal. Ajahn Pasanno uses this moment of physical transition to guide practitioners in maintaining an unbroken thread of mindfulness.
* **People Involved**: Ajahn Pasanno (speaker), monastic Sangha, retreat stewards, and lay retreatants.

---

## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**Sustaining Continuity of Mindfulness Across Posture Changes and Sensory Contact**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
The transition from the meditation hall to the dining room is the most common place where mindfulness fractures. Food contact is primal, and the mind easily falls into absent-minded consumption. Ajahn Pasanno instructs meditators to use every posture change, plate handling, and bite of food as prime opportunities for cultivating an even, unbroken continuity of awareness.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:** So we can transition to the meal, using mealtime also as a time for mindful awareness and contemplation.

All of these changes of posture and different objects of sense contact are opportunities for contemplating the Dhamma. Particularly, mealtime is usually a time on retreat when the mind can be settling or just looking for an object, and food is very primal.

To be able to establish that continuity of awareness and recognize that it is possible to develop an evenness in the practice—an evenness in all postures of meditation—is a wonderful training for the mind.
