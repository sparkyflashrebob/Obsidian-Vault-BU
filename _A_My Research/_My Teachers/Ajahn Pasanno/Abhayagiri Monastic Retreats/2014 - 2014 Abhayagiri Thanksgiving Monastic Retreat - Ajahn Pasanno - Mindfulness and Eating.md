## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. Extending the Field of Sati Beyond the Cushion
Meditation is frequently compartmentalized into quiet sitting in the shrine room. Ajahn Pasanno dismantles this divide by framing the dining hall as an equally potent arena for contemplative practice. Food is an intense point of sensory contact (*phassa*), evoking instant preferences, visceral anticipations, and habitual reactions.

### 2. Guarding the Sense Doors at the Table
When we approach food, the mind instinctively categorizes: *'I want this, I don't want that; there isn't enough; they took the best piece.'* Bringing alertness to these subtle surges of excitement and dejection enables practitioners to witness the arising of craving (*taṇhā*) without being hijacked by it. Mindfulness acts as a skillful gatekeeper, preventing the formation of unnecessary mental friction and suffering.

## 📜 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Meal as Contemplative Territory**: Ajahn Pasanno reminds the retreatants that mindfulness is not something to be packed away when the bell rings to end sitting. Approaching the food table is one of the most revealing moments of retreat life, showing us exactly where our desires, anxieties, and social judgments still hold sway.
