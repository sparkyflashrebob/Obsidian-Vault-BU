### Theme of the Dharma Talk
A line-by-line exposition of the Buddha's primary discourse on loving-kindness, unpacking the 15 moral and psychological qualities required to embody boundless goodwill.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered on the evening of September 10, 2008, Ajahn Pasanno unpacks the Karaṇīya Mettā Sutta. Before the sutta describes radiating love like a mother protecting her child, it outlines fifteen prerequisite virtues: being capable, upright, straightforward, easy to instruct, gentle, humble, content, and unburdened with duties. Mettā is a whole-life training, not just a sitting technique.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The 15 Moral Qualities**: *Sakko* (capable), *ujū* (upright), *sūjū* (straightforward), *suvaco* (amenable to advice), *mudu* (gentle), *anatimānī* (unpretentious), *santussako* (content), *subharo* (easily supported), *appakicco* (few duties), *sallahukavutti* (light livelihood).
- **The Mother's Love Simile**: Loving all beings as a mother protects her only child with her life—protecting the quality of love itself against defilement.
- **Standing, Walking, Sitting, Lying Down**: Maintaining unshakeable goodwill in all postures until falling asleep.

## 4. Notable Sutta Passages & Practical Anecdotes

Ajahn Pasanno shares the canonical origin of the sutta: monks meditating in a forest were terrified by tree spirits; the Buddha taught them the Karaṇīya Mettā Sutta as their armor, transforming the spirits into protective friends.
