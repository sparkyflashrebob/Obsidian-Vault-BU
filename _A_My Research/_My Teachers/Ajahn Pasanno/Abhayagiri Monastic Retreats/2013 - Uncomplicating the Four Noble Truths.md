## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**Squeaky Wheels and Sāriputta's Counsel: Simplifying Practice to the Direct Relief of Stress**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Human beings have an extraordinary genius for over-complicating simple truths. Meditators construct elaborate metaphysical architectures, agonizing over esoteric maps while overlooking immediate distress. Ajahn Pasanno recounts Venerable Sāriputta's brilliant, direct counsel to Venerable Anuruddha, who had developed the celestial eye but couldn't attain liberation. The Four Noble Truths are not a theoretical philosophy; they are a practical repair manual. Like greasing a squeaky wheel exactly where it squeaks, practice simply means noticing where the mind is creating friction and letting go.

## 📖 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **Sāriputta Unmasks Anuruddha's Conceit (AN 3.128)**: Ajahn Pasanno tells the wonderful canonical encounter between Anuruddha and Sāriputta. Anuruddha comes complaining: *'Brother Sāriputta, with my divine eye I can survey a thousand world systems. My energy is tireless; my mindfulness is unwavering. Yet my mind is not free from the āsavas. Why is that?!'* Sāriputta looks at him with piercing clarity and says: *'Friend Anuruddha, your thought "I see a thousand world systems" is pure conceit. Your thought "My energy is tireless" is pure restlessness. And your thought "Yet my mind is not free" is pure remorse. Abandon these three things, stop making a show of yourself, and direct your mind to the deathless element.'* Anuruddha took the advice and became an arahant shortly after.
* **The Squeaky Wheel Simile**: Ajahn Pasanno uses the down-to-earth image of an old cart with a squeaky wheel. When a wheel squeaks, you don't write a PhD dissertation on the physics of friction, nor do you rebuild the cart; you simply take an oil can and grease the squeak. Dukkha in meditation is that squeak: notice the craving, let go of the grasping, and the noise stops.
