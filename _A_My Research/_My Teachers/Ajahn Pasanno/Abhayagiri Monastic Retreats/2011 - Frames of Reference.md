## 📖 Executive Summary & Core Teachings

Ajahn Pasanno emphasizes that mindfulness (*sati*) is not a cold, disembodied detachment or aloof dissociation from life. To possess mindfulness (*satimā*) means to be fully inhabited, lucidly present, and intimately engaged with what is occurring across the four frames of reference: body, feeling tones, mental states, and Dhammas. By balancing ardency (*ātāpī*) with clear comprehension (*sampajañña*), awareness becomes a warm, steady refuge.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:**

The formal request for teaching Dhamma is an ancient custom that reminds us of the value of what we are undertaking. 

When the Buddha speaks of the Four Establishments of Mindfulness—the four frames of reference—he is giving us specific domains to anchor our attention so that the mind does not wander into delusion. 

Crucially, one is not separated from experience or disembodied. True mindfulness is engaged. 

The third quality in the classic formula—*satimā*—means one who possesses mindfulness, one who embodies awareness. 

When you are mindful of the body, you are right here in the sensations of the breath, the contact of the feet, and the posture. When mindful of feelings, you clearly comprehend whether sensation is pleasant, painful, or neutral without grasping or averting. 

You establish this frame of reference, and you rest in that grounded knowing.
