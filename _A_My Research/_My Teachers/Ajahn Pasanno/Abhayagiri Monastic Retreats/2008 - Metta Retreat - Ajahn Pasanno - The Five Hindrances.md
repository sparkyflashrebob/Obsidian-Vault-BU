### People Involved

### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Deconstructing the Five Hindrances with specific emphasis on ill will (*byāpāda*) and restlessness (*uddhacca*), utilizing mettā as the master psychological solvent.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered on September 10, 2008, Ajahn Pasanno investigates the obstacles that cloud the mind. Mettā is the direct antidote to the second hindrance (ill will), but it also soothes restlessness and doubt. By welcoming hindrances without aversion, we strip them of their power to disturb.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **The Water Similes**: Ill will is like boiling water; sensual desire is like dyed water; sloth is like water choked with algae; restlessness is like water whipped by wind; doubt is like muddy water in darkness.
- **The Second Arrow of Aversion**: Getting angry at anger compounds suffering. Mettā meets the hindrance itself with kindness.
- **Starving the Hindrance**: Denying ill will the fuel of negative storytelling (*ayoniso manasikāra*).
