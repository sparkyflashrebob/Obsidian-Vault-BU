## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**The Compass of Authentic Teaching: Gotamī's Eight Criteria and Sweeping Leaves at Wat Pah Pong**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Ajahn Pasanno addresses the essential monastic retreat theme of continuity of awareness. To discern whether our internal attitudes and practices are genuine Dhamma, the Buddha gave eight clear criteria to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī. Sharing the famous story of Ajahn Sumedho struggling with resentment while sweeping leaves at Wat Pah Pong, Ajahn Pasanno illustrates how suffering is not in the work or the place, but in the complaining mind that demands things be different.

## 📊 Doctrinal Comparison & Analytical Framework

| Criterion | The Course of Defilement (*Kilesa*) | The Standard of True Dhamma (*Gotamī Sutta*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Passion** | Fuels craving and sensual infatuation (*rāga*). | Leads to dispassion and cooling (*virāga*). |
| **Bondage** | Entangles in opinions, grudges, identities. | Leads to unfettering and liberation (*visaṃyoga*). |
| **Accumulation** | Hoards possessions, experiences, mental stories. | Leads to shedding and unburdening (*apacaya*). |
| **Desires** | Constantly expands demands and preferences. | Cultivates fewness of wishes (*appicchatā*). |
| **Contentment** | Chronic dissatisfaction; looking for faults. | Deep contentment with simple requisites (*santuṭṭhi*). |
| **Company** | Craves entertainment, socialization, noise. | Delights in internal and external seclusion (*paviveka*). |
| **Effort** | Sinks into sloth, complacency, and laziness. | Arouses steadfast spiritual energy (*vīriyarambha*). |
| **Burden** | High-maintenance, demanding, difficult to support. | Modest, light, and easy to maintain (*subharatā*). |

## 📖 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **'Sumedho, Is Wat Pah Pong Suffering?!'**: Ajahn Pasanno recounts the classic story of a young Ajahn Sumedho at Wat Nong Pah Pong in Thailand. Assigned to sweep the vast sand paths in the blistering tropical heat, Sumedho was internally fuming: *'Why do we have to sweep all these leaves every afternoon? They're just going to fall again tomorrow! This is ridiculous, irrational, and completely pointless!'* As he stood leaning on his broom, lost in righteous indignation, Ajahn Chah quietly walked up behind him, paused, and whispered gently: *'Sumedho... is Wat Pah Pong suffering?'* Then he smiled and walked away. Sumedho was stunned: Wat Pah Pong wasn't suffering; the leaves weren't suffering; the broom wasn't suffering. The only suffering was the complaining mind creating friction with reality.
