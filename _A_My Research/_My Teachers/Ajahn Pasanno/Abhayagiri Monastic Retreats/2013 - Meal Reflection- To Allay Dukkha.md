## 🏛️ Structured Contextual Metadata

### Canonical & Contemporary Setting
* **Canonical Setting**: Reflects the classical requisite reflection from MN 2: 'I eat this food wisely reflecting: not for intoxication, not for physical adornment, but simply for the continuation of this body, to allay hunger, so that old feelings of pain are terminated and new feelings of pain do not arise' (iti purāṇañca vedanaṃ paṭihaṅkhāmi navañca vedanaṃ na uppādessāmi).
* **Contemporary Recording Venue**: Angela Center dining hall, Santa Rosa, California. Delivered right before the retreat midday meal.
* **Circumstances & Occasion**: Transitioning from silent seated meditation to the daily communal meal. Ajahn Pasanno uses this moment of physical transition to guide practitioners in maintaining an unbroken thread of mindfulness.
* **People Involved**: Ajahn Pasanno (speaker), monastic Sangha, retreat stewards, and lay retreatants.

## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**Eating to Relieve Old Pain Without Creating New Pain**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Ajahn Pasanno connects the midday meal to the daily morning chant on requisites: eating purely to relieve the natural discomfort of hunger without overeating. In Pāli, the phrase means 'allaying old dukkha without creating new dukkha.' In daily life, we constantly leap out of the frying pan into the fire by chasing sensory comfort that creates further distress; eating in moderation trains us to resolve suffering cleanly.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:** Transitioning to the meal and using that opportunity to establish mindfulness and contemplation. Again, in the chant we did this morning—the contemplation of the four requisites—there is that reflection on food:

There is one line there: 'I will allay hunger without overeating.' In the Pāli, it is literally: 'I allay old dukkha without creating new dukkha.' It is that sense of the resolution of dukkha, contemplating dukkha.

We see that not only in the meal, but in all the different ways where we habitually leap out of the frying pan and into the fire. Mindful eating teaches us how to allay discomfort with simplicity and wisdom.
