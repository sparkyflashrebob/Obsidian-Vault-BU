## 📖 Executive Summary & Core Teachings

A common mistake in ascetic practice is blaming external sensory objects—believing that sights, sounds, food, and the material world are evil traps that must be violently suppressed. 

Ajahn Pasanno clarifies this through the profound *Koṭṭhika Sutta* (*SN 35.232*):
* The eye is not a fetter to forms, nor are forms a fetter to the eye.
* The ear is not a fetter to sounds, nor are sounds a fetter to the ear.
* The fetter (*saṁyojana*) is the **desire and passion (*chanda-rāga*)** that arises in dependence on them!

Sāriputta illustrated this with the simile of two oxen—one black, one white—bound together by a single yoke: neither the white ox is the fetter to the black ox, nor the black to the white. The yoke that binds them is the fetter. Renunciation is not destroying the senses; it is breaking the yoke of craving.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Pasanno:**

Last night we chanted the *Ādittapariyāya Sutta*—the Fire Sermon. 

The Buddha delivered this discourse at Gayāsīsa to one thousand fire-worshipping ascetics, declaring: *"All is burning! The eye is burning, forms are burning, eye-consciousness is burning... burning with the fire of greed, hatred, and delusion."*

It is easy to misread this teaching as a rejection of the physical world. We think: "The world is bad; sensory objects are dangerous." 

In the *Koṭṭhika Sutta* (*SN 35.232*), Venerable Sāriputta resolves this completely. He asks: Is the eye a fetter to sights? Or are sights a fetter to the eye? 

And he answers: Neither! The fetter is the desire and passion (*chanda-rāga*) that arises in dependence on them. 

He gives the image of a black ox and a white ox harnessed together by a single yoke. Is the black ox the fetter to the white ox? No. Is the white ox the fetter to the black ox? No. The yoke is the fetter! 

Our senses are natural biological faculties. Sights, sounds, smells, tastes, and sensations are simply conditions. The entire work of Dhamma is unhooking the yoke of grasping. 

When you see a sight, let it be just a sight. When you hear a sound, let it be just a sound. In the seen, there is only the seen; in the heard, only the heard. That is the end of suffering.
