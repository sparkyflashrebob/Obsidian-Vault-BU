## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Chanted Invitation to Teach
Before every formal Dhamma discourse in the Theravāda tradition, the assembly recites the ancient verses requesting the Dhamma. Luang Por Pasanno unpacks the historical and cosmic weight of this chant: the Buddha, recognizing the sheer depth and subtlety of Nibbāna, initially inclined toward silent dwelling until Brahmā Sahampati interceded.

### 2. The Lotus Simile (*Uppala Simile*)
Looking out upon the world with the eye of an Awakened One, the Buddha saw beings like blue, red, or white lotuses growing in a pond:
- Some submerged deep under the water, still budding.
- Some rising through the water toward the surface.
- Some already standing clear of the water, untouched by the muddy pond.
Every human being has this latent potential for awakening; we differ only in how much conceptual dust obscures our vision.

### 3. Dust Is Not the Eye
Luang Por Pasanno emphasizes that dust on a mirror is not the mirror itself; dust in the eye is not the eye itself. Defilements (*kilesas*), anxieties, and moods are external visitors (*āgantuka*). When we stop identifying with the dust and rest back in the knowing awareness (*the one who knows*), liberation is immediate.

## 📜 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Muddy Pond of Uruvelā**: Luang Por Pasanno vividly describes the Buddha sitting on the bank of the Nerañjarā River after enlightenment, gazing into a lotus pond. The lotuses did not need to be dragged out of the water; they naturally opened when touched by the morning sun. The Dhamma is that morning sun.
