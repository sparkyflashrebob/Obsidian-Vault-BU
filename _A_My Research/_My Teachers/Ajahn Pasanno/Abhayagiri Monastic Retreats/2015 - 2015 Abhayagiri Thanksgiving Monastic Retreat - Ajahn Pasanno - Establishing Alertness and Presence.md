## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. Physical Posture as the Mirror of Citta
Meditation does not occur in an abstract vacuum; it takes place in a biological body. When the spine is slumped, awareness sinks into dullness (*thīna-middha*). When the spine is hyperextended, tension sparks restlessness (*uddhacca*). Balanced posture embodies dignity, ease, and wakefulness.

### 2. Establishing the Anchor in Front of You (*Parimukhaṁ*)
The suttas speak of establishing mindfulness *parimukhaṁ*—literally 'in front of the face' or 'at the forefront of attention.' Ajahn Pasanno explains this as placing awareness at the center of current experience, watching sensations of the breath arrive and depart like visitors at a doorway.

### 3. Receptive, Non-Interfering Knowing
We do not breathe to create a masterpiece of breathing; we simply know the natural breath as it is. Long or short, smooth or ragged, we meet the breath with patient, uncritical friendliness.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Breath Rhythm** | Shallow, unnoticed, leading to snoring. | Controlled, forced, unnatural, air-hungry. | Organic, rhythmic, soothing, completely natural. |
| **Mental Quality** | Sleepy, fogged, wandering into daydreams. | Anxious, irritable, frustrated with distractions. | Clear, bright, luminous, present, receptive. |

## 📜 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **The Goldilocks Posture**: Ajahn Pasanno chuckles as he describes walking through the meditation hall early in the morning: some meditators look like they are about to fall over asleep, while others look like statues carved out of granite with clenched teeth. Practice is finding that delicate middle where the body is as relaxed as sleep, but the mind is wide awake.
