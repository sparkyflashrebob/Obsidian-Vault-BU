### Theme of the Dharma Talk
Distinguishing genuine mettā from romantic sentimentality or transactional affection, cultivating loving-kindness as an unshakeable, mature spiritual faculty.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Delivered on the morning of September 8, 2008, Ajahn Pasanno examines the nature of mettā. Conventional affection is conditional—dependent on people behaving well and pleasing us. Genuine mettā is a mature spiritual capacity (*appamañña cetovimutti*) that wishes well-being to all, regardless of conditions, without demanding anything in return.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings
- **Mettā vs. Pema (Affection)**: Pema is sticky, selective, and easily flips into aversion when crossed; mettā is spacious, unpossessive, and boundless.
- **The Near and Far Enemies**: The far enemy of mettā is ill will (*byāpāda*); the near enemy is sensual attachment and sentimental codependency (*pema*).
- **Cultivating Sovereign Goodwill**: Resting in the heart's natural warmth without needing external validation.
