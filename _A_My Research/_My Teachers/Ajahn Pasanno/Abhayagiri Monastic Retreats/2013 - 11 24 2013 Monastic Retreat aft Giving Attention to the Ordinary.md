## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**The Treasure in Front of Us: Ordinary Experience and Surviving Dengue Fever**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
We constantly search for profound spiritual revelations in distant, extraordinary states, ignoring the living Dhamma displaying itself in ordinary life. Ajahn Pasanno reminds us that our breath, bodily sensations, and everyday moods are continuously preaching the truth of anicca, dukkha, and anattā. Sharing a gripping personal memory of contracting severe dengue fever in rural Thailand with temperatures exceeding 106°F, he shows how the mind creates catastrophic suffering by projecting fear and demand, and how peace arises the moment we surrender to the present reality.

| Dimension | Seeking the Extraordinary | Attending to the Ordinary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Field of Truth** | Awaits exotic visions, cosmic lights, mystical states. | Discovers truth in the ordinary breath, footsteps, aches. |
| **Response to Sickness** | Resents illness; panics about future prognosis. | Experiences sensations as raw elements; lets the body heal. |
| **Mental Posture** | Straining, forward-leaning, dissatisfied with now. | Resting in receptive presence; reading reality clearly. |
| **Spiritual Fruit** | Dependent on external stimuli and easily shattered. | Unshakeable peace grounded in the reality of the present. |

## 📖 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **Dengue Fever in the Thai Jungle**: Ajahn Pasanno shares a harrowing illness in Thailand when he was struck with dengue fever. For two weeks, his fever spiked to 105°–106°F with blinding headaches and bodily agony. In the second week, severe panic set in: *'What if I die? What if I suffer brain damage? When will this end?!'* He realized the physical fever was manageable, but his mental terror—the second arrow—was torturing him. The moment he surrendered his demands and agreed to be sick right now, deep stillness flooded his heart, and the suffering vanished even while the fever raged.
