## 💡 Teachings Overview

### Central Contemplative Theme
**The Empirical Laboratory of Practice: Inconstancy, Sandiṭṭhiko, and the Stillness of Insight**.

### Condensed Version of the Teachings
Entering Day 8 of the retreat, Ajahn Pasanno explores the difference between mere absorption calm and the unshakeable stillness of insight. When we encounter pleasant or unpleasant sensory contacts, the untrained mind loses its poise. True insight, as Ajahn Chah taught, produces an internal stability that cannot be shaken by worldly winds. Translating anicca not just as 'impermanence' but as 'inconstancy' (unreliability), Ajahn Pasanno reminds us that we are researchers in our own laboratory: it is up to each practitioner to experiment and verify the Dhamma directly.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **View of Anicca** | Abstract concept: 'things change eventually.' | Visceral recognition of inconstancy in real time. |
| **Relation to Technique** | Clings rigidly to one method; fears distraction. | Flexible, experimental: applies whatever skillful means are needed. |
| **Spiritual Fruit** | Dependent on quiet rooms and protected conditions. | Autonomous freedom (*sandiṭṭhiko*); independent of conditions. |

## 📖 Stories & Anecdotes Offered

* **Ajahn Chah on the Stillness of Insight**: Ajahn Pasanno shares Ajahn Chah's teaching: *'Tranquility without insight is like a stone placed on top of grass—the grass stops growing, but as soon as you lift the stone, it shoots up again. But the stillness of insight pulls the roots of craving completely out of the soil.'*
