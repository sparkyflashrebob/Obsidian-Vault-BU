## 📖 Expanded Version of the Teachings

### 1. The Human Reality of Loss in the Sangha
Even among advanced disciples, grief can strike with immense somatic weight. Upon hearing of Sāriputta's death, Ānanda exclaimed that his limbs felt paralyzed, his throat was constricted, and darkness clouded his mind. The Buddha did not chastise Ānanda, but met his grief with clear, compassionate discernment.

### 2. What Cannot Be Taken Away
The Buddha asked Ānanda: *'Did Sāriputta take away the aggregate of virtue? Did he take away the aggregate of concentration, wisdom, release, or the knowledge and vision of release?'* Ānanda acknowledged that none of these internal spiritual attainments had vanished. The Dhamma remains whole and accessible within the living heart.

### 3. 'Attadīpā Viharatha': An Island Unto Yourself
The Buddha then issued his famous exhortation: *'Live as islands unto yourselves, with yourselves as a refuge, with no external refuge; with the Dhamma as an island, with the Dhamma as a refuge, with no other refuge.'* The Buddha explicitly defined how to create this unshakeable island: by ardently, clearly comprehending, and mindfully contemplating the body in the body, feelings in feelings, mind in the mind, and dhammas in dhammas, putting aside craving and distress with regard to the world.
