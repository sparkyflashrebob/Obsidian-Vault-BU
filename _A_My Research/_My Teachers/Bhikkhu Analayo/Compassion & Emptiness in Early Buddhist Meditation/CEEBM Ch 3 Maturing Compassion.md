## Radiating Compassion and Absorption

	- Boundless radiation of the brahmaviharas points to a concentrated mind 
		- referred to as BECOMING GREAT & BOUNDLESS
		- Key component of all of the brahmaviharas is that they are selfless and radiate in all directions
		- but does this entail attaining absorption/deeper states of meditation ?
	- Teachings say that this is not necessarily so
	- Practice itself is another important aspect of this
		- Benefits all levels of practice 
	- #### Compassion as a Liberation of Mind 
	- Usually, when speaking about the divine abodes, they stand in place of the deeper meditations
	- In other places in the teachings though they stand side by side
	- When Meditating on other objects (other than brahmaviharas ) a Liberation of mind comes about when these deep jhana states
	- With the brahmaviharas, a temporary liberation of mind is possible with a lesser degree of concentration simple due to their boundless nature
	- Practicing them in this boundless way actually can offer a distinct advantage to meditation practice versus the pressures that can come up to enter deep meditation states thru the usual process of mediation alone.
		- this can be a distinct obstacle for some
	- The truth of the matter is though that one who actively practices compassion will not push too hard with their practice.
- In the same manner practice of the brahmaviharas will also be greatly benefitical for anyone facing difficult external situations 
	- for instance - when facing distracting sounds - one can continue to meditate smoothly
		-  actually make part of the practice 
			- compassion for ourselves, the disturbance itself  and for others 

## COMPASSION AND KARMA

	- Beside benefitting our ability to cultivate tranquility and concentration thru the boundless radiation of compassion and the other brahmaviharas, the teachings point to a relation between these divine abodes and fruition  of [[karma]] 
	- As we have discussed, karma in early Buddhist thought is closely related to intention, since intention is what is responsible for deeds by body, speech and mind. What was done in the past will in some way come to fruition, but the way this happens depends on what one does now. 
	- The suttas state: “Formerly my mind was narrow and not well developed; now my mind has become boundless and well developed.” 
	- When the mind of a learned practitioner has in this way become boundless and well developed, if because of associating with bad friends one formerly was neglectful and performed unwholesome deeds, those deeds cannot lead one along, cannot defile one, and will not come back to meet one.
	- Another way this is thought of is if the same piece of salt were placed in a glass of water or into a fresh water lake. The amount of salt in a glass will taste stronger than if tasted directly from the lake. 
	- Likewise, having been changed by the practice of compassion and the other divine abodes, the present repercussions from the past will have a different outcome on us.  They will be minimalized.
	- One of the Buddhas disciples, Angulimala, who was said to have murdered many, when we became a monk at first he was attacked.  As the story goes, he had compassion for a woman in labor one day and help here with the birth. From then on, it was one way he was of service to others, helping other women in the same situation. At first taking lives and then helping to bring them in.  His karma had changed.  And eventually he became enlightened.
	- In practical terms when unwholesome thoughts arise and distract the mind, these can be delt with by just returning to the boundless condition of the mind.
	- Unwholesome intension require some about of mental narrowness to survive.

## COMPASSION AND Insight

	- With the maturing of compassion practice comes with it the potential for the cultivation of liberating insight.
	- In a couple of the teachings it is implied that with the maturing of ones' practice with the brahmaviharas, one will have a good chance to reach at least the 3rd of the 4 stages of enlightenment, which is that of a non-returner, if not the highest liberation.   #stages_of_awakening [[4 Stages of Enlightenment]]
	- #### Relationship Between Tranquility and Insight 
	- Four different approaches to this are described with three of these involving tranquility and insight.  
		- There is Insight that is preceded by tranquility, 
		- tranquility preceded by insight, 
		- and tranquility and insight conjoined. 
	- So it appears that there is no hard as fast rule the either tranquility or insight must be practices first. 
	- Some of us may be better by placing emphasis on insight (vipassana) while others will prefer to begin instead on calming down the mind and then turning to insight.
		-  This would depend on our own personal preferences and disposition
		- Here, we are implying that tranquility is associated with cultivation of the brahmaviharas
	- There is no situation where either tranquility or insight would lead to liberation  
	- At some point they need to be combined. 
	- The important contribution made by tranquillity, aka compassion practice, is that it brings about a change in the attitudes of the mind, by providing it with a higher form of pleasure and inner satisfaction.
	- Tranquility though does not stand on its own.  In it clear in the teachings that the factor that eradicates ignorance and leads to liberation is actually having [[insight]]. 
	- #### Cultivating two dharmas
		- One way of looking at the practice of mediation is by means of cultivating two dhammas
			- A. tranquility 
			- B. Insight 
		- If one gains the calmness of tranquility they will be successful in having morals and not violating any of the precepts, generating all virtues.
		- If one has gained insight, they will contemplate what is dukkha, knowing it as it really is, and, with contemplating it arising and cessation, the way out, knowing it as it really is.
		- Having contemplated in this way one attains liberation from what is called the [[influx]] of sensuality, the influx of becoming, and the influx of ignorance.
			- This is what is meant by gaining our freedom or another way of saying being  Enlightened 
		- Another way of saying this is that 
			- thru tranquility the mind is developed & lust is abandoned; 
			- thru insight wisdom is developed and ignorance is abandoned.
		- Insight takes as it vehicle contemplation of the 4 Noble Truths - which leads to final liberation.
		- Tranquility adds to this by bringing about a change in attitude in the mind by providing it with a higher form of pleasure and inner satisfaction 
		- This mental attitude that the brahmaviharas engender is integral to the path to awakening
	- It is important to note that Tranquility itself does not stand on its own
		- the factor that eradicates ignorance and leads to final liberation is [[Insight]] 
	- However, the teaching reckon the four absorptions (deeper meditations) as instances of a practitioners' "happiness" and the the brahmaviharas as instances of a practitioners' "wealth"
	- So in this way, the practice of compassion also has a positive effect on others.
	- They sense the harmony and well-wishing that accompany the words and actions of one whose mind is steeped in compassion. 
	- It is seen as a direct expression of harmlessness
		- one of the [[three modes of right intention]], the 2nd factor of the [[Noble Eightfold Path]] 
	- Engaging in compassion is also a means of implementing [[Right Effort]]
	- And as we have seen, successfully practicing compassion can come to fulfil the path factor of [[Right Concentration]] 
	- And finally, in terms of Compassion and Insight, cultivation of compassion could be used as one's main vehicle of practice.  
		- This is where we turn to the awakening factors, those qualities that need to be brought into being for awakening to take place.

## COMPASSION AND THE AWAKENING FACTORS

	- Metta can be combined with the Awakening Factors [[7 Factors of Awakening]]
		1. [[Mindfulness]] 
		2. [[Investigation-of-dhammas]]
		3. [[Energy]]
		4. [[Joy]] 
		5. [[Tranquillity]] 
		6. [[Concentration]] 
		7. [[Equanimity]] 
	- Two dynamics taken into account
		- sequential build-up
			- gradual build-up
		- Helpful when sluggish or when agitated
			- balance 
		- When sluggish 
			- investigation, energy, joy need to be strengthened
		- When agitated 
			- tranquillity, concentration & equanimity need to be stronger
		- Both situations, mindfulness equally helpful 
	- When in harmonious balance, actual practice can lead to awakening when combined with four insight related themes:
		1. [[seclusion]]
		2. [[dispassion]]
		3. [[cessation]] 
		4. [[letting go]]
	 - So a practice undertaken in this way offers a possibility to progress on the path of awakening having make compassion as one's main vehicle 
	 - This would need to be counterbalanced with dedicating some time to the other brahmaviharas - and well as insight contemplatation.

## COMPASSION AND INFINITE SPACE

	- Teachings combine the brahmaviharas with the Awakening Factors brings an additional perspective that relates to the [[immaterial spheres]].
	- Previously mentioned that the culmination point of metta is found in [[mental beauty]]
	- With the other 3 brahmaviharas their respective culmination points are the 1st three of the immaterial spheres 
	1. Compassion = infinite SPACE 
	2. sympathetic joy = infinite consciousness 
	3. equanimity = sphere of nothingness
	However to speak of these as the culmination only holds true for those who have not penetrated to a superior liberation.
	- these three immaterial spheres are the culmination point of the three brahmaviharas within the realm of mental tranquillity. 
	- These speak to the boundless nature of the radiation of the divine abodes themselves 
	- It also speaks to the shared tendency towards some degree of one's holding on to a sense of self.
	- Going beyond a sense of self requires the cultivation of liberating insight 
	- We will be turning towards that in the weeks to follow
	- The immaterial spheres have some potiential to undermine the grosser manifesations of a senses of self.

> 	- The same holds for the brahmaviharas themselves, as they similarly are able  to diminish major forms of self-centeredness thru the experience of the boundless radiation of a mental attitude that wishes others to be well.
