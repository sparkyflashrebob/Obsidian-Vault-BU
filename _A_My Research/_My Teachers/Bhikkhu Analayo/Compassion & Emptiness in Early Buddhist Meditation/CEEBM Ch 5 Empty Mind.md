### Examine Instructions in Maha Sunnata Sutta regarding how to relate to emptiness in daily activities

## Infinite Consciousness

Instructions
- now not give attention to infinite space but to infinite consciousness
- there is this non-emptiness: unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness
- only weariness because of this perception
- Whatever is not present, one sees as empty. Whatever else is present, one see as present. Dwell in emptiness without distortion

Avoid perception previously
but not lose what was previously accomplished.
	know where one has been

With this step turning to the mind itself
	by earlier being absorbed with the notion of infinite space, consciousness itself has come to be at one with the experience of infinity.
	turn back to the mind itself enable the perception of infinite consciousness
	consciousness becomes the object and content of one's experience

Reveals the foundational role consciousness plays in subjective experience
	infinite space involves the whole world without limit
	perception of infinite consciousness turns attention to that which has been aware of infinite space
		in a way then, the whole world is now seen to be in one's own mind.

Early Buddhist thought does not consider consciousness to be the source of the external world
	the external world exists as a conditioned and impermanent process independent of our act to cognize it.

In terms of my subjective experience though, consciousness is its very foundation.
	our experience of the world is impossible without conscioiusness

In other words
	for phenomena in the world to exist for me, and be experienced by me, consciousness is indispensable.
	Consciousness is the source of my world
	Provides the ground that my world experience unfolds in

Once we get beyond "space" the subjective experience of infinite consciousness becomes the pervasive theme of meditative practice

This step reveals the substantial contribution made our own minds to our experience of the world

## THE NATURE OF CONSCIOUSNESS

Early Discourses frequently analyze the [[5 Aggregates]]
	Main thrust to reveal the empty nature of all the aspects of experience that one might identify with and cling to as "I" and "mine". 
	Earlier reference to the performance of a magician
	comparable to a hallucination

Within the context of the 5 Aggregates, consciousness is only one aspect of mind
	something distinct from feeling, perception and formations.
	which stand for the affective, recognizing, and conative dimensions of the mind

In other passages, consciousness can stand for mind in its totality.
with this meditation training on the sphere of consciousness
our perceived experience converges on consciousness
	it has become all-embracing
	the other aspects of mind corresponding to the mental aggregates
		feeling, perception and mental fabrication
	have become subdued so much that they are barely noticable
	In this way, in actual meditation practice
	consciousness can come to represent mind in its totality

The quality of knowing and being aware is a constant given in mental experiences
	this may lead us to believe that consciousness is permanent
	This is a common belief - even at the time of the Buddha.
	
	There is an exchange between a monk named Sati (:)) and the buddha
	coming to the conclusion that this same consciousness is what is reborn 
	(you may want to think of this as this is the same consciousness that goes to heaven.)
	
> 	Being informed about this, the Buddha asked him to explain his understanding. Here is their exchange as reported in the Madhyama-āgama version. 
> 	The Blessed One asked him: “Is it true that you speak in this way: ‘I understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One in this way: it is this consciousness now which is going to be reborn, not another?’” 
> 	The monk Sāti replied: “Blessed One, I truly understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One in this way: ‘it is this consciousness now which is going to be reborn, not another.’” 
> 	The Blessed One asked him: “What is this consciousness?” 
> 	The monk Sāti replied: “Blessed One, this consciousness is that which speaks, feels, acts, instructs, gives rise to, appears, it is that which does good or bad deeds and experiences their results.” 
> 	The Blessed One reprimanded him: “Sāti, how come you understand the Dharma taught by me in this way? From whose mouth have you heard that I give such a teaching? You are a fool.”
	   
	   Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

Here clearly, that idea that consciousness is a permanent entity that transmigrates from one life to the next is not what I Buddha thought.

## Dependent Arising

Conditionality of experience is a theme that to some degree underlies the teaching on emptiness in the [[Cūḷasuññatasutta]] MN 121, which is the primary discourse on Emptiness that we have been exploring

Conditionality underpins the reflection regarding "what weariness has been overcome and what weariness is still here".

Repeating the phase so we are reminded of the context:
> Again, Ānanda, if ... one wishes to dwell much in emptiness ... one should not give attention to the perception of earth and not give attention to the perception of the sphere of infinite space, but should frequently give attention to the unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness. 
> In this way one knows that this is empty of the perception of earth and empty of the perception of the sphere of infinite space. Yet there is this non-emptiness: just the unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness. 
> [One knows]: “Whatever weariness because of the perception of earth there might be – <u>that is not present for me</u>; whatever weariness because of the perception of the sphere of infinite space there might be – <u>that is also not present for me</u>. There is only the weariness because of the unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness.” 
> Whatever is not present, one therefore sees as empty; whatever else is present, one sees as truly present. Ānanda, this is called truly dwelling in emptiness, without distortion.

Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

Weariness that has been overcome and what weariness is still there.
%%Talking about stress%%
The experience of infinite consciousness is a conditionally arisen mental state
	reached by leaving behind the other conditions, the steps, in this reflection on emptiness.
	that currently the perceptions of earth and infinite space
	this sets up the conditions for the present moments experience

"weariness" for the conditions left behind
	brings out an aspect shared by all that is conditioned
	at some point on should become weary of it
However sublime it is, it is a conditioned product of the mind and as such something wearisome.
Qualifing perception as a form of weariness
	combines this insight into conditionality with a direct appreciation of dukkha
Prepares us for the gradual entry into emptiness
	which is stepping out of depending on any experience at all
	UNCONDITIONED

> One meditates in such a way that one <u>does not</u> cultivate meditation in dependence on the earth, one <u>does not</u> cultivate meditation in dependence on water, fire, wind, [the sphere of boundless] space, [the sphere of boundless] consciousness, [the sphere of] nothingness, [the sphere of] neither-perception-nor-non-perception, not in dependence on this world, not in dependence on that world, not [in dependence on] the sun or the moon, not [in dependence on] what is seen, heard, experienced, and cognized, not [in dependence on] what one has attained, not [in dependence on] what one searches for, not [in dependence on] what conforms with one’s experience, not [in dependence on] what conforms with one’s contemplation; yet one cultivates meditation.

Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

This form of meditation can be accomplished once one is able and willing to let go of these perceptions. 
	Requires us to become disenchanted with them
	This is the purpose for the gradual entry into emptiness
	Progress takes place by leaving behind this weariness

The practice is designed to ensure that we see each stage as merely a stepping stone on the meditative path
Even the sublime experience of infinite consciousness is a type of weariness
	It is conditioned

			name-and-form (namarupa) being (feeling, perception, intention, contact & attention)

		What does this mean?
			If consciousness were not to enter the mother's womb, would name-and-form maniftest?

> 					for this reason it should be understood that this is the cause of name-and-form, the source of name-and-form, the origin of name-and-form, the condition for name-and-form, namely consciousness. Why is that? Because conditioned by consciousness there is name-and-form.

The idea that consciousness conditions [[name-and-form]] and name-and-form condition consciousness see pretty clear.

The main point here is understand this basic principal of conditionality.
	This that mentality

		when this is not, that is not

	Neither can claim independent existence

	conditioned experience arising from them

#### THE BAHIYA INSTRUCTIONS
%% This is one of my favorite teachings - on par with [[B_Book Markups/Readwise/Books/The Heart Sutra]]%%
Bahia was a well-respected practitioner
who wondered if he was an [[arahant (enlightened)]]
As the story goes he was informed, first of all, that he is not an arahant,
and secondly,
that there is a real arahant living some distance away (The Buddha).
	Even beyond this he was told that he is not even on the path of arahantship.
	So this get him curious. 
And so Bahiya travels to see the Buddha. It was a long trek but eventually he meets the Buddha when is on alms round.
Imploring him for a teaching. Saying he might die at any moment.
Finally, after asking him 3 times the Buddha then gives him the instruction
that he should remain conscious of just the base sense experience as a way to transcend [[Google Keep/Dukkha]]

Translation from the Udana 1.10

> "Herein, Bahiya, you should train yourself thus: 'In the seen will be merely what is seen; in the heard will be merely what is heard; in the sensed will be merely what is sensed; in the cognized will be merely what is cognized.' In this way you should train yourself, Bahiya.
> 
> "When, Bahiya, for you in the seen is merely what is seen... in the cognized is merely what is cognized, then, Bahiya, you will not be 'with that.' When, Bahiya, you are not 'with that,' then, Bahiya, you will not be 'in that.' When, Bahiya, you are not 'in that,' then, Bahiya, you will be neither here nor beyond nor in between the two. Just this is the end of suffering."

Or in another way:
“See by limiting it to seeing, hear by limiting it to hearing, feel by limiting it to feeling, cognize by limiting it to cognizing.” 
Then [the Buddha] spoke in verse: “If you are not in that, and [not being in] that you are also not in this, and you are also not in between the two, this then is the end of dukkha indeed.”

Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

Staying with the bare experience
limiting one's mental processing to the bare act of cognizing
By staying present with the awareness of experience
we can avoid the proliferations that usually arise.
Seeing forms without the mental chatter

Once we see there is just the seen, one realizes one is not that which is seen.
We will not be carried away by the conditionality of the perceptual process.
In a sense, we will not identify with this process of experience of what is taking place. 
Nor any desire or aversion to what is conditioned.
We stand in the middle ground. Not reacting or identifying any aspect of experience and one avoids taking a stance on anything.
