### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Closing Session 5, Watts portrays experienced reality as a multi-dimensional loom where light, sound, touch, and feeling cross like warp and woof. Rather than ordering someone who cannot relax to 'calm down,' Watts advises going *with* the tension—rocking with the boat rather than bracing against its heave—because ecstasy awaits at either extreme. Cautioning against stopping at cool private detachment (the Mahāyāna *Pratyekabuddha*), Watts points toward the *Bodhisattva*, who recognizes all suffering strangers as nerve endings on an extended cosmic body and returns fully into ordinary life.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Watts gathers Session 5's strands into a sweeping artistic synthesis: reality is neither dead matter nor grim ordeal, but a shimmering loom of interlocking spectra—color, sound, scent, touch, and emotion crossing like warp and woof so each side reveals the pattern of the whole. People panic at high intensity ('too much!') or sink into boredom at low intensity ('not enough!'), while therapists fruitlessly shout 'relax!' at tightened nerves. Just as sailors ride heavy seas by swaying *with* the pitch of the vessel, Watts suggests following the line of least resistance—going wholeheartedly into whatever pulse is already moving—until realization dawns that the universe exists precisely as music and fireworks: inevitable ecstasy.

Yet awakening brings one delicate hazard: early disenchantment with earthly ambitions can freeze a practitioner into aloof indifference—what Mahāyāna Buddhism contrasts as the private *Pratyekabuddha* compared to the compassionate *Bodhisattva*. Knowing everything is fundamentally whole does not breed coldness; once insight matures, other people cease to look like isolated strangers and reveal themselves as sensitive nerve endings of one extended body. Rather than preaching from outside, one answers life's mystery directly through lived presence.
