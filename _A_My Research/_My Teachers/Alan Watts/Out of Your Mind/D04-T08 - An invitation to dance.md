### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Closing Session 4, Watts reflects on modern humanity's strange habit of 'dis-enjoying' itself, echoing H. L. Mencken's critique of industrialized ugliness and joyless moralism. True generosity requires overflowing delight rather than grim Quakerish duty; trying to enrich oneself by staring inward like a self-help course is like trying to survive by chewing one's own fingers. Even the cosmic *Ouroboros* (snake swallowing its tail) stays fresh only because of an 'unconscious gap'—that blind spot behind the eyes that turns monotonous repetition into lively rhythm.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Watts contrasts grim, earnest activism with the spirit of the Pied Piper extending an invitation to dance. Quoting H. L. Mencken's *The Libido for the Ugly*—with its Pennsylvania mining towns and joyless stone churches looking 'like a Presbyterian with a grin'—Watts critiques both philistine utilitarianism and self-conscious modern artists who paint soup cans or compose screeching dissonances merely to mirror social ugliness. Even if automation fed and clothed every person on earth, a solemn, joyless mind would only hunt for someone else to fix rather than actually knowing how to live.

Joy cannot be manufactured by narcissistic self-improvement. People become interesting not by taking courses on 'how to win friends,' but by becoming fascinated by the world outside their own skulls. Trying to feed on one's own personality is a vicious circle—like trying to eat your own fingernails and fingers for nutrition. On a cosmic scale, the universe indeed feeds on itself like the archaic *Ouroboros* biting its tail, yet avoids dreary boredom because of an essential blind spot—the 'unconscious' gap where the spark jumps. Just as music and shifting fashion hemlines rely on silent intervals and surprise, digging into who is dancing reveals onion layer after onion layer until the seeker disappears into the living rhythm itself.
