### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Describing a 'really swinging human being' as someone dancing fluently on both the social ego stage and the cosmic ground simultaneously, Watts warns against turning survival into grim compulsive drudgery passed down like an anxious curse to one's children. Invoking Taoist *zìrán* ('so of itself'), he tells the hilarious memory of an accented Zen master lecturing pious New York devotees that Buddhism means *'no purpose'*—compared frankly to passing gas (*'at nine o'clock I drop fart'*—not scheduled, happening naturally!). Commanded play ('Now dear, play for Auntie!') paralyzes children; true artistic mastery learns Saburō Hasegawa's secret: the *controlled accident*.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

What separates liberated vitality from dreary conformity? Watts sketches a balanced human life moving effortlessly on two registers at once: playing everyday social roles gracefully without mistaking them for ultimate reality. Whenever survival becomes a neurotic commandment—grinding through work solely to breed anxious children who repeat the grind—joy vanishes, leaving Watts gently disarming suicidal callers by confirming they aren't trapped in compulsory existence at all.

Turning to Taoism's core word for nature, *zìrán* ('spontaneously so of itself,' unforced like hiccups or blossoming), Watts shares one of his funniest New York memories: an elder Japanese Zen roshi clad in formal gold brocade behind candlelight solemnly instructing upright Western disciples that Buddhism has *'no purpose; purpose is restlessness—when you drop fart, you don't say at nine o'clock I drop fart, it happen of itself!'* Leaving pious devotees stifling laughs in handkerchiefs, the roshi embodied how foolish forced solemnity becomes. Ordering a child at Thanksgiving to perform on cue freezes natural grace—yet every musician, painter, or dancer must learn how to play on schedule, uniting discipline with spontaneity in Japanese painter Saburō Hasegawa's luminous paradox: the **controlled accident**.
