### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Watts explores why extremes of panic, pain, and erotic rapture trigger deep cultural embarrassment: all three launch an uncontrollable 'oscillating process' that wrecks our dignified self-image ('you wouldn't tell from a snapshot of your face whether it was pleasure or pain'). Without endorsing pathology, Watts examines sadism, masochism (sharing the punchline of the masochist begging to be beaten while the sadist replies, 'I won't!'), and ecstatic abandon (*'tue-moi!'* / 'kill me!') to demonstrate how the exact same physical vibration shifts value depending on total self-surrender versus terrified holding-back.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Continuing his inquiry into why society polices 'the heebie-jeebies,' Watts highlights an insight rarely examined outside taboo boundaries: when any sensation hits extreme amplitude, the nervous system enters a convulsive oscillation akin to orgasm. Looking at a frozen close-up photograph of someone gripped by intense agony versus total erotic rapture, one can scarcely tell the two apart—because in both, a cosmic tide overwhelms deliberate social composure and deportment.

Venturing carefully into why human minds confuse agony and ecstasy in sadism and masochism, Watts rejects purely dismissive psychiatric labels in order to uncover the underlying principle. Masochists learn early to survive inevitable hurt by eroticizing the vibration—giving themselves completely to the wave—while sadists crave a partner who willingly participates in that intensity (illustrated by the classic wry joke where the masochist begs, 'Beat me!' and the sadist smirks, 'I won't!'). In moments of wild self-abandon, resistance flips into total oneness with what is happening, proving that contrasting emotional worlds can inhabit the very same spectrum of nervous vibration.
