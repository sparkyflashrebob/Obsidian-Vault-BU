### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Eighteenth-century thinkers realized the God-hypothesis added nothing to scientific prediction, so they discarded the Lawmaker while keeping 'natural laws'—reimagining the universe as clockwork billiard balls driven by blind force. Under this fully automatic myth, intelligence and love seem accidental flukes (monkeys typing *Encyclopaedia Britannica*), leaving humans feeling trapped inside skin-bags fighting an alien cosmos. Watts counters that people grow out of the universe just as apples grow off an apple tree: this universe 'peoples.'

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Watts traces how Western science emerged directly out of Abrahamic theology: God laid down the cosmic Logos (divine/natural law), initially pictured like train tracks dictating how phenomena move (quoting the famous determinism limerick: *'not even a bus, I'm a tram'*). In the Enlightenment, thinkers noticed science works strictly as prophecy—studying past regularities to forecast future events—and that adding 'God wills it' changes no prediction whatsoever. They therefore dropped the Lawmaker while preserving the metaphor of 'Law,' rebranding mere observed regularities as blind mechanical determinism: Newtonian billiard balls colliding, Haeckel and Huxley's blind energy, or Freud's blind *libido*.

The psychological fallout of this *fully automatic* model is profound alienation. If humans are a statistical fluke—like a thousand monkeys at typewriters accidentally producing *Encyclopaedia Britannica* before relapsing into gibberish—then consciousness appears as an alien anomaly forced to wage war against nature with bulldozers and rockets. Watts dismantles this antagonism with disarming botanical clarity: just as an apple tree 'apples,' this universe 'peoples.' We did not *come into* this world like foreign invaders; we *came out of* it like fruit from a tree. Treating consciousness as merely trapped inside a bag of skin breeds existential hostility, racism ('defining outsiders as unpeople'), and ecological violence.
