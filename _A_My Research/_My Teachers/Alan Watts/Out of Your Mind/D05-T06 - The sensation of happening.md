### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Watts exposes a final subtle trap: telling oneself 'I can't do anything' while secretly hoping that resignation will trigger enlightenment. When all strategies truly collapse, life continues seamlessly on its own—neither blind mechanical determinism (there is no helpless puppet being pushed) nor bondage to history. Listening closely with eyes closed shows sounds erupting out of *now* and trailing off into memory like a ship's wake; just as a wake never pushes the ship, the past never moves the present. Realizing you *are* the happening fuses doing and happening into one—omnipotent as the universe, while remaining an ordinary, non-pretentious human being.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

At the edge of non-doing lurks a clever irony: even saying 'there is nothing I can do' often hides a secret bargain expecting something dramatic to click in reward. Only when every strategy genuinely fails does one notice that breathing, hearing, heartbeats, and awareness go right on effortlessly without a supervisor. Listeners often mistake this effortless flow for fatalistic determinism, yet Watts demolishes determinism on two grounds: first, there is no separate helpless victim being determined; second, sensory observation reverses our notion of time.

If you close your eyes and attend strictly to raw hearing, sounds do not arrive pushed forward from yesterday—they spring directly out of immediate silence and fade backward into brain echoes called memory. Likewise, watching movement reveals only the eternal now followed by a trailing echo, like foam behind a vessel. Because a wake cannot drive a ship, blaming present habits on past conditioning is merely a convenient alibi—one that leaves behaviorist 'scholasticism' unable to explain genuine novelty. Once you feel in your bones that doing *is* happening and happening *is* doing, you see how all existence moves as one self—while prudently remembering that you are omnipotent *as* the universe, never in the theatrical stage role of 'Alan Watts.'
