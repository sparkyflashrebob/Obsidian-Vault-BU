### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Testing both horror stories against the imagination—vanishing cleanly forever versus swinging endlessly between joy and misery—Watts invokes the Hindu cosmic cycle of 4,320,000 years saved from boredom only by cosmic 'forgettery' (the quiet trough between ocean waves). Even Buddhism initially celebrated escaping *saṃsāra* ('House-builder, I have found you!'), before Bodhisattvas recognized that turning away leaves everyone else behind—and Mahāyāna dialectics proved nirvana and birth-and-death imply each other completely. Ultimately, whether born as human, spider, or fish, every creature standing on its own vessel in mid-ocean stands equally 'in the middle' between macrocosm and microcosm, forever occupying this exact immediate now.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Watts invites a playful imaginative experiment comparing two destinies: total annihilation after a brief burst of vitality versus endlessly cycling between happy days and miserable days without exit. The ancient Indians envisioned vast 4,320,000-year cosmic epochs vanishing and restarting repeatedly—rendered tolerable solely by the merciful 'forgettery' between crests, like the dip between rolling waves. Exhausted by endless wheeling, early Buddhists cheered the Buddha's enlightenment cry—*'House-builder, I have found you!'*—seeking total exit from *saṃsāra*, until compassionate insight asked what happens to everyone left behind, and sharp dialectic showed that nirvana defined against birth-and-death locks both poles together.

Turning the trick playfully upside down, Watts points out that *every* conceivable incarnation in any universe feels like this exact one right now. A spider looks around at fellow spiders and considers eight legs the self-evident 'human shape,' while to an elegant gliding fish we look hilariously clumsy! Likewise, because every perceiving observer looks outward evenly from where they stand—like gazing across a 360-degree horizon from a boat in mid-ocean—every creature always stands squarely 'in the middle' between overwhelming natural forces above and tiny troublesome viruses below, experiencing life forever from right here.
