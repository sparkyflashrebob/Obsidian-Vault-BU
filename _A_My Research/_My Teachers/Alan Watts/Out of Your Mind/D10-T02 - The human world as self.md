### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Reiterating *tat tvam asi* ('you *come out of* this world as leaves grow out of a tree'), Watts shifts from cosmos to human civilization across three eras: hunting, agrarian, and industrial. Lone hunters mastered every craft and bred lone 'weird' visionaries—*shamans* initiated solo in forest or mountaintop solitude. Settling at crossroads into fenced agrarian towns ('within the pale,' noting Spanish *palo* for stick—and Bible trivia that Noah planted vineyards right after the Flood!) produced specialization, hereditary *priests* ordained by tradition rather than solo visions, and India's four foundational castes: *Brāhmaṇas* (priests), *Kṣatriyas* (rulers/warriors), *Vaiśyas* (merchants), and *Śūdras* (laborers)—all playful social masks worn by one underlying *Brahman*.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Opening Session 10 (*The World as Self, Part 2*), Watts reminds listeners that human beings do not crash into the planet as alien visitors—we sprout directly out of Earth 'as leaves come from a tree,' rooted in one universal Self. Tracing human society as an expression of *Brahman*, he contrasts early hunting bands—where lone forest wanderers mastered every skill and produced self-initiated *shamans* ('weird' in the older magical sense, encountering spirits alone in mountain isolation)—with settled agrarian cities.

Once farming anchored populations inside timber stockades ('within the pale' and 'beyond the pale,' recalling Noah immediately planting vineyards post-Flood!), survival split into specialized trades governed by ordained *priests* inheriting collective authority rather than personal wilderness visions. Fitting naturally into four-gated crossroads cities, traditional India crystallized four overarching social castes: *Brāhmaṇas* (keepers of sacred ritual and wisdom), *Kṣatriyas* (rulers and warriors), *Vaiśyas* (traders and merchants), and *Śūdras* (craftsmen and laborers)—setting the stage to view every human vocation as a living dramatic mask worn by the Self.
