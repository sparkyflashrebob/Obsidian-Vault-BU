### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Closing Disc 01, Watts replaces both the ceramic and automatic models with the *dramatic image*: life as a cosmic game of hide-and-seek whose secret is taught intuitively to every giggling baby playing peek-a-boo. Because opposite poles—life and death, black and white, self and other—belong to a single magnet (cut off the South Pole and a new South Pole immediately appears), conflict is actually secret polarity. In Hindu culture (*līlā*), awakening to 'I am God' earns congratulations rather than an asylum, yet even ordinary physiology proves our unconscious genius—beating hearts and growing hair effortlessly without words, which are as clumsy for explaining existence as a fork is for drinking the ocean.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Having dismantled the *ceramic* and *automatic* models, Watts introduces his third root-image: **the world as a drama**. Every plot and adventure begins with the primordial game of **hide-and-seek**, enacted naturally when an adult plays peek-a-boo with an infant. Why does the baby burst out laughing? Half-jokingly calling the baby 'a recent incarnation of God,' Watts explains that the child intuitively remembers what formal education deletes: opposites are never absolute enemies in mortal combat, but **poles of one indivisible magnet**. Chop the South Pole off a bar magnet and the remaining bar instantly sprouts a new South Pole. Life and death, light and dark, being and non-being imply one another concentrically—just as self and other do.

Consequently, when someone suddenly wakes up and announces *'I am God,'* cultural conditioning dictates the aftermath. Raised under an autocratic King-God myth, the visionary behaves megalomaniacally and lands in a psychiatric ward. Raised in India's *līlā* worldview, friends laugh and say, *'Congratulations, at last you found out!'*—not because you consciously micromanage galaxies like a tyrant, any more than Shiva micromanages ten arms, or you consciously calculate how to grow hair, digest lunch, and beat your heart. Like playing a complex pipe organ, our bodies enact effortless cosmic mastery below conscious chatter. Remembering childhood rainy afternoons when he imagined asking God why leaves are green (*'because of chlorophyll'*), Watts points out that in the Hindu universe God simply laughs—because asking how existence works in verbal terms is like trying to drink the ocean with a fork.

## Stories, Analogies & Anecdotes Offered

- **Playing Peek-a-Boo with a Baby**: Watts uses a book-and-face peek-a-boo game with a giggling infant to show that children intuitively delight in cosmic hide-and-seek before schooling teaches dualism.
- **Chopping Off the South Pole of a Magnet**: Trying to eliminate the South Pole by sawing a magnet in half proves opposites cannot exist apart—the remaining piece automatically manifests a new pole.
- **Wet Afternoons in Heaven: 'Because of Chlorophyll'**: Recalling his mother's promise that God would explain all mysteries on rainy days in heaven, Watts imagines polite theology collapsing before wordless action (*'I just did it'*).
- **Drinking the Ocean with a Fork**: Attempting to explain verbally how nature grows mountains or beats a heart is as futile—and endlessly tedious—as sipping the sea one forkful at a time.
