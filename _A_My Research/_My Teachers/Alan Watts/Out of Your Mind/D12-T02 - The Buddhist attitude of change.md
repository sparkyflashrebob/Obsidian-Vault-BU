### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Launching Session 12 with an uproarious imitation of a Scottish hellfire preacher decrying golf and 'vanity of vanities,' Watts notes how initial Buddhist warnings about flux unsettle seekers into chasing an escape-hatch *Nirvāṇa* detached from *saṁsāra*. That exact tension split early Buddhism (*Theravāda* / polemically called *Hīnayāna*) from *Mahāyāna*: trying to clinch timeless victory turns someone into an isolated *Pratyekabuddha* ('stone Buddha'), confusing escape with liberation. By contrast, the *Bodhisattva* returns directly into the sensory street—symbolized by thousand-armed Guanyin / Kannon rescuing all beings—just as Watts once suddenly beheld the entire bustling city of Kyoto (police, cabbies, crooked merchants alike) moving as one thousand-armed Kannon.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Opening the closing session of the series, Watts first mimics a theatrical Scottish Presbyterian minister thundering 'Vanity of vanities!' over fine coats and Sunday golf—noting how Buddhism initially wields the same shock tactic to wake people out of stubborn clinging. Yet if seekers chase *Nirvāṇa* purely as a frozen fortress far outside *saṁsāra*'s wheel, they quietly smuggle permanence back through the exit door.

Right at this pivot arose the historic split between southern *Theravāda* ('doctrine of the elders,' nicknamed polemically *Hīnayāna*, or 'little ferryboat'—though Watts cheerfully jokes about living aboard a Sausalito ferryboat himself!) and *Mahāyāna* ('great ferryboat'). Clinging smugly to quiet emptiness turns a meditator into a *Pratyekabuddha* ('private stone Buddha'), stuck in counterfeit stillness. Mahāyāna counters with the Bodhisattva—personified in compassionate hermaphroditic Guanyin (Japanese *Kannon*) wielding a thousand golden arms of skillful care. Recalling a sudden illumination walking through Kyoto at night, Watts saw firefighters, grumpy bureaucrats, taxi drivers, and shopkeepers not as profane distractions from enlightenment, but as Kannon's own thousand bustling arms sustaining human life—revealing *Nirvāṇa* right here whenever objection ceases.
