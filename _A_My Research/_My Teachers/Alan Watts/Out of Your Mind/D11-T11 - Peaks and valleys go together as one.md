### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Closing Session 11, Watts explains why human awareness grows one-sided: scanning life exclusively for upward crests—wealth, youth, and mountaintops—while ignoring troughs and valleys. Pointing to a buzzsaw, he notes that blade tips cannot cut without V-shaped valleys carving space between them. Similarly, conscious attention dismisses empty space, silence, and musical intervals as mere 'nothing,' missing that interval *creates* melody and space *defines* solids just as back goes with front. Fearful dread that death and darkness might eventually conquer life rests on splitting inseparable poles apart—whereas awakening accepts night, space, and non-being as vital partners of every joyful crest.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Summing up Session 11 (*The World as Emptiness*), Watts exposes the perceptual blind spot that breeds existential dread. Because cultural value prizes upward movement—peaks, heights, achievements, youth—ordinary minds screen out every downward trough except to dread 'the valley of the shadow of death.' Yet examine a carpenter's saw: although sharp tooth tips appear to do all the cutting, those teeth could never bite wood without opposing V-shaped valleys carved cleanly between them.

Extending this insight from geometry to music and cosmology, Watts observes how habit treats empty space as blank nothingness and ignores intervals between musical notes—forgetting that a melody literally consists of relationships across intervals rather than isolated frozen pitches! Front and back, solid and space, crests and troughs mutually constitute each other. Overemphasizing the neglected 'valley' side intentionally corrects our frantic one-sidedness: darkness, rest, silence, and death are never hostile invaders threatening to swallow light, but the essential obverse partner letting being vibrate and live at all.

They seem to do the cutting, not the valleys between the teeth, but do you see you couldn't have tips of teeth without valleys?

But seriously, we don't praise the peaks of being high and blame the valleys for being low.

[01:20] But it is so, you see, that we ignore the valley aspect of things. And so all wisdom begins by emphasizing the valley aspect as distinct from the peak aspect. We pay plenty of attention to the peak aspect. That's what captures our attention. But we somehow screen out the valley aspect.

[01:52] But that makes us very uncomfortable. It seems that we want and get pleasure from looking at the peaks. But actually, this denies our pleasure. Because secretly, we know that every peak is followed by a valley, the valley of the shadow of death. And we are always afraid because we are not used to looking at valleys, because we are not used to living with them.

[02:17] They represent to us a strange and threatening unknown. Maybe we are afraid the principle of the valley will conquer, and the peaks will be overwhelmed. Maybe death is stronger than life. [02:56] And so we resist change, ignorant of the fact. That change is life, and that nothing is invariably the obverse face of something. Most people are afraid of space. [03:42] If I say there is a universe in which there isn't anything but space, you are a space between what? Space is relationship. And it always goes together with solid, like back goes with front, but the divisive mind ignores space. Actually they go together.

[05:13] But I say the interval is sometimes more important because we under emphasize it, so I am going to overemphasize it as a correction. So space, night, death, darkness, not being there, is an essential component of being there. You don't have the one without the other, just as your buzz saw has no teeth without having values between the tips of them.
