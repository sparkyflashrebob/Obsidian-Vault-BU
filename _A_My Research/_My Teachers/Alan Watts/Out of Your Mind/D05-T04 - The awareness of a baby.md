### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Watts invites listeners to drop effort and adopt an infant's pre-verbal awareness: hearing speech merely as sound and watching thoughts drift like clouds across the sky or the crackling of a fire. The fact that you cannot forcefully stop thinking proves there is no separate controller—only a unitary happening (*Tao* or *tathatā*, 'suchness') prior to near, far, inside, or outside. Splitting a person into controller and controlled is grammatical superstition: how can a static noun start a living verb?

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Moving from theory into gentle guided immediacy, Watts asks listeners to inhabit the openness of a newborn before distance, ownership, or naming has been installed. Rather than straining to force thoughts to stop, one simply listens to ambient thoughts and muscular habits as passively as clouds drifting overhead or logs crackling in a hearth. The very inability to stop thinking by sheer willpower ceases to look like meditative failure; instead, it demonstrates cleanly that the isolated command-center was always imaginary.

From this unguarded standpoint—like an infant reaching a finger toward the moon because near and far have not yet been divided—reality reveals itself simply as 'this' going on: what Chinese philosophy calls *Tao* and Buddhism calls *tathatā* ('suchness'). Nothing is happening *to* you; rather, what you call 'you' belongs seamlessly inside the happening itself. The demand for an outside supervisor ('control yourself!') splits one whole living movement into a phantom noun barking orders at a verb.

**Alan Watts:**

[00:00] But let's suppose now you are babies again, and you don't know anything. Now, don't be frightened because anything you know you can get back later. But for the time being, here is our awareness. Unless suppose you have no information about this at all and no words for it. And that my talking to you is just a noise.

[00:35] Now, don't try to do anything about this. Don't make any effort. Because naturally, by force of habit, certain tensions remain inside you, and certain ideas and words drift all the time through your mind, just like the wind blows or clouds move across the sky. Don't bother with them at all. Don't try to get rid of them. Just be aware of what's going on in your head, like it was clouds in the sky, or the crackling of the fire.

[01:20] There's no problem to this. All you have to do, really, is look and listen without naming. And if you are naming, never mind. Just listen to that. Now that you can't force anything here, that you can't willfully stop thinking and stop naming, is only telling you that the separate you doesn't exist.

[02:14] It isn't a mark of defeat. It isn't a sign of your lack of practice in meditation. That it runs on all by itself simply means that the individual separate yous, the figment of your imagination. So you are aware at this point of a happening. Remember, you don't know anything about the difference between you and it. You haven't been told that. You have no words for the difference between inside and outside, between here and there.

[03:02] Nobody has taught you that what you see out in front of you is either near or far from your eyes. Watch a baby put out a finger to touch the moon. You don't know about that. Just, therefore, here it is. We'll just call it this. And if you will feel it, the going on, which includes absolutely everything you feel.

[03:58] Well, whatever that is, it's what the Chinese call Tao, what Buddhists call suchness, or tathatā. And it's a happening. It doesn't happen to you, because where is that? You, what you call you, is part of the happening, or an aspect of it. Has no parts.

[04:44] It's not like a machine. And it's a little scary because you'd feel, well, who's in control around here? Why should there be anyone? That's a very weird notion we have that processes require something outside them to control them. We'd never occur to us that processes could be self-controlling. Even though we say to someone, control yourself. In order to think about self-control, we split a person in two.

[05:20] So that there's a you separate from the self that's supposed to be controlled. Well, how can that achieve anything? How can a noun start a verb? Yet it's a fundamental superstition that that can be done.
