### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Launching *The Web of Life*, Watts describes ordinary awareness as a narrow bandwidth tuned to survive inside social games while editing out the vast 'lowdown' of existence. Like the underside pattern of a woven rug where threads duck beneath the surface, or the unplucked strings on a harp, perception must ignore infinite frequencies (gamma rays, cosmic bands) to weave distinct notes and shapes. Asking 'What did you forget as you walked in today?'—not your glasses or sweater, nor 'Paul Jones,' your surface alias—Watts uncovers the hidden cosmic foundation (God, Brahman, Tao) we deftly pretend not to be.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Opening Session 3 (*The Web of Life*), Watts reveals a foundational paradox: consciousness works through **exclusion**. Like a tightly tuned radio receiver, biological senses capture only a thin ribbon of light, sound, and touch amid roaring oceans of ultraviolet, cosmic, and subatomic vibrations. Comparing attention to weaving on the rug before the seminar room—where black threads plunge underneath white patterns to create the hidden reverse design—or to picking selective strings on a heavenly harp rather than mindlessly strumming every wire at once, Watts shows why selective ignorance builds beauty yet hides our deepest peace.

Playfully teasing the audience (*'My goodness, you all forgot something—no, not your sweater, glasses, or wig!'*), Watts leads up to the classic self-inquiry: *'Who are you really?'* Pushing past convenient labels like 'Paul Jones' or defensive shrugging (*'just me'*), he points toward the forgotten underside of the tapestry: every individual is a focused performance of the whole cosmos—called God, *Brahman*, or *Tao*—engaged in an astonishingly convincing masquerade of forgetting itself.
