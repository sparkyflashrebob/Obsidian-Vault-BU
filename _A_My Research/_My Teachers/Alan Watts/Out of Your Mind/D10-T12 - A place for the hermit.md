### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Comparing medieval Western openness to hermits and wandering clerics with modern suburbia's hostility toward Sausalito boat-builders and non-consuming dropouts, Watts diagnoses why democratic conformity grows intolerant of outsiders. Whereas confident hierarchical societies revered mountain yogis and kept court fools—like Richard II's 'antic' mocking royal pride—insecure societies demand every citizen play the consumer game, twisting 'all are equal before God' into 'all are equally crooked and inferior.' Closing Session 10 with Thoreau's warning that neighbors chase you even into the forest, Watts pleads for cultural room for peaceful non-joiners who remind the city that politics and commerce are only games.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Drawing Session 10 to a close, Watts notes that traditional civilizations—whether Hindu *saṁnyāsins* or medieval European monks, nuns, and wandering clerics—explicitly protected spaces where contemplatives could check out of economic and family games. Paradoxically, medieval clerical literacy even opened upward mobility across rigid castes, whereas contemporary industrial democracies secretly despise peaceful dropouts precisely because hermits make terrible consumers: no suburban lawns, dishwashers, or status-signaling cars.

Watts traces modern intolerance to an insecure distortion of mysticism. While Christianity originally uttered the mystical truth that all persons are equally divine in God's sight, literalist bureaucracy flips that maxim into its sour parody: *all citizens are equally inferior crooks*. Hence rude clerks, invasive tax interrogations, and nervous conformity masked as 'rugged individualism'—drifting dangerously close to fascism whenever outsiders threaten the collective consensus. In contrast, vital cultures welcome mountain hermits—and wise kings kept sharp-tongued court fools like Shakespeare's 'antic' in *Richard II*—to puncture official pomp with one liberating message: what society takes dead seriously is still only a passing game.
