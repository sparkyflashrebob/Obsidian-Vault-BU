### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Wrapping up Session 2, Watts summarizes awakening as an unhurried re-examination of rigged everyday assumptions—like idioms telling us to 'face the facts' as alien adversaries. Just as jet travel naturalized the spherical Earth (eliminating ancient fears that people in the Antipodes might fall off) and physics normalized Einstein's curved light, feeling continuous with the universe can become ordinary common sense—freeing humanity to steer science and technology with care rather than warlike conquest.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

In this crisp closing reflection for Session 2, Watts reframes enlightenment in disarmingly practical terms: questioning what everyone takes for granted simply because it feels familiar. Phrases like 'face the facts' quietly reinforce a warlike stance toward life as a foreign enemy. Drawing on the historical shift from flat-earth anxieties—when Europeans wondered why folks in the southern Antipodes didn't tumble into space—to effortless modern jet travel, and from Euclidean common sense to Einsteinian curved light, Watts anticipates a coming cultural shift where non-dual continuity with nature feels completely natural. Once that new common sense dawns, he concludes, humanity can finally guide its vast technological powers with affection instead of fear-driven domination. A brief Sounds True announcement closes Session 2 and cues Session 3.

## Full Verbatim Transcript

**Alan Watts:**

[00:00] What I think, you know, awakening really involves is a reexamination of our common sense. We've got all sorts of ideas built into us, which seem unquested, obvious, and our speech reflects the commonest phrases. Face the facts as if they were outside you, as if life was something you simply encountered as a foreigner.

[00:48] Face the facts. Our common sense has been rigged. You see, so that we feel strangers and aliens in this world, and this is terribly plausible, simply because it's what we're used to. That's the only reason. But when you really start questioning this, say, is that the way I have to assume life is? I know everybody does, but does that make it true?

[01:27] It doesn't necessarily. It ain't necessarily so. And so then you, as you question this basic assumption, that underlies our culture, you find you get a new kind of common sense. It becomes absolutely obvious to you that you are continuous with the universe. For example, people used to believe that the people who lived in the Antipodes would fall off, and that was scary.

[02:00] But then when somebody sailed around the world, and we all got used to it, and now we travel around in jet planes and everything, we have no problem about feeling that the Earth is globular. None whatever. We got used to it. So in the same way, Einstein's relativity theory is the curvature of the propagation of light. That began to bother people when Einstein started talking like that, but now we're all used to it. Well, in a few years, it will be a matter of common sense to very many people that they're one with the universe.

[02:33] It'll be so simple. And then maybe, if that happens, we shall be in a position to handle our technology with more sense, with love, instead of with hate, for our environment.
