### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Exploring Zen's famous riddle—'Why did the bearded barbarian come from the West?'—Watts insists like Bodhidharma that he has nothing extra to sell ('like picking your pocket and selling you your own watch'). Tracing Qingyuan's three-stage Arc (mountains are mountains -> not mountains -> mountains are mountains again) and *mushin*/*munen* (thinking intellectually *without* being hypnotized by names like 'fan'), he surveys classic direct-pointing episodes: a master trapping Watts over washbasin drain-swirls ('tuk-tuk-tuk!'); Bodhidharma telling Emperor Wu 'no merit' and 'I don't know'; Dōgen returning from China observing 'eyes horizontal, nose perpendicular' before writing the massive *Shōbōgenzō*; Bokuju advising 'dress and eat'; a modern teacher asking 'Where are your footprints?'; and Jōshū declaring 'Everyday mind is the Way—when you try to accord, you deviate.'

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Opening with the classic Zen inquiry—why did fiery-eyed, bushy-bearded Bodhidharma trek from India to China if he claimed to teach nothing?—Watts laughs that trying to preach enlightenment would be like picking a listener's pocket and selling them their own watch. Instead, Zen works like the celebrated progression: first mountains are naively separate objects ('mountains are mountains'); under scientific and philosophical dissection, isolated things dissolve into empty relativity ('mountains are no longer mountains'); at final awakening, form stands luminous right here again ('mountains are mountains').

Through rapid-fire examples of 'direct pointing,' Watts clears up a pervasive myth about *mushin* ('no-mind') and *munen* ('no-thought'): Zen never promotes mindless anti-intellectual stupor. Rather, it frees thinking from hypnosis—remembering that the sound 'fan' neither cools you nor forbids using a folded paper fan as a back-scratcher. From a Japanese teacher playfully ambushing Watts over sink-drain water ('tuk-tuk-tuk!'), to Bodhidharma shattering Emperor Wu's merit-collecting piety ('Vast emptiness, nothing holy' / 'I don't know!'), to Dōgen bringing back nothing fancier than 'eyes horizontal, nose perpendicular' (then cheekily authoring the massive *Shōbōgenzō*!), to Bokuju's 'put on your clothes and eat,' a modern master's 'Where are your footprints?', and Jōshū's 'Everyday mind is the Way,' every arrow aims directly at the balloon hanging unnoticed right in front of our noses.
