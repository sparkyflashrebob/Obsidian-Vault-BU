### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Unpacking classical Hindu *sādhana* and *yoga* down to Sanskrit root *yuj* (cognate with Latin *jungere*, English 'junction,' 'union,' and 'yoke'), Watts contrasts strict meaning—where *yoga* signifies realized oneness of *jīvātman* (ego) with *Ātman/Brahman*—against popular usage turning *yoga* into strenuous workouts aimed at earning future union. Because striving implies absence, chasing the Self endlessly pushes reality away! Watts seals this with Ch'an master Nanyue's timeless rebuttal to young Mazu sitting stiffly in meditation: finding the student meditating 'to become a Buddha,' the teacher grabs a brick and scrubs it briskly—explaining calmly that no amount of brick-rubbing yields a mirror, just as no amount of forced *zazen* manufactures Buddha-nature.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Focusing directly on Sanskrit foundations, Watts tracks *sādhana* (whole-life practice) and *yoga* straight back to their Indo-European root *yuj*—meaning to join or yoke oxen, cousin to Latin *jungere*, 'junction,' and 'union.' In purest philosophical rigor, *yoga* names not an exhausting obstacle course, but the accomplished state itself wherein *jīvātman* (apparent separate soul) realizes identity with supreme *Ātman-Brahman*; only secondary custom demoted *yogi* to mean a panting traveler chasing future union.

Yet chasing union embeds one fatal flaw: all frantic seeking assumes union is absent right now, turning discipline itself into an endless deferral strategy! To cut through that delay, Watts retells Zen's famously irreverent brick-polishing tale—quietly unpopular in formal Japanese halls today! Seeing an earnest disciple cross-legged striving 'to become a Buddha,' the old master picks up a common clay tile or brick and scrapes away furiously. When the confused monk objects that scrubbing bricks never yields a glass mirror, the master smiles right back: neither will tense *zazen* create what you already are.

[00:00] In Hindu traditions, the realization of who you really are is called basically sadhana. And sadhana means the discipline, the way of life that is necessary to follow in order to escape from the illusion that you are merely an in skin encapsulated ego. And sadhana comprises yoga, from the root, yuj, which means to join. And so from that, in Latin, we get jungere to join, and in English, junction, and also yoke.

[00:52] And junction is also the word union, you see. All this derives from the Sanskrit root, yuj. A yoke is also a discipline. When you yoke oxen, that is a kind of a discipline. Now strictly speaking, in the very strictly sense, yoga means the state of union, the state in which the individual self, what is called the jīvātman, jīvātman is approximately translatable as ego.

[01:41] Jivaatman finds that it is ultimately atman, which equals Brahman, the Supreme Self. So yoga is the state, the strictest meaning of yoga is the state of union, and a yogi means one who has realized that union. But we find that the word is not normally used in that way, in that strict sense. Yoga in the normal way of use means the practice of meditation, whereby one comes into the state of union, and the yogi means one who is a traveler, a seeker, who is on the way to that point.

[02:42] But again, strictly speaking, there is no method to arrive at the place where you are, and no amount of searching will uncover the self, because all searching implies the absence of the self, the big self, the self with a capital S. So that to seek it is to thrust it away, and to practice a discipline to attain it is to postpone realizing.

[03:18] There is a famous Zen story told of a monk who was sitting in meditation, and the master came along and said, what are you doing? He said, I'm meditating to become a Buddha, where upon the master picked up a brick that was lying nearby and started polishing it, rubbing it. And the monk said, what are you doing?

[03:46] He said, I'm rubbing this brick to make it a mirror. He said, by no amount of rubbing, could you ever make a brick into a mirror? The master replied, by no amount of Zazen, could you become a Buddha? Zazen means sitting meditation. They react very badly to the story in modern day Japan.
