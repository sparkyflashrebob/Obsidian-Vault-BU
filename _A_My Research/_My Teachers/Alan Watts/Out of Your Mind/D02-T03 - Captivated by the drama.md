### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Through a vivid thought experiment—gaining unlimited power to dream 75-year lifetimes every night—Watts shows why anyone who starts with endless pleasure would soon crave unpredictability, danger, and eventually dream the exact uncertain human life they are living today. Drawing on *per sona* (the ancient theater mask), he shows that 'being a real person' literally means 'being a genuine fake.' Rather than blaming parents as reluctant authors of an unwanted birth, viewing yourself as choosing to enter this comedy or tragedy dissolves victimhood and restores cosmic zest.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Developing his dramatic worldview, Watts invites listeners into an unforgettable thought experiment: imagine you could dream anything you wished each night, packing 75 years of subjective time into a single night's sleep. Naturally you would start with bliss, palaces, and every fulfilled desire. Yet after several nights of total predictability, boredom sets in—you would ask for a dream out of control, full of risk, close shaves, and surprises, spiraling outward into stranger gambles until finally you dreamed the exact precarious human life you are living right now.

Just as skilled theater actors try to make the audience forget they paid tickets and wept over fiction, cosmic awareness plays its stage parts with breathtaking conviction. Unpacking the Latin *per sona* ('that through which sound comes'—the megaphone mask worn in Greco-Roman amphitheaters), Watts delights in pointing out that 'a real person' is a contradiction in terms unless read as a marvelous masquerade, with an off-stage 'green room' backstage where masks come off. Instead of resenting existence as a trap sprung by parents' desires, imagining *yourself* as the original gleam entering existence reframes even suffering as fearless improvisation—so much so that recognizing non-bliss contrasts bliss itself, like stopping hammering yourself on the head so you can feel how marvelous relief is.
