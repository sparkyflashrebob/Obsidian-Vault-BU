### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Watts exposes a quiet logical trick hidden in hard-nosed materialism: treating nothingness after death as scary and final while completely ignoring the identical nothingness *before birth* out of which our entire lives burst forth! Quoting poets on transience—turned ashes and the deserted banquet hall strewn with crumpled napkins—alongside physicists' heat-death models, he flips bleakness inside out: if everything dissolves into nothingness, then everything *comes from* nothingness! Recounting the Sixth Patriarch Huìnéng's Zen poetry contest—answering Shenxiu's dust-covered mirror with *'There is no mirror... where can dust collect?'*—Watts contrasts dead blank apathy with living cosmic space holding sun, moon, stars, and all creatures.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Continuing from *is* and *isn't*—where transience makes living forms poignant rather than trivial—Watts spots how intimidating materialists weaponize 'when you're dead, you're dead!' By casting nothingness as utterly powerless and dismal, hard-nosed skeptics conceal a glaring asymmetry: what about the boundless nothingness before we were born? Nothingness is demonstrably capable of blossoming into stars, music, and consciousness—just as a hole sits invisibly at the center of reality even for an imaginary creature with spherical 360-degree eyes.

Touching classic poetry—Omar Khayyám's snow upon the desert and the lonely banquet hall of overturned glasses and fading echoes—plus thermodynamic heat-death physics, Watts asks cheerfully: why suppose entropy proves reality ends poorly rather than revealing what fertile ground birth springs from? He seals the point with the legendary Zen succession story of the Sixth Patriarch Huìnéng (*Huineng*). While senior rival Shenxiu wrote of constantly polishing the mind-mirror free of dust, Huìnéng exposed the deeper truth: since reality's root is intrinsically clear void (*'pure'*, not puritanically scrubbed, simply boundless like empty space), no mirror exists for dust to soil—and genuine void is never cold indifference, but open sky holding saints, scoundrels, rivers, mountains, and insects all together.
