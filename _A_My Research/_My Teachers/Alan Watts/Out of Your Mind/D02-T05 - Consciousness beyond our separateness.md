### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Building on the dramatic model (*Ātman* as the hidden cosmic actor, carried as a quiet German *Hintergedanke* in the back of the mind), Watts probes how we define 'I.' When you decide to open your hand, do you first decide to decide? Even breathing straddles voluntary control and spontaneous flow. Like a centipede walking smoothly on a hundred legs until forced to analyze them, our body runs glands, circulation, and bone-growth 'superconsciously'—revealing specialized conscious attention as merely a narrow spotlight inside vast, unbroken cosmic awareness.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Using Paul Tillich's phrase 'ground of being' alongside the German word *Hintergedanke* (a quiet thought tucked far in the back of the mind), Watts diagnoses why we feel disconnected from the universe: we draw an arbitrary fence around a handful of 'voluntary' decisions and exile everything else—breathing, heartbeat, thyroid chemistry, bone growth, and digestion—into 'things that happen to me.' Yet ask yourself how you decide to open your hand: you don't decide to decide; deciding itself erupts spontaneously, and breathing glides seamlessly back and forth across the line of conscious effort.

Instead of belittling somatic intelligence as dead 'unconscious' machinery, Watts urges renaming it **superconscious** wisdom. If you had to flip every neural switch manually every morning, you'd freeze like the proverb's centipede tangled in its own legs. Conscious attention resembles a narrow flashlight beam: when you glance across a room, broad awareness registers far more—every texture, dress, and shadow—than deliberate attention notices (as post-hypnotic recall proves). Seeing that circulating blood and shining suns share one seamless energy field restores awe for the superconscious genius already running our lives.
