### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Returning to weaving's over-and-under threadwork and the Chinese character for human (*rén*, two mutually supporting sticks), Watts proves rigorously that existence equals relationship. A single ball suspended in infinite void cannot move—motion emerges only between two (and 'direction' becomes democratic only with three!). Likewise, a falling tree only generates *sound* alongside an eardrum or microphone, and solar rays cross pitch-black space invisibly until meeting a reflective planet. Wrapping into the Taoist *yin-yang* fishes—spiraling like galaxies and lovers' embraces—Watts shows how apparent opposites curl inward into secret unity.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Exploring the physical foundation of *The Web of Life*, Watts unpacks how woven cloth coheres strictly because warp and weft pass alternately over and under—mirroring Japanese folk etymology of the character for person (*rén*), pictured as two leaning sticks holding each other upright. Extending this into pure relativity physics, he walks through one, two, and three balls floating in endless space: one lone sphere possesses zero velocity; adding a second creates pure relative motion with no privileged observer; adding a third establishes democratic consensus.

Answering the ancient Zen-like classroom chestnut—*'If a tree falls in a forest with nobody around, is there noise?'*—Watts distinguishes air vibrations from **noise**, which exists solely in relationship between vibrating air and an eardrum (or microphone). Even stars shine dark through empty vacuum until light-waves encounter planetary dust or retinas! Uniting physics with Taoism, he unfolds the **yin-yang circle** (*taijitu*): twin black and white comma-fishes each carrying the opposite seed-eye, spiraling dynamically in a **helix**—echoing grand spiral galaxies, DNA, and human lovemaking where two partners discover they are secretly one.
