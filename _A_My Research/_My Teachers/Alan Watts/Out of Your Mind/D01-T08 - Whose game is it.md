### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Watts links the ceramic model's monarch-God to ancient Tigris-Euphrates ziggurats, Hammurabi's codes, and Akhenaten's monotheism, noting the irony that politically democratic Americans still imagine the universe as an autocratic monarchy. That same hierarchical habit makes us picture an 'ego-governor' perched behind our eyes running the body. Using the brain-versus-stomach paradox and the classic fable of body limbs striking against the stomach, Watts asks whether reality could be an organism—governed from within by mutual interdependence rather than dominated from above.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Revisiting why the ceramic myth compels gratitude-on-probation—like guilt-tripping parents demanding obedience from a 'beatnik' child—Watts traces its historical DNA to Near Eastern empires: Hammurabi's Chaldean codes, Babylonian ziggurats, and Pharaoh Akhenaten (Amenhotep IV, whom Freud linked to Mosaic monotheism). All cast order as a pyramid tapering up to one supreme Boss. Crucially, this theological pyramid duplicates inside individual psychology: we imagine a tiny ego-governor sitting between the ears and behind the eyes issuing commands to the flesh, echoing the psalmist's cry, *'O Lord our governor.'*

Watts highlights the bizarre contradiction in modern life: citizens proudly champion self-governing democracy on Earth while insisting the universe cannot run without a cosmic monarch! Breaking free from the false choice between *machine with a driver* and *driverless clockwork*, Watts proposes a third possibility: **an organism**. Inside a living body, there is no boss. Did the brain evolve the stomach to keep itself fed, or did the stomach evolve the brain to hunt better food? Neither rules the other—recalling the ancient fable where hands, feet, and mouth staged a labor strike against the 'lazy' stomach, only to grow faint together until recognizing they form one mutual organism.

[04:35] And an organism is a thing that governs itself. In your body, there is no boss. You can say, you can argue, for example, that the brain is a gadget evolved by the stomach in order to serve the stomach for the purposes of getting food. [05:07] It doesn't make, actually, they are mutual. The brain implies the stomach, the stomach implies the brain, and neither of them is the boss. So there is the possibility then that we are not in the kind of system that these two myths delineate, that we are not living in a world where we ourselves, in the deepest sense of self, are outside reality and somehow in a position that we have to bow down to it and say as a great favor, please preserve us in existence, nor are we in a system which is merely mechanical and in which we are nothing but flukes trapped in the electrical wiring of a nervous system, which is fundamentally rather inefficiently arranged.
