### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Looking at an ordinary paper cup, Watts shows that perception is already radiant revelation: without eyes, ears, and soft skin, the universe holds neither brightness, sound, nor hardness—your organism literally evokes the cosmos out of silent quanta. Because ordinary consciousness already *is* the divine state, chasing yoga or 'letting go' to conquer desire merely deepens the split, trapping seekers in Christian or spiritual double binds ('you must love/be humble on purpose'). Like Bodhidharma asking Huike to bring forth his troubled mind, or walking due west to cure a flat-earther, rigorous practice either pushes folly to collapse or reveals emptiness right now.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Dismantling the myth that divine brilliance hides behind a veil, Watts insists your ordinary perception of an everyday paper cup *is* the cosmic radiance—its intensity only finely pixelated so ten thousand suns don't blind your retinas! More remarkably still, sensory properties don't sit out in dead objects waiting to hit us: our eyes evoke light from radiation, soft skin evokes hardness from timber, and eardrums evoke music from quivering air. Far from being negligible specks on a minor planet, living organisms bring the universe into vibrant color, texture, and self-awareness.

If ordinary seeing is already undivided miracle, why do spiritual traditions teach meditation, desirelessness, or humility? Watts explains with sharp irony: telling someone to 'give up desire' opens a dialogue rather than closing one, for seekers immediately reply, *'Yes, but now I am desiring not to desire!'* Commandments like *'Thou shalt love'* or *'Stop being selfish'* construct painful double binds—as impossible as refusing to think of a green elephant while swallowing medicine. Great gurus use *reductio ad absurdum*: pushing you to hunt for your supposed ego-problem until, like the monk begging Bodhidharma to pacify his mind (*'Bring it out here before me'*), you discover there is no isolated troublemaker inside—or like sailing straight due west along one latitude until the flat-earther steps back onto his starting dock.

## Stories, Analogies & Anecdotes Offered

- **Ten Thousand Suns Inside a Paper Cup**: Staring deeply at a disposable cup reveals the very same brilliance as cosmic light—filtered softly through retinal cells so it won't blow out your eyes.
- **Not Thinking of a Green Elephant**: Ordering yourself to stop wanting or force sincere humility backfires just like taking medicine under orders never to picture a green elephant.
- **Bodhidharma Pacifying the Monk's Mind**: To the anxious disciple begging 'Please pacify my mind,' Bodhidharma replies: 'Bring it out here before me!'—when it cannot be found, it is already pacified.
- **Walking Due West Around a Flat Earth**: Since nobody gets talked out of an illusion, the only remedy for a flat-earther is marching straight west along one latitude until landing back home.
