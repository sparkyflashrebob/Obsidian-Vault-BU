### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Comparing resistance to drowning resistance against a river—where swimming *with* the stream leaves you miraculously calm inside rapid current—Watts turns squarely toward our sharpest fear: going over the final waterfall of death. Not preaching grim stoic grit, he grounds peace in physics and form: waves cannot sport crests without troughs, nor can an organism exist half-alive without dying. Zooming into molecules floating like separated tennis balls moving in unison like Sausalito mudflat sandpipers turning mid-flight with zero leader, Watts reveals space as seamless connectedness rather than bleak nothingness. Embracing *memento mori* and Gurdjieff's teaching that every acquaintance will soon die becomes not morbidity, but total release from futile self-clutching.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Moving straight into humanity's deepest taboo, Watts illustrates non-resistance hydraulically: fighting a river slams water noisily against your ribs, whereas drifting smoothly *with* the current creates stillness right inside rush-hour speed. That exact principle governs dying. Rather than telling anxious minds 'you *ought* to muster artificial courage,' Watts dismantles death-terror philosophically: an ocean wave possessing crests minus troughs is physically impossible, and a 'half-human' claiming birth without disappearance makes equal nonsense.

Magnifying his own hand until atoms resemble tennis balls suspended in spaciousness—yet swinging effortlessly as one hand, much like flocks of local sandpipers exploding skyward from mudflats and banking cleanly together without an elected bird-general—Watts shows space operates as unifying bond rather than hostile gap. Letting go of held breath (*Nirvāṇa*) ends compulsive attempts to freeze one's form like museum statues. Thus ancient *memento mori* wisdom and Gurdjieff's sober counsel—seeing every person walking the street as already short-lived—prove supremely freeing: since you literally *cannot* hang onto yourself anyway, liberation arrives already accomplished.
