### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Surveying Patañjali's *Yoga Sūtras*, Watts highlights its crucial opening prerequisite ('Now yoga is explained'—assuming candidates are already civilized in *artha*, *kāma*, and *dharma*, since nobody teaches yoga to baboons!) before defining *yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*: calming mind-ripples so the reflecting pool shines clear. Outlining classical stages (*prāṇāyāma*, *pratyāhāra*, *dhāraṇā*, *dhyāna*, *samādhi*) and guiding listeners through gazing out-of-focus then deep inside a bright copper candle-reflection, Watts exposes the guru's subtle ambush: once asked *why* you practice, admitting you want to escape pain reveals your whole practice rests on fear! Chasing an unselfish 'pure motive' traps mind in what Sanskrit calls *mūḍha* ('crazily stuck' like picking feathers off molasses-coated fingers or racing the Red Queen's squirrel cage in *Through the Looking-Glass*)—a relentless double-bind he credits Jiddu Krishnamurti with wielding masterfully.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Examining Patañjali's foundational *Yoga Sūtras*, Watts points out a witty prerequisite hidden inside its very first word (*'Now yoga is explained'*): serious practice begins only *after* a candidate has matured through worldly citizenship (*artha*), aesthetic sensitivity (*kāma*), and social integrity (*dharma*). From there Patañjali defines *yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*: quieting agitated ripples upon *citta* (consciousness as a mirror-like pool) via voluntary/involuntary breath work (*prāṇāyāma*), sense-withdrawal (*pratyāhāra*), intense focus (*dhāraṇā*), unbroken union (*dhyāna*—source of Zen!), and absorption (*samādhi*). Watts illustrates *dhāraṇā* practically with a bright specular highlight on polished brass or glass: blurring focus first reveals delicate light rings before narrowing crisp gaze down a glowing optical tunnel.

Right at that intoxicating peak, however, the discerning guru springs his devastating test: *'Why do you practice so fervently?'* Confessing hope to escape daily anguish immediately exposes the motive as trembling fear! Trying then to manufacture a pure, selfless motive ('I must repress selfish desires!') triggers a fresh trap: suppressing bad impulses strictly in order to become spiritually victorious leaves the original selfish ambition disguised underneath. Caught in *mūḍha*—tangled like hands coated in sticky molasses and feathers, sprinting faster inside Lewis Carroll's Red Queen treadmill to win stage sixty-four out of sixty-four—the seeker finally hits bedrock realization: egoic willpower cannot unlock its own cell, precisely the razor-sharp method deployed by Jiddu Krishnamurti.
