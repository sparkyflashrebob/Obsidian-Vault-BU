### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Explaining why Hindu cosmic play (*līlā*) jars Western minds, Watts points out two deep cultural divides: Western art never depicts a smiling Christ (casting God in grim tragic severity), and St. Augustine cemented *linear time* ('once for all' salvation climbing from Alpha to Omega) against ancient Hindu and Greek cyclic time—where Hindus care so little for chronology that scribes calmly swap old kings' names for contemporary ones so stories stay timeless archetypes! Critiquing naive linear utopianism (reminding us children love building sandcastles just to smash them—and humans harbor terrifying destructive fury alongside beauty, symbolized unflinchingly by Śiva-Kālī), Watts solves the riddle of eternal boredom: 'Oh Brahma, don't you ever get tired?' 'No—because Brahma doesn't remember anything!' Only sticky memory grows jaded; realizing cosmic cycles go *nowhere* slows down time so we finally hear music and blink open in astonishment right here and now.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Wrapping up Session 9, Watts pinpoints why Western audiences squirm at cosmic play: Christian iconography almost never paints a laughing Christ—always a solemn visage hinting 'we need a serious talk.' Deeper still lies a clash over time itself. St. Augustine rejected classical cyclic epochs out of anxiety that cosmic repetition would require crucifying Christ repeatedly, locking Western imagination inside strictly *linear* Jewish-Christian history racing toward some terminal Utopia. Indian tradition cares so little for linear dates that chroniclers happily update old king names in classic myths so tales stay living archetypes rather than dry history.

Linear progress breeds naive faith that humanity marches inevitably toward spotless perfection—ignoring the restless urge in every child who smashes toys purely to shout 'Look, let's start again!', and the terrible shadow later revealed in modern atrocities and ecological waste. Hindu realism faces splendor and terror together under *Śiva-Kālī*: love and destruction dancing as two faces of one vortex. When Hindus ask in exhaustion whether endless cosmic cycles (*per omnia saecula saeculorum*) leave Brahma bored to tears, wisdom laughs: Brahma feels zero tedium because Brahma accumulates no stale memories! Seeing life as music going nowhere invites you to stop hurrying the tempo—widening eyes in quiet astonishment at the detail shining right now.
