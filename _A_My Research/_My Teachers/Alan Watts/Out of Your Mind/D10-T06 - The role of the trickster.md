### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Asking the final playful riddle—how can someone trapped in skin-bound illusion travel to 'where you already are'?—Watts explains why teachers (*gurus* of every stripe) act as beneficent tricksters: it is impossible to plan a genuine surprise for yourself! Distinguishing formal 'square gurus' (like respected Kyoto abbot Oda Rōshi at Daitoku-ji) from unpredictable 'beat' or 'rascal gurus' (like Gurdjieff), plus non-human teachers—situations, books, crises, and friends—Watts sketches a hilarious short story: a potential yogi sits across from an ordinary insurance salesman in an Automat diner, worships the man's bewildered denials ('isn't he modest!'), meets him every day for lunch, and ironically gets genuinely enlightened by his own projected awe while the salesman hasn't a clue what happened!

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Turning to the practical puzzle of Session 10—how an individual convinced he is sealed inside his skin ever wakes to *Brahman*—Watts smiles at the paradox of taking a long expedition strictly to discover the chair you never left. After wandering through exotic disciplines only to exhaust fake paths, you circle home at last. Along that road, authentic *dharma* relies on beneficent tricksters because deliberate self-surprise is an impossibility.

Surveying the guru spectrum—from orthodox, orderly 'square gurus' like Daitoku-ji's respected Oda Rōshi down to provocative 'rascal' teachers like George Gurdjieff, alongside life-crises, books, and subtle friendships—Watts shares his favorite unwritten comedy sketch. A hungry guru-hunter strikes up conversation in an Automat cafeteria with a puzzled stranger, insisting the man radiates deep hidden realization. When the stranger honestly protests, 'Actually I sell insurance!', the seeker gasps in reverence, *'Look how astonishingly humble the master is!'* Day after day at the lunch counter, the bewildered salesman unintentionally triggers the seeker's genuine breakthrough—proving that the teacher's sole art is mirroring back what the seeker already holds.

## Full Verbatim Transcript

**Alan Watts:**

[00:00] And so finally we come to the last consideration, which is the question, in what way and by what means, can an individual who is under the impression that he is a separate individual, limited by and enclosed in his bag of skin, how can such a person effectively realize that he is deep down, the universal self, the Brahman.

[00:39] This, of course, is a curious question. It proposes a journey to the place where you already are. Now it's true that you may not know that you are there, but you are. And if you take a journey to the place where you are, you will visit many other places than the place where you are. And perhaps when you find through some long experience that all the places you go to are not the place you want to define, it may occur to you that you were already there in the beginning.

[01:26] And that is the dharma or method, as I translated that word, which all gurus, teachers of spiritual development, use fundamental.

[01:49] They are all of them tricksters, but in the most beneficent sense of the word trickster. Why trickster? Because do you know it's terribly difficult, in fact it's impossible to surprise yourself on purpose. And yet to be surprised is a great thing, but you can't plan a surprise for yourself. Somebody else can do it for you. And that is why so often a guru or teacher is necessary in this process.

[02:34] But let me say right from the start that a guru, there are many kinds of gurus. First of all, among human gurus, there are square gurus and there are beat gurus. There are gurus like, well, let's say a great Zen master today. Let's take Oda Rōshi at Daitoku-ji, who is a square guru and a very good one.

[03:09] But you go through regular channels. Then there is a guru like Mr. Gurdjieff, who is a rascal guru, who leads you in by means that are very, very strange indeed. Then there are gurus that are not people. The gurus may be situations, a certain kind of problem or encounter. Even a book can to some extent be a guru.

[03:42] A friend can be a guru. I've often thought of writing a story about a man who is some sort of guru seeker and potential yogi, who goes one day into an automat and sits down at a table where there is another fellow, and he sort of thinks that this man looks wise. And he projects onto him the idea that he is a guru.

[04:08] And he says, I feel there is something special about you. And the man says, oh, really? Well, actually, there is nothing special about me. I happen to be an insurance salesman. And this other fellow says, isn't that fascinating how modest he is? And then I want to develop this story step by step. They keep meeting each other because they both eat at the same automat regularly for lunch.

[04:38] And although the fellow really is an insurance salesman and doesn't know a thing about these things, it in the end results in the enlightenment of the person who projected this image upon him. So there are, as I say, many kinds of guru. But the problem of the guru is to show the enquirer in some effective way that he already has what he's looking for.
