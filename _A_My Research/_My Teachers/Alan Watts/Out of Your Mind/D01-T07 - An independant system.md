### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Rebutting the delusion of isolated egos trapped inside skin, Watts shows that no behavior can even be described without including the environment: walking implies a floor, and speaking implies listeners. Citing a rabbi's paradox and leaning sticks, he demonstrates that self and other co-create one another. Like a recognizable whirlpool through which stream water constantly rushes, your body is a temporary pattern through which food, light, and cosmic energy endlessly flow—inviting us to feel ourselves as the universe itself before collective fear leads to self-destruction.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Watts returns to dismantle the popular American delusion that the fully automatic model is somehow more 'scientific' than a bearded sky-father. In reality, love, beauty, and intelligence appear inside human beings, proving that the universe itself is capable of generating them—just as apples reveal the nature of an apple tree and geologic balance reveals the galaxy. The Earth is no dead rock infested with organisms, any more than a skeleton is dead bones infested with living cells.

Turning to rigorous behavioral science, Watts points out that no living organism's action happens inside the skin alone. You cannot describe someone walking without describing the floor, nor Watts lecturing without describing the listening room. Self and environment form *one* interdependent system—illustrated both by a wise rabbi's reciprocity riddle (*'if I am I because you are you...'*) and two leaning sticks holding each other upright (*pratītyasamutpāda* / mutual dependence). Your skin does not lock you away from reality; it acts as a living bridge. Using the vivid analogy of a **whirlpool in a stream**, Watts shows how we recognize a friend day after day not because static water remains trapped inside them, but because the cosmos keeps spinning the same recognizable vortex out of flowing food, sunlight, air, and cosmic rays. Until humanity feels this continuity emotionally rather than intellectually, Watts warns, skin-encapsulated paranoia risks planetary suicide via hydrogen bombs.

## Stories, Analogies & Anecdotes Offered

- **Two Leaning Sticks**: Watts illustrates interdependence with two sticks propped against each other: each stands solely by supporting the other; pull one away and both fall.
- **The Wise Rabbi's Riddle**: Quoting a Hasidic/rabbinic paradox (*'If I am I because you are you, and you are you because I am I, then I am not I and you are not you'*), Watts dissolves solitary ego-identity.
- **The Whirlpool in the River**: Like recognizing a familiar whirlpool near a riverbank even though fresh water constantly rushes through it, human bodies are enduring patterns spun by a continuously flowing cosmos.
