### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Watts traces the ceramic model to ancient Near Eastern monarchies, showing how Catholic basilicas (royal court architecture) and Protestant sanctuaries (judicial courtrooms) dramatize a cosmic King or Judge ruling inert matter. Yet modern physics abandoned the search for basic 'stuff' long ago: zoomed into clear focus, subatomic 'matter' turns out to be rhythmic form, behavior, and self-designing pattern—closer to a dance than to clay.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Deepening his analysis of the ceramic model, Watts shows that the artisan God is simultaneously modeled on an ancient absolute monarch: *'King of kings, Lord of lords, the only ruler of princes'* from the Book of Common Prayer. Drawing a sharp political analogy, he notes why a ruler sits with his back to the wall flanked by henchmen—because the king is the strongest competitor in a fearful hierarchy—and traces how church architecture reproduces these power symbols. Traditional Catholic basilicas (*basileus*, 'king') place the altar-throne against the east wall with priests acting as royal viziers conducting Byzantine court etiquette, while Protestant churches mirror law courts complete with black-robed judge-ministers and jury-box pews.

Crucially, this political-craft cosmology forces a split between inert *matter* and commanding *spirit*. Yet Watts points out that physics itself undermined that split generations ago. Whenever scientists tried to isolate fundamental 'stuff,' they could only describe behavior, shape, and movement—atoms, electrons, protons, and sub-nuclear resonances. 'Stuff,' Watts quips, is simply what the world looks like when our eyes are out of focus. Once brought into sharp focus, solid clay vanishes into self-moving, self-designing *pattern*—a cosmic dance whose reality our slow everyday common sense has not yet caught up with.
