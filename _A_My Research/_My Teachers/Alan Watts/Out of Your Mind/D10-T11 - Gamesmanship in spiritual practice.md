### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Turning a humorous eye on inter-religious competition, Watts traces how psychoanalysis, Roman Catholicism, missionary Buddhism, and Hindu schools each flatter visitors briefly before hinting that *they* possess one subtler secret degree missing elsewhere. Sometimes mere vanity, this game also functions as a master's deliberate *upāya* test—measuring whether you have the nerve to stand right where you are rather than endlessly chasing higher rungs like Freemasonry's 33 degrees. Comparing gradual stage-counting to Zeno's puzzle of Achilles and the tortoise, Watts observes that infinite mathematical subdivisions never prevent Achilles from rushing right past; only endless mental calculations postpone sudden, instant seeing.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Watts skewers the subtle hierarchy games pervasive across psychological and religious movements. A traveler leaves psychoanalysis only for Catholics to sigh politely that therapy stops shallow of grace; next, a Buddhist group gently grades Catholicism as elementary morality lacking serious meditation; finally, a Hindu sage smiles indulgently at Buddhist emptiness while claiming something even higher awaits beyond. Whether peddled as genuine guru fraud or wielded playfully by masters as *upāya* (skillful testing), the lure works only when listeners suspect something lacks inside themselves and crave yet another 'inner circle.'

Like 33rd-degree Freemasonry or esoteric orders endlessly splitting initiation ranks, stage-hunting turns awakening into an infinite staircase. Invoking Zeno's paradox—Achilles seemingly unable to catch the tortoise if we halve the remaining gap forever—Watts points out that Achilles overtakes the tortoise effortlessly in fact; *only our calculating divisions* postpone arrival. Whether after thirty years of disciplined sitting or three minutes, realization never arrives piecemeal by accumulation: it dawns suddenly when calculation stops.
