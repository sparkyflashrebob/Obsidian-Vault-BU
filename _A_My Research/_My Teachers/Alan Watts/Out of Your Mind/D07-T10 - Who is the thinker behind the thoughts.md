### Condensed Version of the Teachings

Wrapping up Session 7, Watts sweeps away centuries of substance-versus-form metaphysics: since nobody ever saw shapeless clay or disembodied geometry, 'form' and 'stuff' name a single ongoing stream—experiencing needs no separate experiencer underneath. Better yet, notice mutual interdependence directly: inside *your* experience I appear as a passing external character, while inside *mine* you appear as my outside world—meaning 'internal' and 'external' share one common world. Turning toward why Zen uses loud belly-shouts of *Mu* rather than polite salon chatter, Watts notes that raw unfiltered candor in ordinary cocktail parties ('never telling white lies') causes social chaos; thus Zen builds a dedicated monastic enclosure where students train unhesitating spontaneity until competent in any setting.

### Expanded Version of the Teachings

Resolving Session 7's critique of *tabula rasa*, Watts asks two disarming questions: what does pure matter look like stripped of all shape, and what does pure shape look like devoid of substance? Because neither has ever been witnessed apart from the other, separating thinker from thought—or experience from an experiencer enduring the storm—is superfluous linguistic clutter. Even 'inside me' versus 'outside world' collapses under shared inspection: I stand visibly inside *your* external scenery at the very moment you stand inside *mine*, weaving us cleanly into one shared world-process.

How then do teachers push students past theoretical agreement into full-bodied realization of who thinks behind thoughts? One vivid training tool demands standing before the master and bellowing *Mu* straight from the belly—not testing whether one's shout sounds 'authentic' or 'fake' in the throat, but jumping wholly into action without split calculation. Because practicing brutal, unfiltered spontaneity at ordinary dinner parties would shred polite social fabric, Zen established protected monastic training grounds where candidates learn effortless directness safely before walking back out into public life.

## Full Verbatim Transcript

**Alan Watts:**

[00:00] What is stuff like apart from form? What is form like apart from stuff? All those problems which have bothered people for centuries are based on asking the question in the wrong way, on having used the wrong image for the process. Actually, since nobody ever saw a piece of shapeless stuff, and nobody ever saw a piece of stuffless shape, the whole thing really is saying that they are the same.

[00:33] There isn't any necessity even to think of a difference between them, even the contrasting words, form and substance, or form and matter, are in nuisance. There is process, there is the flow of thought, and the flow of thought doesn't have to happen to anyone. Experience does not have to beat upon an experiencer. There is all the time, simply, the one stream going on, and we are convinced that we stand aside from it and observe it. Because we've been brought up that way.

[01:13] But you know, in your stream of thought and experience, I am an object, and a very fleeting and passing one, and also in my stream of experience, you also are people who come and go. We are all, you see, living in the same world. We think there is me, and there is an external world around me, but I am in your external world, and you are in my external world, and if you think about that, you see we are all in one world, going along together.

[01:53] There isn't really the internal and the external. There is simply the process. It's very important to get rid of that illusion of duality between the thinker and the thought. So find out who is the thinker behind the thoughts? Who is the real genuine you? And so one of the methods that is used is shouting. The Zen master would say to a student, now, I want to hear you. I want to hear you say the word Mu, and really mean it. Because I want to hear not just the sound, but the person who says it. Now produce me that. There is moo, and the Zen teacher says, no, no, not yet. Moo!

[02:45] And he says, it's only coming from your throat. I want to hear your belly. And always you see, it will never come. While the person is trying to make a differentiation between a true Mu and a false Mu, to act with confidence, you just do it. But since people are not used to that, it is necessary to set up protected situations in which it can be done.

[03:32] If we just in the ordinary way of social intercourse, acted without deliberation, we would get into amazing confusions. As when people say, always speak the truth. Never tell a white lie. And they say exactly what is true and what they think about other people. Well, they can raise a great deal of trouble. But the experience of Zen has been that there should be a kind of enclosure.

[04:08] In which this kind of behavior can be done until the people are expert in it and know how to apply it in all situations.
