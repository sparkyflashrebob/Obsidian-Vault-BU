## 1. Teacher Profile & Contemplative Scope

Ven. Ajahn Ñāṇiko was born in California and ordained at Abhayagiri Buddhist Monastery under Ven. Ajahn Pasanno. Currently serving as co-abbot of Abhayagiri alongside Ven. Ajahn Karuṇadhammo, his teachings are renowned for their direct, authentic warmth, humor, and practical focus on translating the monastic forest tradition into accessible tools for modern lay and monastic practitioners.

His talks emphasize cultivating spiritual endurance (*khanti*), transforming ordinary daily routines into continuous meditation halls, grounding in direct sensory experience to dismantle proliferation (*papañca*), and the courageous, trusting spirit of forest wandering (*tudong*).

## 3. Core Contemplative Pillars & Doctrinal Themes

### The Tudong Spirit & Radical Simplicity
In *Circles of Blessing: Our Redwood Tudong*, Ajahn Ñāṇiko captures the living heart of the Thai Forest dhutaṅga tradition transposed onto the coastal mountains of California. Walking for weeks without money, carrying only the alms bowl and three robes, relying entirely on the unsolicited generosity of strangers, tudong reveals the deep trustworthiness of the Dhamma and breaks the illusion of material security.

### Patient Endurance (*Khanti*) & Somatic Grounding
Ajahn Ñāṇiko consistently emphasizes that practice is not an aggressive struggle to eliminate unpleasant feelings, but the cultivation of a heart large enough to hold them without contracting. When bodily discomfort or emotional turbulence strikes, the mind learns to pause, notice physical tension, and rest in *khanti*—the supreme incinerator of defilements.
