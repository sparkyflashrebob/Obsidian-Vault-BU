## 📖 Executive Summary & Core Teachings

When meditation becomes mechanical or grim, the Buddha recommended the Ten Recollections (*Dasa Anussati*)—especially the recollection of generosity (*cāgānussati*). 

Ajahn Yatiko shares his somatic exploration of the "open palm":
* A clenched fist represents grasping, defensiveness, and fear of loss.
* An open palm turned outward represents pure relinquishment: *"This is for you; take it. I want nothing in return."*

The somatic feeling of the open hand immediately relieves constriction in the chest and belly. Generosity is not merely donating material goods; it is cultivating a heart that does not cling to its own experience.

## 📜 Full Talk Transcript

**Ajahn Yatiko:**

Today is the seventh day of our retreat. 

I lived for five years in a monastery in Thailand, and one of the practices that continually sustained my training was the Buddha's teachings on the Ten Recollections (*Dasa Anussati*): recollecting the Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha, virtue (*sīla*), generosity (*cāga*), the devas, death (*maraṇassati*), the body, the breath, and peace (*upasamānussati*). 

In the West, we often overemphasize technical analysis and forget the devotional and gladdening practices. 

Consider the recollection of generosity (*cāgānussati*). 

Think about the somatic sensation of extending an open hand. When you hold something tightly with a clenched fist, there is tension in the forearm, tension in the shoulder, and an underlying anxiety that someone will take it from you. 

Now, imagine turning your hand outward and opening the palm: *"This is for you. I want you to have this."* 

Notice what happens in your body. There is an immediate release of pressure. The chest expands; the belly softens. There is no expectation of getting anything back. 

That physical feeling of an open palm is the essence of renunciation. When we bring that open-palm attitude to our thoughts, our feelings, and our breath, clinging vanishes and peace effortlessly appears.
