## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Anandabodhi.

* **Theme**: The Four Foundations of Mindfulness (*Cattāro Satipaṭṭhānā*) as fields of receptive awareness; guarding the six sense doors (*indriya-saṃvara*); the causal sequence of contact, feeling, and craving (*Paṭiccasamuppāda*); taking refuge in the living Triple Gem.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered as an evening Dhamma discourse on Day 3 of the 10-day Angela Center October Retreat.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Ajahn Anandabodhi opens with the traditional homage to the Buddha (*Namo Tassa*), setting an atmosphere of reverent investigation. She unpacks the four 'fields' or pastures (*gocara*) of mindfulness: the body (*kāya*), feeling tones (*vedanā*), mental states (*citta*), and dhammas (*dhammānupassanā*). Mindfulness acts as a vigilant, gentle gatekeeper at the six sense doors. Sensory contact (*phassa*) inevitably produces feeling—pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. Without mindfulness, unpleasant feeling immediately triggers resistance and anger, while pleasant feeling triggers clinging. By inserting awareness at the moment of contact, the runaway train of reactivity halts. When turbulent mental states threaten to overwhelm us, we take living refuge in the Triple Gem: trusting that Buddha (pure awareness) is spacious enough to hold any condition; Dhamma (the truth of impermanence) reminds us that even the darkest mood is changing; and Sangha connects us to our innate capacity to remain steadfast, walking in the footsteps of awakened ancestors.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

1. **Pausing at Sense Contact**: When checking our phone, opening email, or walking into a challenging meeting, notice sensory impressions entering our senses. Before reacting, take one conscious breath into the belly, breaking the compulsive habit loop.

2. **Taking Living Refuge in Real Time**: When caught in acute emotional turbulence—whether anxiety about finances, health, or conflict—silently take refuge: *Awareness can hold this (Buddha); this is impermanent and changing (Dhamma); I have the inner capacity to be here (Sangha).*
