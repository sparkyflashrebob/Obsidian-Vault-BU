THE DIRECT PATH

-   MN
    

-   direct (one-going) path
    
-   purification of beings
    
-   **surmounting** sorrow and lamentation
    
-   disappearance of dukkha and discontent
    
-   **acquiring** the true method
    
-   realization of Nirvana
    

-   MA
    

-   one path
    
-   purification of beings
    
-   going beyond sorrow and fear
    
-   **eradicating** dukkha and distress
    
-   **abandoning** weeping and tears
    
-   **attaining** right dharma
    

-   EA
    

-   one-going
    
-   purification of actions of living beings
    
-   **removing** worry and sorrow
    
-   **being** without vexations
    
-   **attaining** great knowledge and wisdom
    
-   **accomplishing** the realization of Nirvana
    
-   **the 5 hindrances** should be abandoned and 4 satipatthanas should be attended to
    

  

SUMMARY of DIRECT PATH

-   Direct Path
    
-   Mahasihanada Sutta - and EN - simile of trying to get to a far away pond
    
-   Oppressed by heat and thirst (NEED TO COOL OFF AND QUENCH THIRST)
    
-   Knowing that another person has reached the pond
    
-   No direct relation to meditation
    
-   One path leads directly to the pond
    
-   Others
    

-   Converging point
    
-   unification
    
-   converging of practices
    
-   unified or integrated path
    

-   Satipatthana is one of several practices
    

-   Cultivation of the Noble 8fold Path
    

-   Not support as a converging point
    
-   Convergence is part of early texts but not in terms of Satipatthana
    

-   power of wisdom
    

-   convergence of 5 mental faculties
    

-   Wisdom  - the peak of a roof - stability of rafters
    
-   **Wisdom the converging point**
    
-   mindfulness one of the factors that converge on wisdom
    

-   Noble 8fold path leads to 10fold path of an arahant (fetters)
    

-   right knowledge and right liberation
    

-   **ALL THINGS HAVE LIBERATION AS THEIR ESSENCE**
    
-   Liberation could be considered a converging point
    
-   Also not a converging point for other paths
    
-   Not one direction only
    

-   Satipatthana has range of benefits
    

-   Body Contemplation
    

-   overcoming discontent
    
-   deep concentration
    
-   gaining insight
    
-   Anuruddha
    

-   attainments
    

-   Satipatthana lead to more than one direction
    

-   builds a foundation for developlent of insight as well as tranquillity
    

-   Satipatthana - direct path for purifying the mind and proceeding towards liberation
    

-   Mindfulness directly feeds development of insight
    
-   directly faces one's present moment experience
    
-   uncovering its various features
    
-   Directly facing one's condition right now
    

-   informs progress on the path
    
-   appropriate **attitude** that one should maintain during formal practice and in everyday life
    

-   **Facing things directly with awareness**
    

**DEFINITION OF SATIPATTHANA**

-   Mental Balance
    

-   free from desire and discontent
    
-   removing evil thoughts and being free from worry and sorrow
    

-   EN
    

-   Experiences joy in oneself
    
-   even in relation to reviewing the body/stages of decay of a corpse
    
-   also internally & externally
    
-   others in refrain
    

-   Abhidharma Theravada - **Vibhanga**
    
-   Clearly knowing
    
-   Removing desires and sorrow
    
-   Internally, Externally, both internally & externally
    
-   Mindfulness require to be established with CONTINUITY
    

-   diligent/ardent
    
-   clear knowing or clear comprehension
    
-   what is being held present in the mind thru mindfulness
    

-   **Leads to a balanced mental attitude**
    

-   **not shaken by desires, worries, or dejection in regard to the world**
    

  

**THE REFRAIN OF SATIPATTHANA**

-   after each of the individual contemplations
    
-   body,feelings, mind, dharmas
    
-   EN gives a more detailed account of what can be expected
    

-   Full awakening
    

-   MA does not mention contemplation of impermanence
    
-   Contemplation of Impermanence should be an INTEGRAL part of the practice
    
-   Mindfulness of Breathing
    
-   Breath as way of integrating all 4 Satipatthanas
    
-   Background of awareness of breath moving in and out
    
-   Continuous reminder of the impermanent nature of the breath
    

-   and impermanence for the whole of one's experience
