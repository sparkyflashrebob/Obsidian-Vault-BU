Chapter 1 - Aspects of Cultivation of Compassion

Early discourses do not offer a succinct definition of the term "compassion"
- later literature
- Similes more helpful
- describes a situation that arouses feelings of compassion to illustrate the attitude one should cultivate towards someone who immersed in unwholesomeness
	- Extended Journey on long road
	- becomes sick halfway
		- exhausted and suffering extremely
		- alone and without companion
		- A village is far away
	- Another person comes and sees this and thinks
	- "If one were to get an attendent, emerge from being far away and reach a town, were give medicine and be fed and nourished, be well cared for, then in this way this person's sickness would certainly subside."
	- So that person has extremely compassionate, sympathetic, and kind thoughts in the the mind towards this sick person"

Concern for others to be relieved from suffering and affliction.
- The vision is for the sick person to be cared for.
- Mentally dwelling on the actual suffering would be contemplation of dukkha.
- Contemplation for the meditative cultivation of compassion.
- Contemplation itself in the wish for others to be free from dukkha

Cultivation of compassion can only lead to deeper concentration if it is undertaken with a positive or even joyful mind.
- steer clear of sadness
- important to monitor ones's own response to the affliction of others
- compassion does not mean to commiserate to the extent of suffering alone with the other
- This is the "near enemy" of compassion (sadness)
- Cruelty is the "far enemy" of compassion (opposite)
- both to be avoided (middle way/equanimity)

Compassion is directly opposed for the wish for others to be harmed.
- cruelty cannot pervade a mind who practices, cultivates and comes to concentration by compassion
- this is the release of all cruelty
- one who has truely developed compassion will no longer be overwhelmed by cruelty
- meditative cultivation of compassion affect's ones' character trait
- no more broad range of unwholesome activities

Moral Conduct is an expression of compassion
- one who kills living beings lack compassion
- one who abstains from killing living beings acts with compassion
- refraining from theft is compassionate
- refraining from sexual misconduct is compassionate
- all physical activity that avoids harm of others is compassionate

Compassion and 4 Noble Truths
- teaching is compassionate
- impulse to teach others first manifests as a sense of gratitude
- 4 Noble Truths as medical diagnosis
- disease is dukkha
- pathogen is craving
- health is nirvana
- cure is Noble 8fold Path
- 4 Noble Truths relationship to wholesome states
	- elephants footprint compares to other animals
	- 4 Noble Truths comprise all wholesome states
- An expression of the Buddha's compassion
- The Noble Truths also have a direct relationship to compassion
	- One does not think to harm oneself or others
	- One thinks of benefitting oneself and others
	- Benefiting many people our of compassion for the affliction in the world
	- seeking what is meaningful and of benefit to angles and humans
	- seeking their ease and happiness
	- in this way "one is bright, intelligent, and with vast widsom."
- 4 Noble Truths and Compassion are two complementary facets of wisdom
	- penetrative & vast
- Compassionate activity should be based on the perspective of the 4 Noble Truths
	- see actual pain and affliction of others (1st)
	- conditions that have led to this (2nd)
	- conditions that can lead out of it (4th)
	- Motivating force of compassion is the wish for others to be free from pain and affliction (3rd)

Compassion and Teaching:
- request for instruction references such a teaching being given "out of compassion"
	- anukampa - compassion in action
	- karuna - meditative practice of compassion
	- both stand for complementary facets of early Buddhist notion of compassion
	- those who request and those who deliver teaching
	- not teach so that others will make offerings to oneself
	- proper motivation by the wish to benefit others
	- Others duties to provide guidance (Sariputta)/responsibility
	- Taking responsibility and reacting with compassion
		- not standing by when action should be taken
	- Meditation is compassionate
		- benefits oneself and others
		- "by going forth to tame oneself, one can indeed benefit many"
	- Teaching by example
		- does not need to be verbal
	- Dwelling in seclusion
		- "compassion for later generations"
		- Buddha set an example to be emulated
		- Motivation comprises the aspiration to benefit others
	- Regularly retreating for dedicated practice is an integral part of the cultivation of compassion
		- Dedicated training of the mind lays the proper foundation for being able to react with patience and compassion when interacting with others.

Compassion in Meditation:
- meditative cultivation of compassion
- compassionate responses in life will strengthen the compassionate disposition of one's own mind
- cultivation of compassion and other brahmaviharas as a "liberation of the mind" (temporary)
	- different types of liberation - some temporary
- compassion contributes to, but is not itself, final liberation
- ethical basis for overcoming the hindrances (which are impediments for successful mental culture).
- kasina 
	- spread everywhere
	- totality of one's experience
	- mind that was once narrow now become boundless

Objects of Meditation:
- lacks any reference to an object
- instead of mentally commiserating with instances of suffering and affliction, at the heart of compassion stand the wish and aspiration for others to be free from suffering and affliction.  Such as wish can and should be free from sadness and grief.
- boundless radiation of compassion to the living beings in all directions
- later teachings point to taking actual persons as it object
	- for when the mind is defiled and unable to practice boundless radiation
	- gradually extending the object of one's practice until it becomes universal
