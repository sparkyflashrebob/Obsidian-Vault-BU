Benevolence (Metta)
- loving-kindness
- Analyo prefers benevolence
- root word  - mitra = "friend"
- basic sense of friendliness and an attitude of friendship
- nuance of mutual benefit and assistance
- Not like love of a mother to their child
	- this is called apekkha, pema or piya
- stanza gives this impression
	- Just as a mother who has an only son would protect her own son with her life, so one should cultivate a boundless mind towards all living beings.
	- Protection is the main theme here
- providing and receiving projection is reoccurent aspect of metta in early discourses
- examples are metta can project from snake bites, protect against non-human beings
- Metta-Sutta encourages cultivation of an attitude of protection towards others
	- not  of a form of love
	- seen as a form of attachment
- Metta should be boundless and free from attachment
- metta most frequently mentioned brahmavihara
- Foundation for the cultivation of the other three
- Simile of TREE OF COMPASSION
	- water is the metta that nourishes the tree
	- illustrates how metta nourishes the root of the tree
- Expressed thru bodily, verbal and mental activities
- six principles that lead to cordiality in one3's relationship to others
- only brahmavihara explicitly highlighted in the early discourses
- used as a testing ground for one's meditation practice
- permeates all aspects of one's behaviours and activities
- fertile soil for compassion and other brahmaviharas to grow and flourish
- foundation of kindness in one's activities, ones communications, ones mental attitude
- broader scope for expressing one's compassionate intent
- cultivating the appropriate attitude to other and perhaps more ordinary daily activities
- best under the mode of metta

KINDNESS IN DAILY CONDUCT
- harmony of three monks living together
	- share in chores
- spirit of kindness
- taking responsibility as an aspect of compassion
- who comes first clears the way for others
- who comes last makes sure everything tidy
- do not damage environment
- conducive for mediation practice - given priority
- silent cooperation
- also meet regularly to discuss the Dharma or meditate together
- harmonious mental attitude towards living together
- blessing to have our companions
- practice towards such companions whether seen or unseen
- equally and without difference
- verbally and mentally
- set aside my own attitude and follow the mental attitude of my friends
- never harbor a single disapproving mental attitude
- in this way I am always at ease and lack nothing

Blending Like Milk and Water:
- appreciiation towards others (see sympathetic joy)
	- a gain and a blessing to have such companions
	- WHY DO WE GET ALONG?  BECAUSE WE WANT TO.
	- Benefit one derives from whom one is close
	- Not take others for granted
	- Making an effort to counter this tendency can be helpful in one's cultivation of compassion and kindness
	- Can take place unseen
	- inner strength of metta
	- does not need public recognition
	- rooted in a mind steeped in metta, any outer expression of metta is sufficient in itself
	- LET GO OF ONE"S PREFERENCES
	- Be willing to go along with what others prefer
	- Not merely to submit
	- an attitude that does not pin ones happiness on being able to get everything one wants
	- To be at ease and lack nothing comes about by letting go of wanting to have things one's way.

FACING AGGRESSION:
- metta can come to one's aid when having to face others who act aggressively
- if others should beat you, punch you, etc... your mind does not change
- based on this - you arouse a mental attitude of metta and compassion for those who beat you
- you will not utter unkind words
- being without mental shackles, resentment, ill will or contention,with a mind imbued with metta is vast and great, boundless and well cultivated, dwell pervading the entire world

Drastic Example:
- Simile of the Saw - bandit cut you into pieces
- can not imagine any kind of unpleasant speech that one would be able to bear
- meant to encourage patience and forbearance in other situations less threatening

MENTAL BEAUTY:
- when anger arises, even if within, metta is the antidote
- ill-will cannot arise in someone who cultivates liberation of mind by metta
- having overcome ill will, one achieves metta
- metta directly opposes ill will
- deprive of its nurishment and sustenence
- metta is the way to face ill will, anger, and aggression whether arisen in oneself or confronted by others under the influence
- if one has to reprimand another
	- it should not be done with an angry mind
- when being criticized, justified or not, an attitude of metta is beneficial
- those who become angry have an ugly appearance (TRUE)
- We can verify this.
- A tendency to be angry leads to being reborn ugly (karma)
- Not to judge those who are born unattractive
- highlight repercussions of being carried away by anger and irritation
- cultivation of metta leads the mind to supreme beauty.
- Physical and mental beauty
	- in one's action and words 
- one rooted in kindness and compassion
- attitude of kindness and benevolence recommended for any situation
	- simple
		- clean up after a mean
	- difficult
		- being cut into pieces by bandits
- metta the forerunner on the path to true beauty 
- the beauty of the compassionate mind
- compliments the other two brahmaviharas
