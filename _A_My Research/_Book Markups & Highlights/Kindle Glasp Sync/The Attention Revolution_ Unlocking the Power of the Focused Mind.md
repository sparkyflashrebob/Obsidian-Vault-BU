> &ldquo;posture of vigilance&rdquo;:

> Vigilance in the supine position is mostly psychological, an attitude that regards this position as a formal meditation posture, and not simply as rest.

> Be at ease. Be still. Be vigilant.

> Once you have settled your body with these three qualities, take three slow, gentle, deep breaths, breathing in and out through the nostrils. Let your awareness permeate your entire body as you do so, noting any sensations that arise in relation to the respiration. Luxuriate in these breaths, as if you were receiving a gentle massage from within.

> Now settle your respiration in its natural flow.

> Observe the entire course of each in- and out-breath, noting whether it is long or short, deep or shallow, slow or fast. Don&rsquo;t impose any rhythm on your breathing.

> Especially with each out-breath, relax your body, release extraneous thoughts, and happily let your attention settle back into the body.

> If any tension builds up in your shoulders, face, or eyes, release it.

> Breathe so effortlessly that you feel as if your body were being breathed by your environment.

> Breathing in long, one knows, &ldquo;I breathe in long.&rdquo; Breathing out long, one knows, &ldquo;I breathe out long.&rdquo; Breathing in short, one knows, &ldquo;I breathe in short.&rdquo; Breathing out short, one knows, &ldquo;I breathe out short.&rdquo; One trains thus: &ldquo;I shall breathe in, experiencing the whole body.
