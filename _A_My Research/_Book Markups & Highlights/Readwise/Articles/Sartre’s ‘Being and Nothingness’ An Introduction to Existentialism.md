- Category: #articles
- Summary: Jean-Paul Sartre's major work, "Being and Nothingness," explores existentialist philosophy and the concept of human freedom. Unlike animals with predetermined natures, humans have the power to create their own meaning in life. Sartre distinguishes between "being-in-itself," a concrete reality devoid of subjective experience, and "being-for-itself," which experiences anxiety and can make choices with greater objectivity. Sartre emphasizes the importance of human freedom and encourages individuals to take responsibility for their own decisions. The book delves into concepts such as consciousness, bad faith, and the significance of individual choice in creating meaning in life.
