## Metadata
- Author: [[Spinal Cord Journal]]
- Full Title: How Not to Chatter Like a Toddler When Giving a Scientific Presentation
- Category: #articles
- Document Note: It is not uncommon for scientific presentations to be overly detailed and hard to decipher, much like the way a toddler talks. To prevent this from happening, presentation coaches suggest specific steps to limit the scope of presentations and limit the tendency to ramble. These steps include writing a simple narrative, practising with two types of listener, building up instead of cutting down, and creating slides with an overarching message and minimal information. Hand sketching slides can be a helpful way to stay focused on the overall purpose of each slide.
   Take some highlights and try this prompt again.
   This article provides strategies to help scientists create more concise presentations by limiting scope and detail. Specific steps include writing a simple narrative first, practising with two types of listener, building up the presentation rather than cutting down, and creating slides with an overarching message. These strategies can help scientists to create clearer, more-concise presentations.
