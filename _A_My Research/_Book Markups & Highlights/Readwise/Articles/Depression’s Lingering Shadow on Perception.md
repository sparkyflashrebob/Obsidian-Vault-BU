## Highlights
- Individuals who have rebounded from a major depressive episode display a distinctive cognitive pattern, processing negative information more extensively and positive information less so than those without a history of depression. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dwz9wdjgqk38j63bhhfdyt))
- Drawing from a meta-analysis of 44 studies encompassing over 4,000 participants, the study unveiled that while healthy participants swiftly responded to both emotional and non-emotional stimuli, their counterparts with a past tryst with depression had a pronounced tilt towards negative stimuli. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dwzp5ak00ne7c64fy58fvr))
- This pattern not only signifies a decreased control over the kind of information processed but also hints at an inherent bias leaning towards negativity. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dwzx8f9mhy7bjfdtdfc6jf))
- **Key Facts**:
  1. Over 50% of individuals with a first-time major depressive episode will experience subsequent episodes, often relapsing within two years of recovery.
  2. The study’s meta-analysis included 44 studies, with 2081 participants with a history of major depressive disorder and 2285 healthy controls.
  3. Those recovered from major depression showed a stronger bias for processing negative information over positive or neutral information compared to controls. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dx0gjnngjtbd6xvwa6scbv))
- People who have recovered from a major depressive episode, when compared with individuals who have never experienced one, tend to spend more time processing negative information and less time processing positive information, putting them at risk for a relapse. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dx0vgzwthx7z3zcv8h723f))
- “Because more negative thinking and mood and less positive thinking and mood are characteristic of depression, this could mean that these individuals are at a greater risk for having another depressive episode.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dx19sh0vqdpmnhdktaevbb))
- Defined as a period of at least two weeks of a depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure in daily activities, major depression can interfere with or limit a person’s ability to carry out major life activities. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dx1zqwahbdvkwmjhchg2qc))
- Despite well-established treatments for depression, relapse rates for major depressive disorder remain high, ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dx28jvnk7ggqgk38bmyedm))
- Thus, more insight is needed into the risk factors involved in major depressive disorder to improve treatment and prevent relapse. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h8dx2g7kf9yjnmzfk5x7q0qf))
