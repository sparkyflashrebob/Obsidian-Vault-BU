- The two truths doctrine states that there is:
  • *Provisional* or conventional truth ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) **saṁvṛti***-satya*, [Pāli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81li) *sammuti sacca*, [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_alphabet) *kun-rdzob bden-pa*), which describes our daily experience of a concrete world, and
  • *Ultimate* truth (Sanskrit, *paramārtha-satya*, Pāli *paramattha sacca*, Tibetan: *don-dam bden-pa*), which describes the ultimate reality as *[sunyata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunyata)*, empty of concrete and inherent characteristics. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gvsn987kfhgq0tdfqvf91a5j))
- [Chandrakīrti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrakirti) suggests three possible meanings of **saṁvṛti** : [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_truths_doctrine#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatilal2002203–208-1)
  1. complete covering or the 'screen' of ignorance which hides truth;
  2. existence or origination through dependence, mutual conditioning;
