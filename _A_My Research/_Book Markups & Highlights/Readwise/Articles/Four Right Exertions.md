- Four Right Exertions[[edit source](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Four_Right_Exertions&action=edit&section=2)]
  The Four Right Exertions (*cattārimāni sammappadhānāni*) are defined with the following traditional phrase:
  "There is the case where a monk generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his intent for:
  "[i] the sake of the **non-arising** [*anuppādāya*] of evil, unskillful qualities that have not yet arisen.
  "[ii] ... the sake of the **abandonment** [*pahānāya*] of evil, unskillful qualities that have arisen.
  "[iii] ... the sake of the **arising** [*uppādāya*] of skillful qualities that have not yet arisen.
  "[iv] ... the **maintenance** [**ṭhitiyā**], non-confusion, increase, plenitude, development, & culmination of skillful qualities that have arisen."[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Right_Exertions#cite_note-2) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gtnkn13tt5v8m7h2wzh0hffg))

- The four exertions (*cattārimāni padhānāni*) are summarized as:
  1. **Restraint** (**saṃvara padhāna**) of the [senses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatana).
  2. **Abandonment** (**pahāna padhāna**) of [defilements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilesa).
  3. **Cultivation** (**bhāvanā padhāna**) of [Enlightenment Factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojjhanga).
  4. **Preservation** (**anurakkhaṇā padhāna**) of [concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam%C4%81dhi_(Buddhism)), for instance, using charnel-ground contemplations.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Right_Exertions#cite_note-15) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gtnkq7ma6ard1ydeyevqwge7))
