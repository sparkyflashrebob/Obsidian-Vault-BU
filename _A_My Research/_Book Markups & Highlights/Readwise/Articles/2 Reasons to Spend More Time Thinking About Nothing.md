# 2 Reasons to Spend More Time Thinking About Nothing

## Metadata
- Author: [[Davia Sills]]
- Full Title: 2 Reasons to Spend More Time Thinking About Nothing
- Category: #articles
- Document Note: Slowing down and taking breaks from the fast-paced world can have mental health benefits and optimize cognitive abilities. Two reasons to take time out to relax are the reduction of decision fatigue and improvement of decision quality, backed up by research showing better decision-making in judges earlier in the day. Mindfulness practices can help with attention and emotional well-being, making it essential to embrace a slower pace of life in an increasingly digital world.

## Highlights
- y slowing down, we are potentially able to stave off decision fatigue, which is when our ability to make decisions becomes depleted due to the large number of decisions we have already made. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49d9757xazzgz4x5vr3fsrt))
- The pressures of our fast-paced world may call for a number of decisions to be made, but by consciously slowing down and making time to, say, enjoy an evening stroll in the park, we offer ourselves the opportunity to recharge and make wiser decisions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49d9drzjk0nrq3ct3hz7nsy))
- Research backs this up ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49d9p3vem2bdx4sgd39vyxm))
- A 2011 [study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1018033108) published in the journal *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* found that parole judges, who are expected to make rational decisions based on the facts presented to them, were more lenient earlier in the day compared to later in the day, indicating that [decision-making](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/decision-making) is often influenced by extraneous factors. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49da6dpey5ty51rt625m3sz))
- The study concluded that decision fatigue caused the parole judges to be less lenient as the day progressed. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49dacmqs1rcjv81wm8rdse1))
- We all make several decisions throughout the day. These decisions use up brain power, which is a finite resource. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49dajtx5t0m5hxmcmvgydt5))
- Dedicating time to doing nothing means that, temporarily, you won’t be in charge of making high-stakes decisions. These "decision savings" can then be used to make better, higher-quality decisions when the situation calls for it. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49datyvh0xdvcb3ybps60j2))
- 2. Giving your brain time to relax helps it perform better. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49dayrn0e4ba1m1y7ywq0qh))
- A 2020 [study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7366948/) shows that digital technology is causing us to display more [ADHD](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/adhd)-like symptoms and is negatively impacting our ability to understand each other emotionally and socially. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49db7as43jf08xfw3q92qzn))
- A 2012 [study](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00133/full) published in *Frontiers of Human [Neuroscience](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/neuroscience)* found that people who have been long-time [meditation](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/meditation) practitioners performed better than those who have never meditated on a test of spatial [attention](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/attention), which is an information-processing task. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49dbmvh4eea739jm6wxg491))
- Similarly, a separate [study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10608-007-9119-0) published in *Cognitive [Therapy](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/therapy) and Research* investigated the effects of a 10-day intensive [mindfulness](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/basics/mindfulness) training course on attentional control, cognitive style, and emotional well-being. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49dbywmm3a896hnwz3ksv6h))
- The results revealed that participants with no prior meditation experience exhibited significant improvements in attention, reduced rumination and dysfunctional attitudes, and increased positive affect and self-compassion. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h49dc50ax448zd2ehacsfr5v))
- These findings emphasize the significance of embracing a slower pace of life and integrating mindfulness practices—especially in the context of a digital lifestyle—highlighting the positive impacts they can have on attention, self-reflection, and overall mental well-being.
