## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speakers**: Ajahn Amaro (Theravāda) and Joseph Bobrow Roshi (Zen).

* **Format**: Joint Question & Answer session resolving practical, psychological, and doctrinal queries raised by retreatants following the morning keynotes.

* **Theme**: The relationship between brain science and spiritual realization; unconscious motivation and psychological maturity; karma, moral responsibility, and non-dual freedom; Case 2 of the *Gateless Gate* (Baizhang's Wild Fox).

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Retreatants explore whether spiritual realization can be reduced to neurobiology ('finding God in the neurons'). Bobrow Roshi and Ajahn Amaro clarify that while neurobiology illuminates physical brain correlations, reductionism misses the living, experiential depth of awakening. Crucially, they address the peril of using non-duality to dismiss moral causality. In Zen Case 2 (*Baizhang's Fox*), claiming that the enlightened person is 'exempt from karma' results in karmic entrapment. Authentic non-duality does not bypass cause and effect—it frees the mind to act with complete, selfless responsibility within cause and effect.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

1. **Ethical Impeccability in Non-Duality**: Non-duality never excuses careless, self-centered speech or actions. When we notice rationalizations like 'It's all empty anyway,' recognize this as subtle delusion. Non-duality makes us infinitely more sensitive to the impact of our words and deeds on others.

2. **Looking Beneath the Question**: When caught in repetitive loops of questioning or spiritual anxiety, gently look beneath the words: *What is feeling uncertain or threatened right now?* Hold that somatic knot with warmth.
