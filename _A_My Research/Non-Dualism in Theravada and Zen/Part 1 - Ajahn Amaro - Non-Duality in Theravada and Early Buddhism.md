# 🎙️ Non-Dualism in Theravāda and Zen: Part 1 — Ajahn Amaro Opening Exposition
### *Ajahn Amaro & Joseph Bobrow Roshi — Daylong Seminar on Non-Duality (2007)*

> *"Theravāda is often stereotyped as rigid dualism—saṃsāra versus nibbāna, good versus bad, purity versus defilement. But in the suttas, the Buddha’s most radical teachings point beyond all dualities: 'In the seen, only the seen; in the heard, only the heard... when there is no you in terms of that, you are neither here, nor beyond, nor between the two. Just this is the end of suffering.' Non-duality is not a philosophical thesis to defend; it is the natural, unentangled reality that reveals itself when the grasping hand of self lets go."* — **Ajahn Amaro [14:57 / 42:14 / 76:54]**

## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speaker**: Ajahn Amaro (Theravāda perspective).

* **Dialogue Partner**: Joseph Bobrow Roshi (Zen Master in the Sanbo Kyodan lineage, author of *Coming to Life: The Emergence of Self in the Everyday Miracle of Zen*).

* **Theme**: Dismantling the myth that Theravāda Buddhism is purely dualistic; unearthing the profound non-dual foundations of the Pāli Nikāyas; bridging Early Buddhism with Zen and Chan.

* **Circumstances**: Delivered at the Insight Meditation Center (Redwood City, CA) as the opening address of a groundbreaking daylong seminar co-hosted with Audio Dharma.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: In modern Buddhist discourse, non-duality is frequently claimed as the exclusive domain of Advaita Vedānta, Tibetan Dzogchen, or Zen, while Theravāda is mischaracterized as a gradual, dualistic path focused strictly on escaping a polluted saṃsāra to reach an external Nibbāna. Ajahn Amaro proves that this stereotype ignores the deepest canonical discourses. In the *Kaccānagotta Sutta* (SN 12.15), the Buddha explicitly refutes the dualism of existence and non-existence (*atthitā* and *natthitā*). In the *Bāhiya Sutta* (Ud 1.10), the Buddha reveals that when awareness rests in pure sense contact without mental proliferation (*diṭṭhe diṭṭhamattaṃ*), the illusion of a subject-object split collapses. Drawing upon Ajahn Chah's realization—*'Neither moving forward, nor moving back, nor standing still'—Ajahn Amaro demonstrates that non-duality is not a conceptual stance, but the unconditioned peace of non-clinging.

* **Expanded Version of the Teachings**: Dualistic language in the suttas is a pedagogical convention (*sammuti*), not an ultimate truth (*paramattha*). The Buddha uses conventional terms (good/bad, path/goal, delusion/wisdom) to orient practice, but warns in the *Mūlapariyāya Sutta* (MN 1) against *maññanā*—the cognitive process of conceiving an essence out of experience. When we conceive 'I am practicing non-duality,' we have re-established duality. True non-duality is embodied in *atammayatā* ('not made of that')—the radical refusal of awareness to take form as any identity, state, or concept. When we realize that nothing is lacking in the unconditioned nature of the present moment, compulsions vanish and true compassion emerges.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

In bringing Ajahn Amaro's non-dual reflections into daily mindfulness and sitting meditation:
1. **Opening the Grasping Hand in the Body**: When anxiety or frustration arises—whether dealing with bills, technology glitches, or difficult news—notice the physical clenching in the body: our stomach is tight or knotted up, our shoulders raise, our breathing becomes shallow. There is no need to fight this holding. We can simply invite the grasping hand to open, feeling the stomach soften into ease.
2. **Practicing Bāhiya's Instruction**: Throughout the day, practice resting as pure sensory contact: *In the seen, only the seen; in the heard, only the heard.* Let sounds enter the ears without manufacturing an internal commentator.
3. **Resting in 'Nothing is Lacking'**: Whenever the familiar tug of restlessness or inadequacy whispers that we need something else to be happy, pause and ask: *"What is lacking in this present moment of unentangled knowing?"* Abide in that sufficiency.
