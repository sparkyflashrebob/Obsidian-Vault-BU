## 🧭 Structured Contextual Metadata

* **Speakers**: Ajahn Amaro and Joseph Bobrow Roshi.

* **Theme**: The metaphor of the mind as a mirror; Huineng vs. Shenxiu in the *Platform Sutra*; the two truths (*sammuti-sacca* and *paramattha-sacca*); Sri Ramana Maharshi and the 'I Am' (*asmi-māna*); the Well and Groundwater analogy; *Dhammatā* as the ordinary nature of reality.

* **Condensed Version of the Teachings**: Commencing the afternoon session, the teachers examine the classic Buddhist metaphor of the mirror. Bobrow Roshi and Ajahn Amaro contrast Shenxiu's gradual approach (polishing the mirror to remove dust) with Huineng's radical non-dual realization (there is no mirror, no dust, no stand). Ajahn Amaro demonstrates how this reconciles with Early Buddhism: conventional effort (*sammuti*) is necessary to remove defilements, but from the ultimate standpoint (*paramattha*), the unconditioned nature of awareness is never defiled. Like digging a well, practice does not fabricate Nibbāna; it removes obstacles to uncover the already present groundwater. Remarkably, the Thai word for 'ordinary' (*tamadah*) derives from Pāli *dhammatā*—revealing that the sacred is none other than this unremarkable present moment.

## 🌿 Contemplative Reflection & Everyday Integration

1. **The Groundwater of Awareness**: When feeling spiritually dry or struggling in meditation, remember that peace is not something we have to manufacture through straining. Like groundwater, the quiet presence of knowing is already beneath the surface. We only need to cease piling on the dirt of thinking.

2. **Sacred Ordinariness (*Tamadah*)**: There is no need to chase mystical fireworks. The sound of traffic, the temperature of the air on our skin, our breath rising and falling—this ordinary moment is *dhammatā*.
