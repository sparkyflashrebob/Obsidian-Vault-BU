Key Features of Roar
Since you're working with audio and music production, Roar is a particularly powerful addition to your toolkit. It is much more than a simple distortion plugin:
Multistage Saturation: It features three saturation stages that can be routed in Series, Parallel, Mid/Side, or Multiband configurations.
Modulation Matrix: It includes an extensive modulation section with two LFOs, an envelope follower, and noise sources, allowing you to create evolving textures rather than static distortion.
Feedback & Compression: It has a built-in feedback generator and compressor, which can transform the device into a resonator or a chaotic sound design tool.
Coloration: It ranges from "mastering-grade" subtle warmth to complete signal degradation.

As an engineer, you might find these specific "pro" workflows interesting:
The "Yellow LED" Rule: Always look for the small LED next to the Drive knob. If it isn't lighting up yellow, your input signal is too quiet to trigger the saturation curves properly.
Feedback as a Resonator: In "Feedback" routing mode, Roar can behave like a physical modeling synth. By modulating the frequency of the feedback loop, you can turn a simple kick drum into a tonal bass instrument.
Mid/Side Mastering: Try using Roar on your Master bus in Mid/Side mode. Apply a very subtle "Tube Preamp" curve only to the Mid channel to add punch to the kick/snare, while keeping the Sides clean and wide.
Color Compensation: If your distortion is making the sound too "muddy," toggle the Color Compensation button. This applies a reciprocal EQ curve to the output, keeping the tonal balance consistent while adding the grit.
