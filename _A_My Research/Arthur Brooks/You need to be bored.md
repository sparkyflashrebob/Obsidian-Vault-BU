The need to be bored stems from the crucial function that periods of mental downtime play in fostering self-discovery, coherence, and a sense of meaning in life. Sources indicate that if a person never experiences boredom, they are likely to have less meaning and be more susceptible to depression.
Boredom is necessary because it facilitates a shift in cognitive function, enabling deeper introspection and addressing core existential concerns:
The Role of the Default Mode Network (DMN)
Boredom triggers the use of a specific area of the brain known as the default mode network (DMN).
• The DMN consists of structures that activate when a person is not otherwise occupied cognitively. For instance, when waiting at a traffic light and forgetting one's phone, the DMN switches on.
• When the mind wanders during boredom, it often focuses on uncomfortable existential questions, such as "What does my life mean?". This exploration of large questions concerning purpose, meaning, coherence, and significance is deemed incredibly important and beneficial.
• However, many people find boredom highly unpleasant because the DMN directs the mind toward these mildly uncomfortable or difficult questions. Some studies have shown that a majority of participants would even choose to give themselves a painful electric shock rather than sit alone for fifteen minutes with instructions to do absolutely nothing but think.
Boredom and the Existential Vacuum
The deliberate avoidance of boredom can lead to significant psychological distress, often associated with a lack of meaning:
• The inability or refusal to engage with boredom contributes to an explosion of depression and anxiety in society today, as people neither know nor look for the meaning of their lives.
• The widespread phenomenon of existential vacuum—a feeling of emptiness, futility, and meaninglessness—manifests mainly as a state of boredom. This experience can result from the twofold loss of basic animal instincts and rapidly diminishing traditions, leaving modern individuals uncertain about what they wish or ought to do.
• By constantly eliminating slight boredom (often by pulling out a phone), people prevent the DMN from activating, making it harder to find meaning and creating a "doom loop of meaning" that leads to hollowness, depression, and anxiety.
• When the existential vacuum takes hold, the immediate seeking of pleasure can take over, which is why existential frustration often leads to compensations like the will to power (including the will to money) or the will to pleasure.
Boredom as a Pathway to Self-Mastery and Virtue
Boredom offers essential opportunities for psychological and philosophical development:
• Learning Self-Reliance: Historically, parents often expected children to remain quiet in public spaces and deal with tedium, leading the children to learn how to entertain themselves. Rushing to rescue a child from boredom (or constantly entertaining oneself) denies them the chance to learn how to "make your own fun". Many young people struggle to identify their talents because they have "never been bored enough to figure it out".
• Mental Toughness: Embracing periods of boredom (starting with periods of 15 minutes or longer) allows individuals to become skilled at managing this state. This ability subsequently translates into being less bored with ordinary things, such as one's job and relationships.
• Philosophical Practice: Ancient philosophers emphasized using time for higher purposes rather than relieving tedium. Philosophy is not "devised for show" or pursued so that "our leisure may be relieved of a tedium that irks us". Instead, it is meant to "build and shape the soul," ordering life and guiding conduct. If events require spending time alone, one should look upon it as tranquility, talking to oneself and training thoughts, rather than being unhappy because "leisure, it's boring".
In essence, boredom acts as necessary cognitive friction. Just as an athlete must endure the strain of training to gain strength, allowing the mind to wrestle with the uncomfortable questions that arise during boredom provides the necessary challenge to develop inner resources and clarify one's purpose, safeguarding against meaninglessness.
