Neoliberals may want to devalue certain aspects of education primarily because their ideology reconceives education through a market-driven lens, shifting its purpose from a public good to an individual investment in "human capital" [1-6]. This reorientation leads to a devaluing of traditional educational goals and practices for several reasons:

* **Economization of Education and the "Human Capital" Model**: Neoliberalism "economizes every sphere and human endeavor," including education [2, 7]. It frames human beings as "human capital," who are tasked with improving and leveraging their competitive positioning and enhancing their value across all endeavors [8-10]. In this framework, **education is no longer viewed as a democratic public good but as a personal investment** by human capital in its own future market value [2, 4, 7, 11, 12]. This transformation renders the idea of education as a public good "literally unintelligible" [2]. Consequently, the value of knowledge, thought, and training is almost exclusively tied to its contribution to capital enhancement, not to broader purposes like developing capacities for democratic citizenship or sustaining culture [4].
* **Shift from Public Good to Private Investment**: Under neoliberalism, **public goods of any kind become increasingly difficult to justify or secure publicly** [3, 13, 14]. The market metrics that contour every dimension of human conduct and institutions make it difficult to explain why universities, libraries, or public services should be publicly accessible and provisioned [3]. The cost of education is seen as something to be borne only by those who "consume" it, with government acting as merely another market actor, rather than an entity identified with the public interest [3].
* **Focus on Job Training and Market Needs**: The prevailing neoliberal view pressures universities to prioritize job training and skills development that align with market demands [1, 7, 15]. **Education becomes a means to produce "shovel ready" graduates** for particular jobs, rather than fostering critical thinking, democratic participation, or a broad understanding of the world [1, 15, 16]. This instrumental view leads to the marginalization or elimination of academic disciplines and practices that do not serve market norms or block market flows, such as tenure, academic freedom, and research without inherently marketable purposes [15, 17-19].
* **Cost-Cutting and Austerity Measures**: Public universities face declining state support, forcing them to operate more like businesses and rely on high tuition rates [11, 20, 21]. This leads to efforts to "trim costs by compressing time to degree and making extensive use of summer and online courses taught by casual academic labor" [15, 19]. This economic pressure **devalues the quality of teaching and research that does not directly contribute to "capital appreciation"** [22, 23].
* **Erosion of Democratic Values and Citizen Subjectivity**: Neoliberalism's economization of education contributes to the **"undoing" of democracy** itself [24, 25]. It de-democratizes political culture and discredits norms like inclusion, pluralism, tolerance, and equality [26]. When students are reduced to "human capital" and education to mere training, the capacity for democratic self-rule and the desire for it can be extinguished [1, 5, 27-30]. The "citizen is transformed into a consumer," laying the foundation for self-seeking agents indifferent to the growth of despotic power [1].
* **Promotion of Individual Responsibility and Blame**: Neoliberalism promotes a culture of self-reliance, where individuals are expected to take responsibility for their own successes and failures [31, 32]. This means that **educational outcomes are often attributed to individual effort rather than systemic inequalities** like racism or sexism, which neoliberal ideology tends to discount or deny [9, 15, 16, 33, 34]. This "responsibilization" burdens the individual and can lead to self-blame even in the face of structural barriers, further reinforcing neoliberal ideology [9, 16, 35, 36].
* **Suppression of Dissent and Critical Consciousness**: By focusing on market principles and individual performance, neoliberalism "represses a genuine critical education" and any viable notion of dissent [1, 18]. It makes "individual or collective emancipatory projects that would challenge neoliberalism’s basic conditions" appear "delusional and incoherent," particularly revolutionary struggles, which are cast as "archaic" and "ideologically backward" [37]. This systematic undermining of critical thought further devalues education as a site for social change and collective action [38].
