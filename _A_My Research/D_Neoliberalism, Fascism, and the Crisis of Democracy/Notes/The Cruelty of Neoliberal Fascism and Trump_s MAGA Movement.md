The perceived cruelty of Donald Trump and the MAGA movement is attributed to a complex interplay of factors rooted in neoliberalism and its convergence with elements of fascism, driven by deep-seated fears, resentment, and a "politics of disposability" [1-7].

Here are the key elements contributing to this perceived cruelty:

* **Neoliberalism as an Enabling Force**

  + Neoliberalism, characterized as an **extreme stage of predatory capitalism**, aims to consolidate power in the financial elite and operates under the assumption that the market should drive all aspects of social life [8-10].
  + It promotes **self-interest, unchecked individualism, and a "survival-of-the-fittest" ethos**, which systematically undermines compassion, mutual care, and social responsibility [8, 10-12]. This market-driven ethic can lead to indifference towards the suffering of others [12].
  + It **weakens democratic institutions, public goods, and social provisions** like welfare and education, contributing to civic illiteracy and leaving individuals feeling voiceless and powerless, thus making them susceptible to simplistic emotional appeals from authoritarian figures [13-22].
  + This economic framework legitimizes the **sacrifice of individuals** or entire populations for the sake of macroeconomic goals, transforming citizenship into a willingness to endure insecurity and deprivation to maintain the nation's economic competitive positioning or credit rating [23-29].
* **Fusion with Fascism (Neoliberal Fascism)**

  + The concept of "neoliberal fascism" describes a **mutually compatible project and movement** that fuses the excesses of capitalism with fascist ideals [1-3, 5, 30, 31].
  + This fusion brings forth elements such as **white supremacy, ultra-nationalism, misogyny, militarism, and state violence**, coupled with a hatred of reason, truth, and democratic principles [2, 3, 5, 30, 32, 33].
  + It actively **suppresses freedom and dissent** and seeks to redefine or eliminate the "social contract" and "common good" in favor of market imperatives [2, 30, 32-34].
* **Psychological and Social Drivers**

  + **Fear and Status Anxiety:** Trump is highly skilled at **stoking fear**, notably by portraying immigrants as threats, which activates authoritarian personality traits in his supporters [35-38]. MAGA supporters are driven by a profound **sense of "lost status" or "cultural dethronement"**, particularly among white men who perceive women and immigrants as usurping their traditional dominance [36, 39, 40]. This anxiety extends to some minority men and white women who feel their male or gendered status is threatened by women's empowerment [41].
  + **Resentment and "Bile":** This status anxiety generates **"fierce resentment" or "bile"** towards perceived "usurpers" [39, 42]. The act of pushing "other" groups down is seen as a "medicine" that alleviates this resentment, leading supporters to "revel in the notion of seeing them pushed down" [39]. This explains why supporters "don't necessarily cheer policy; they cheer cruelty" [42].
  + **"Othering" and Disposability:** Neoliberal governments, struggling for legitimacy amidst stagnation, resort to "othering" by **constructing external threats** (e.g., migrants, racialized communities, trade unions) to rally support for coercive measures [43-47]. Trump's "politics of disposability" views individuals without economic value as "refuse, waste and excess," extending this dehumanizing logic to anyone not fitting a "white nationalist script" [7, 48-50]. This translates into policies like separating immigrant children from their parents, which resonates with historical fascist brutalities [25, 51-53].
* **Role of Language and Media**

  + Fascism often begins with language [37, 38]. Trump uses **dehumanizing rhetoric**, calling immigrants "animals" or "rapists," and employing terms like "infest," which draw parallels to Nazi tactics of demonization and "othering" [51, 54-56]. This language normalizes racist beliefs and incites violence against critics and marginalized groups [54, 56-58].
  + His administration actively **attacks truth, facts, and scientific reason**, replacing them with "fake news" and spectacle [13, 17, 20, 59-63]. This "post-truth society" makes it impossible to criticize power, as there is no basis in reality to do so [60, 62].
  + Corporate-controlled media and a celebrity culture contribute to **civic illiteracy** and a "culture of vulgarity," further eroding critical thinking and empathy [4, 6, 17, 20, 63-65].
* **Societal Indifference to Cruelty**

  + Many people find Trump morally acceptable because he is an "exaggerated version of the kind of person modern society was designed to create" [66]. This is linked to "emotivism"—the idea that moral judgments are merely expressions of personal preference—which thrives in capitalist societies focused on individual consumer choice [67].
  + In the absence of a common good, societal differences are increasingly settled through **coercion or manipulation** [68]. This shift leads to a "radical depression of conscience," where values become fungible and trivial, making people less concerned about the effects of their actions on others or the future [69-71]. The freedom championed by the right, unyoked from conscience, becomes "doing or saying what one likes without regard for its effects" and can even be "festive" in its destructiveness [70].
