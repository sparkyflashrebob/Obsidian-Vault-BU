Meditative poems are incredible tools for grounding. Unlike short quotes, they invite you to sit, breathe, and slow down your internal rhythm to match the pace of the words.

Here are four deeply grounding, contemplative poems that speak directly to finding stillness, self-acceptance, and peace.

1. The Peace of Wild Things — Wendell Berry

This poem is a sanctuary for moments when anxiety or thoughts of the future take over. It reminds us to rest in the present grace of the natural world.

The poem encourages finding peace by observing the untroubled, natural world, urging the reader to rest in the present moment.

Selected lines: "I come into the peace of wild things / who do not tax their lives with forethought / of grief... For a time / I rest in the grace of the world, and am free."

2. Lost — David Wagoner

Often used in mindfulness practice, this poem advises that when feeling lost or disconnected, one should stand still and allow their immediate surroundings to act as an anchor.

Selected lines: "Stand still. The trees ahead and bushes beside you / Are not lost. Wherever you are is called Here... The forest breathes. Listen. It answers, / I have made this place around you."

Translated by Coleman Barks, this poem teaches that true peace comes from welcoming all emotions and experiences—even difficult ones—as temporary guests that provide guidance.

Selected lines: "This being human is a guest house. / Every morning a new arrival... Be grateful for whoever comes, / because each has been sent / as a guide from beyond."

— David Whyte

This piece acts as a counter to the noise of the modern, digital world, emphasizing the value of quiet, genuine moments over overwhelming information.

Selected lines: "This is not the age of information. / Forget the news, and the radio, and the blurred screen... and one good word is bread for a thousand."

Contemporary poets offer a distinct voice for mindfulness. They speak directly to the unique pacing, distractions, and noise of modern life. These four beautiful selections from 21st-century and contemporary writers lean heavily into the medicine of silence and grounding.

— Billy Collins

The former U.S. Poet Laureate explores the different textures of silence in modern life—comparing the heavy silence of a sudden event to the gentle, pristine silence of a fresh morning before the day begins.

Selected lines: "And there is the silence of this morning / which I have broken with my pen, / a silence that had piled up all night / like snow falling in the darkness of the house— / the silence before I wrote a word..."

2. Moments in Stillness — Rohit Kalsariya

Published in early 2025, this poem acts as a literal breathing exercise. It encourages you to slow your heart rate down, visualize the deep roots of trees, and find a safe harbor inside your own skin.

Selected lines: "Breathe in the calm, release the strain, / A fleeting thought, like gentle rain... / The earth inhales, the rivers flow, / A rhythm only silence knows. / The trees stand tall, their roots run deep..."

Written amidst the quiet valleys of Granada, Spain, this modern reflective piece reminds us that true stillness isn't completely rigid or empty. Instead, stillness is full of subtle life, slow breathing, and the gentle alignment of our thoughts.

Selected lines: "Movement is loved within seeming stillness... / a great arrival of form through all our hidden themes."

This contemporary travel poem redefines silence not as the absolute absence of sound, but as an internal feeling of being firmly anchored, heavy, and settled into the earth.

Selected lines: "Silence meets you alone in the forest, / presses your body into the Earth... / What if silence isn't the lack of sound, but a feeling? / The wind blows your hair and the leaves flutter / and not even the problems of a few minutes ago matter."
