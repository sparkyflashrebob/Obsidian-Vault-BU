### Introduction

#### “Do we know one another?” Two people stand across from each other at a social function. They have

#### been looking at each other for a while, as is common at such occasions. That first glance might have only lasted two or three tenths of a second, but it was more than long enough to arouse that mutual mixture of feelings (curiosity, elation, and slight embarrassment) which was leading them closer to each another. They then talk and try to figure out where they might have met before and under what circumstances. They divulge a detail or two about themselves and make inquiries to discover what they might have in common. If the conversation leads to the sharing of names, occupations, opinions or viewpoints, they will later claim with conviction that they know who that other person is.

#### But do they really? I think first and foremost that the question is whether we ever really know ourselves. Aside from any conversation we might have and apart from what can be read in our passports or résumés, can we claim with absolute certainty that we know who we are? I have my doubts, and these suspicions came to the surface in lucid form after I read this autobiographical novel from Mario Mantese.

#### The author has been active as a wisdom-teacher since he was flung to the outermost fringes of existence decades ago. At that time many years ago, he hovered between life and death for weeks on end. He returned from this transitional realm as something else, as a person who knows with absolute certainty that he is not the things which are written down in his passport

#### But then, who or what am I? Journey through Eternity explores this question through the means of a literary narrative. We follow the protagonist of the story to various places and into different time-periods. The goals of his journey might be comparable to the pursuits of a modern world-traveler, who seeks respite from the everyday world and hopes to learn a few new things about life. However, as the plot unfolds, it becomes increasingly clear that the tale of this journey is not going to share that kind of story. This man’s adventure follows an internal map, a hidden plan, though he goes on for quite

#### a while without clear awareness of it. It is the itinerary of his soul. In this life, he is traveling in order to find his purpose and to fulfill his destiny.

#### These are formidable words. Reading about them in the form of a novel allows us to approach their implications more comfortably. Perhaps. We are not exactly sure, but we have our intuitive sense of what is happening. There is a resonance when we read sentences like, ‘My gazing at the mummy had abruptly opened an inner door. In fractions of seconds or minutes, I traveled back hundreds, even thousands of years into my past.’

#### This scene takes place in the Egyptian section of the British Museum in London. That visitors to the museum are filled with notions of previous eras is part of the intended effect of such an exhibition. But something else is being described here. The protagonist travels thousands of years back into his own past. We will experience in an early chapter of the book what he lived out in his previous life in ancient Egypt. Gradually, the precious experiences of his past are pushed back up into his consciousness. He is in the process of gathering deeper remnants from earlier incarnations. Though it is not his conscious aim, a new wholeness emerges, and a purpose which extends far beyond the perimeters of a single person.

#### That’s not all. The central figure of this novel interacts with others who have advanced considerably along the path of liberating consciousness. These beings are extremely difficult to grasp. They are inconspicuous in the external world, and they appear to move in realms beyond those recognizable to us. They show up in the story to accompany and assist the lead character as he gains extraordinary insights into the nature of his own being: “Although I had known this man for only a few minutes, his being seemed deeply familiar. It was as if we had known each other forever, although any previous memory of our companionship was buried in the vague obscurity of past ages.” Our man contemplates his encounter with his soul-guide. “Although I knew nothing about him, not even his name, he seemed to know quite a bit about me. I had the odd impression that my soul was an open book for him, which he could browse through as he pleased.”

#### These helpers would never want to pressure or force their protégé into anything. Absolutely not! Although they are deeply convinced of the magnitude of their mission, they always consider the free will of the one they accompany. He has to take responsibility for each step he takes.

#### A delightful story it is, illustrating the unfathomable possibilities of human development. Should we believe it, or is it all too beautiful to be true? That is

#### not the point. Here, we are invited to ponder these prospects without the slightest intention to be persuaded. Maybe this is how it really is. And if it is, it wouldn’t be a bad thing at all.

#### Stating it in another way: I believe at least as deeply in the authenticity of this story as I do that we both know each other when you read my name below.

#### Martin Frischknecht

### Shanghai

#### A loud knock on the ramshackle wooden door tore me out of a deep dreamless sleep.

#### “Sir, your bathwater,” called out the ship’s mate. His raspy smoker’s voice had become quite familiar. Bearing a strong accent and considerable vanity, he had informed me several times that his name was Manolo, and that he came from Santo Domingo. His thick oily hair was always tightly combed back, and he always wore the same red shirt. His mood was easily recognizable by the tone of his voice, as was the amount of alcohol he had consumed the night before. But today there was unusual agitation in the tone of his speech.

#### I stretched my stiff, weary limbs and listened to his impatient, snorting respiration behind the closed twin doors. My gaze wandered over to the tattered calendar hanging on a rusty nail at the foot of my bunk, as it did first thing every morning.

#### “Your water,” repeated the sailor insistently from behind the door. His summons this time sounded almost threatening.

#### “I’m coming,” I said, attempting to pacify him. I slowly rose out of the rock-hard bed with a lethargic groan and opened the door.

#### Manolo stepped into my room hastily and set a battered tin bowl down on the low table which was fastened to the floor. The oily fish smell which had etched itself into the wood of the tabletop drifted unpleasantly into my nostrils every morning as I leaned over the bowl for morning washing.

#### “Mister Park, we have arrived at our destination,” proclaimed Manolo with an impassioned voice, and then exited my cabin.

#### ‘Finally!’ I thought to myself. With a deep sigh of relief, I tore out what would be the last page of the calendar. It was the 12th of February. The ship’s siren bellowed two times, the deep penetrating tones reverberating like yawns of a generic sea monster. The harsh voice of the captain could be heard above on deck. His orders were brief and very direct.

#### A powerful quake shook through the old boat, causing it to echo with moaning, suffering wails. Lars, a bear-like Swede with a thick red beard, had

#### cut the engine. He had spent most of the voyage in the interior of the ship; “in the bowels,” as he fondly called his engines. Those machines were his sole concern.

#### The team of six seamen was a colorful assortment of wild characters. An anonymous play of fate had brought this peculiar constellation together. The Swede had invited me to his room once to show me photos of his homeland and the faded foldouts of scantily clad women posted up on the walls of his cabin. But the penetrating stink of sweat and rum in his quarters was unbearable. I found a courteous way to get myself out of there and move as quickly as possible up the narrow steps to the deck, where I could breathe fresh air again.

#### The water Manola brought me for my morning wash was ice cold and had a musty smell. I looked into the mirror that hung crooked over the sink from a single nail on the wall. I gazed beyond the cracks running through it and was stunned. There were green-yellow rings around my sunken eyes, and my gaunt face was overgrown with gray whiskers. Seasickness had crept into my body like a crippling poison during the long journey and never subsided. The miserable feeling of being on the verge of vomiting had installed itself in my abdomen like a tormenting ghost. Feeble and exhausted, I sat on the cold chair in my cabin and began to shiver.

#### The thought of being able to leave this perpetually swaying ship stirred ambivalent feelings. It was strange: there was no sense of elation, none of the euphoria I had expected to feel upon finally reaching my destination, Shanghai.

#### Ouch, this dull razor! Blood flowed down my left cheek and fell in droplets over the sink, giving the water a reddish hue. With great effort I managed to pull out the woven suitcase I had inherited from an uncle from under the cot. I opened the buckles attaching the tightly drawn leather straps. Impatiently rummaging through my clothing, I found the small pouch containing bandages. I placed one over the cut on my cheek, but I forgot to first apply something to halt the bleeding.

#### Loud knocking on the door pulled me out of my thought-world once again. The captain had come to say goodbye. Ian Mackintosh was his name. He represented the fourth generation of captains with the name Mackintosh, a long-established Scottish family. He had invited me into his sparsely furnished cabin many times to play chess. He was particularly proud of his prized chessboard with hand-carved figures made of ivory. An Arab sultan

#### had offered it to him as a gift after he had safely guided a freighter carrying premium spices and valuable silks from India. All the cargo arrived undamaged, which had been no simple job. Before he brought his ship to port in India, he had his crew search the entire ship for rats. He recalled this story and details of the rat-inspection more than once for me.

#### A lacquered chest stood in one corner of his room where a large colony of woodworms had made their home. The many small holes in the wood were evidence of their industrious existence. There was a logbook on top of the chest, and next to it was a sextant. The captain had meticulously demonstrated the use of this navigational instrument on deck for me several times. I was awe-struck each time I was allowed to gain a glimpse the night sky studded with shining stars. The never-ending universe seemed so near, the stars appearing almost within grasp. This powerful experience affected my consciousness. I had a profound vision of the earth as a luminous ship gliding without a sound through a magical, immeasurable sea of stars.

#### The captain was notably pleased every time he could display his remarkable knowledge of the astral bodies. He claimed he knew the night sky so well that he could cross all the oceans without using a compass or sextant. It was not completely clear to me whether this was a seaman’s yarn or the actual truth. But all his observations came across as logical, clear, and deeply understood.

#### I sometimes had the feeling that this headstrong Scotsman possessed a sort of astronomical clairvoyance. His deep understanding of the greater correlations between heaven and earth affirmed his penchant for metaphysics. He once stated that the infinite sea of stars most certainly emerged from the exhalation of the Creator. There were a few magical moments on deck when I would inconspicuously observe him. At times, his face lit up as if he was enraptured in another world. In those moments, he and the stars appeared to melt into one grand unity.

#### Mackintosh could always be seen in the same dark blue jacket embellished with brightly polished brass buttons and reeking of tar. Under it, he wore a gray-white starched shirt with a standing collar buttoned to the top. His watery eyes and fleshy red bulbous nose belied his intimate bond with Scottish malt whiskey. But he was never seen drunk. His copper-red hair and bushy beard, neatly shaved to a point on his extending chin, emphasized his indomitable authority and tenacity. He had the team on board entirely under his control.

#### “I will see you again in three weeks for your return trip,” announced Captain Mackintosh. “Don’t forget that we pull up the anchor at 5:00 a.m. Enjoy Shanghai. It’s a very interesting city. And you’d better find yourself a doctor. You have to get yourself strong and healthy again for the trip back.” He then reached out his hand. The grip of his handshake felt like a vise. “Ok then, see you soon,” he said finally, and briskly exited the cabin. He had many things to do before the ship would reach port.

#### Anxious voices blared on deck. The clamorous ringing of the large bell on the bow signaled that we were nearing the dock. The portal in my room was so fogged up and dirty that I could hardly see through it. This hadn’t bothered me before, because there wasn’t much to see out on the open waters during the previous weeks. In any case, with quite a bit of effort I was able to pry the window open and have a look outside.

#### I watched as a junk moved extremely close to a freighter and then passed by our ship. Our tan-colored sail was drawn taut by a cool sea breeze. The briny brown water approaching the waterfront smelled of fish, motor oil, and feces. Docks with huge warehouses adjacent to them lined both sides of the long entrance to the harbor. There was also a flurry of human activity, as dockworkers loaded and unloaded cargo in the early morning light.

#### A strong jolt almost knocked me off my feet. Lars had throttled the motor. Through the morning mist, I glimpsed the huge gray stone houses that outlined the district of Shanghai called Waitan, often referred to as ‘The Bund’.

#### Sharp calls and nervous laughter exposed the tension that had accumulated amongst the sailors. After several weeks at sea, they could hardly wait to roam the streets and the shady dives located near the port of this vital city.

#### “Sir, excuse me. I will take the washing bowl,” said Manola, who was standing behind me. I was absorbed in thoughts and had not even noticed him entering. He looked at me questioningly and was visibly relieved when I nodded back and said that he could take the bowl. He moved hastily, and I saw a feverish glow in his eyes. This was for a good reason. His first objective when he reached land was to visit a bordello. He had declared this intention many times with euphoric anticipation, and now he could hardly wait.

#### For days, I was preoccupied with other thoughts, thoughts that lurked like vague shadows in my brain. I was seriously asking myself whether the

#### contact person who was supposed to pick me up at the pier would be there. Did this person know that we had arrived a day later than scheduled because of bad weather at sea?

#### I put on my dark gray suit and tried to make it fit properly, but to no avail. I had lost a huge amount of weight due to exhaustion and anxiety caused by seasickness. The suit that had been neatly folded in my suitcase had long awaited its employment, but now it hung saggy and shapeless over my weary frame.

#### Feeling woozy, I cautiously stepped over the swaying narrow footbridge and disembarked the ship. Finally, I felt solid ground under my feet. The captain waved to me from the command bridge, and I had a rush of mixed feelings as I walked alongside the rusty freight ship. Ian Mackintosh had never once asked me the reason for my journey, and I had great appreciation for his discretion.

#### I followed a sign with Chinese characters but also showing a bit of English writing. An arrow also indicated the way to customs and immigration. I was already glad that I had studied some Chinese for several months with my friend Professor Stark, and that I had thought to stick the Chinese dictionary he recommended in my luggage.

#### “How long will you stay in China?” the officer asked in a mistrustful tone. “Three weeks,” I answered in an awkward voice, as if the question had

#### called me back from a faraway dreamland. Shortly afterwards, I was standing out on the street with my suitcase. The

#### shock was extreme. It was as if an invisible force was squeezing my brain into a tight ball. I stood alone, suddenly engulfed in a bustling throng of human beings. My fragile health struggled to cope with the intensity. The impact threatened to trigger a breakdown then and there, or at least that is how it felt. Close to collapsing, I simply sat down on my suitcase.

#### For many weeks and for most hours during those days, I had been alone in my cabin, drifting on an ocean that refused to expose any land, or so it seemed. And now, here I was in the middle of a pulsing horde of humanity. The noise was unbearable. The dull lethargy still clinging to my body was my only sense of myself, and even that last trace of vitality was being sucked out of me. I was beyond miserable.

#### This grim, lifeless condition robbed me of any trace of identity. In its place, it imparted an odd feeling of anonymity. Two older men stopped and

#### scrutinized me with curiosity and bewilderment, as if I was a misplaced being from a distant star. A layer of gray clouds hung over the city on that rainy morning. Adrift in a thoughtless mind-state, I had completely lost track of my original purpose for coming to China.

#### “Mister Park, Mister Park.” A voice I had never heard before snatched me out of my semi-consciousness. My swollen eyes fell upon a small older man with a sinewy body. He was also barefoot. “Come along,” he said, and directed me to take a seat in the rickshaw that stood next to him. With a skillful lunge, he heaved the heavy suitcase onto the seat next to me. He then paused for a moment, grinned, and scratched his chin. After taking his place between the two long wooden poles with which he towed the cart, he pulled them up, and we were off.

#### The hardy but faded navy-blue jacket the driver wore was buttoned to the top. Beneath his round black cap, a narrow braided pony-tail swung back and forth over his collar.

#### “Where are we going?” I wanted to know. “Guesthouse,” was his curt answer. He also gestured to me that I should

#### hold onto my suitcase. We rode first through narrow, putrid-smelling side-streets, until we turned

#### into the Zongshan Road which follows the Huangpu River. The driver skillfully guided the rickshaw through a dense sea of bicycles, pedestrians, and hopelessly overfilled buses. We passed other rickshaw drivers, to whom he always offered a few remarks. I presumed his topic was the foreign passenger he had just picked up at the port.

#### Occasionally, a black limousine could be seen heedlessly forcing its way through the crowds. They transported members of the anointed upper-class who allowed themselves to be chauffeured through the city traffic. The drivers adapted their mannerisms to their stiff uniforms and were well aware of their privileged status. They drove into the heap of humanity without the slightest concern for anybody’s safety, causing many frightened walkers to jump out of the way.

#### The morning was cold and I began to shiver again. A group of drunk sailors groveled their way through the crowds. On the other side of the street next to the water, I noticed a sizeable pedestrian zone. It was a park with a long promenade lined with leafless trees, where hundreds of people old and young were doing their daily Tai-Chi practice. Their deliberately executed movements expressed tranquility and harmony, offering a glaring contrast to

#### the haste of the rushing masses. I had to think of the yin-yang emblem, symbolizing the unification and

#### coinciding eradication of all dichotomies. After a while, we turned into Nanjing Road. My breath stopped and I had

#### to rub my eyes to be sure my senses were not deluding me. I thought that the street that we had just left had to be the most crowded street in the city. But I was mistaken. What a scene! Thousands of people were moving up and down the streets on foot or on their bicycles; human traffic beyond imagination!

#### An image from early childhood arose in my consciousness. I remembered being at the edge of a forest with a friend and poking at an anthill with a stick. The resulting chaos was tremendous. That seething maelstrom sprawling in every direction was quite similar to what I saw here.

#### My senses were overwhelmed. After so many weeks at sea, I struggled to process the intensity of all this sensory input. Again, a stifling pressure condensed inside my head. I was exhausted and could hardly wait to arrive at the guesthouse and take refuge in my own private space. I was in dire need of recuperation from my travels.

#### I suddenly noticed that someone was tugging on my bag and trying to pull it out of the rickshaw. A smartly dressed Chinese man with a European hat and foxlike eyes was trying to separate me from my suitcase. Luckily, I had listened to the advice of the rickshaw driver and was holding tightly to both leather straps. Despite several strong blows to his fingers, this fellow had no intention of letting go of my luggage. But our little ruckus caught the attention of my driver, who swiftly turned and shouted at the thief. Many people were standing by and watching. Frightened by the fierce reaction of the driver, the man immediately released his grip on my belongings and disappeared into the crowd. The driver wore a triumphant smile and signaled to let me know that I had been wise to follow his advice.

#### After a long and strenuous ride, we turned into the Hubai-Lu Street and then into a small dark road lined with rundown houses. I began to seriously ask myself what kind of accommodations my host had arranged. I knew him only from a private exchange of letters over the previous year.

#### White-grey banners hung across the lane high up between the houses. The rough-trodden cobblestones were slippery from the drizzling rain, which made challenging work for my driver as we passed numerous squalid little shops. The smell of urine and feces was everywhere. These unpleasant odors

#### gradually crept through my nasal passages, prompting a spell of nausea which I could barely manage to control. Once again, I had the constant feeling that I was about to throw up at any second.

#### A shoemaker was sitting in front of his shop surrounded by a mound of old shoes. I could smell the mix of aged leather and old sweat coming from the pile. He acknowledged my driver as he cheerfully poured himself some green tea from a small canister, and my driver responded with a short bow as we drove by. A nightingale confined in a tiny wooden cage sang out a melancholy ballad, giving a special mood to the street life. Six men passed barefoot in a convoy with empty expressions on their faces. They were hauling heavily loaded baskets balanced with bamboo rods. Their teetering steps and the arched poles suggested that the loads were extremely full. I could hear the loud, agitated chortle of ducks from two of the baskets. They were on their way to the market, soon to face their inescapable demise.

#### The driver suddenly stopped and pointed to the house next to us. Above the entrance was a weathered sign upon which ‘Chefoo Guesthouse’ was written. I stood next to the rickshaw with consternation and attempted to suppress my disappointment. The guesthouse was anything but what I had imagined as my journey’s final destination. Then again, I had no choice. This was the place that my host had chosen for me.

#### An obese, out-of-breath Chinese man stepped out of the front door and indicated after a resolute handshake that I should go inside. “Welcome,” he said. “I am the owner of this house.” I was also welcomed by the unpleasant smell of mildew coming from old furniture in the small lobby. I soon noticed a large spittoon next to the reception desk, but was determined not to look at the contents, knowing my stomach would probably not be able to handle it.

#### The innkeeper ceremoniously opened the guestbook and showed me where to enter my name. I had to smile a bit at his proud manner, because the page was completely empty and I was the only guest. He gave a commanding call towards a room separated from the lobby by a curtain. An older boy popped out and stood for a moment as if rooted to the floor, staring at me with utter astonishment. He had obviously never seen a foreigner before. He then eyed the rickshaw driver who was standing next to me waiting impatiently for his money. I reached into my pocket and gave him almost double the fee we had agreed on. He thanked me several times, stuck the money in his left pocket, and exited in a hurry. Moments later, I could hear him running his rickety

#### machine up the street.

#### In the meantime, the owner of the hotel looked into the guestbook and read my name out loud with earnest enthusiasm. He then raised his head and told me his name was Shie Kein. He informed me that he was born and raised in the local neighborhood. The boy picked up my suitcase and carried it up a steep, creaking wooden stairway. We moved through a dimly lit hallway, and I immediately noticed the empty spittoons on the ground set next to the doors of each guestroom.

#### I finally was inside my room. The furnishings consisted of a shabby carpet, a narrow bed, a chair, and a table. The single window provided a view of the street below. There was already a thermos can filled with hot water on the table and a plate with small satchels filled generously with green tea. Behind a sliding panel I discovered a wooden sink with a mirror hanging over it. I laughed when I saw the surface of the mirror covered with grimy stains allowing only small, disconnected sections of my face to be seen.

#### Someone knocked on the door. It was Shie Kein. With a friendly smile and without saying a word, he handed me a bath towel and left. The towel was frayed and almost transparent. It was impossible to guess just how old it was.

#### As I got myself cleaned up, a question shot through my mind: when would my contact pick me up and bring me to the Lodge of the Golden Dragon? I had no idea. I re-read the parched letter once again to assure myself that he would vouch for my entry into the secret lodge. I was curious what awaited me there. The inner spiritual work was naturally familiar to me, but why hat someone called me to Shanghai? I knew that it must be important, and that it was very unusual for a European to be invited to this lodge. Hardly anyone in the world knew of its existence.

#### I sat on the chair and looked out the window again. As my eyes wandered down to the small street below, I thought, ‘Maybe I will be taken tonight!’ In order to distract myself, I began to read out of a book I had brought along which reviewed the various Chinese dynasties and their ancient traditions. But I was actually very tired and couldn’t concentrate on the text at all. Still, my attention did fall on one sentence quoting a Taoist sage: “Open the mystery of the golden blossom. It is the light of heaven, the light of the Tao.”

#### At some point, I must have fallen asleep on the chair. A loud knock on the door abruptly tossed me out of the world of sleep and back into the waking world. My first thought was that I was being picked up. But no, it was Shie

#### Kein. He wanted to know whether I was satisfied with my room, and then persistently tried to pull me into a longer conversation. I noticed his slyness. He was trying to coax me into explaining the reason for my stay in Shanghai. Vulgar curiosity bolted through his eyes. At some point he noticed that his strategies were getting him nowhere. He then said a gruff goodnight and left. Night had fallen over the city as I climbed into the bed and immediately fell asleep.

#### The next morning, the loud cries of children from the street woke me from a dreamless sleep. My first activity was to check the time. An unnerving realization came over me; my clock had stopped in the middle of the night. Cronos, the god of time, had cast a spell. For the time being, I was exiled into a timeless existence.

#### After I washed up, I immediately went down to the reception to ask whether someone had left a message. To my surprise, there was no one there. The entryway was barred and the shutters were closed on the one window that opened to the street. It was bizarre: I was actually locked into the guesthouse like a prisoner under house arrest.

#### Uncomfortable thoughts stirred in my mind. What if my contact came to pick me up but found the door locked? Though these doubts arose, I knew on a deeper level that I could trust the higher power that guides all of life.

#### I sat down on an old dusty sofa in the lobby and waited. Cracks around the window allowed daylight to enter, creating a strange atmosphere. The ocean of time seemed to move in its own rhythm within the bounds of this city, and I needed more time to acclimate.

#### I finally heard the turning of a key in the heavy lock of the front door. Shie Kien stepped into the lobby with two full shopping bags. When he saw me sitting there, he was so surprised, he let one of the bags fall to the ground. He was extremely embarrassed to have left me locked inside the hotel. He apologized and then told me why he had done it. There were plenty of walk-in thieves in this part of the city, thugs who would slip in anywhere and steal everything they could get their hands on. Thus, he never forgot to lock the door when he left the house. After telling me this, I understood his precautions very well and told him so. I also told him that I was expecting someone and asked him to inform me right away if someone asked for me.

#### I stayed in bed reading and sleeping the entire day. Shie Kein brought my

#### food to the room. I felt I couldn’t leave the guesthouse, because I had no idea when my contact would appear.

#### More days passed in this same manner. I waited and waited. Every morning, I would go down to the reception and ask Shie Kein whether someone had left a message for me, and each time he shook his bald round head with a negative reply. Time went by at a sluggish pace. Hours on the clock seemed to extend endlessly. This uncertainty began to wear on me, yet there was no option but to remain patient.

#### Then, on the dawn of my sixth day in Shanghai, I heard light footsteps moving through the hallway. I thought it would be Shie Kien, but it wasn’t. Someone slid a letter under my door and into the room, and again I heard steps moving away quickly. I hurried to the door and opened it but couldn’t see anyone in the hallway. My hands shook as I opened the letter which was sealed in yellow wax with the emblem of a small dragon.

#### The message was short: “Come to Hu Sing Teahouse tomorrow at eight o’clock.” I ran down the stairs and asked Shie Kein whether he himself had left the letter for me, or whether it had come by courier. He said no to both possibilities, and assured me that he had been at the reception the entire morning. Nobody had used the stairs. He was deeply disturbed when I told him that someone had slid a letter under my door a few minutes before. The circumstances were puzzling indeed. Shie Kein became very unsettled and spoke of invisible spirits. I went back to my room and was so excited that I could hardly sleep.

### The Lodge of the Golden Dragon

#### The next day, I was already down in the lobby at the crack of dawn. Shie Kein came in accompanied by a rickshaw driver he had organized. The driver looked at me for a moment and then went over to the spittoon, which he used with loud vigor. He then came back and asked, “Hu Sing Ding?” I nodded and followed him out to the rickshaw.

#### The city was already congested with early morning traffic. Thousands of people were commuting towards their workplaces. The driver pressed forward through narrow streets attempting to avoid the large boulevards. An ice-cold rain shower slapped down on the mass of people like blows from a whip, pursuing them mercilessly through the city.

#### After passing through the French Concession, we arrived at the Chinese Garden. There was a huge pond which served as home to uncountable goldfish, and in the middle of it was an elegant old wooden teahouse. A long footbridge zigzagged across the pond and led us over to the large round two-story building. It was believed that this configuration helped to keep away evil spirits, since such ghosts could only move along straight paths. I was heedful as I crossed over this uniquely constructed walkway, constantly sidestepping those who were leaving the teahouse. A colorful hexagonal roof hung over our heads like a huge fan. At each extended corner of the roof was a statue of a fearsome demon. These were the guardians of the teahouse.

#### Business was bustling inside. Tables were laid out in every crack and corner of the antiquated building. As I looked around, I saw that there were no free places. The chairs were filled mostly with older men wearing the same type of dark blue coat buttoned all the way to the top. Being the only European in the place, I was surveyed with cold distrustful glares.

#### I went up a thin staircase to the next level and saw a free table next to a window. I had hardly settled into my seat before a man arrived with a can of green tea and a cup. He set them both down and said, “Ni Hao”, and quickly went away again. I gazed out the window and observed the numerous people crossing the footbridge to enter the teahouse. It began to rain more forcefully. Heavy drops splattered down on the pond and united with the larger body of

#### water. I occasionally saw a fish jump up and attempt to snatch an insect from the surface of the pond.

#### The pleasant warm smell of tea was soothing as it wafted through my nose and down into my throat. I looked at the time; it was a few minutes after eight. Just at that moment a man suddenly appeared and asked, “Are you Mister Park?”

### Parasites from the Other World

#### I laid down on the bed and immediately fell asleep. Just like a film in the cinema, the curtain of the dream world was drawn back and strong images rose up within me. I had a vision of a luminous figure arising from the unfathomable depths of the celestial realm. It approached and handed me a golden chalice filled with blood.

The seven regions of my brain were suffused with radiant light, and I was the source of this light. This conscious mind-state seemed very familiar, as if an immense divine power that had been sleeping within me was awakened once again.

It was something tender and extraordinarily deep. It had captivated me from the first moment I saw her in the library and never receded. It seemed as if we had been walking together through the infinite revolutions of the stars from time immemorial, and forces of fate had separated us and brought us back together time and time again.

### Kings of Darkness

#### Sitting on the edge of my bed, I could see out through the window and look out over the street. I saw a woman creeping past the gray houses like a cat that had slept too long. Somehow, the moonless night produced an atmosphere that awakened strange ideas in my head. What a curious existence it is, I thought to myself, that I share and experience this life with innumerable creatures and living beings within this colossal living space called the world.

#### The fire in the hearth flickered restlessly, as if it was participating in my inner state of mind. Why was it so difficult for me to accept these feelings of happiness? Was it my fear of pain, the pain of being left alone again? Or was it the fear of disappointment after experiencing too much insensitive treatment and too many empty promises? Anxiety, insecurity, and mistrust were wrapped together and draped over my heart like a thick overcoat that I wanted to take off but couldn’t.

#### I lay down and tried to chase away my fear-ridden feelings, but it was impossible. Though I wanted to sleep, the thinker went on thinking without my consent.

#### It was already light outside when I found myself waking from a deep dreamless sleep. An uncommon stillness hovered over the city. I enjoyed this soothing tranquility which was only to be experienced on Sunday mornings, when people had time to recover from the bustle and stress of their workweek.

#### After breakfast, I wanted to write a letter. An organization had written me, calling itself The Association for the Protection of Ancient Cultural Heritage, and I wanted to respond. I had given a lecture for them two months before, and they were asking me to come again. Apparently, my talk on ancient Egypt and its grand architectural achievements had elicited considerable interest.

#### Oliver Brandon, a pharmacist with his shop just down the street, was the chairperson of the organization. His reputation in our neighborhood was

#### rather dubious. It was rumored that he earned extra money performing medical procedures which were evidently far beyond his competence. Although no one knew exactly what he was up to, he was referred to as ‘the quack’, though no one would say it to his face.

#### A few times each year, I would stop by the drugstore to get some medicine. I didn’t pay any attention to the rumors concerning Oliver Brandon, but he was certainly a unique person. He was always very polite, but he would contradict himself at times with some of the things he said.

#### We had run into each other a few months before in the library. He had observed that I took down several books from a shelf covering the old Egyptian dynasties. He came over immediately. We started talking, and this time it wasn’t about medicine. He looked very interested in the books I had chosen and stated his keen interest in lost civilizations from ancient times. The cult objects of those societies fascinated him most of all.

#### At the end of our talk I mentioned to Brandon that I had already presented some lectures on ancient Egypt. It was then that he told me about the fellowship he belonged to. Two weeks later, I received an invitation to attend a gathering of this society, with the request that I offer a lecture. Eventually, I did attend a meeting and give a talk. Brandon functioned as the chairperson for the group and introduced me to its members. He also sat in the front row during the lecture. The prodigious powers of the Pharaohs and their extraordinary buildings aroused considerable interest, and I had to answer many questions after my presentation.

#### It was not long after that evening that I had another quite notable encounter due to my affiliation with Oliver Brandon. A few days after my lecture at the fellowship, I found another invitation in my mailbox. This one came from a highly exclusive Gentlemen’s Club, which only allowed membership to traditional upper-class citizens of London. They invited me to give a talk on The Age of the Pharaohs, and I sent my acceptance in writing. On the arranged day, a chauffeur driving a black limousine came and picked me up.

#### The Gentlemen’s Club was located in the most upscale neighborhood in the city, where only well-established families and old aristocrats could afford to live. Inside the mansion, the rooms were tastefully decorated with luxurious furnishings. Interesting artifacts from around the globe were set out in the room.

#### Thick clouds of cigarette smoke filtered out the door of one of the salons. I went in and saw several well-dressed men sitting in large comfortable leather

#### armchairs. Some were engaged in political discussion, speaking in their refined highborn English, while others read the newspaper or took slow sips from heavy crystal glasses filled with Scottish malt whiskey.

#### The lecture was well-attended. While addressing the audience, one older charismatic gentleman attracted my attention. He stood before me and groaned while he stretched his back. “Mister Mongrave, thank you for coming by. I was quite upset by what happened yesterday. I still feel some strong pressure in my chest. Otherwise, I do feel better.”

#### “I’m glad to hear that,” I said. “I will stop by again in two weeks’ time. Perhaps the Egyptian writings will have arrived by then.”

#### I got into a red double-decker bus and arrived at the front door of the Romanowskis a half-hour later. I rang the bell and heard someone turning the key in the lock. The door opened and Natascha was standing there. Again the feeling of deep closeness was there immediately. “How wonderful that you visit us, Mister Mongrave. Unfortunately, my father is not here. He will return in the evening. But please, do come in.” As I looked at her, I was taken aback by her appearance. Her face was ash-colored and her body was extremely frail.

#### We sat down in the library upstairs on comfortable lounge chairs and remained silent for a long time. It was excruciating to look at her. Her health was clearly deteriorating. I gently took her hand in mine and held it firmly. It was warm, and the gift of her loving gaze permeated my entire being.

#### “My dear Natascha,” I began, the words bubbling out of my mouth, “You were constantly there in my heart and in my thoughts the entire time I was home. It feels as if our souls have melted into one. An inner voice is telling me that we have already met so many times amidst the infinite expanse of the stars.”

#### She nodded and said, “Yes, I feel the same way. The golden band apparently guides us beyond death and back to each other again and again.” Her words were absolutely electrifying. Her mention of the golden band

#### penetrated unlocked depths within my being. We stood on the last rungs of an endless dream ladder stretching through time, which we had surely ascended together through countless incarnations. We were now preparing to leave the ladder behind us once and for all and enter the mystery of no-birth, the pure light of eternity.

#### A rose-colored art nouveau lamp spread warm light through the room, enveloping us in a stillness which eclipsed time. Our tongues did not dare cause any disturbance within this divine silence. All sense of time outside our bond seemed to pass idly and go on endlessly. It was as if we could hear the rupturing of the old-world order, which seemed ready to collapse at any moment.

#### Natasha eventually asked me if I wanted a cup of tea, and I did. I sensed a great necessity to tell Natascha about my encounter with Sir Arthur Cunningham. She listened very attentively, but I noticed that my description affected her is a strange way. I asked her several times if I should stop, but she didn’t want me to and insisted that I tell her everything.

#### When I finished, she said in a tranquil voice, “Those are the mock kings of this world. They are full of cravings for power and always driven by selfish motives. My father has told me about such people. They are not aware how much a prisoner they are of their own spells. Their actions tarnish the inner world with gloomy colors. With the steely forces of their wills, they injure the divine order. Sir Arthur Cunningham is certainly not a bad person, but his mind is focused on the material side, and it seems he has succumbed to the fascination of controlling and guiding its tenets. Very few, Mister Mongrave, are able to ascend to a higher way of life and transcend the processes of time.”

#### I had not yet mentioned my episode with the black book but decided to change the subject. “I read in the paper that Mozart’s The Magic Flute is playing at the Royal Opera House. I would like to invite you and your father to see it.”

#### I could tell by the way her eyes lit up that my invitation was a bullseye. “You really make me happy, Mister Mongrave. I have always wanted to see this opera but never had the chance. I am sure my father would also be delighted by your invitation. He is visiting a friend at this time who is also a collector. Those two can talk for hours about the origins of art pieces and the different periods of history they come from. But he should be here soon.”

#### I was incredibly moved by her refined presence. It seemed all things past

#### were absent from her being, reduced to a nadir of zero. Our shared oneness dwelt in an unfathomable divine source. I saw in my innermost core how the old contaminated waters that still circulated in my soul were purified through heavenly light. The ‘not-self ’ was more apparent, and my physical body and everything that arose through it was that which I NEVER AM.

#### The doorbell sounded below. “That must be my father. I will go open the door,” said Natascha as she moved swiftly down the stairs. I could hear the warm, melodic voice of Sergej Romanowski speaking Russian as he greeted his daughter. As soon as she told him that I was there, he continued to speak with her in English as a courtesy to his guest.

#### I heard their steps as they came up and entered the library. “Mister Mongrave, how pleasing it is to see you. Natascha has already told me of your invitation to the opera. Thank you, we would like that very much.”

#### It had gotten quite late, and I soon said goodnight to the Romanowskis and headed home. The gentle whisperings of the evening breeze inspired me to go a part of the way on foot. The sweet throes of being in love had captured me in a tenuous web of my own spinning. I enjoyed those moments of subtle bondage like a sip of a superb wine. Although I didn’t want it to be true, my heart and all my thoughts were engrossed in generating oceans full of sentiments and yearnings, and then clinging to them. I felt like the leaf of a tree being swept along in a mighty torrent.

#### At the same time, I was painfully conscious that Natascha’s pure love bound itself to nothing and no one. She also knew very well that her time in a manifested physical body was approaching its end.

#### I laid down in bed and fell asleep immediately. A few moments later, I was traveling with my dream-body through pleasing illusory landscapes and experiencing uncontrollable fluctuations of feeling.

#### When I awoke the next morning, all my limbs hurt. A strong fever bound me to my bed. Its heat was like a purifying fire ravaging within, causing a large and very unpleasant cold sore to appear on my lower lip. I couldn’t trace the cause of the fever. It was as if during the night an invisible hand had set my blood on fire.

#### I was happy that my invitation to the Brandons was for the following evening. Hopefully, the strange fever would wane from my body by then. I managed to drag myself into the kitchen and cook up a pot of tea. Happily,

#### Clementine wasn’t coming that day, as she would leap into alarm whenever I wasn’t feeling physically fit.

#### Later, I sat in my armchair and attempted to have a look through the book that Natascha had passed on to me from the library the night before. She had already read the whole thing. My tired, swollen eyes read one line after the other, but the words only ricocheted on the outskirts of my mind, and I could absorb nothing.

#### A severe chill came over me. Wrapping myself in warm blankets didn’t help. The bed was drenched in sweat, while debilitating pains coursed through my entire body. My eyelids were as heavy as lead and remained shut whether I wanted them to or not. The medication I took numbed all my senses. I watched my body toss and turn in a fitful sleep from a distant inner vantage point.

#### But the next morning when I awoke, the fever had passed. I was still quite weak and forced myself to eat an adequate breakfast. I then hauled my sluggish body out the door and over to the park. My idea to take an invigorating walk actually became an agonizing ordeal, as I had to sit down every few yards to rest.

#### While I was sitting on a bench, a squalidly dressed old man asked me for a cigarette. He was bitterly disappointed to hear that I didn’t smoke and then asked for some money. The fellow pressed every button until he found my weak point. He told me he hadn’t eaten in two days and was hungry. I opened my wallet and put a banknote in his open hand.

#### I continued to observe the man and watched him move through the park. He begged for money from everyone he saw. Most of the people ignored him, but one older lady took a few coins from her purse and gave them to him. Just in front of the park exit, he approached a young well-dressed man who felt the need to defend himself and gave a hard kick to the beggar’s behind.

#### I encountered this old man again when I was on my way home. He was just coming out of a shop. He waved at me, holding a small bottle of liquor and smiling. He had evidently got himself some fluid nourishment with the proceeds of his begging rounds.

#### I went over to Brandon’s place early that evening. They had also invited their grandson John, whom they were very proud of. Years before, I had often seen him as a small child with his mother in the park. He had grown into a handsome young man, and his well-groomed Eton-English and impeccable

#### manners attested to the quality of his education. He had attended one of the most expensive prep schools in the country.

#### I had hardly had a look at Anne Brandon before she came at me with a large bottle filled with brown tonic. “Mister Mongrave, you look so pale and weak. Take this bracer, it will surely do you some good,” she said in a worried tone. I wanted to speak up and tell her that I was feeling better, but she was already holding up a large spoon filled with sweet, sticky syrup. “Now, open your mouth, Mister Mongrave,” she commanded. There was no point in resisting. I swallowed the pasty concoction without complaint, and Mrs Brandon disappeared into the kitchen to prepare our meal.

#### John took over the conversation and talked enthusiastically of his ambitions. A career in politics was his future goal. He also mentioned his mentor, a certain Sir Arthur Cunningham. The man had supported him financially and helped him find a good position in the Ministry of Economic Affairs. “I am very grateful to Sir Cunningham for all he has done,” said John. “But without my grandfather, who has known him well for many years, none of it would have been possible. He is the one who has paved the way.”

#### It was obvious that Mister Brandon had not reckoned with John mentioning his mentor by name. He was visibly distressed, and his anxious manner betrayed that he feared my reaction. I remained placid and said nothing, knowing that the topic of Sir Arthur Cunningham should be discussed more privately.

#### But I now understood how Cunningham and the secret society functioned. He was obviously the chairperson. Like a refined chess-player, he was recruiting his people and positioning them in influential posts according to their potential abilities. Of course, the mastermind wielding the real power was acting in the background, appearing in public as a benign, charitable soul. Oliver Brandon had assumed that John was about to ask me whether I also knew Sir Arthur Cunningham (indeed, he was about to!), but Mrs Brandon brought in the food in at exactly the right moment.

#### We played bridge after dinner, as was the ritual with the Brandons. It was interesting that John was such a bad loser. After each unsuccessful round, he lost his good manners, swearing loudly and exhibiting a surprisingly crude vocabulary. I observed Oliver Brandon as we played and wondered what was going on inside him. Ever since I had known him, he always wore the same type of collared shirt; only the color varied. Something was bothering him. He and his wife were always friendly and accommodating, and I liked them

#### both. But on this night there was something masked behind his sealed facial expressions. What was it?

#### When I left late that evening, Anne Brandon put a bottle of her tonic in my hand. Before I walked out the door, I had to promise her that I would drink some of it every day.

#### I was drowsy with exhaustion as I fell into bed. Church bells rang, signaling that it was midnight. The fever assailed my body once again, and I had to get up and lay a towel soaked in cold water over my head to cool myself. I lay down afterwards and was able to fall asleep quickly. Before I did, I visualized the face of Natascha. My aim was to bring her with me into the dream world.

#### But to my horror, that realm would be much different than I had hoped. I abruptly found myself standing in the large hall of a medieval castle, where approximately thirty monks had gathered. They were chanting ancient liturgies together in Latin. They all wore long black frocks with wide hoods raised up and pulled over their faces so their eyes remained hidden.

#### When the abbot entered, the chanting stopped. “Everything is prepared, my brothers,” he told them. “Follow me.” Although I was with them all in the room, I was not sure whether I actually belonged to the community of monks or was only an invisible spectator.

#### With hands folded in silent prayer, we moved down a long, cold stone stairway to a dungeon. With great effort, the abbot pushed open a heavy, musty-smelling door. We entered a large room lit with torches: the torture chamber. A henchman was lashing a man chained to a wall. Shrieks of pain and horrid groans filled the macabre, gloomily lit room.

#### When the henchman saw the monks, he paused and immediately approached the abbot, who gave him a brief command: “Bring the others.” The henchman nodded and went into another room. He quickly returned with a young woman and an older man. Their hands were tied with ropes, their clothes torn, and their bodies covered with open wounds. They had been subjected to unbearable cruelty.

#### The henchman forced the two of them to kneel before the abbot, who shouted at the woman, “You have confessed that you have the evil eye, that you are a witch.”

#### “I am innocent, O Lord. I beg your grace,” she pleaded. But the abbot ignored her words.

#### He turned to the man: “You have been accused of heresy and blasphemy,

#### and you have confessed. Do you wish to say something more?” The man remained silent.

#### While the monks prayed for these lost souls, the abbot spoke in a callous, authoritarian voice: “According to the laws of our church, you are to be burned at the stake. But I shall let mercy prevail, and I thus hereby sentence you to death by the ax. You shall be beheaded; only in this way can your souls be saved. The sentence is to be carried out immediately. Executioner, do your duty!”

#### A ghostly hush spread through the chamber. The young woman suffered from shock and collapsed, twisting to the ground. A bucket of cold water was poured over her head to retrieve her from her unconscious state. She screamed as she saw the henchman pick up a large ax. With brutal force, he pressed her down on her knees in front of a wooden block stained with dry blood. He then forced her to slant her head over it. Behind the block was a basket where her head would land after it was severed from her body. When she saw it, she fainted again.

#### The executioner waited for a sign from the abbot, who had turned to the side. For a few moments I could see the stern, angular features of his face. My spirit began to howl. It was Sir Arthur Cunningham who was responsible for delivering this soul over the threshold of death. His eyes were flickering with the exhilaration of power as he raised his hand. Using violent force, the henchman separated the head of the young woman from her body. Moments later, the same fate befell the older man.

#### I began to scream in horror, and my cries woke me from my sleep. But I continued to scream a while longer, until I was fully conscious that I was in my bedroom and in my bed, and that what I had experienced was only a petrifying nightmare.

#### It was pitch-black outside, but I was wide awake. I turned on the light and saw it was three o’clock in the morning. Afraid of returning to the same terrifying scene in my dream, I didn’t dare shut my eyes again.

#### I sat down in the kitchen and drank an herbal tea that was supposed to calm the nerves, but it did not entirely produce the desired effect. I sensed an unusual need to unite with the eternal light. Auspicious words surged up within from fathomless depths. Prayers were spoken. Luminous powers harmonized my interior being and erased the dull fears which had imbedded themselves through the forceful dream. This ghastly experience in all its intensity was impossible for me to evaluate or interpret. After about an hour,

#### my inner being calmed down and peace and quiet returned. The stillness of the night was agreeable. I stood at the window and

#### observed the shadowy outlines of the surrounding roofs in the soft moonlight. In those houses, sleeping bodies lay in their beds, and I wondered what they were all dreaming about.

#### I forced myself to lay down in bed once again, hoping to get a couple more hours of sleep. I wanted to attend the opera later that evening with the Romanowskis and didn’t want to suffer through it in an exhausted condition.

#### I awoke the next morning feeling remarkably rested, though a bitter aftertaste of the horrific nightmare still lingered. I tried not to focus any thoughts on that gruesome experience, knowing it would only resurrect and recharge those images again. I knew the key that enabled me to emancipate myself from the slavery of my own thought-world.

#### Certainly, there were still unconscious gaps in my awareness which prevented me from ascending to the unborn for good. The same old solidified internal constructs prevented this transfiguration. I sometimes thought of myself as a fish in the sea, constantly asking what water is and what he has to do to recognize it. It was as though every gateway to my previous lives had opened at the same time. Thousands of ugly faces were grinning at me, and thousands of beautiful faces as well. It seemed I was trapped in a bowling match controlled by a higher power, while the forces of fate rolled me through the universe from one life to another.

#### When I went to prepare breakfast, I saw that the cupboards and refrigerator were almost empty. There was no avoiding a trip to the nearby supermarket, so I went immediately. Bright sunshine greeted me as I left the house and walked down the street. There were children playing football in the back alley where the shop was located. They were daring enough to pass me the ball, and I made a neat pass back to them, which they found very amusing.

#### The stuffiness and glaring neon lights irritated me every time I entered the grocery store. As usual, I tried to get the shopping done swiftly and get back out in the fresh air as quickly as possible. But before that could happen, I received a slap on the bottom from a corpulent younger woman with curlers in her hair. She wanted to let me know that I was blocking her path through the aisle of the store. I excused myself for being in her way. She smiled back and said that it was no problem, and that she would be happy to invite me for a cup of tea. She said she lived there on the same street. I deliberately failed

#### to understand clearly what she was saying and fled to the fruit section. The shop was frequented by people of various nationalities. It smelled of

#### freshly-ground coffee, exotic spices, fruits, vegetables, laundry detergent, and perspiration. For as long as I could remember, the cashier was the same dark-haired woman, colorfully dressed and always in a good mood. An older man with severe asthma went by coughing. He offered a pleasant greeting and stood behind me in the queue.

#### The cashier flirted a bit with a young man and wouldn’t let herself be disturbed by the impatient people waiting in line. But the store manager came by and put a quick end to the fun. She apologized to her boss, and the young man’s pride appeared bruised as he quickly left the store.

#### I walked out with sacks full of groceries and headed home. After breakfast, I sat in my armchair and read the newspaper. A loud bang frightened me. A painting of an angel that someone had given me for a birthday present had fallen to the ground. I looked at the clock to see if I should start to get changed for my evening at the opera, but it was only ten after five. I still had enough time to get a new nail and hang the picture up where it belonged. I was happy it wasn’t damaged.

### Cultivation and Realization

#### I arrived dressed in my best clothes just before 7:00 p.m. at the doorway of the Romanowski family. I rang the doorbell and Sergej Romanowski invited me inside. He was wearing a dark, perfectly tailored suit. Something in his facial expression troubled me, but I had no idea what it was.

#### He spoke softly: “Come in. Let’s go upstairs.” I thought we would go into the library as usual, but he opened the door to another room that I had never entered.

#### “Go inside,” he told me in a grave tone. My breath stopped. Natascha lay there in a splendid evening dress with her

#### hands folded over her chest and her eyes closed. Her face appeared harmonious and serene. Next to her was an antique seven-arm candelabra with white candles burning.

#### “This afternoon at ten minutes past five o’clock, she left her mortal body forever,” Mister Romanowski said in a low voice. “With her last breath, she asked me to share a message with you. The voice of Mister Romanowski trembled slightly while he recited her words:

#### “‘Mister Mongrave, do not be sad. I have returned to my celestial home. The pure love that moves us is boundless and never chained to the material: this you know. You will live inside me eternally, in pure inseparable being.’”

#### After a moment of deep sorrowful silence, he spoke again: “She is a wonderful, mature, radiant soul. I will miss her physical presence.”

#### The picture on my wall had fallen to the ground at ten past five p.m. I had finally found the woman of my life, and in my head I was planning an unspoken future with her. Again the fool, I had once more fell victim to the enchantment of my own desires.

#### A burning pain flashed through every cell of my body, as if an invisible hand were wrenching my heart right out of my chest. It was the same suffering cry, the same pain I had known for millenniums: the feeling that a part of me was being shredded to pieces.

#### I felt deeply torn within, vacillating between profound despair and sincere gratitude for the short time I was allowed to share with Natascha. I stared at

#### her inanimate body stupefied and helpless. Something in me refused to accept the fact that she had left this world forever.

#### Mister Romanowski had noticed the depths of my pain and gently put his hand on my shoulder. “There is something which we can still do for her,” he said. “We will accompany her internally, so that she can enter the pure land without any hindrances.” We sat in silence, focusing the energy of our souls so that no dualistic influences in the other world could disturb or detain her.

#### We then stood and went next to the bed. Mister Romanowski spoke purposefully: “The pure light points the way for you into the sacred primordial mystery, the home of the divine masters. Like a radiant star, you are filled with heavenly power. All your illnesses are healed; everything worldly is extinguished. Go now, beloved daughter; enter the land of silence, the land of peace.”

#### We went to the library, where Mister Romanowski spoke once again: “Her soul liberated itself from its earthly body with a delicate tremor. It was gentle, effortless, just as the skin falls off an overripe piece of fruit. I will keep her body here for three days so that it is not exposed to any turbulence. In this way the final disengaging processes will be completed harmoniously. The body will then be passed on to fire.”

#### I was speechless. There were no appropriate words to tell Mister Romanowski how sorry I was. He was very composed. This event had not taken him by surprise. He had always borne in mind that this final hour would come. After his experience with his wife, this was all quite familiar.

#### I sensed that he wanted to be alone. The new circumstances of his life needed consideration. “I will go home now,” I told him. “If it is alright with you, I will telephone in two weeks. It is good to know that Natascha was able to return to her spiritual home. But I will miss her very much.”

#### “I will too, Mister Mongrave. I will too,” he replied. “But please come again in four days. We can share a meal together. I know a good restaurant nearby.”

#### He brought me to the door. There was an extraordinary silence permeating the entire house. He took my hand without saying a word. I sensed his deep sorrow.

#### I also felt that a deep friendship bound us in a way I had never known before.

#### The taxi driver was obviously drunk, and he knew that I was aware of it. He slurred as he informed me that he had drank a few beers with some lads at work, but in his case it wasn’t a problem. He claimed he was a very experienced driver and knew the city better than anyone. He desperately tried to engage me in conversation, but I had no inclination to speak. My heart was filled with grief and pain.

#### He suddenly turned his head to me in the backseat. Under his peaked cap I saw a spongy face with bushy eyebrows and a dark red pug nose.

#### “I’m not good enough for you. You can’t speak to me, huh?” he said in an aggressive tone, concluding his short rant with a vulgar clicking of the tongue.

#### “Stop the car right now! I’m getting out. You’re drunk!” I commanded. My directions were clear, but his reaction was quite contrary to my

#### instructions. He pushed down on the gas pedal like a crazy man and zoomed through the streets at racing speed. “You won’t leave the car until I get to your place,” he said with threatening hostility.

#### “That’s fine,” I said, trying to calm him down. I was more than happy when I was finally able to hop out of that taxicab.

#### I sat in the living room lost in thoughts as I stared at the cold ashes in the fireplace. I suddenly became frightened when an undesired and very unpleasant image waded through my mind. The ashes reminded me of Natascha’s cremated body; that is to say, of what would remain of it. I tried with all my might to banish these thoughts from my brain, which I was able to accomplish.

#### My heart was quarantined in profound grief. How I would have loved to follow Natascha on the wings of dawning light into our spiritual home. It was evident that the moment I was to become as light as a feather and transcend the forces of creation had not yet arrived. But the mysterious maturation-process of transforming the blood was completing itself in faultless splendor within me. The fruit would eventually ripen; of this I was certain.

#### Once again, my glance wandered over to the gray ashes. But this time another symbol arose in consciousness, namely the phoenix, the mystical bird that is resurrected from cold ashes and ascends to new life. But I soon returned to feeling lethargic and lost in self-pity. Although my stomach growled, I wanted nothing to eat. I should have been enjoying those hours with Natascha and Sergej Romanowski at the opera. But the powers of fate

#### had switched my path into a very different and unforeseen direction. I dragged myself off to bed and closed my eyes, hoping I would never have

#### to open them again. Tears wanting to flow lost their momentum before they could come out. They turned back and streamed inwards, flooding my heart.

#### I didn’t know whether I was asleep or awake, when suddenly a tremendous calm came over me. I saw Natascha in radiant light, and I perceived a message emitted by this light:

#### “Do not dwell too deeply in sorrow. Soon, a wonderful and important task will be given to you and my father which will be very gratifying and will bestow new meaning to your life.”

#### Dazed and amazed, I opened my eyes and looked around. I knew with great certainty that this was no ordinary dream. Natascha had returned briefly to release me from my wretched state and to reveal that there would soon be profound changes in my life.

### A New Vision

#### I forced myself to eat something the next morning, though I lacked any appetite. I contemplated what assignment might lie ahead for me and Mister Romanowski, but had no idea what it could be.

#### An insatiable longing to touch the deepest core of spiritual freedom continued to burn within me like a thousand suns. There had to be some elixir that could ultimately enable me to cross the threshold of space and time. I had read many books and heard lectures from people with great expertise in humanities and the natural sciences. But the fundamental force that had generated birth and death within my being over thousands of years still held me tightly in its grip. I was thankful for the profound friendship which united me with Sergej Romanowski in the depths of our souls. Our bond was a flowing source of wisdom, inspiration, and spiritual energy that would never run dry.

#### A bizarre, blurry feeling came over me. It was the first time I felt like a guest in my own apartment. The walls were too close together, the ceiling far too low. Did this strong sense of being crammed into my own apartment, even caged in, originate from my fervent desire for inner freedom? My life seemed a paradox. Forces of destiny had driven me through excruciating ordeals, but also to the borders of pure light. The old knots of the personality had begun to untangle themselves, and with the loosening of these ties, the perpetual burden of death yielded as well.

#### But how many disappointments were still waiting like gargoyles in front of destiny’s gates, ready to set fire to my insides and tear them apart? At times I wanted run away from this life, but I was always aware that I alone am responsible for my life’s path, and ultimately for the powers of fate that guides it. I knew as well that transcending time, space, life, and death would be the ending of these unpredictable forces. The personality is the designer and manufacturer of these strong, time-restricted energies, which construct and maintain the entire superimposed world. Thousands of incarnations, like the invisible rings of a chain, had molded my present existence. These cumbersome links to space and time would not allow themselves be stripped

#### away easily.

#### Before going to bed that evening, I settled my exhausted body into my antique, four-footed bathtub. I poured some herbal essence in and looked at my naked body through the greenish water. The heat and the herbs were so relaxing that I fell sound asleep and woke up an hour later in cold water.

#### I got into bed as quickly as possible, but there was no chance of sleeping. The movie screen in my mind was filled with dramatic scenes manifesting in numerous locations. Although I was only an audience to these happenings, it was still impossible for me to shut off the internal projector. Fragmented thoughts rambled through my mind uncontrolled, spreading like multiplying cells within my midnight-consciousness.

#### At some point in the middle of the night, I was still wide awake and felt the need to get some fresh air. I decided to go for a walk through the empty streets around my neighborhood. I enjoyed the tranquility of the city at night. A cool breeze blew across my face, so I pulled up my collar and began to walk faster. It was an odd stillness. The pale moonlight broke through the layer of clouds which were slowly being pushed north by the wind. I could hear my breathing and the echoes of my footsteps reverberating against the walls of the houses. Only every second street lantern was lit up during these hours; one of the city’s cost-cutting measures. Thus, the dim light they shed over the sidewalks was interspersed with very dark zones where night expanded over the space like a black curtain.

#### The loud, unruly voice of a woman cut through the silence. A few moments later, a lady with her husband and six children emerged from the shadows. I caught a glimpse of them standing very close to each other under a lantern up ahead of me. The children appeared tired and deprived. Their father, dressed in shabby rags, groveled in front of his stout, domineering wife, who carried two worn bags in her shovel-sized hands.

#### “You bonehead, you moron, you good-for-nothing,” she jeered at her sickly man. He shriveled under her pounding words, not daring to speak up for himself. Though appalled and intimidated, all the children still clung to the mother’s broad skirt.

#### “Come along right now!” she roared. Spiritless, humiliated, and with head hung low, the man followed his family through the dark coolness of night.

#### I passed by the thrift shop on the way home. I looked through the window into the interior of the shop which was covered in darkness, but I could still

#### see the wraithlike figures of the old marionettes outfitted in colorful garb. They dangled on thin threads connected to a special wooden fixture, patiently waiting for a puppeteer to bring them to life. Their almost-human faces were faintly illumined by a bluish light from the lanterns which cast a spooky tint onto their pale pointed faces. It seemed that if only a little life were breathed into them, they would bear their own independent existence in an irrational, parallel world.

#### Fatigue crept into my limbs. When I was home again and in bed, my brain involuntarily produced an abstract world of marionettes. I had taken impressions from the shop window into my dream world. In this dimension of my imagination, I had breathed life into the puppets, creating a menacing vision. It was fascinating but also frightening, and I was glad when I awoke the next morning in familiar surroundings.

#### Two day later, I decided to make my planned visit to the British Museum. I was about to leave my apartment in the early afternoon when someone knocked on the door. There were small guesthouses, junk dealers, fabric stores, tobacco shops that also sold lottery tickets, and vegetable shops with cows standing in front of them, waiting to gobble up unsold goods thrown out onto the street. It was remarkable to see one of the cows in stoic serenity eating up an entire newspaper, apparently with great pleasure. “You’ll get special alphabet milk from her,” Mister Romanowski joked.

#### Two boys from the slums in torn shirts who had crossed the river took hold

#### of one of the cows and dragged her to a dark corner behind a house. One of them proceeded to milk the cow with surprising skillfulness, while the other one, visibly tense, acted as a lookout. What they were doing was dangerous. If the police or anyone else caught them, there would be dire consequences, and they knew it.

#### After a while we came into a wealthy part of town and drove by a large well-groomed park. Old trees lined the streets providing shade. Among them was a century-old banyan tree. The large whitewashed villas in this neighborhood were mostly hidden; shielded from the outer world behind high walls.

#### The driver turned into a gateway that stood wide open. We approached a stately two-story house which was surrounded by red flowering trees. This was the home of Sri Ventakaraman. The front door of the house opened just as we were getting out of the car. With quiet footsteps and hands folded together, Sir Ventakaraman approached us and said, “Namaskar, welcome to our home!” His voice carried a mild meditative calm. “You are Mister Mongrave. I am very pleased to make your acquaintance. I have heard that this is your first visit to India. The ancient transcendent radiance of this country will certainly provide you with deeper spiritual insights.” He turned to Mister Romanowski and said, “It has been a long time since we last saw each other. Please excuse me that I could not meet you personally at the airport. I have just arrived from Calcutta last night. But please come in.”

#### Sri Ventakaraman was a neatly groomed older man with snow white hair combed straight back. His brown face had no wrinkles. He wore traditional clothing: a white lunghi (long waistcloth) and a blue and white shirt. There was something honorable about him, an inner beauty that projected outwards.

#### The slamming of a car door tore me out of my thought-world. It also prompted me to turn my gaze back before entering the house. Through the dusty windshield of the car, I saw the driver grinning at me. He communicated that he would stay and take a nap until we came out again.

#### We took off our shoes in the cool foyer and entered the living room barefoot. I was surprised to see how spartanly furnished the room was. When we were seated, the wife and daughter of our host came in with folded hands and greeted us. “Unfortunately, they only speak a little English,” explained Sri Ventakaraman.

#### I had to check myself to avoid staring at the two women in a disrespectful way. Their gracious presence caused me to completely forget where I was.

#### His wife was clothed in a modest dark green sari, her fine temples lined with silvery hair. Her open, kind-hearted face revealed a life of fulfillment, and her natural religious humility reflected an internal beauty which permitted one to recognize the true divinity of a human being.

#### The daughter, standing shyly behind her mother, wore a sky-blue sari with delicate gold embroidery. A garland of fragrant jasmine blossoms was tied to the back of her long dark hair. A soft endearing light gleamed through her eyes. Her quiet charm and unobtrusive bearing touched me in an unusual way, and very deeply. A feeling of fondness free of any thoughts streamed from my heart. Her simple presence had aroused this flowing affection in me, which I found astonishing.

#### Our hostess invited us into another room and informed us that food was prepared. When we sat down at the table, both women stood behind us and gave instructions to the cook. I couldn’t understand why they weren’t sitting down with us. But when Sri Ventakaraman left the room for a moment, Mister Romanowski explained to me the rules of hospitality in this country.

#### After eating, the women went off by themselves and the men moved to the back of the house where we sat in comfortable armchairs on a small covered veranda. Night had fallen over India. Frogs sitting on the leaves of lotuses croaked from a pond close by. Myriad stars glittered in the dark blue sky like silver flower buds. For a while we sat listening to the sublime serenity of night, which had absolved itself from day and covered the world in silence. This silence dispelled my inner agitation and expanded through my over-stressed nervous system like a soothing potion. A warm breeze from the sea nearby carried pleasant spicy scents with it, doing its part to help me feel more comfortable.

#### Sri Ventakraman sat peacefully in full-lotus posture on a wide stool, and Mister Romanowski also seemed very relaxed. The cook brought us chai and left the room with a friendly smile.

#### We continued to sit without speaking, engulfed in a current of silence flowing inward. It seemed as if this exalted silence was dissolving all borders of an inner and outer world. I had assumed that the two of them had a great deal to talk about after not seeing each other for such a long time. But the opposite was true. Nothing trivial moved their tongues. They were old traveling companions in the truest sense and knew neither separation nor distance.

#### The chirping of countless cicadas began to fill the balmy night. Sri

#### Ventakarman meditatively lit a small oil lamp and put it on the table. He then spoke: “A few days ago in Calcutta, I was informed that the masters are residing in the north. I do not know exactly where. But I know someone in Benares you can contact. He can certainly help you. I will give you the address and write a note for you as well. That should make the meeting go more easily. You already know the person, Mister Romanowski. His name is Bhaktananda Maharaj. He lives in the old city of Benares near the Ghats.”

#### A flash of deep familiarity arose within me. This country was not foreign after all. Undoubtedly, I had wandered across the holy earth of this country in an earlier life. We sipped our heavily sweetened chai without hurry. Sri Ventakaraman spoke again: “There are many, many pilgrims on their way to the holy city. You will probably have to wait a few days, since flights to Benares in this season are usually filled. If you like, I can ask at the airline office tomorrow. I know some people there who can help you get the earliest flight available.” We gladly accepted his offer and thanked him.

#### Mister Romanowski spoke: “Later, I would like to visit the Theosophical Society in Adyar again. It has been many years since I was there.”

#### “That is a good Idea. I would be happy to join you. I too have not visited there in a long time,” replied Sri Ventakaraman. He wanted to say something else but hesitated, as if he had a strange forewarning. “How is your daughter, Mister Romanowski?” he asked.

#### The question instantly generated a sharp pain in my heart. A bleak shadow came over my soul and tried to settle itself there. But I wouldn’t allow it. I had no more interest in being pulled down into desolate inner abysses.

#### Mister Romanowski replied in a composed voice, “A few weeks ago, Natascha left her ill body behind. She was well prepared for the moment. Her pure soul was free of all images and objects and transcended directly into the everlasting light. Like nature itself, she was empty. And like nature as well, she was filled with the eternal.

#### “A few months before, Natascha met Mister Mongrave. Through this meeting she was able to fulfill her destiny on earth. Through him, her soul was made innocent and the forces of procreation were silenced. She had returned to the spirit of the depths and was able to see her final destination.

#### “As fate would have it, Mister Mongrave recently met Mister Wang again. They had known each other from earlier times in the Lodge of the Golden Dragon in Shanghai.”

#### Sri Ventakraman listened very calmly. His eyes were luminous, reflecting

#### the soft moonlight. He took a sip of tea before he began to speak again: “It is important that the members of the universal lodge find each other again in all periods of time, on all continents. In this difficult phase of human evolution, new celestial foundation stones of the grand cosmic structure are being integrated, in which the eternal is reflected.

#### “The instructions and blessings of the completely realized masters will always be important. The shadow-worlds in the hearts of human beings are becoming very dense. They obscure the spirit. Very few empty their hearts and are detached from fears and desires. Very few preserve this divine emptiness and no longer orient themselves in the external world.”

#### The veil dance performed by a slow-moving array of clouds temporarily covered the golden crescent moon. Small shadows scurried through the garden and danced over bushes and trees. The power of darkness conveyed something secretive. It had slowed the pulsation of the world and enveloped it in silence.

#### “It is late. I think we should be making our way,” said Mister Romanowski in a whisper, as if he didn’t want to disturb the tranquility that night had bestowed to the creatures in the garden.

#### I was ready to go as well. I couldn’t shake off the fatigue that had settled in my limbs and was having trouble keeping my eyes open. The driver was startled to death when Sri Ventakaraman gently knocked on the car window and woke him from his deep sleep. “Drive my guests back to their hotel and then you can go home. Give my best regards to your family.”

#### When I got back to the hotel room and was lying on my bed, my thoughts wandered back to three words that Sri Ventakaraman had uttered outside on the veranda. When he spoke of a “completely realized master”, I directly experienced a sort of elevating of the soul. I was permeated with intense joy and could feel the unfathomable, supra-personal presence of the masters deep within my entire being. I had heard a voice in my heart: one that spoke without words.

#### I was tired and wanted to sleep, but an uncontrollable stream of thoughts prevented it. It was not possible for me to impede the current of images or bring this externally oriented movement to a standstill. Without intending to do so, I contemplated on how I always reacted in the same way to certain people and situations. Over the course of millenniums, my thoughts and ways of seeing were conditioned and programmed by an enormous compilation of diverse experiences. According to these superficial processes which I myself

#### had set in motion, my inner being was constantly searching for possibilities of adaptation and alignment with conventional views within the transitory world order. In this life I had unconsciously assimilated into a kind of collective world uniformity.

#### But now, these old influences had finally lost their power over me. The vibration of the personal will had become much smaller, and the luminous powers that govern all of life were able to gradually reveal themselves in all their splendor.

#### I woke up the next day very relaxed. It was still early, so I enjoyed the peace that had expanded over my heart like a gown of light. The noise from the street below no longer disturbed me at all.

#### When I went down through the small hotel lobby, Mister Romanowski and Sri Ventakaraman were already there to greet me. “Did you sleep well?” inquired Sri Ventakaraman. “I have called the airline office, and they have assured me that someone will deal promptly with your flights and contact me as soon as everything is arranged.”

#### He continued, “We could then go directly to Adyar and take our breakfast there. I know a small restaurant near the Theosophical Society.”

#### During the ride, Sri Ventakaraman told me some things about colonial times in India and spoke with the highest reverence of Mahatma Gandhi, whom he had met personally and admired greatly.

#### After more than an hour’s drive through the dense traffic, we arrived in Adyar and found the quaint European style restaurant. We were the only guests there. We had just started eating when the door opened and an old, haggard sadhu in the traditional ocher-colored robes of a monk stepped in. He limped and used a long wooden stick for a cane. He was visibly weak and coughed constantly. He had long knotted hair with an equally matted beard, and his emaciated body suggested serious health issues. Without saying anything, he approached our table and stuck out his empty bowl. Sri Ventakaraman didn’t hesitate for a moment and put some coins in it. The Sadhu turned and hobbled out the door without thanking him or showing the slightest reaction.

#### Sri Ventakaraman told me that these ascetics have taken a vow which requires them to give up everything worldly. “They subject themselves to the most rigorous disciplines to achieve final liberation – ‘mukthi’ or ‘moksha’. In India, all those who forsake the world in order to seek ultimate liberation

#### are supported in some way or another so they are not forced to concern themselves with worldly things. Thus, they can follow the path out of darkness and mortality into light and immortality without any obstacles.”

#### We were soon standing at the large gate in front of the Theosophical Society. The facility lay along the shore of a river and extended all the way to the sea. A watchman in uniform came out of his small guardhouse and blocked the lane. “Are you members?” he wanted to know. His manner was rigid and his voice was harsh.

#### “No, but we have a letter of recommendation from Mister Patel,” answered Sri Ventakaraman. After the guard had read over the document, he opened the gate for us. He reminded us that we were not allowed to enter the temple, since we were not members of the society.

#### The moment we entered the expansive property of the Theosophical Society, it seemed we had slipped behind an invisible curtain and arrived in a land of peace and quiet, a land where time no longer existed. The sacred silence was palpable.

#### We walked along a narrow asphalt road lined on both sides with lush tropical vegetation. Whitewashed bungalows stood on small platforms. Padlocks sealed their windows and doors. There were no humans to be seen. The buildings emitted an uninhabited, forgotten feeling. The empty houses had become mute witnesses to an illustrious past.

#### We approached the temple, a majestic white structure that looked like an edifice out of Greek antiquity. The other houses were erected in classical English colonial style. An intimation of earlier historical periods engulfed this magical place. I also sensed the advanced powerful energy-field which radiated from the temple. For decades, concentrated spiritual research, rituals, and secret initiations had taken place here.

#### Some comfortable reed chairs were set out in the shady portico of the temple which was buttressed by huge pillars, and we sat down for a while. Sitting there, I looked up to the ceiling and saw colorful flags from all the countries which housed branches of the Theosophical Society.

#### A stone-carved statue of Gautama Buddha in perfect lotus posture was set in an alcove next to the temple entryway. The gentle harmonious face and the introverted smile affirmed his absolute freedom and boundless silence. He was no longer affected by things formed and constructed, things born and becoming.

#### We strolled past the temple and came upon a huge Banyan tree. The roots

#### and offshoots had taken over an enormous space. A sign proclaimed that this was the largest banyan tree in the world.

#### Further along our path, we sat down on a bench which was located under more large old trees. Their long branches and thick foliage provided cool shade, and I allowed the serenity of the place to sink in more deeply.

#### As we approached the large building where the dining room and lodgings were located, I was amazed that there was still no one around. But then came a quick surprise. I almost stepped on a man sleeping on the ground in front of the open door of the dining hall. He stood up with a smile and greeted us cheerfully. “Please feel free to look around. There is isn’t much to see,” he said, and laid down once again. The two long wooden tables, the neatly stacked silverware on the shelf, and the smell of curry gave evidence that the dining area was in daily use.

#### Framed photos of H.P. Blavatsky and Annie Besant, of Charles Leadbeater, Major Olcott, and Jiddu Krishnamurti hung on the walls. These were the people of great significance who had lived at the Theosophical Society and left their mark. It was here that they endeavored to bring light into the darkness of the sunken world. These pioneers were truly the spiritual secret agents of the universe. They ventured into dark waters so that the spring at the original source would flow once again.

#### A bust of Leadbeater was set out prominently across from the library and stared directly towards the entrance. In one of the rooms of the library we encountered a young Japanese woman and two older American men. They were very concentrated on their studies of old transcripts.

#### We were back in Madras by late afternoon and picked up our plane tickets on the way back. Two seats for an afternoon flight were reserved for us.

### The Holy City

#### We drove to the airport early the next afternoon with Sri Ventakaraman. As we said goodbye, he gave Mister Romanowski a piece of paper with the address of the contact person in Benares and told us, “Bhaktananda Maharaj lives in the old city of Benares, which is like a huge labyrinth. But you know this, Mister Romanowski, you have been there several times before. Ask a rickshaw driver. He will bring you close to the house where he lives.”

#### When we were settled in the airplane, I realized something and was suddenly alarmed. From the moment of arrival in India, I hadn’t wasted one thought about my life in England. It was as if my existence there had been completely erased.

#### “Look below. That is the Ganges,” whispered Mister Romanowski. I gazed through the small round window and saw a broad current slowly moving through the arid landscape.

#### The small airport in Benares had its own special charm. It was a relief to discover that it wasn’t as hot or humid here as in Madras. A large group of men hurried past us as we left the airport, calling the name of a man that had just gone out the exit door. They literally stalked him until they could lay their bright flower garlands over his neck. The man didn’t seem particularly impressed with the reception. “What a scary welcome,” I thought to myself.

#### “Come, Mister Mongrave,” said Mister Romanowski, “there is a taxi up ahead. The driver must certainly know a good hotel near the Ghats.” And he did. The driver had a brown cloth tied around his head and I had to chuckle, recalling a similar fashion among the old ladies at the market in my London neighborhood. He examined us with eagerness and revealed his almost toothless smile. And again, our driver was seriously convinced that he was the best driver in his city. To make the claim more believable, he casually hung his right arm out the window. This gesture had a practical aspect: the blinkers of his car didn’t work. But he told us this was not a problem.

#### Our driver passed slower moving busses filled with people, sometimes to the left and sometimes to the right. In fact, he grazed an oxcart and almost slammed right into a cow meandering along the road. The traffic in this city

#### was incredibly thick and chaotic, but amazingly, it all seemed to function for everyone.

#### The ride seemed endless, but we finally made it to the outer lying district of the old city. I was thankful when we could finally get out of the car. “Come, I will bring you to the best hotel in the old part of the city,” said the driver. We followed him through small narrow streets where humans, goats, and water buffalo were also roaming around. We finally came to our destination and found ourselves standing at the entrance of a seedy guesthouse. Maharaj Palace was written on the sign over the door. “A very good hotel!” our driver assured us more than once.

#### The scheming pretense of the hotel manager was already written clearly on his face. It took some negotiating before we could agree on an acceptable price for the one room that was still free. Sergej Romanowski had asked me whether it was inconvenient to share a room with him for a few days, but I was fine with the arrangement.

#### The large damp room was a lot like a dormitory. Six beds were crammed together in a space that was otherwise undecorated. The hotel manager had heaped the highest praise on this room, as if we were entering the main suite of a five-star hotel. A family of geckos eyed us with curiosity from the ceiling. The manager added his perspective: “Geckos are good house pets. They eat the mosquitos.”

#### “We could freshen up and then try to find Bhaktananda Maharaj,” suggested Mister Romanowski. An hour later, we stopped a bicycle rickshaw at the upper end of the old town. The skinny, spindly-haired driver made enough gestures to communicate that he knew the address Mister Romanowski was showing him. We were trusting enough to take seats in his wobbly contraption. He pedaled patiently through the traffic, calling to other rickshaw drivers as he passed by as if he were asking questions. We had already sensed that he had something to hide, and it was soon clear that the old fellow had no idea at all where we wanted to go. He finally dismounted from his bicycle with a forced smile and led us into a small shop where we could buy a city map. The shopkeeper made it clear that not all streets in the old city were included on any map. He suggested we continue our search on foot. Mister Romanowski paid the rickshaw driver nevertheless, and the old chap was visibly relieved. He thanked us and dashed out of the shop.

#### Benares was utter craziness. The narrow streets of the old city were completely overfilled by the many human beings and lingering cows, who

#### also seemed to be on their early evening strolls. The streets were lined with small restaurants and shops pressed up against each other. The noise of the traffic and human voices echoed through the extended labyrinth of tiny streets. One heard bargaining, discussion, and laughter coming from the shops. Cows made their way through the masses of people and checked every recess for something edible. The uneven, rock-strewn road was soiled with cow patties, which made our path slippery.

#### Several women were sitting on the ground in an open space. With their dexterous fingers, they were tying together garlands of fragrant jasmine flowers which would serve as offerings for the regiment of gods that resided in the many surrounding temples. Next to them was a shop that sold pictures of those gods. These representations would be hung up in living rooms and placed on altars. Ganesha, Lakshmi, Sarasvati, Shiva, Lord Subrahmanya, Vishnu, Durga, Sri Krishna, Murga, Parvati, and many others were portrayed in all their splendor, framed, and awaiting a buyer.

#### Children were sitting in freestanding spaces behind large baskets filled with fruit. They caught our eye and raised their baskets up to us in joyful excitement, hoping for a sale. The sweet smell of incense sticks wafted from many of the shops. We were constantly asked where we came from and why we were there.

#### Loud drumming and the ringing of bells from nearby temples filled the air. The priests were performing their pujas, calling upon the divine powers and blessings of the godheads, so that those present could experience and feel them. These complex rituals are thousands of years old.

#### A larger boy wanted to be social with us and approached enthusiastically: “Come along. I will show you the golden temple, and then we can go to the silk factory. You don’t have to buy anything, just look.” It took a while to convince the obstinate boy that we had no time. When I asked him whether he shouldn’t be in school, he gave a mischievous smile and said, “I have free today.”

#### “Mister Mongrave, the house where the Maharaj lives must be close by,” said Sergej Romanowski as he showed me the long street we were following on the city map. It led all the way down to the Ganges. “It is best if we ask around whether someone knows him,” he suggested. We went into a fabric store, and there we saw three older gentlemen sitting barefoot on colored pillows having a conversation. One of the men stood up and greeted us in a

#### friendly manner. We figured out that he was the owner of the place. He invited us to sit down and drink a coffee. His eyes started to sparkle with hopes of profitable business.

#### Before we could get around to explaining what we were doing there, the shop owner quickly pulled down bundles of fine silk from the shelves and rolled them out under our feet. He raved about the quality of his goods, telling us that we had really come to the right shop and that no one else in Benares sold such exquisite silk.

#### “Excuse me,” Mister Romanowski finally cut him off, “but we don’t want to buy anything. We are looking for one Swami Bhaktananda who lives in the area. We were hoping that you might be able to help us.” Mister Romanowski then passed him the note with the address written on it. The disappointment of the salesman didn’t last long. He grabbed the paper and reached into his white Ali Baba pants. He then pulled out some old metal-rimmed spectacles from a deep pocket and put them over his nose.

#### “Swami Bhaktananda,” he mumbled to himself and showed the address to the other men. Soon they were all standing and staring at the paper together with great absorption. It turned out that each of them knew a Swami Bhaktananda, or had at least heard of one. But the Swami they thought we were seeking didn’t live in Benares anymore. He had moved to another city. One of them supposed that Swami Bhaktananda had immigrated to the United States. Mister Romanowski thanked them all for their help. We left the shop and continued our search through the old city.

#### Mister Romanowski had a sudden intuition and stood still. “It must be here. I remember this small shop where they sell fruit juice. We walked by it before, but we didn’t see that little side street behind it.”

#### We turned into a dingy narrow road which was very damp. We came upon a mother who was busy washing her small child on a stone slab next to an open drain. The little boy screamed while she poured pitchers of cold water over him to rinse the soap from his body, while five other children looked on entertained. A grandmother was nearby, removing lice from the hair of a small girl sleeping on her lap.

#### The intensity of these strange scenes was a strain on my perceptive faculties. There was no time to process the frenzy of impressions which invaded my senses. Everything that had happened that day could not be integrated in my rational view of the world, even though I felt intimately connected to where I was.

#### We eventually found the house and pushed on a frail door that squeaked as it opened. We followed along a poorly lit hallway that smelled of mold until we reached a steep stairway leading up to the second floor. The door above was slightly open, but Mister Romanowski still knocked before we entered.

#### The small room was empty except for a raffia mat on the ground. Bhaktananda Maharaj was sitting relaxed on the mat in perfect lotus posture, absorbed in deep meditation. He was dressed in a long ochre robe like all those who had renounced the world. His long thin white hair, lengthy beard, and broad ash-covered forehead were confirmation of his long, focused journey inward.

#### He had retracted his senses and retreated from this world. His body didn’t stir in the slightest. Silence and peace filled the room. Nothing and no one could have disturbed his meditation. Even if the entire world crashed down on him, this event would have had no effect on him. Something extraordinarily sublime radiated from his being.

#### We waited patiently until the evening had extracted the mild sunlight from the holy city. It was then that the Maharaj emerged from his internal absorption. He opened his eyes and said in a calm voice, “I have expected you. I know that you want to meet the masters. They will receive you. At this time they reside in a small monastery at the foot of the Himalaya. I never leave the holy city and it is not my job to take you to them. But I have organized someone who knows the way. Tomorrow at 5:00 p.m. you must be at the Tibetan monastery in Sarnath. Lama Geshe will meet you and bring you to the masters.”

#### I was amazed. Our journey was already prepared in advance for us. Truly, a higher power was laying out the path. Bhaktananda Maharaj told us what we needed to know and nothing more. Sitting there looking at us, his serene gaze was timeless. He bowed cordially before shutting his eyes and returning to deep meditative silence.

#### Soon we were back in streets overflowing with human beings and animals. Night had wrapped itself over the city. Mister Romanowski suggested that we go down to spend some time at the Ghats: long stone stairways built in ancient times. These rows of steps extended along the shore of the Ganges for a considerable distance. Millions of pilgrims from all over India were packed together at this sacred mythological spot next to the riverbank. It is believed in Hinduism that to bathe in the holy Ganges River while in Benares frees

#### one from all sins. Several water buffalo were being led up the high steps towards the streets

#### of the city. Their bodies left a penetrating smell behind them. We strolled along the faintly lit Ghats and sat down on one of the stairways. The stone was still warm from the sun’s heat.

#### Although darkness had set in, there was plenty of activity down at the river. An old man was standing up to his hips in cold water brushing his teeth. When he finished his washing, he gazed up at the moon and recited several mantras. Someone had brought an offering to the river, and flames from small oil lamps danced upon the silent water as it flowed by. Several sadhus were sleeping on the hard stone steps. They were drawing in the full force of their faith here at the edge of eternity.

#### The moonlight manifested long silver streaks over the Ganges which gave the whole region an enchanting ambience. I looked up to the next bend of the river and saw that the sky had turned an ominous deep red. An older man going by whispered to me, “That is Manikarnika Ghat. The bodies of the dead are cremated there. It is a great blessing if you are able to die in Benares.”

#### An oppressive feeling came over my heart. I asked Mister Romanowski whether we could go down to that Ghat, and he agreed. A beggar with leprosy stuck his rotted hand out to me, and I set some coins in his palm. The illness had not only eroded his fingers. His nose was also eaten away.

#### Fires were blazing on many of the platforms at the cremation site. Bright red flames flickered up as the fire tenaciously burned away the cadavers. An old man and his son took a ceremonial bath together in the river. They had shaved their heads bald as part of their ritual. Afterwards, they took some fresh white clothes out of a rolled-up blanket and quickly put them on. The corpse of a woman, certainly the wife and mother of the two, lay next to them on the ground. She was bound in red cloth and covered with wreathes of flowers. The husband sprinkled the body with holy water from the river, and then set it down on a stack of wood. Without any hesitation, the older man lit the funeral pyre on all four sides (the lifeless body must burn away completely!) and then threw the torch into the river.

#### The sweetish smell of burning flesh filled the air as powerful flames shot high into the dark evening sky. I could also witness the entire spectacle reflected in the slow current of the river. The atmosphere on the Ghats was heavy and oppresive, influenced by the pain and suffering of the surviving.

#### Only cold ash remained at some of the funeral pyres, although tiny flames still darted up here and there. This was a place where the soul imprisoned in the physical body could be liberated. It took about six hours for a corpse to burn away completely, which allowed the soul to enter the celestial world of the gods.

#### Down at the river, a cow peacefully ate the leftovers of a flower garland. Children played with bones that had been piled up and become coals. New fires were lit, and the names of the gods were repeated. “Ram, Ram, Ram,” resounded through the stillness of night, accompanied by the crackling and hissing of fires.

#### Here at the Ghats, I became more conscious than ever of the inescapable impermanence of my own body. This experience penetrated deeply but did not generate any fear or sadness: quite the opposite. In these moments I was granted an even deeper sense of freedom from the misconception that I am this transient body. My true being does indeed live in this body, but I was aware that I am never the recognizable object. The traces of identification were illusory.

#### I had to think of Mister Wang. The forces of impermanence had no authority over him. He had dissolved the powers inside him which manifest life and death centuries ago.

#### When we arrived back at the hotel, there was only a red night light burning in the lobby. The musty smell of old furniture mixed with the scents of curry and mothballs was ever-present. Men were sleeping on the floor, covered in blankets and snoring loudly. Without standing up, one of them gave us our key. The beds in our room were covered with torn sheets and squeaked noisily when we laid down.

#### The manager of the hotel had organized a car and driver for our journey to Sarnath the following afternoon. It turned out to be his brother, and he gave us the same assurances as others before. He was the best driver in Benares, and all the other drivers in the city knew this. We could trust him, though he would like to take us to a factory where fancy silk shawls were produced. It was on the way. “All tourists love to go there!” he told us emphatically.

#### After a forty-five-minute drive, we arrived in Sarnath. The driver was frustrated that he was unable to persuade us to visit the silk factory, which would have provided him with a small commission. We gave him a good tip, and his confidence was soon restored.

#### We walked through the Gazelle Park where, according to legend, the Buddha gave his first oral teachings. There was a tranquil atmosphere in this expansive park adorned with tall green trees and blooming bushes. Tibetan men and women with tan skin and hair in tight braids tirelessly turned their prayer wheels as they circumambulated the large stupa, where a bone of the Buddha was said to be stored. This spot on the earth was particularly sacred for these devout mountain people.

#### “We should go towards the monastery,” said Mister Romanowski. My sense of time had briefly gone astray in this historical place. The soothing silence which reigned here had absorbed my senses and transported me away from all the turbulence of the world.

#### There were several small restaurants at the large square where the dusty street branched off to the monastery. Large swarms of flies were flying around, searching impatiently for nourishment on the tables. A Tibetan monk wearing deep red robes and supporting himself with a large stick limped across the plaza. A burly billy goat spied the stiff-legged holy man and went right up to him. He put his horned head down and rubbed it against the arm of the monk. The monk stood for a moment and laughed. Then he spoke to the animal in a loud voice and gave him a good scratch on the neck. Apparently the two of them were already well-acquainted.

#### We walked through the open temple gates and followed a narrow path leading directly to the main building. There were a few simple two-story guesthouses built on the grounds for pilgrims, who frequently traveled here from great distances.

#### We took off our shoes before entering the temple. Sitting on a pedestal was a human-sized statue of Buddha in meditative posture. His eyes gazed down on the pilgrims, and one had the sense of really being seen by him. A row of oil lamps burned at his feet. Daylight penetrated through small windows atop the high walls of the room. Just below the ceiling, thin ropes were tied across the entire space with small, colorful prayer flags fastened to them. In one corner of the temple were two large prayer wheels waiting for pilgrims to spin them, which allowed the invocations inscribed on the cylinders to be brought to life.

#### We sat down on the cool stone floor and waited. Soon there were footsteps behind us and we stood up. Lama Geshe greeted us: “Welcome to our humble little monastery!” His open, smiling face and the extraordinary warmth with which he greeted us immediately instilled a feeling of joy in my heart. It

#### seemed as if light from the sun had emerged from inside the temple. I knew that I was standing here in front of a very unusual human being. In his presence, everything appeared luminous, and the borders of the world were dissolved. His eyes exuded spiritual power, awareness, and kindness. I was delighted that he would travel with us to see the masters.

#### We introduced ourselves. The lama nodded and said, “I am assigned to bring you to the high masters. I do it most gladly. It is an honor for me. We will be traveling for many hours by bus. We are heading north, and the roads are not always very good. Unfortunately, I am very busy today, but we will meet tomorrow at 7:00 p.m. at the Benares bus station.” We exchanged a few more words about details of the trip and then left the monastery.

#### Our driver was sitting on a wall next to his car. He appeared relieved to see us. He had had enough time to consider what price he wanted to charge us for the journey back to Benares, and it didn’t take long for him to bring up the subject. As soon as the car was moving, he launched into a long speech of absurd justifications for a ridiculously inflated price. In addition, there would be a fee for his waiting time, but he could make an exception if we stopped at the silk factory.

#### When he noticed that all his chatter didn’t help him accomplish his goal, he changed tactics: “I can bring you to the Ghats tomorrow morning at sunrise. Thousands of pilgrims and sadhus take their ritual baths there every morning. The Dasawamedh Ghat is the most holy place; you really have to visit it.” When we were up near the old city and climbed out of the car and paid a fair price. The fellow was happy enough as he drove away.

#### All the businesses were open, brightly lit, and stuffed full of people shopping. Busses pressed their way recklessly through the traffic, which was so thick that the people walking were moving faster than anyone else. Rickshaws, enormous oxen, and countless pedestrians all forced their way through the crowded streets. We had to cross the main street to get into the old city. This seemed to me like a life-threatening endeavor, but we both somehow made it across in one piece.

#### “Baba, come, look. Mongoose and cobra fighting!” A man sitting in a corner on the ground was calling out to us. He was holding a restless mongoose on a short leash in one hand, and in the other was a stick with which he raised the lid of a basket. The cobra rose up out of the basket hissing and ready to attack. The scar-faced fellow with the scarlet red turban had something creepy about him, and we walked by quickly. A few steps

#### ahead came an old toothless beggar holding the hand of a small child. He approached and silently stretched out his empty tin can to us.

#### We took our dinner on the outskirts of the old city in a narrow back courtyard. Two tables there were filled with young Europeans. They looked tired and unhealthy. They babbled on about spirituality and drugs, but it was obvious that their obsessive drug-use had weakened their bodies and stripped them of their inner force.

#### “We can go down to the Ganges very early tomorrow. It is certainly an unforgettable experience,” I suggested. Mister Romanowski nodded in agreement and said, “Yes, you really should see that. A higher power seems to bless that place.”

#### We walked through the pulsing streets in the upper part of the city as we made our way back to the hotel. Garbled music blared from a transistor radio in one of the shops. A huge elephant suddenly emerged from one of the side streets and trotted in our direction. The young man on the back of the old female elephant steered her through the commotion on the road with clear commands.

#### A driver yelled out curses through the open window of his bus. He had been forced to make space for the huge animal to pass and unintentionally knocked over a fruit stand in the process. A flock of people quickly gathered around, all gawking at the elephant and the fruit dealer. The merchant was able to raise his stand and gather up most of his fruit with the help of some local men. The elephant’s gaze was greedily fixed on some fruits still laying on the ground. She cautiously stretched her trunk ahead and scooped up a papaya, which she chomped down straightaway. While the fruit seller was occupied in a loud argument with the bus driver, a young boy grabbed several mangos and ran away. When the dealer saw the thief, he ran after him screaming, but the boy quickly disappeared in the throng of people. The bus started on its way again, and the elephant and her boss also moved along cheerfully. Only the fruit seller remained behind, forlorn and furious.

#### When we arrived at the guest house, I wanted to hang my clothes in the closet. But the smell of moth balls was so strong that I changed my mind and hung them up on the rusty nail on the wall. The laugh of the Tibetan lama was still reverberating within me. I was happy we would be seeing him again soon. As I lay on the bed with closed eyes, I had the strong sense that a special field of light floated over this city, welcoming all the pilgrims and bathing them in divine energy.

#### Just before sunrise, we descended the long, worn-down stone steps to the Ganges. Hundreds of other people were all doing the same. People with deformed bodies afflicted with leprosy sat along both sides of the stairway. The horrid illness had eaten away their noses, fingers, and toes. They sat closely huddled together and hoped for handouts from the pilgrims. Cows made their way to the river through the waves of human beings, acting as if they too had to hurry to be down by the water on time. Mild morning light soundlessly swallowed the shadows of night. Darkness faded gently from the streets of the ancient city as if night had been but a dream.

#### The first rays of sunlight arrived like golden angels in the morning sky, submerging the Ghats in a mystical atmosphere. Thousands of pilgrims stood in the river. Sadhus, yogis, and many others washed their bodies in the cold water. Prayers and mantras were chanted, humbly calling the Lord Shiva and the divine Mother Ganga. Devotees plunged below the surface of the holy water and immediately sprung up again.

#### Two yogis were practicing their asana (physical yoga practices) on a raised platform. Their bodies were extremely smooth and powerful. Further ahead and somewhat secluded from the energetic activity sat a guru, apparently a renowned sage. He had several devotees with him, and all were facing towards the rising sun. The serenity and silence that emanated from them was inspiring.

#### Brahmins were sitting on wooded platforms under large parasols, ceremoniously oiling their bodies. For a certain sum of money, they were prepared to perform specific complicated rituals for the pilgrims. Everyone wanted to purify themselves at the holy river: the rich, the poor, the crippled, the healthy, the holy, the high-caste Brahmins, and those without caste.

#### I felt transported to an era that existed long ago. And yet, what I experienced here was not foreign; it was deeply familiar. Feeling a call beyond all things temporal, I was touched by a radiance untouched by all that dies.

#### A bell rang out from an old temple tower, which had grasses and shrubs growing out of the cracks in its walls. The voice of a priest then arose from the same tower through loudspeakers and repeated the same words over and over: “Om Namah Shivaya.”

#### We sat for a while at the shore. A half-burned body floated slowly by with two crows resting on top of it. A group of older men and women sang divine hymns in honor of the goddess Paravati, the wife of Lord Shiva. Through

#### their voices, the gravity of worldly things was weakened and hardly noticeable. Many of them had come to Benares to make their last journey. Their heartfelt chanting merged with the steady splashing of water against the lowest ledges of the Ghats. The most intimate hopes of the singers penetrated the depths of the hidden world. They hoped that their prayers would be heard and admit them to a world of light after their death.

#### In the meantime, the sun stood high on the horizon and I could feel it getting hotter. Most of the people had already retreated to the cool streets of the city. Many would return the next morning before dawn. Things have been this way here every day for centuries. Mister Romanowski noted, “This river has an extraordinary magnetism.”

#### We took breakfast a bit later in a small overcrowded restaurant. I sat and ate and was there, but at the same time I was not there. I simply watched myself; a witness observing the one eating breakfast.

#### As evening approached, we said goodbye to the hotel manager. His brother took us to the bus station, which was bursting with activity. Car horns and dark stinking clouds of exhaust fumes filled the air.

#### We saw Lama Geshe coming out of a bus that had just arrived and waved him over. He walked with a sprightly step in our direction and greeted us with a hearty smile: “Look over there at the old bus in the back row. We will take that one. I already have the tickets. A young monk from the monastery was in Benares yesterday and bought them. He reserved three places for us.”

#### We were not alone. Many stood waiting for the driver to open the door. The guy was already inside the bus reading a newspaper and was in no hurry. When he finally set aside his paper and opened the doors, people filed in rapidly. In just a few minutes, all the seats were occupied. But eventually, many more people pushed their way onto the bus. Bodies were pressed up against other bodies in the middle aisle without a handbreadth between them.

#### We had to wait a long time, and it was dark when we finally began to move. Although the bus was already overfilled, people continued to climb in along the way and find space. The driver guided the bus into the black night over bumpy potholed streets and through sparsely lit villages. We were headed north, and it was getting colder. I started to shiver.

#### After several hours of driving, we stopped in a small village to take a thirty-minute rest stop. We had hardly stepped down from the bus before being besieged with vendors and beggars. A man with no legs dragged himself over the dusty floor towards us and stretched out his empty hands

#### with a submissive smile. His body was covered with lumps and bruises. Fate had tested him severely. Surely the man must have often considered death as an alternative to this difficult condition.

#### We drank a coffee before setting off once again into the darkness of night. Billions of stars glittered in the firmament. The waxing moon cast cool light down upon the earth and covered it in a silent mystical atmosphere.

#### I slept a few hours without being aware of it. As I awoke, the first beams of sunlight were providing a golden hue to the landscape and erasing the last hovering shadows of night. Lama Geshe sat next to me and said that we would soon have to transfer to another bus. The next ride would also last several hours. It would be a while before we reached our destination.

#### We got off at the next stop. Luckily, there was a small restaurant near the bus stop where we could wait. It was not particularly inviting, with two rickety wooden benches and one long table. We were the only guests there, and it would be two hours before we could continue. We had more than enough time to take breakfast.

#### There was more room in our next bus, though the driver gave us a distrustful glare as we boarded. Locals were suspicious of foreigners coming into this remote area. Most of the passengers were farmers, and they were all engaged in animated discussion. The rear of the bus was filled with sacks of grain and other goods. Agitated clucking came from a closed basket containing several chickens.

#### We rode for a while through lush green scenery, but soon the landscape became more mountainous. An hour later we were driving up a steep, snake-like road through a narrow gorge. On this small dusty street which zigzagged at dizzying heights, the driver often had to move out of the way of vehicles coming from the other direction, forcing us inches away from the edge of the road. Every time that happened, the view of the vast chasm below caused me to hold my breath.

#### After a long drive, we finally emerged from the gorge. A stunning view awaited us at the top of the road. In the distance, shining snow-capped summits appeared, huge mountains ascending majestically into the steel-blue sky. We were approaching the first high peaks of the Himalayas.

### The Sublime Masters

#### We travelled through broad, desolate terrain until Lama Geshe signaled for the bus driver to stop. “This is the place. We will get off here,” he said to us. It was a bit scary. Besides barren rock, there was nothing to be seen in this no man’s land. Looking at us out on the road, the driver wanted to be absolutely sure that this was where we wanted to leave the bus. It was still a three-hour hour drive to the next village. The curious farmers also looked down at us through the dirty windows of the bus with puzzled stares. They certainly wondered what a lama and two Europeans were looking for in this uninhabited stone desert.

#### A biting cold wind was a harsh welcome to this landscape, and the chill penetrated all the layers of my clothing. I began to freeze. “Come, we have a good walk ahead of us. Friends of mine have a house behind those pointy gray cliffs,” Lama Geshe informed us confidently. “We will stay the night there and seek out the masters tomorrow.”

#### This segment of the journey was longer than I expected. When we got to the craggy bluff, we still had to make our way up a narrow incline that was not without hazard. The ascent turned out to be a challenge requiring extreme mindfulness. Rocks often slipped out from under my feet. The only sound to be heard was the rustling of the wind sweeping over solitary bushes. Otherwise, absolute silence reigned here. A few gnarled trees stood at the top of the ridge like hermits in this rough deserted place, where people on the move stood out like beings from another planet. Nestled within this solitude in a small sheltered valley below us was a house encircled by fruit trees and a garden. A well next to the house was the hidden source of survival for this tiny oasis in the middle of this parched, rocky region.

#### We carefully descended. There were a few chickens in front of the house. A donkey tied to a tree eyed us timidly. Lama Geshe knocked on the door, but no one answered. He gestured to us that we should simply go inside. We did so, and came into a very modest room that offered a warm, comfortable feeling. In one corner was a Buddha-statue placed on a low altar with an oil lamp burning in front of it.

#### We sat at the table and Lama Geshe provided more information: “We will leave tomorrow at dawn. The way is not so far, but it is difficult. The masters will receive you. They are true gifts of the universe. Through their silent presence and non-doing, they guide the spiritual processes of the cosmos.”

#### The donkey brayed loudly and another donkey nearby gave a response. “Our hosts are coming. We should greet them in front of the house,” said the lama. We went out and saw an old couple in the distance. They had already seen us. The donkey was loaded up with firewood on its back and trotted at a relaxed pace next to them. I was quite impressed that these two elderly people lived alone here in such a barren world of stone, a world completely devoid of people and so far away from the noise of civilization.

#### The woman moved quickly to reach us as fast as she could. “Lama Geshe, what a joy that you are blessing us with a visit. It has been a long time since you were here!” Her husband followed and was overjoyed as well. His eyes gleamed as he reached out his hand to the lama.

#### Lama Geshe wanted to introduce us. “I have brought two friends. This is Mister Romanowski and Mister Mongrave.” We were greeted with the same congeniality they had shown Lama Geshe, as if they had always known us. We helped the man unload the wood from the donkey. After we all helped stack the wood, we went inside the house.

#### Over the course of living together for decades, our hosts had come to look very similar to each other. Their faces were furrowed, but a spiritual freshness beamed from their clear eyes. The old man’s back was curved from age and years of hard physical work. The waning of life-energy was causing him problems. The woman had lost none of her agility with age. She practiced yoga daily, her husband told us.

#### Evening clouds drifted overhead, drawing shadow-images over the tranquil landscape. The subtle margin separating day and night was gently traversed.

#### “You are taking your guests on the mountain?” asked our host. “Yes, we will go tomorrow morning,” answered Lama Geshe. “About five months ago, we were up on the mountain and met one of the

#### masters,” said the old man. “He told us you would come.” His wife went into the small open kitchen. “You are hungry, of course. I

#### will make you something to eat.” After our dinner, Mister Romanowski asked Lama Geshe if he always

#### stayed in the monastery at Sarnath. That wasn’t the case. “I spend several

#### months each year in a monastery in Sikkim,” he said. “The area is very difficult to reach, very high up on a steep mountain. Come and visit us there when you can.” His warmheartedness touched me deeply.

#### The rooster crowed into the early dawn light as we gathered in front of the house. We said our farewells, and our friendly hosts let us know that they expected us for dinner.

#### I noticed Lama Geshe took off his shoes and left them in the house. He would walk barefoot. Thorns and sharp stones didn’t seem to bother him at all as he went along. He even increased his pace from the day before, and I had trouble keeping up. We went into the shadows of a high mountain and continued through a valley for some time.

#### During a short rest, the lama pointed to another tall mountain in front of us and said, “We have to go up there.”

#### I felt apprehensive. The daunting thought of having to climb up that peak robbed me of my last vestiges of self-confidence. I wasn’t sure I was even capable of making this ascent, but there was no turning back.

#### “We must go on now,” said the lama, and we were on our way once again. Then, something remarkable happened as we climbed upwards. All my

#### doubting thoughts vanished entirely. In the space of just a few moments, I was untroubled and moving with lightness again. I also marveled at how gracefully Sergej Romanowski hiked up the mountain. And Lama Geshe was adept in guiding us amid the huge boulders.

#### As we crossed a smooth plateau above, I couldn’t believe what I saw. The lama had entered a spiritual dimension that was both astonishing and transcendental. I had read some things about Tantric Buddhism and various meditation techniques. But what I witnessed here surpassed by far all that I had read.

#### Each of Lama Geshe’s steps became engraved about half an inch into the rock. The markings were distinctly visible, with a fine golden dust pressed into the stone footprints. Truly unbelievable! Mister Romanowski was equally astounded.

#### The experience transported me into a wondrous radiant state. Every cell of my body was infused with an intense feeling of joy. I was not sure whether my feet were touching the ground, or whether I was floating over the mountain. All the tiredness had disappeared from my limbs, and I knew there would be no more difficulties the rest of the way.

#### When we arrived at the top, I was amazed once again. Down on the flat terrain, sheltered in a hollow in the stone, stood a small monastery. Green trees and bushes with delicate red flowers in bloom thrived in the small park-like garden that surrounded the building. Clear water spilled forth from large porous stones into a pond filled with splendid lotus blossoms.

#### “Go inside. I will wait here in the garden,” said Lama Geshe. We entered the building and followed along until we came to a bright room in the back of the house with four chairs set out. We sat down on two of them and waited. It was totally silent. Looking out through the high windows on the wall, we saw white clouds floating across the sky.

#### Suddenly, the wall between the visible and non-visible realms collapsed. The heartbeat of the world stopped. The light-force that expanded through the room was incredibly strong, almost unbearable. I thought my physical body was going to split open and disintegrate. Moments later, the masters were standing in front of us.

#### Both masters were wearing long ochre-colored robes. I was sure that they had not entered through the door or through any window. Time stood still. Their presence was overwhelming. Their bodies were completely spiritualized. A titanic radiance exuded from them.

#### The older of the two was holding a long wooden stick in his right hand. The silence that surrounded him was boundlessly deep, a supreme divine power originating in pre-worldly being. One word from him and all the oceans of the world would rise.

#### The facial features of both masters were smooth and symmetrical. Infinite kindness and pure astral power emanated from their mysterious eyes. I saw an Eastern influence in their appearance, but there was no way to categorize them according to any particular culture. I had the feeling that the entire celestial universe was standing in front of me. The brilliance of their light was almost blinding.

#### The older master began to speak: “Welcome, we have been expecting you. Your efforts to vitalize the light of the heavens and bring it into the world is extraordinarily important in this phase of human development.

#### “This old civilization will soon be eliminated, and then be renewed. The order of the entire world will be revolutionized and transformed. The difficulty is that most humans experience transformation outside the guiding principles of divinity. Their focus is on external objects, and they never stop

#### producing the laws that support death within their consciousness. Most of the time, they are preoccupied with their outward condition. That is why they overlook the boundlessness of the heavens within them.

#### “To never stagnate in life and to live from pure divine power is unknown for most. For many, the upheaval that is taking place on the earth will be bound with great difficulties. Master Jesus said: ‘The time will come when the wheat will be separated from the chaff.’

#### “We have three new higher frequencies of light-force to share with you. They arise from pre-worldly divine essence and they are sealed. Each of these three rays has a particular intensity and effect. Each seal can be lifted with the appropriate word, which we will give you. You will then be able to open and activate these rays at the proper time.

#### “At the moment you speak these specific words, the rays will be revealed, and you will be unified with these hidden sacred light-energies. The radiance, splendor, and enormous virtue of these divine rays will open new possibilities for you to complete the great work of liberation on this earth. Pass on these activating words to Master Wang as well, and later to all those who are worthy and prepared to accept them.”

#### The younger master added, “One who lives from these divine resources has the possibility to sublimate matter, to reshape, condense, or dissolve it. Through your presence on the earth, human beings will become aware of their limitless nature and divine purpose.”

#### The old master closed with these words: “The universal high masters bless your tireless liberating work for humanity and the planets. Go in peace. We are with you!”

#### The three secret words then flowed into our consciousness. Moments later, the masters disappeared before our eyes, transported from the visible to the invisible divine universe. Their timeless supernatural presence had touched me in the deepest possible way. They themselves were beams of sunlight. They had appeared without a sound, illuminated the room, imparted us with light, and silently withdrawn.

#### The power within their words was forceful. These words were so loaded with energy that it felt like my earthly being was burning and melting away. It was clear that these masters had the power to alter developments on the earth instantaneously and completely. But most humans would never be capable of taking such a quantum leap. It was therefore correct and important to accompany humanity step by step back to their heavenly home, in

#### correspondence with the processes taking place collectively in this phase of evolution.

#### A deep internal shift had taken place. My being shined like the rising sun. The sublime masters had flushed out the last constraints of this world from my being. I felt the three immense sealed light-powers within me waiting to be revealed through the secret words. I knew the moment to remove the seal would show itself. I also understood that these light-forces would burn up my past, my future, and my external present in just a few short moments. Absolutely nothing would remain.

#### Mister Romanowski sat in silence. The transmission of the powerful seals had evidently provoked a deep transformation within him as well. He eventually stood up and said, “Come, Mister Mongrave. We can go back now. Today, my being has attained its ultimate fulfillment.”

#### When we stepped out into the garden, we saw the lama sitting once again in lotus posture in front of a lovely blooming bush. His voice was very soft and he was laughing. He was obviously in the highest state of bliss. It was a glorious sight. The flowers were no longer facing the sun; they were bowed towards the lama, as if listening to his every kind word. When he saw us, he stood up: “Blessed you are. You have been accepted to the temple of the universal masters; in the temple of the immortals,” he said with a solemn voice. You have been provided with enormous mystical, divine powers. Through them, eternity will be vital and well-preserved within time, and the invisible gate will open infinitely wide.”

#### He continued to speak: “Up ahead, behind the large gray rocks sticking out, I have found another path leading down the mountain. Let us go now. We should make it back before darkness falls.” We followed him without speaking. The path was narrow and steep, and he went rapidly. After a long, strenuous descent between two mountains, we finally came into a deep gorge covered in shadows. Following it to its end, we came to a massive mound of piled stones. After climbing over it, we could see the house where our hosts lived. Twilight had begun to spread over the sky, and it was pitch dark by the time we got to the house.

#### We settled ourselves inside, where the warm flickering light of the petroleum lamps conjured life-like shadows that constantly danced around the room. The mild light also crossed over the face of our host, which appeared gaunt and sickly. He coughed over and over, excusing himself each time. “I will go out to make a quick check on the donkeys. I will be right

#### back,” he said finally. The concerned look of his wife followed him to the door. She gave a sigh, but was steadfast within and gave no heed to unpleasant thoughts.

#### We spent the evening in tranquility. Lama Geshe talked for a little while about the monastery in Sarnath and recounted some teachings of the Buddha. I admired the innate discretion of our hosts. They didn’t ask one question about our meeting with the masters on the mountain. Our hostess informed us that there is only one bus a day that would start us on our journey back to Benares. In order to reach the road in time, it would be best to leave very early in the morning.

#### We said our goodbyes to the elderly couple at daylight. Lama Geshe offered them and their house a blessing, and we were on our way.

#### After a long arduous journey, we made it back to Benares. Here the lama left us. Our wonderful guide spoke very briefly in parting: “My bus to Sarnath is already there. If you ever come back to India, write to me. It would be a pleasure to see you again,” he said and laughed. I loved his open, friendly way of communicating.

#### Mister Romanowski shared his deep reflection on our farewell: “Beyond the threshold of thought, there is no separation, not anywhere. The timeless spirit protects everything without attaching itself to anything. In our hearts, we are always one.”

#### Lama Geshe took our hands in his. Moments later, he was waving to us from the window of his bus as it drove away.

#### Outside the old city of Benares, we found a small, peaceful hostel with a garden. The heavy-set owner of the house was well dressed and shared with us that he had studied in England. We sat outside in front of our rooms in the shadows of a large tree and drank tea.

#### The enormous presence of the light emitted by the masters was still flooding through me. Not only my body, but my entire being was saturated with this magnificence. It was like an internal chain reaction: internal gateways and passages were continually exposed, as if divine hands were pushing them open. Insight upon insight manifested in my awareness as my being was absorbed by the pre-worldly foundation of all being.

#### While I dwelled in this inner freedom and lightness, this unbounded sense of delight, my physical being was able to overcome old age and other deeply

#### established limitations and boundaries. The last external forces of resistance were loosened and then completely dissolved.

#### “We will fly back to England in two days,” Mister Romanowski told me as he sipped his tea. The thought of going back to London prompted strange feelings. The world where I lived and worked seemed suddenly very foreign and unreal. I was frightened to feel this way.

#### I knew, of course, that we had to return. Mister Wang was expecting us, and I was aware that a great spiritual task awaited me. Sergej Romanowski had become a true and deep friend. It was certainly no coincidence that we were called together on this journey to the sublime masters.

#### We continued to sit there quietly, and I suddenly noticed that I was seeing the world with new eyes. I witnessed my physical body sitting peacefully as if from a distance. I knew the body, but it didn’t know me. It was more of a permeable focal point through which divine energy flowed, as when sunlight shines into a dark room through a glass window. Darkness and shadows are wiped away and the things of the world are made visible. Still, the essential aspect of my being was invisible, beyond name and form: an eternal mystery.

#### Two days later we flew to Bombay, and the following day we arrived in England. The stewardess reported, “In a few minutes we land in London Heathrow. The temperature outside is twelve degrees Celsius, and it is slightly raining.” The plane broke through a thick layer of gray clouds. Soon we felt a short jolt. We had landed.

### The Secret Seal

#### I was a sleepwalker once again as I got into the taxi. My soul was still in India. It felt like I had been away for months and experienced a mild culture shock. It would take a while to accustom myself completely to the old surroundings.

#### In the taxi, Mister Romanowski asked, “Would you like to come spend some time at my house now?” I was happy to take him up on his offer. I didn’t feel ready to walk into my apartment and into my old life.

#### Actually, Sergej Romanowski had invited me to move into his large house before we left for India, and now I felt ready to do it. I envisioned clearing out my old apartment as quickly as possible and fully embracing the next new chapter of my life. I would inform him of my decision at the next convenient moment.

#### Soon after arriving, I was sitting again in the same lounge chair in the library where I sat when Natascha was still alive. Beautiful memories arose, as did the pain of loss. It was all there, but the experience had lost its dominating power over me.

#### We conversed about the masters and our time in India. At a pause in the discussion, I gathered all my courage and said to Sergej Romanowski, “Before our journey, you offered that I move in with you. I have given your proposal much thought and have come to a decision. We will be working more closely with each other in the future, and I would like to accept your invitation.”

#### I could see that Mister Romanowski was extremely pleased. It was a touching moment for him, and he thanked me for my decision. I knew that I had set down an important cornerstone for my future.

#### Mister Romanowski then said, “We want to contact Mister Wang as soon as possible, though he probably already knows we are back. With his cosmic omniscience, nothing escapes him.” We discussed a few details of my move, and I left early that afternoon with a plan to come again the following day.

#### I felt weary as I moved up the stairs and entered my apartment. The furniture

#### was covered with a fine layer of dust, and the soot-covered opening of the cold fireplace gaped at me like an open mouth. Exhausted, I let myself fall into the old armchair, which greeted me with a loud squeak, as if it had missed me.

#### Sitting alone in the apartment, I noticed that I missed India; all of it. I recalled with joyful longing the incessant honking and noise on the street, the torrid heat, the multitude of scents in the air, the smiles, the friendly, uncomplicated people, and even the hordes of persistent beggars. The culture shock that I was experiencing had intensified and now felt worse than what I went through during my first days in India.

#### Once I was able to lay down, I fell asleep immediately. In the other world, I saw Mister Wang and Mister Romanowski sitting at the top of a mountain. They were silently peering out into an endless expanse. I then discerned the voice of Mister Wang: “There is no empty space.”

#### After waking up the next morning, I needed some time to orient myself: to realize that I was in London, in my apartment, in my bed. I opened the window before unpacking, which allowed some fresh cool air to flow in and drive out the stale smell that had filled the apartment while I was away. I looked out the window and saw a few rays of sunlight beaming through from between the dark gray clouds passing by. The temperamental weather seemed to be calming down a little.

#### A knock on the door came shortly after midday. I opened it and was amazed to see Mister Wang. “Welcome back, Mister Mongrave. It looks like the journey did you well,” he declared. “And you have met with the high masters. This I can see very clearly.”

#### “Yes,” I confirmed, “this encounter completely transformed me. It has made me aware of my true purpose. The invisible sacred gate into eternity was pushed wide open. All the old limitations were burned away. And I am conscious that the spiritual Lodge of the Golden Dragon has existed since the beginning of time.

#### “The supreme ones imparted to us three sealed keys to light-powers. They gave us the task of transmitting them to you as well. Through specific words, these sacred seals are opened and the light-energies are activated. The sublime masters explained that we should open these seals together at the same time, and that we would recognize the right moment to do so.”

#### We left with Mister Wang’s car immediately. We were on our way to see Sergej Romanowski, who was expecting us. Mister Wang had informed him of our coming telepathically.

#### After arriving, we went upstairs to the library. I looked through the window down into the garden and let my gaze fall on the small mound of earth under Natasha’s favorite tree, where her ashes were buried. Her father had planted a rose bush next to it.

#### Mister Wang’s voice retrieved me from my thought-world. I moved away from the window and sat down with the other two men. Master Wang had some important information to disclose: “About twelve years ago we bought some property outside the city. There is a country house on the land which serves as a temple. About one hundred and eighty people from different countries meet there once a week. Only those that carry the secret light-insignia are admitted to the innermost sanctuary. Up to this point, we were mainly busy with preparatory work. But now, an essential constellation has been consummated. Three members of the ancient Lodge of the Golden Dragon have found each other once again, and the sublime masters have given us three enormous sealed energies to be revealed to the world through us. Through our presence, rays of divine light with their source in eternity will be established within time. The golden band is never broken.”

#### The potent words of Mister Wang transported me into a state where gods, angels, humans, animals, plants, and stones all melted into one entity within splendid pre-worldly light. All things seen and unseen were expressions of the unfathomable mystery, yet each was nothing more than a short breath in time.

#### Everything was within me, but I was in nothing. I was finally awakened from my restricted physical body. Images from my nebulous primal past rose up in rapid succession in front of my spiritual eye. Millenniums issued forth and dispersed into nothing. I could recognize again how my mental activity and its accumulative tension had always bound me to the material world.

#### Over the course of the afternoon, we spoke about various important aspects of the spiritual work we would be sharing in the future. We decided to call the new temple ‘The Lodge of Awakened Dragons’. We wanted to drive out to the property together in two days. Mister Wang would report the new name of the lodge to the members and introduce us to them.

#### My apartment seemed like a very strange place. Having decided to move to

#### Mister Romanowski’s house, I suppose I had already cut the cord with it and said goodbye.

#### I lay down in bed and fell asleep. My body was lying very relaxed between the sheets, but my spirit was wide-awake. I was aware that something in me never slept. My deepest inner core had entered the heavenly gates after undergoing the universal mysteries of initiation.

#### I awoke in the middle of the night. My body was gone. I was a gigantic omnipresent light, an all-encompassing divine power, a supra-personal presence.

#### After some time, I gradually became conscious of my physical body lying in bed again. I was breathing through the body, formless and nameless, but now the breathing was no longer limited to my physical body. I was breathing through the entire universe and through all living beings.

### United in Divine Light

#### Mister Wang picked us up in the late afternoon on the arranged day. We had to cross through the city, and the evening traffic was heavy. Sitting in the car, I felt like I was an extra in a film being produced in a remote world. My eyes could perceive the ethereal world, the vibrations, the flowing energy of all living beings. Unattached and silent, I saw people’s self-seeking, overcharged thought-constructs and observed the interaction of these energies which humans are so entangled in without being aware of it.

#### Having left the city behind us, we drove through forests and entered a landscape of green hedgerows and fields. Cows plucked at the soft grass and chewed slowly in meditative calm. Their stoic satisfaction was undisturbed by the sound of car engines.

#### A half-hour later, Mister Wang stopped the car and said, “Here it is, we have arrived.” We were on the side of the road facing two wide hedges. A sign on a gate between the hedgerows had written on it: ‘Private -Trespassing Forbidden’. Mister Wang got out and opened the gate, and we drove further along a bumpy field path up to a farmhouse. The building stood alone in the middle of a peaceful landscape. It was much bigger than I had expected and was surrounded by numerous stately old trees.

#### Mister Wang rang the doorbell. An older woman and her husband greeted us. They obviously lived there, and Mister Wang introduced us: “Mister and Mrs Scott, please meet Mister Romanowski and Mister Mongrave.”

#### “It is an honor to welcome you both here,” said Mister Scott. Mister Wang has already told us a bit about you, and also of your participation in the Lodge of the Golden Dragon in Shanghai. Few have had the privilege to be accepted there. And you have recently met with the high masters. Please come in. I will introduce you to the other members.”

#### We followed him into the reception area in front of the temple. Here we became acquainted with the others in attendance. They were all new faces for both of us, and they all emanated from within a radiance that distinguished and qualified them as members of the Lodge of Awakened Dragons.

#### After some time, Mister Scott made an announcement: “We are all here.

#### Let us go into the temple. Today is a very special and important day. Mister Wang, Mister Romanowski, and Mister Mongrave are direct emissaries of the exalted universal masters. From now on, they will be the masters and guides of this lodge, which will assemble under the name: Lodge of Awakened Dragons.”

#### In that moment, something got stuck in my throat. The pronouncement that I should be a master of this lodge was a surprise indeed. But I knew that everything here had its reasons, and I could be sure that I would enjoy the task ahead.

#### We followed a corridor that led us to the temple. It was a large circular building with a high ceiling. A part of the ceiling was made of glass, which allowed us to see the light of the moon and the glittering of a few stars between thin clouds in the evening sky. The inner sanctuary had subdued lighting, and through its large windows we could see a well-tended park area and an illuminated fountain.

#### The smartly dressed men and women of the lodge moved to their designated seats silently. On a low pedestal in front of the assembly, three chairs were set out for us. Mister Wang sat in the middle.

#### A large, artistically forged golden sun hung on the wall behind us. Thirteen rays beamed from this sun. I found this symbol very familiar, though I could not recall at that moment how I knew it.

#### The solemn moment had come. In soft voices that were inaudible to anyone else, we uttered the sacred words and opened the three seals. The enormous spiritual power released in those moments surprised us all. The entire room was transformed into a boundless ocean of light. This radiance flowed through all those present, and they themselves were awed by the attributes of this immense energy. The room remained completely silent.

#### After some time had passed, Mister Wang spoke to the members. He explained that this divine primordial power was now the foundation of our spiritual work. The words had hardly come out of his mouth when, all the sudden, an unexpected event took place: one of the high masters, the older one, was in the room next to Mister Wang.

#### It was truly unbelievable: he appeared in the temple out of nowhere! Again, he wore a long ocher-colored robe and was holding the same long wooden stick in his right hand, just as we had experienced him in the small monastery in the Himalayas.

#### The sublime master brought his left hand upwards like a chalice.

#### Immediately, thirteen delicate rays were emitted from the palm of his hand, fine beams like flames of white light.

#### Everyone there was awe-struck and deeply moved by this magnificent event, enthralled by the intense energy exuded by the immortal high master. His presence was truly astounding, uniting diversity of being into oneness, into the original source of all being, into the Great Mystery.

#### Then he started to speak: “One who overcomes possesses the four faces of God and the thirteen keys to divine power. One who frees himself from the obstacles of space and time is boundless and does not live anywhere. The entire heavenly universe is his home, for within the absolute there is neither a here nor a there.

#### “An eternal divine light now shines through your souls and through your physical forms, which will transform and spiritualize your bodies and the bodies of all living beings on the planet. Your non-doing accomplishes this! Everything exists Here and Now, the finite as well as the infinite, the seen as well as the unseen. There are no limits or boundaries anywhere; no life and no death. Your true presence is primordial. The universal masters bless your activity!”

#### He then raised his left hand and directed the thirteen luminous rays toward us. The next moment, he disappeared before our eyes into the invisible heavenly universe.

#### The effects that these thirteen incredible divine light-energies have had on us cannot be expressed. Indeed, no more should be told: the rest should remain secret.

#### We sat for another hour together in silence. We were profoundly moved by the experience we had had together, and we felt this deep connection as we said goodbye.

#### Our drive through the night and back to the city was also quiet. Up in the sky, Venus sparkled just in front of the crescent moon. The headlights of the car were like two restless eyes seeking their path in the darkness that had laid its veil over the countryside. Shadowed bushes and trees sporadically appeared within the beams of light, emerging like ghosts on the side of the road.

#### It was shortly past midnight when we got out of the car and I said goodbye to Mister Wang and Mister Romanowski. I felt somewhat lost. It was like arriving in another world as I stood on the empty street in front of the building where I still lived in. It would be quite a job to pack and get my

#### move started.

#### After a month, my apartment was cleared out and I had cleared out as well. I was very content in my new surroundings in Mister Romanowski’s house. I felt a little guilty about Clementine. But after I talked to Mister Romanowski about her, he agreed to hire her as our housekeeper.

#### We went to the temple of the lodge of Awakened Dragons several times a week. Over the next few years, more than one hundred new members joined the Lodge, bearing hope for humanity and for the world.

#### And by the way, until today we have still not revealed the three secret words and three seals to anyone else!

#### We were sitting in the library one evening when Mister Wang revealed a surprising piece of information. He was considering returning to China in the near future. There he would retreat from the noise of the world and spend the rest of his life in solitude and silence.
