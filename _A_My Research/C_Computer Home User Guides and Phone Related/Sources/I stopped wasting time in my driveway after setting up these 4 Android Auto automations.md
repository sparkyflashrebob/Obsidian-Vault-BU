Android Auto is a highly convenient feature on most modern cars, even if it's a bit underused. If you're not using it, now is a good time to start. And once you do, you'll want to take advantage of a few automations and routines that'll transform your driving experience.
We've all rushed to the car while running late. Once you get in, you have to fumble with cables to plug in your phone, find Google Maps to start navigation, dig around for a playlist or your streaming service of choice, and hit the road, all without spilling your coffee or missing a turn.
There's a better way to use Android Auto, and here's what you need to know.

Your streaming app and playlist start playing. The screen switches back to light mode, and maybe even the routine fires off a text to your significant other that you're headed to work.

#### How to set up routines in Android Auto

##### The Google Home app does all the magic

The way this works is that we'll set up Google Assistant routines and automations in the Google Home app, not in Android Auto. However, once you're done, you'll be able to access them using your voice or even create custom shortcut icons on your Android Auto home screen.
It's easier than it sounds. To start, open the Google Home app, then tap the **Automations** tab at the bottom right. From here, select the **+ Create** button, and choose **Automations** .
Close
[✕ Remove Ads](https://www.howtogeek.com/premium/?ref=ad_removal)
Now, you'll see three main options: **Starters** , **Conditions** , and **Actions** . This is where you want to be. The "Starters" are how you'll trigger this routine, whether that's voice commands, time of day, or even your location. For example, you can set it to start once you leave your neighborhood, although I'd choose something like voice commands so you can use the routine before leaving the driveway.
Conditions ensure that any specific automation or routine doesn't start unless you actually want it to. Then, **Actions** are where the automation magic happens. Create an action, like navigating to work, and stack another on top for launching Spotify or YouTube Music, then a third or fourth, depending on what you want.
You can even tap into smart controls for home lighting, the garage door, and the thermostat. The options are endless. Create a starter, choose some actions, add in a condition or two, and hit **Save automation** .

#### Routines that automate everything

##### Powerful tools that you'll love

Credit: Joe Fedewa / How-To Geek
Now that you understand how Google Assistant routines can help you in Android Auto, and how to create a few, here are some examples I've been using:
\* **Heading Home:** Triggered by saying, "Hey Google, head home." Get navigation directions instantly, play music or a news podcast, enable DnD to keep your focus on the road, and text your spouse to let them know you're heading home.
\* **Arriving Home:** Triggered by your location or a voice command, use this to open your smart garage door, turn on the hall or entry lights in your house, adjust the thermostat, etc.
\* **Picking up the kids:** Triggered by your location at the kids' school. This can instantly send an "on my way home" text message, switch to a different music playlist, check the weather for after-school activities, and start navigation to avoid traffic.
\* **Night vibes:** Triggered at sunset or a specific time. Have the system switch to night mode, play a casual playlist, or even a podcast, dim the lights at home, etc.

Once you create an automation or three, head to **Settings** on your phone and open Android Auto. Dig through the Android Auto settings menu until you find **Customize Launcher** . This lets you adjust which icons and apps are on the home screen launcher. Tap **Add a shortcut > Assistant action > and pick a routine** from your list.

##### Supercharge Android Auto

It's worth noting that you can do similar tasks right inside the Android Auto app by creating an " **Assistant action** " in the Customize Launcher settings menu mentioned earlier, but those are tied directly to Android Auto and happen one at a time. By making these routines in the Google Home app, you get more flexibility, and they work great when you're a passenger, too.
