The Push DJ Handbook: Master Your Performance Mix

51f13240-df73-4d98-9b15-f69911b0139a

DJing with Ableton and Push requires setting up your tracks in Session View, warping them to a common tempo, and using the Push's encoders and pads for launching clips, EQing, and applying effects. Unlike traditional CDJs, this allows for dynamic live remixing and instant beat-matching.Step 1: Prepare Your TracksBefore touching the Push, you need to prepare your music in Ableton Live on your laptop.Warping: Drag your tracks into audio tracks in Session View. Double-click each track and ensure Warp is turned on. Set the warp mode (e.g., Complex or Beats) so the tracks lock to your master tempo without altering their pitch.Launch Quantization: Set your Global Launch Quantization (at the top left of your screen) to 1 Bar. This ensures that whenever you trigger a new track or loop from the Push, it will perfectly wait for the start of the next musical bar.Drop Markers: Set up Cue Points or Warp markers at the intro, drops, and outros so you can easily jump around the track using the Push.Step 2: Build a DJ TemplateCreate a clean, standardized layout in Session View that mimics a traditional DJ mixer setup.Decks: Create two to four main Audio Tracks (Deck A, Deck B, etc.).Channel Strips: Drop an EQ Eight (or EQ Three) and an Auto Filter (for low/high pass) onto each deck.Color Coding: Color code your clips or tracks (e.g., green for instruments, red for vocals) so you can easily find your cues on the Push pads.Step 3: Map the Push ControlsThe Push 1, 2, or 3 is brilliant for tactile control over your mix.EQ & Filters: Use Ableton’s MIDI Map mode to map the rotary encoders above the screen to the Low, Mid, and High knobs of your EQ Eights. This lets you use the hardware knobs to blend frequencies just like on a standard DJ mixer.Clip Launching: Use the 8x8 pad matrix in Session View mode to trigger specific tracks, loops, or acapellas. The illuminated pads will show you which clips are playing and where they are in the phrase.Volume Control: Map the encoders or use the master fader to control the volume of your individual decks. (Note: If using Push to control track volumes, it's a best practice to keep Ableton's Master channel untouched, and route all decks through a separate "Mix" or "Bus" track).Step 4: Perform and MixOnce your setup is mapped and your tracks are warped, you are ready to mix.Triggering: When track A is playing, navigate with the arrows on your Push to select a clip on track B. Push the pad to fire the clip. Because of your quantization settings, it will start completely in sync.Mixing: Slowly bring up the volume or filter in track B using the Push encoders.
