"Follow the earnings" is an investing philosophy stating that a company's stock price will ultimately track the growth of its profits over the long term. While market sentiment drives short-term fluctuations, persistent earnings growth is the most fundamental factor in building lasting wealth in the stock market. [1, 2]
Key Concepts Long-Term Correlation: While share prices can detach from reality over days or weeks, over multi-year periods, stock returns are dominated by fundamental cash flows. A company that consistently grows its profits year over year will almost always see its share price rise alongside them. Earnings Season: This refers to the designated multi-week periods following the end of a corporate quarter (e.g., January, April, July, October) when publicly traded companies release their quarterly financial reports. Investors and analysts parse these reports to determine if the company is growing. The Bottom Line: At its simplest, a company's earnings are its revenues minus its expenses—essentially, its net profit. Forward Guidance: In addition to reporting past results, companies issue forward-looking statements predicting future profits. Often, the market reacts more strongly to these forecasts than to the historical results. [5]
Why Investors Track Earnings Valuation (P/E Ratio): By dividing the share price by the earnings per share, you get the Price-to-Earnings Ratio. This helps investors determine if a stock is overvalued or undervalued. Beating Estimates: Analysts build financial models to predict a company's earnings. If a company beats these expectations, the stock price usually jumps. If they miss, the stock often drops. Corporate Health: Consistent earnings growth indicates operational efficiency and high demand for a company's products. [2, 11, 12, 13, 14]
