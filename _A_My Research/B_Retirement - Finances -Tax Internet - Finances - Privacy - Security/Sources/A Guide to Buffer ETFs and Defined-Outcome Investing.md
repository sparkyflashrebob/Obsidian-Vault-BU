A "buffer ETF" (also known as a defined-outcome or structured ETF) is an investment fund that tracks an underlying asset (like the S&P 500) but provides built-in downside protection. In exchange for capping your maximum potential gains, the fund absorbs a set percentage of market losses. [1, 2]

How They Work

Buffer ETFs use a mix of options contracts (usually FLEX Options) to create strict boundaries on your returns over a set "outcome period," typically lasting 12 months:

The Buffer (Downside Protection): The fund shields you from a specific percentage of losses (e.g., the first 10% or 20% of market drops). You only begin losing money if the underlying index drops below that buffer amount.

The Cap (Upside Limitation): Because buying downside insurance is costly, the fund caps your maximum profit. If the market has a massive rally, your returns are limited to the fund's preset cap.

The Outcome Period: These ETFs operate on a cycle—often 12 months long. To get the exact advertised buffer and cap, you need to hold the ETF for the entirety of this period. [3, 4]

Real-World Example

Imagine a 1-year buffer ETF tied to the S&P 500 with a 10% buffer and a 15% cap:

If the market drops 8%: You lose nothing, as the 8% drop falls entirely within your 10% buffer.

If the market drops 25%: You absorb the losses past your 10% threshold, resulting in a 15% loss for your investment.

If the market gains 20%: You get capped at a 15% return. [2, 6, 7, 8, 9]

Key Things to Consider

Entry Timing Matters: The stated buffers and caps are calculated from the fund's price at the beginning of the outcome period. If you buy the ETF mid-cycle, your protection levels and potential caps will differ from the stated metrics.

No Dividends: Buffer ETFs typically forego dividend payments to help finance the cost of the options.

Higher Fees: Because they are actively managed using complex options strategies, these ETFs carry higher expense ratios (often 0.50% to 0.85% annually) than standard index funds.

Not Risk-Free: In extreme market crashes that exceed the buffer limit, you can still lose a significant amount of money. [4, 12]

Who Should Use Them?

Buffer ETFs are generally favored by conservative investors, retirees, or those saving for a specific short-term financial goal (like buying a home) who want exposure to market growth but cannot stomach significant market drawdowns. [2, 13]

Major providers in this space include Innovator ETFs, First Trust, and BlackRock (iShares). You can explore specific funds and their current outcome periods using the Innovator ETFs Buffer Dashboard or the iShares Buffer ETF Explorer. [12]
