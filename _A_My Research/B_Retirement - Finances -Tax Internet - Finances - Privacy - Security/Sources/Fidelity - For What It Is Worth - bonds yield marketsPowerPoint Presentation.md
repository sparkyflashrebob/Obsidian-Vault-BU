How to evaluate bonds?

Rate trajectories and credit fundamentals matter greatly to a bond's yield and price

| Risks | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Interest Rates | The risk of price fluctuations due to changes in interest rates. |
| Reinvestment Rates Inflation Call or Pre-payment | The risk of reinvesting recycled capital, in bonds with lower interest rates in the future, due to a general decline in market yields. The risk of loss in purchasing power due to higher inflation levels, which can weigh on bond prices. The risk that a borrower will "retire" a bond before its maturity, leading potentially to an less optimal time to reinvest proceeds. |

|  | Risks | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Credit or Default | The risk of borrowers' defaulting on their payment covenants, which will likely trigger sharp declines in bond prices. More nuanced than pay or not pay and includes fundamental analysis of the issuers credit worthiness, access to markets, and desire and willingness to pay. |
| a | Credit Liquidity Currency | The risk of loss from inability to sell a bond in the secondary market without undercutting its price. Liquidity and market access are a critical underpinning of the review of an issuer's credit quality. The risk of loss from fluctuations in foreign exchange rates, particularly for bonds not denominated in US dollars. |
