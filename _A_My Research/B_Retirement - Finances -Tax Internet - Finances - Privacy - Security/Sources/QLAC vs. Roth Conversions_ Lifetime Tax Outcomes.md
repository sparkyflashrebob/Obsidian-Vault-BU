### Search QLAC vs. Roth Conversions: Lifetime Tax Outcomes

Shawn Plummer, CRPC
Retirement Planner, Financial Advisor, Annuity Broker, and Insurance Agent

#### Comparing Two Tax Strategies to Maximize Retirement Income and Minimize Lifetime Taxes

RMDs (Required Minimum Distributions) are one of the biggest tax headaches in retirement. Once you reach age 73–75, the IRS forces withdrawals from Traditional IRAs, [401(k)s](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/what-is-a-401k/), and similar accounts, creating taxable income whether you need it or not. For retirees with large pre-tax balances, this can lead to higher lifetime taxes, Medicare surcharges, and less flexibility. Two of the strongest tools to control this problem are **Qualified Longevity Annuity Contracts (QLACs)** and **Roth conversions** . Both work differently but can be blended for powerful results.

#### How a QLAC Works

A QLAC is a [deferred income annuity](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/types-of-annuities/deferred-income-annuity/) purchased with qualified retirement funds. It allows you to defer up to $210,000 (2025 [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/) limit) or 25% of retirement balances (whichever is less) past RMD age, with payments starting as late as age 85.

* **Pros:** Defers a portion of RMDs until later, lowering taxable income in your 70s.
* Provides contractually guaranteed lifetime income, reducing longevity risk.
* Simplifies budgeting for healthcare or late-life costs.
* **Cons:** Capped at $210,000 or 25% of balances.
* Income is fully taxable when it begins.
* Less flexible—once purchased, it’s irreversible.
* May leave less for heirs unless survivor benefits are added.
* **Best for:** Retirees with large IRAs/401(k)s who want to shrink near-term RMDs, hedge longevity, and lock in future income.
* **Not ideal for:** Those prioritizing legacy, liquidity, or tax-free income growth.

#### How Roth Conversions Work

A Roth conversion moves funds from a Traditional IRA/401(k) into a Roth IRA. You pay taxes at the time of conversion, but all future growth and withdrawals are tax-free.

* **Pros:** Eliminates future RMDs on converted balances.
* Growth is tax-free for life.
* Provides heirs with tax-free [inheritance](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/how-to-spend-an-inheritance-efficiently/).
* Flexible—funds can be withdrawn tax-free after the rules are met.
* **Cons:** Creates an immediate tax bill.
* May push you into higher brackets temporarily.
* Reduces investable capital upfront.
* **Best for:** Younger retirees or those with temporarily low tax rates who want to shift money out of pre-tax accounts before RMDs start.
* **Not ideal for:** Retirees already in high brackets or those who need current cash flow.

#### Example: Lifetime Tax Impact

Imagine a retiree age 73 with a $1,000,000 IRA. Without planning, RMDs start at about $40,000 annually, all taxable. A $200,000 [QLAC could reduce RMDs](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/qlacs-to-reduce-rmds/) by roughly 20%, cutting taxable income to $32,000 until payouts begin at age 85. A $200,000 Roth conversion instead would create a tax bill in the conversion year, but afterward the $200,000 grows tax-free and is excluded from all RMDs.
**Takeaway:** QLACs reduce near-term RMDs and provide guaranteed income later. Roth conversions shrink lifetime taxes and benefit heirs. Combining both offers a balance—tax deferral now and tax-free growth for the future.

#### Scenarios for Singles vs. Couples

##### Single Retirees:

* More vulnerable to bracket creep: A single filer pays higher tax rates sooner than a couple.
* Roth conversions are especially valuable because they shrink future taxable income, minimizing the impact of higher single brackets after a spouse’s death.
* QLACs can help create a guaranteed income if no pension is available, but survivor benefits are less of a priority.

##### Married Couples (Joint Filers):

* Benefit from wider tax brackets, which make partial Roth conversions more efficient in the early retirement years.
* QLACs with joint-life or survivor benefits ensure the surviving spouse continues to receive income, reducing the risk of a sudden income drop after the first spouse dies.
* Couples may choose a blended strategy: use Roth conversions while both are alive to maximize joint brackets, and add a QLAC for survivor security.

##### Widows and Widowers:

* Often face a “tax trap” when moving from joint to single brackets. RMDs can push them into higher tax brackets even with lower household income.
* A [Roth conversion strategy](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/calculator/roth-conversion-calculator/) before widowhood can significantly reduce future taxes.
* A QLAC can be structured with survivor benefits, but without one, income may stop after the annuitant’s death.

#### Timing Each Strategy

##### Roth Conversions:

* Best executed in the “gap years”: the period between retirement and when RMDs begin at age 73–75.
* During these years, retirees often have lower taxable income, making it possible to convert at lower tax rates.
* Conversions should usually be spread out across several years to avoid jumping into higher brackets or triggering Medicare surcharges.

##### QLAC Purchases:

* Best timed just before RMDs begin.
* This ensures the QLAC reduces the first RMD calculation while locking in future guaranteed income.
* For those with longevity concerns, purchasing closer to RMD age maximizes the deferral window (potentially to age 85).

##### Blended Timing Strategy:

* Do Roth conversions in early retirement while tax rates are low.
* As RMD age approaches, consider using a QLAC to cover late-life expenses and hedge against living past [life expectancy](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/calculator/life-expectancy-calculator/).
* This combination minimizes lifetime taxes and ensures guaranteed income security.

#### Flowchart-Style Sequence for Decision-Making

* **Retiring Before RMD Age (60s–early 70s)?** Yes → Prioritize Roth conversions in lower tax years.
* No → Skip to QLAC timing.
* **Do You Expect High Longevity or Want Late-Life Income Protection?** Yes → Consider a QLAC purchase before RMDs begin.
* No → Roth conversions may provide more flexibility.
* **Married or Single?** Married → Maximize Roth conversions while both are alive (joint brackets), then add a QLAC with survivor benefits.
* Single → Roth conversions usually provide stronger long-term tax savings.
* **Legacy Planning Important?** Yes → Roth conversions + life insurance amplify tax-free inheritance.
* No → QLAC [guarantees income security for life](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/life-annuity/), even if heirs receive less.
* **Best Overall Strategy for Most Retirees** Roth conversions in the gap years.
* QLAC purchase near RMD start age.
* Blend both to balance tax savings, guaranteed income, and legacy.

#### Real-World Case Studies

##### Case Study 1: Married Couple Retiring at 62 with $1M

* Both spouses retire early with no earned income.
* From age 62–72, they are in a low tax bracket.
* **Strategy** : Do annual Roth conversions of $50,000–$100,000 during gap years to take advantage of joint brackets.
* At 73, they purchase a $150,000 QLAC, reducing RMDs and ensuring late-life income.
* **Outcome** : They eliminate most future RMDs, enjoy tax-free Roth withdrawals, and have a guaranteed income starting at 85 for long-term security.

##### Case Study 2: Widow at 73 with $800k IRA

* Surviving spouse now files as single, moving into higher brackets.
* Immediate RMDs create $30,000+ of taxable income annually.
* **Strategy** : At 73, she purchases a $100,000 QLAC to defer some RMDs and generate income at 85. Limited opportunity for Roth conversions due to already high tax bracket.
* **Outcome** : Reduces RMD pressure in your early 70s, smooths late-life income, but overall tax burden is higher than if conversions had been started earlier while married.

##### Case Study 3: Single Retiree at 65 with $600k IRA

* Retires with a modest income and plans to delay Social Security until 70.
* **Strategy** : Convert $40,000 per year into a Roth IRA from 65–72. At 73, she decides not to purchase a QLAC, relying instead on Roth income and Social Security.
* **Outcome** : Eliminates most RMDs, ensures tax-free withdrawals for life, and maximizes inheritance to children.

#### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

* **QLACs** : Not planning for survivor benefits may leave nothing for heirs.
* **Roth conversions** : Converting too much at once can trigger higher tax brackets, Medicare surcharges, or Social Security taxation.

#### Other Insurance to Consider

* [Long-Term Care Insurance](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/life-annuity/) **:** Protects income streams (QLAC or Roth) from being drained by care costs.
* [Life Insurance](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/life-annuity/) **:** Creates a tax-free death benefit that offsets liquidity lost with QLACs and complements Roth planning.
* [Disability Insurance](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/life-annuity/) **:** (if still working) Secures income during conversion years.

#### Funding Sources

* **QLACs:** Purchased with IRA, 401(k), or other qualified funds before RMDs begin.
* **Roth conversions:** Use the same accounts, shifting balances into Roth IRAs.

#### Bottom Line

* **Choose QLACs** : if your priority is lowering RMDs now and guaranteeing late-life income.
* **Choose Roth conversions:** if your goal is to lower lifetime tax exposure and leave a tax-free inheritance.
* **Blend both:** to balance tax control, income security, and flexibility.
