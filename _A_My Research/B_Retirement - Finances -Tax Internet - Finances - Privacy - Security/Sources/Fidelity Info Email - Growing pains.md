Growing pains

What happened: US gross domestic product (GDP), a measure of the total productivity of the economy, rose by just 1.5% in the second quarter. This is down from 2.1% in the first quarter.1

Here’s why: Consumer spending helped boost GDP. But imports exceeding exports and reduced government spending weighed things down.

What it means for you: This low GDP reading might be a blip—next quarter’s GDP is estimated to rise to 5.8%, according to the Federal Reserve of Atlanta forecast as of August 6.2 The economy’s continued resilience could help explain the generally strong stock market too.
