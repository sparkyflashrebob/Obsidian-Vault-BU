In investing, "cap" stands for market capitalization—the total dollar value of a company’s outstanding shares, calculated by multiplying the share price by the total number of shares. Companies are categorized by size, determining their risk and growth potential. [1, 2]
Key Market Cap Categories Large-Cap ( $10 Billion+): These are massive, well-established, "blue-chip" companies (e.g., Apple, Microsoft). They offer greater stability, dividend payouts, and are less volatile, but generally have slower growth rates.$
$Mid-Cap ($ 2 Billion to $10 Billion): These are medium-sized companies in the process of expanding and gaining market share. They offer a balance between the stability of large caps and the growth potential of small caps.$
$Small-Cap ($ 250 Million to $2 Billion): These are typically younger, developing, or niche companies. They often have the highest growth potential but come with higher risk, price volatility, and vulnerability to market downturns. [1, 6]
Which one is right for you?
Most investors utilize a mix of these to diversify their portfolios. Large caps provide a sturdy foundation, while mid and small caps are added for more aggressive growth. [2, 7, 8]
For further reading on how these classifications impact risk and return, you can explore the FINRA Market Cap Guide or the in-depth Investopedia Market Cap Breakdown. [2, 9]
