## Highlights
- fascia ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9784v77f67q5sqktsb82y))
- Fascia is a thin casing of connective tissue that surrounds and holds every organ, blood vessel, bone, nerve fiber and muscle in place. The tissue does more than provide internal structure; fascia has nerves that make it almost as sensitive as skin. When stressed, it tightens up. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh97qdjsw7a3qvrrec673m4))
- made up of multiple layers with liquid in between called hyaluronan ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh98v5hgwaawh87972hvgmf))
- designed to stretch as you move ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh992zvr7emrpg9aaysptz8))
- When it dries up and tightens around muscles, it can limit mobility and cause painful knots to develop ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh99ggnt8kq5b0t6fs67y83))
- Healthy fascia is smooth, slippery and flexible. Factors that cause fascia to become gummy and crinkle up (called adhesion) include:
  • A lifestyle of limited physical activity (too little movement day after day)
  • Repetitive movement that overworks one part of the body
  • Trauma such as surgery or injury ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9a5p09ttzydrtmye84pxw))
- Fascia adhesions tend to feel better with movement and also respond well to heat therapy, which helps bring back the tissue’s elasticity. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9as3sjyte5z9qw8r5vn49))
- adhesions can worsen over time, causing the fascia to compress and contort the muscles it surrounds ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9bvebgs3npqhcjhhbhvgk))
- This can result in hard, tender knots in the muscles, called trigger point ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9c4sd4p84bp3ydcxzeqd8))
- Myofascial pain syndrome is a condition in which those trigger points cause pain to occur:
  • During movement
  • When pressure is applied
  • In seemingly unrelated parts of the body (referred pain ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9cfe14fj1j0cas2kjja6y))
- Treatment focuses on relieving pain and getting tight fascia and muscle fibers to relax ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9css9rqqhhftvjrchp2b5))
- Things you can do to prevent fascia problems include:
  • **Move more:** In addition to a consistent but varied exercise routine, it’s important to be active throughout the day. Have a desk job? Take at least a two-minute break every hour to stand up and move around, which helps fascia stay supple. Consider walking meetings or stand up and walk while participating in conference calls. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9dd13zhx30dvpfsh2p05h))
- **Stretch regularly:** Stretching is essential to good health. It reduces the risk of inflammation and structural problems in the body ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9dj49j91qk2xsyv8w11p2))
- **Focus on posture:** Slumping over a desk or a phone or walking in an awkward way to compensate for an injury can cause fascia to tighten. Try to maintain good posture while sitting or standing ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9dvz0jv4w0gekwqrsvme5))
- Ways to Relieve Fascia Pain
  There are various strategies that work to loosen up painful knots, such as:
  • **Heat therapy:** Apply a heating pad to the affected area or take a warm shower or bath.
  • **Yoga therapy:** See a highly trained yoga therapist to get a regimen of yoga poses targeted to treat your area of pain. ([Yoga therapy](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/sibley-memorial-hospital/patient-care/specialty/integrative-medicine/yoga-therapy.html) works in the same manner as physical therapy — the therapist creates a routine and you practice it at home between visits.)
  • **Foam rolling:** Try a foam roller, a cylinder of hard foam that you roll your body over to release tension. It’s a form of self-massage. You can also do this with a lacrosse ball.
  • **Massage therapy:** Schedule multiple therapeutic massage sessions with an experienced therapist who can find and apply pressure to release knots.
  • **Acupuncture:** The insertion of [acupuncture](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/acupuncture) needles into trigger points can cause tense tissue fibers to relax. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gxh9eb8xqj8gdhs411wwdg0x))
