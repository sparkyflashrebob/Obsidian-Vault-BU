The 30 Best Cyberpunk Movies Ranked

Science fiction films can inspire us with optimistic visions of the future. It's powerful to see a film that predicts humanity will better itself, as it gives us something to strive for. The optimistic cooperation of
the "Star Trek" franchise
https://www.slashfilm.com/718751/every-star-trek-movie-ranked-worst-to-best/
showed how much better the universe would be if different cultures learned to peacefully coexist. Some sci-fi predictions even came true: In 1902, the Georges Méliès film
"A Trip to the Moon"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FExtraordinary-Voyage-Deluxe-Combo-Blu-Ray%2Fdp%2FB0081XAQNE%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-1
showed what a potential lunar voyage would look like over six decades before the "Apollo 11" mission.
However, science fiction films aren't always this optimistic. There are many that believe that the future looks bleak. A science fiction film can be a dire warning about what is to come. If humanity continues to make the same mistakes, what hope can there be for society to just work itself out?
Dystopian sci-fi movies are frightening because their predictions sometimes feel all too real
https://www.slashfilm.com/900681/dystopian-sci-fi-movie-worlds-ranked-by-how-horrible-theyd-be-to-live-in/
The cyberpunk subgenre is a specific niche within dystopian stories. It combines advanced technology, innovations in artificial intelligence, and vast metropolitan environments. While these stunning advancements would seemingly be the pinnacle of mankind's achievements, they only reveal how lonely we've become. Many of the characters trapped within cyberpunk worlds are pushed into the shadows. Heroism is rare, and it often comes from a motley group of protagonists. Cyberpunk cinema counts among it some of the greatest sci-fi classics of all time. Here are the 25 greatest cyberpunk movies, ranked.
30. Tokyo Gore Police (2008)
Sony Pictures
As you can probably guess by the title, this film is about gore and police in the sprawling city of Tokyo. A deranged scientist by the name of "Key Man" (Itsuji Itao) has spread a virus that causes victims to grow strange, Cronenberg-style body weapons that they use with ferocious aggression. To meet this threat of so-called "Engineers," the Tokyo police deploy a neo-samurai force with a license to kill and brutalize.
The first clash begins on a city rooftop, and within a few minutes, we've seen a blade mutilate an arm and a flying chainsaw do terrible damage to a police officer's throat and another's mouth. The cops may look tough, but they're hapless compared to Ruka (Eihi Shiina), a specialist among specialists who uses a rocket launcher as a kind of trampoline to boost herself onto the rooftop, where she tears the "Engineer" limb from limb.
"Tokyo Gore Police"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FTokyo-Gore-Police-Blu-ray%2Fdp%2FB004BF6NZQ%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-3
spends the rest of its 110 minutes in this fashion.
The bloody zaniness is all Japanese
https://www.slashfilm.com/1149255/the-best-japanese-action-movies-of-all-time/
, but it takes cues from elsewhere too — namely Paul Verhoeven's satirical commercials in "RoboCop" and
"Starship Troopers."
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FStarship-Troopers-Blu-ray-Casper-Dien%2Fdp%2FB07288ZGX7%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-3
It's comic book cyberpunk.
29. A.I. Artificial Intelligence (2001)
Dreamworks Pictures
Although the premise suggests that it is a heartwarming family adventure, Steven Spielberg's 2001 cyberpunk film
"A.I. Artificial Intelligence"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FI-Artificial-Intelligence-Blu-ray%2Fdp%2FB004FECNIO%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-4
is a devastating tragedy. It's a story inspired by two genius filmmakers. The concept was initially imagined by the late great Stanley Kubrick, who sadly passed away before he could complete his work on the film. Prior to his death,
Kubrick handed the reins to Spielberg
https://www.slashfilm.com/1172656/steven-spielbergs-divisive-ending-for-a-i-artificial-intelligence-was-stanley-kubricks-original-vision/
, whom he imagined was the only director who could handle the advanced computer-generated imagery the story required.
"A.I. Artificial Intelligence" is set within the 22nd century, where the worldwide population has been severely depleted and androids that look human but lack emotion have been created. The couple Henry (Sam Robards) and Monica Swinton (Frances O'Connor) are grieving after their son falls in a coma, so they adopt a young robot child named David (Haley Joel Osment). David seeks the love of a family but struggles to find acceptance, leading to a story about what exactly it means to be human and whether or not our young hero can ever become a "real boy."
28. Naked Lunch (1991)
First Independent Films
"Without 'Naked Lunch' there would probably be no cyberpunk," wrote Richard Cadrey and Larry McCaffery in their book "Storming The Reality Studio: A Casebook of Cyberpunk." They were referring to William S. Burroughs' seminal beatnik novel from 1959, which David Cronenberg adapted for the screen decades later in 1991.
The nightmarish material was never going to make for a linear adaptation, so Cronenberg drew not just on
"Naked Lunch"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FNaked-Lunch-Criterion-Collection-Weller%2Fdp%2FB06W9M7LQ7%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-5
but also elements of Burroughs' other work and his turbulent personal life. The result is a beautifully made film that uses lighting, set design, and period tailoring to evoke the author's aberrant perspective.
Peter Weller leads as William Lee, a fictionalized version of Burroughs (who used "William Lee" as an early pseudonym). Lee is an exterminator, and he enjoys the work, although his boss reprimands him for running short on insecticide. When we discover that his wife, Joan, is using the bug powder as an intravenous opioid replacement, the film loses whatever touch it had with reality and becomes a hallucinogenic ride of insect typewriters and inexplicable North African locales.
The cyberpunk qualities come not from a dystopian society but from a dystopian mind — Lee's drug-addled point of view is almost entirely psychotic. Like other cyberpunks, technology governs Lee's life, and in this case, it is a series of mutant typewriters that assume bug-like forms, playing with his mind and his sense of reality. Weller carries the role effortlessly with a pensive charisma that balances the film's illusory excesses.
27. Strange Days (1995)
20th Century Studios
"Strange Days"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FStrange-Days-Blu-ray-Ralph-Fiennes%2Fdp%2FB073V9MT21%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-6
directed by Kathryn Bigelow
https://www.slashfilm.com/1130453/every-kathryn-bigelow-film-ranked-worst-to-best/
and based on a script she developed with James Cameron that has all the trappings of the cyberpunk genre — crime, technology, and the city. The crime is gangland anarchy, the technology is a recording device named SQUID, and the city is Los Angeles in the final days of 1999. Navigating this is Lenny Nero (Ralph Fiennes), former LAPD officer and current black market creep.
He deals in SQUID, an illegal headset that records people's memories, stores them on memory discs, and allows others to replay them in intense virtual reality experiences. "This is like a piece of someone's life straight from the cerebral cortex," Nero explains to one of his customers. The possibilities here are endless and endlessly disturbing because Nero's customers don't want the innocent stuff — they want perversion and violence. This technomoralism is part of the film's wider commentary on racism, corruption and social stratification.
In the 1990s, "Strange Days" was a total box office bomb, recouping just under $8 million of its $42 million budget. Today, with its striking imagery, explosive set pieces, and dark cyberpunk trappings, it's a cult film worth revisiting.
26. Johnny Mnemonic
TriStar Pictures
Years before he played a very different kind of sci-fi hero in "The Matrix,"
Keanu Reeves starred as the title character in the 1995 cult classic "Johnny Mnemonic."
https://www.slashfilm.com/1667056/keanu-reeves-sci-fi-flop-johnny-mnemonic/
Set in a dystopian 2021, Johnny is a data courier who stores and transports confidential information in his brain. After Johnny receives data that a yakuza syndicate violently wants to suppress, he finds himself on the run from their assassins. To make matters worse, because of the sheer volume of data in his brain, Johnny must extract the data before it overloads and kills him.
Oozing with '90s style
https://www.slashfilm.com/1102046/keanu-reeves-johnny-mnemonic-predicted-the-future-with-a-90s-take-on-80s-cyberpunk/
, "Johnny Mnemonic" has an over-the-top swagger that suits its premise and presentation well. To add to its cyberpunk bonafides, the movie is written by William Gibson, the author who coined the cyberpunk term in the first place. "
Johnny Mnemonic
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FJohnny-Mnemonic-Blu-Ray-Keanu-Reeves%2Fdp%2FB004R0MF48%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-7
" leans hard into neo-noir with its environment and story, bringing in Dolph Lundgren as a worthy opponent for Reeves' Johnny. Audacious and eccentric, "Johnny Mnemonic" took time to find its audience but always proudly wore its cyberpunk colors on its sleeve.
25. Tron (1982)
Walt Disney Pictures
The cyberpunk genre isn't only popular with cinephiles but with gamers too. Cyberpunk-style concepts have been used since the earliest video games were being developed, and the 1982 film
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FTron-Original-Classic-Two-Disc-Blu-ray%2Fdp%2FB004KPLVW2%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-8
imagined what would happen if someone was transported into the reality of a cyberpunk game.
The film follows the former video game designer Kevin Flynn (Jeff Bridges), who now operates his own private arcade. Flynn is a tech wizard, but his attempt to hack into a system's mainframe goes wrong when he encounters a mysterious Master Control Program. The MCP has become a sentient virtual intelligence program and built an entirely digital world. Flynn is transported into the game he once helped design, where he must use his arcade skills to beat the system and escape back to his own reality.
While the visual effects in "Tron" may seem dated now (it was the first movie to use long sequences of entirely computer-generated effects), the themes are still relevant. And if you're looking for more, the 2010 sequel
"Tron: Legacy"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FTron-Legacy-Two-Disc-Blu-ray-Combo%2Fdp%2FB004K4IZ3G%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-8
successfully modernized the original concept with a heartfelt storyline featuring Flynn and his son Sam (Garrett Hedlund) fighting a new threat together.
24. THX 1138 (1971)
Warner Bros.
Have you ever wondered what it would be like to live on a motherboard, nestled between rows of RAM and processors? George Lucas imagined something approximating this in
"THX 1138,"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FTHX-1138-George-Lucas-Directors%2Fdp%2FB0002CHIJC%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-9
his directorial debut. The film is set in a 25th-century society that's governed by a sterile cyborg entity like HAL 9000, only worse.
However, the titular THX 1138 is not a thing but a person, played by Robert Duvall. He lives in a vast underground complex of stark corridors and enormous assembly lines where nothing is organic, not even his human peers. Everyone and everything is fixed, programmed, and regimented. There is no family or friendship, only work. It's an ant colony of white coats and shaved heads. Who's controlling this? Who would want to live in such a society? Slave handlers above ground, perhaps — we don't know.
Those who come to "THX 1138" from
"Star Wars"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FStar-Wars-Complete-Collection-Material%2Fdp%2FB07GZXFCTF%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-9
may be disappointed because the films couldn't be further apart within the science fiction genre. But as a vision of dystopian institutional hell, "THX 1138" is an interesting experiment.
23. Brazil (1985)
Sunset Boulevard/Getty Images
After his involvement with the British comedy troupe Monty Python,
Terry Gilliam brought the same satirical style to his films as a writer/director
https://www.slashfilm.com/621988/every-terry-gilliam-movie-ranked-worst-to-best/
. Gilliam's work is both whimsical and bleak, and his 1985 cyberpunk story
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FBrazil-Jonathan-Pryce%2Fdp%2F0783225903%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-10
is his most imaginative achievement. The film is set within a totalitarian future overrun by industrialism and corporate synergy. A select group of elites reign supreme, and the majority of citizens work in a dehumanizing bureaucratic metropolis.
The low-level government employee Sam Lowry (Jonathan Pryce) dreams of rising from oppression and becoming a great hero. He even has mysterious visions of saving a beautiful woman, and eventually, he meets a truck driver Jill Layton (Kim Greist), who looks like the damsel he's dreamed of. Unfortunately, she's accused of terrorism by the aggressive Central Services police force. With the government breathing down their necks, Sam dreams of a life of love and romance, but can that be achieved in a world that's basically the absurd version of George Orwell's
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2F1984-Signet-Classics-George-Orwell%2Fdp%2F0451524934%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-10
Complete with a terrific turn from Robert De Niro as rebellious plumber, "Brazil" is equally hilarious and dark as it delves deep into the power of imagination and hope in the face of a faceless dictatorship.
22. Alita: Battle Angel
20th Century Studios
Yukito Kishiro's
best-selling manga "Battle Angel Alita"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FBattle-Angel-Alita-Deluxe-Complete%2Fdp%2F1632367114%2F%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-11
was adapted into a movie in 2019 under the title "
Alita: Battle Angel
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FAlita-Battle-Blu-ray-Jennifer-Connelly%2Fdp%2FB07ML9YZ3L%2F%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-11
" after being stuck for years in various stages of development. 500 years after Earth was devastated by a war against colonies on Mars, the advanced cyborg Alita (Rosa Salazar) is activated. As Alita develops her identity in the slums of Iron City, she befriends a man named Hugo (Keean Johnson) and incurs the wrath of cyborg enforcer Grewishka (Jackie Earle Haley). Alita enters the gladiatorial game of Motorball to earn enough prize money for her and Hugo to enter the floating city of Zalem, only to face Grewishka and his associates.
With filmmaker Robert Rodriguez in the director's chair and James Cameron serving as producer and co-writer, "Alita: Battle Angel" was always going to have gripping action set pieces. But beyond the sci-fi spectacle, this adaptation of Kishiro's story creates an immersive and quintessentially cyberpunk world, complete with a hodge-podge of influences. An immediate cult classic, fans have rallied
to get a sequel made
https://www.slashfilm.com/1129917/an-alita-battle-angel-sequel-could-actually-happen-according-to-producer-jon-landau/
for years, especially given the movie's cliffhanger ending. In the meantime, "Alita: Battle Angel" is a solid cyberpunk adventure, buoyed by propulsive action and its memorable protagonist.
21. Code 46 (2003)
Verve Pictures
Romantic relationships are often doomed in cyberpunk films. Is true love even possible when the future looks so bleak? Michael Winterbottom's highly underrated 2006 film
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FCode-46-Blu-ray-Tim-Robbins%2Fdp%2FB018TJ0LNY%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-12
imagined a cyberpunk society where relationships are predetermined by government-approved DNA pairings. Utilizing genetic manipulation, the totalitarian state designs "ideal" relationships. This means that it's now impossible, and illegal, to fall in love naturally. These rules are established in a series of codes, with the title "Code 46" outlawing genetically similar people from having relationships.
As for the plot, William Geld (Tim Robbins) is an insurance fraud investigator who secretly helps companies identify which of their employees are using false identities. Due to strict travel laws, some "genetically inferior" workers use these false IDs to travel between cities. William interviews the receptionist Maria Gonzalez (Samantha Morton), and they bond after discovering they share a recurring dream. William falls in love with Maria, but their relationship is considered to be illegal.
20. Westworld
Long before it was an HBO series, "
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FWestworld-Yul-Brynner%2Fdp%2FB008ROGQAE%2F%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-13
" was a 1973 movie written and directed by prolific novelist
Michael Crichton (whose books have been adapted plenty of times)
https://www.slashfilm.com/1865957/michael-crichton-movie-adaptations-ranked/
. Set in the near-future, the story takes place in a theme park recreating archetypal moments in human history, including the Wild West. When the theme park breaks down and locks down, trapping its visitors inside, they have to contend with the now murderous androids populating each of the park's sections. This includes tourist Peter Martin (Richard Benjamin), who is stalked by a merciless gunslinging android (Yul Brynner) as he searches for a way out.
The more existential questions
permeating throughout the HBO series
https://www.slashfilm.com/924018/westworlds-plot-is-confusing-heres-what-you-need-to-remember/
are sidelined in the "Westworld" movie for a straightforward sci-fi action movie. The idea of corporate malfeasance is still present, given how much Delos Inc. disregards the lives of its guests and staff through its shutdown process. That said, there are still questions about agency and the existence of the androids in the face of pleasure-seeking human guests in this proto-cyberpunk story. If you're looking for a less overtly thought-provoking take on the futuristic theme park gone wrong, check out the 1973 "Westworld."
19. Dredd (2012)
Entertainment Film Distributors
The Judge Dredd comic book character was developed in the comic strip "2000 AD" as one of the first cyberpunk heroes. The futuristic judge, jury, and executioner was a complex anti-hero. Unfortunately, the 1995 film
"Judge Dredd"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FJudge-Dredd-DVD%2Fdp%2FB0CW2B7X4Y%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-14
starring Sylvester Stallone failed to capture the tone of the original source material. The Stallone film was campy and didn't reflect the more nuanced moral dilemmas that the Dredd character dealt with.
Thankfully, the 2012 film
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FDredd-Blu-ray-3D-Karl-Urban%2Fdp%2FB008OGHUFK%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-14
was a proper adaptation. Karl Urban stars as the titular character, who's one of the many judges working the streets of a dystopian world known as "Cursed Earth." The Chief Judge tasks Dredd with training the new recruit Cassandra Anderson (Olivia Thirlby), a psychic who can invade the minds of suspected criminals. Dredd and Cassandra are assigned to take down the drug warlord Ma-Ma (Lena Headey), who's gathered her criminals in a futuristic sky-rise. Our heroes then must fight their way to the top, so if you're looking for a sci-fi version of
"The Raid: Redemption,"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FRaid-Redemption-Doni-Alamsyah%2Fdp%2FB0085A9J18%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-14
then definitely check out "Dredd."
18. Escape From New York (1981)
John Carpenter's 1981 film
"Escape From New York"
https://r.bttn.io?btn\_ref=org-225e4e0aac880b8f&btn\_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FEscape-New-York-Kurt-Russell%2Fdp%2FB00004Y87O%3Ftag%3Dstaticmedia-slashfilm-20&btn\_pub\_ref=771494-in\_text-15
is set in the far-off future of, um, 1997. Anyway, in this movie's timeline, that's when the United States has been overrun by criminal activity. As a desperate means of managing the growing prison population, the U.S. government has turned New York City into a sprawling maximum-security prison where criminals are dumped. But after Air Force One is hijacked while passing over Manhattan, President of the United States John Harker (Donald Pleasence) is kidnapped by the Big Apple's worst crooks and held for ransom. The military must find a way to extract him, and they turn to an unlikely hero.
The notorious former Special Forces soldier Snake Plissken (Kurt Russell) is given a unique opportunity. Plissken has been imprisoned for robbery, but if he rescues the president he will receive a full pardon. Plissken is no fan of the government or the president, but it's the only option he has to avoid jail time so he ventures into the dangerous wasteland. Russell is a great lead for this mix of action, science fiction, and social satire. He has a defiant attitude that is reminiscent of classic western gunslingers.
17. Demolition Man (1993)
Warner Bros.
There are two main linchpins in the dystopian canon. One is the tyrannical world of "1984," in which brute force is the tool of compliance. The other is
"Brave New World,"

Set in a dystopian future where cities are overrun by crime, "Robocop" follows Detroit police officer Alex Murphy (Peter Weller) trying to clean up the streets, but the cops are simply overwhelmed by deadly criminal gangs that dominate the city. After tracking down the crime lord Clarence Boddicker (Kurtwood Smith), Murphy is shot down and brutally tortured. He dies from his wounds, but an ambitious young executive at the mega-corporation Omni Consumer Products has an idea on how to bring him back to life. Resurrected as RoboCop, Murphy struggles to regain his humanity in a world run by capitalist overlords, and when you couple that with some biting satire, insane effects work, and a truly iconic score, well, movies don't get much better than that.
