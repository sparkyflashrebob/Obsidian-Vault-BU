The sources define and explain the roles of concentration, lovingkindness, and compassion as fundamental practices that support spiritual awakening and freedom, often framed as the "two wings of understanding and compassion" [1].

Here's a breakdown of their uses:

### 1. Concentration

**Definition and Purpose**:

* Concentration is a deliberate training of attention, the ability to **focus the mind fully on a particular experience** [2, 3].
* It serves to **quiet and focus the mind** [4-7].
* The deepest level of concentration allows one to **become unified with a particular experience**, where thoughts and difficulties begin to drop away [3].
* It makes the mind **focused, quiet, peaceful, directed, and often quite ecstatic** [3].
* Concentration supports mindfulness, as it's hard to be mindful when attention is scattered [7, 8].
* While concentration involves effort to sustain and control the mind, it's important not to become caught in a striving effort or judge the meditation as "good" or "not good" [9].
* It can lead to states of rapture and deep peace, but **without a mindful presence, concentration bears no fruit** [7].

**How it's Cultivated**:

* It involves **choosing an anchor** (primarily breath, but possibly sound, sensations, or an image/mantra) and repeatedly returning attention to it [4-6, 8, 10-12].
* Examples include focusing on a candle flame, a sacred mandala, or repeating a sacred sound (mantra) [3].
* The anchor chosen for concentration should have a pleasant or at least neutral feeling tone [6].
* The mind becomes "one-pointed" through repetition, allowing thoughts and other identity aspects to dissolve [3, 13].
* It requires **relaxed attention** [6].

### 2. Lovingkindness (Metta)

**Definition and Purpose**:

* Lovingkindness (Metta in Pali) is described as a **general, open-hearted, caring, friendly attitude** [14-16].
* It is the **grounds of cultivating a healing and wise attention** [16].
* A key synonym for mindfulness is "present heart," which is understood as the **grounds of love in action** [16].
* It awakens us to our **connectedness with all of life** [17, 18].
* It's a way of cultivating resources and creating **more safety, space, and softness** [19, 20].
* When we hold our inner life with love, it **dissolves all sense of being self-centered** [21].
* It helps to **soften and open the heart** [22, 23].
* It is a **natural expression when we are fully awake** [24].
* Lovingkindness, or metta, helps us **de-condition the habit of looking for what's wrong or fixating on the negative** when we are stressed, afraid, anxious, or angry [25, 26].
* It helps balance situations where compassion might turn into excess attachment [27].
* Its goal is to **awaken love and free the heart** [28].

**How it's Cultivated**:

* It involves a deliberate training that counters our conditioning, which often blocks the impulse to give and thank [24].
* It can be practiced through **formal sitting meditations** or **informally throughout the day** [29-31].
* The practice uses specific phrases to send loving and kind wishes to **yourself, loved ones, neutral persons, difficult people, and to all beings everywhere, without exception** [32-34].
* The intention is key; even if the words feel mechanical, the intention to awaken lovingkindness matters most [35, 36].
* It's often recommended to **start with those you love most** or those for whom love comes easily (e.g., a child or a pet) [37-39].
* If you are agitated or upset, the practice can include this reactivity by silently wishing, "May this too be held in lovingkindness" [31, 40].
* It also involves **seeing the goodness in others** [26, 28, 41, 42].
* Placing a hand on the heart can deepen the experience of holding oneself with kindness [43, 44].
* When teaching, it can be framed as an **experiment**, encouraging students to find what works for them and allowing them to customize their practice [45, 46].

### 3. Compassion

**Definition and Purpose**:

* Compassion is the **feeling that arises when we encounter suffering, and we feel a motivation to relieve it** [47]. It includes a desire to help [47, 48].
* It's often described as the **"trembling of our quivering heart in response to suffering"** [49-51].
* It is the **love that accompanies awareness**, especially love towards oneself [52].
* Compassion helps us to **hold suffering without being overwhelmed** [53].
* It offers **emotional safety needed to mindfully open to our pain** [54, 55].
* It is an **active response** [56-58].
* It serves to **reduce negative states of mind and increase positive ones** [59].
* Compassion for difficult others (e.g., family members with different values, public figures) is **essential for freedom and the healing of the world** [60].
* It's a process of **de-conditioning our wiring to turn away from unpleasant experiences** [58].
* It allows us to **care profoundly without becoming overwhelmed** [60, 61].
* Mature compassion includes **mindfulness, kindness, and wisdom** (or common humanity) [62, 63].

**How it's Cultivated**:

* It requires being **willing to turn towards where the suffering is and be touched by it** [58, 64-66].
* It involves **mindful presence to physical or emotional difficulty**, which causes the heart to open [67].
* A key practice is **RAIN (Recognize, Allow, Investigate, Nurture)**, which applies mindfulness and compassion to difficult experiences [68-72].
  + **Recognize** what is arising (e.g., fear, hurt) [71, 73].
  + **Allow** it to be there without judgment [71, 73].
  + **Investigate** the felt sense in the body with interest and care, asking questions like "What is happening?" or "What does this vulnerable place need?" [70, 71, 73].
  + **Nurture** involves actively offering kindness, acceptance, forgiveness, or protection to the vulnerable part, often through words, touch (e.g., hand on heart), or imagery [74-78].
* Compassion begins with **self-compassion** for one's own reactivity and barriers before extending it to others [79-81].
* It can involve visualizing someone's struggles and extending well-wishing phrases [82-84].
* In moments of self-judgment or pain, self-compassion creates a sense of safety to mindfully open to the pain [54, 55].
* It's not about what someone deserves, but about **what you want for your own heart – freedom of love rather than a prison of hatred** [60].
* The practice of "strong back, soft front" embodies compassion, combining care and protection with tenderness and receptivity [85-87].

### The Two Wings of Awakening: Understanding (Mindfulness) and Compassion (Heartfulness)

The sources frequently refer to mindfulness and compassion as **"the two wings of awakening,"** both essential for the path to freedom and healing [1, 67, 88-90].

* **Mindfulness (Understanding/Wisdom)**:

  + Mindfulness is broadly defined as **non-judgmental, caring attention, here and now** [91]. It is a spacious, kind, non-judging awareness of the present [92-94].
  + It's the **awareness of what's happening** [52].
  + Mindfulness helps us **recognize what is present moment-to-moment** without judgment, and includes "clear comprehension" (sampajañña) for an appropriate response [92-96].
  + It allows us to **step outside the "ever-flowing stream of thoughts and witness them"** with loving awareness [97, 98]. This "witnessing" capacity brings clarity and perspective [91, 99].
  + It helps us **distinguish pain from suffering** by dissolving the identification that creates suffering [100].
  + By expanding our "window of tolerance," mindfulness enables us to be present for a greater range of experiences, thus growing peace [101, 102].
  + Mindfulness provides the **clarity and insight needed to see "things as they are"** [67, 103, 104], dissolving limiting beliefs and identification with a limited self [105, 106].
  + It helps us to realize that **awareness itself is our true nature**, which is clear, transparent, and timeless [106-109].
* **Compassion (Heartfulness/Love)**:

  + Compassion, along with lovingkindness, provides the **"fuel" or "motive force"** for spiritual activism and engaging with the world's suffering [110].
  + It is the **love that accompanies awareness**, especially love towards oneself [52].
  + Compassion is an **expression of our evolutionary potential** [111].
  + When love meets pain, it turns to compassion [50].
  + It allows for **healing and transformation** by providing a sense of safety and allowing us to open to pain [54, 55, 112, 113].
  + It enables us to be with difficult emotions without dissociation or being overwhelmed [114, 115].
  + Compassion empowers us to **respond from our wholeness** rather than limbic reactivity [116].
  + It fosters interconnectedness and helps to dissolve the illusion of separateness [102, 117].

**Synergy and Interrelation**:

* Mindfulness is the **first step to compassion** because without awareness of suffering, there cannot be a compassionate response [112, 118].
* Self-compassion **enhances mindfulness** by creating the safety needed to mindfully open to pain [54, 55, 113].
* Mindfulness and compassion are like a **"beautiful dance"**; you can't separate them [54, 55].
* The "present heart" combines wakefulness (mindfulness) with openness and tenderness (love) [92-94].
* Both practices allow us to see and hold the "ten thousand joys and ten thousand sorrows" of life with a **spacious and tender connection** [23, 119, 120].
* They lead to a **shift in identity from being fixated on a story or feeling to realizing our true nature as loving awareness** [106, 121-123].

In essence, concentration steadies the mind, mindfulness provides the clear, non-judgmental awareness needed to understand reality and suffering, and lovingkindness and compassion provide the heart's capacity to engage with that reality and suffering with care, kindness, and a desire to alleviate it. Together, they form a holistic path to awakening and freedom from suffering [1, 89, 124-126].
