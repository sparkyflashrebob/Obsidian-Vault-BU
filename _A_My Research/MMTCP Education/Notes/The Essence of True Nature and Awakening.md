Based on the sources and our conversation history, our true nature is described in several interconnected ways, often contrasted with the conditioned or "small" self:

At its core, our true nature is **awareness or consciousness itself** [1-7]. It is the **unchanging awareness in which all activity takes place** [1]. This awareness is not something we add to ourselves, but is **who you are**, the **essence of you** [4, 5], the **formless, illuminated presence that's the very source of our being** [1]. It is also described as **Being**, the Being dimension [5, 8]. In essence, **"You are"** [5, 9].

This true nature is also inherently connected with **love** and a **fundamental goodness** [3, 7, 9-13]. It is the **original nature of your own mind** [14], comprised of **awareness itself and of love** [7]. Our **deepest longing is not to cause harm** [15] and the ability to **love without holding back** is a natural aspiration, tasting like **beauty and the homecoming, really inhabiting who we are** [16, 17]. Compassionate awareness is seen as a **natural outflow** of mindfulness practice, arising from **interconnectedness** [18]. Indeed, **"the whole thing is about love"** [9].

Other qualities associated with our true nature include:

* **Wholeness** [19, 20]: Recognizing we are caught in an "unreal self" or trance when cut off from this wholeness.
* **Vastness** [21, 22]: Spiritual surrender and attention help us discover how vast we are.
* **Boundlessness and infinite spaciousness** [23]: The space in which change and impermanence occur.
* **Openness and fertility** [6]: Consciousness in its purest state.
* **Innate potential** [24]: A yearning to be all that we can be, aligned with who we are.
* **Presence** [1, 25]: The quality of being before conditioning.
* **Mystery** [7, 26-29]: It's a fundamental aspect of life and being human.
* **Resilient well-being** [30]: A core of peace, contentment, and love that can be cultivated.
* **Uniting or unifying aliveness** [31]: Felt when we sit quietly, connecting us to the greater whole.
* **The "whole world in our heart"** [32, 33]: Holographic science is mentioned as suggesting the part contains the whole.

Our true nature is **always here, now**, and it is **possible to realize** [29, 34]. It is described as our **birthright** [35].

However, this true nature is often obscured by:

* **Conditioning** [34, 36-39]: Fear-based conditioning holds onto limiting beliefs for self-protection [37], creating a **"dream" or "trance"** that keeps us removed from realization [34]. This includes **evolutionary conditioning** [38, 39] and communication patterns that create separation [40].
* **Living in a mind-created or interpreted world** [41-43]: We live in a virtual reality or prison based on thoughts and ideas, confusing the map with the territory or living truth [41-46].
* **"Selfing" or the sense of a separate self** [47-50]: Our identity gets captured by beliefs and the constant self-referencing in the mentally constructed world [49-51]. This feels vulnerable and threatened, leading to blaming [48, 52]. The "solitary, isolated human being is really a fiction" [13].
* **Fear and grasping** [41, 53-55]: When actions or intentions are shaped by fear and grasping, we are "below the line" or in a trance, disconnected from our true intent [54-56].
* **Identifying with emotions or a small part of self** [57-59]: Emotions become suffering when identity takes their shape [57]. The "small self" is often organized around what we are unwilling to feel or be with [58]. We can be identified with a limited part like the victim, aggressor, or defender [59].

Awakening to or realizing our true nature involves:

* **Inquiry** [1, 28, 60-63]: Asking "Who am I?", "What is aware?", "What is listening?", "What is happening?" [1, 28, 60, 62-64].
* **Mindfulness and mindful awareness** [2, 3, 14, 65-67]: Bringing attention to the present moment, including difficult emotions, which allows us to hold "it all" [58, 67] and reveals this timeless awareness [14]. Mindful awareness of the body gives rise to love and compassion [66]. Presence awareness is key [2].
* **Compassion and loving awareness** [3, 9, 17, 26, 66, 68-73]: Turning towards suffering with kindness [72]. Compassion for self and others helps loosen identification [74]. Forgiving blame allows seeing sacredness and our true natures [17]. Loving awareness allows us to witness experience without trying to fix it [68] and is who we are [3, 9, 64, 73]. It is seeing the "secret beauty" in others [7].
* **Letting go of the "net"** [42, 43, 75]: Stepping beyond the net of desires, thoughts, and concepts that divide reality into categories [42, 43, 75]. Quitting believing in the self [76].
* **Returning to the body/present moment** [77-81]: Inhabiting the body's aliveness reveals beingness [77]. Coming back into our bodies wakes up and widens our caring when dissociated [78]. Being in the living reality of the body is different from being lost in thought [79]. Opening to sensations reveals the space of presence [81].
* **Accepting imperfection and impermanence** [9, 23, 73, 82-87]: Recognizing that perfection is an imagination and being perfectly imperfect is the reality of human life [9, 73, 84]. Impermanence is woven into existence [86], and attending to its uncertainty and beauty wakes us up from egoic trance [87].
* **Undoing patterns of separation and blame** [40, 59, 88, 89]: Cultivating an "all-inclusive heart" [88] and shifting from fight/flight/freeze to attend/befriend and interbeing [89]. Widening the lens to see others from awareness, returning to respect [59].
* **Seeing interconnectedness** [2, 7, 12, 13, 18, 90, 91]: Realizing that we are "just one people" [90], that we are interconnected [7, 12, 13, 18], and that the "solitary, isolated human being is really a fiction" [13]. This leads to compassionate awareness [18]. Recognizing "You are all of life" [91].

Ultimately, the teachings point towards **resting in this loving awareness**, which is who we really are [9, 73]. It is the **homecoming** to this loving awareness that is our essence [64]. Everything we seek, we already are [9].
