The two questions, "**What is happening right now?**" and "**Can I be with this?**" are considered fundamental and deeply helpful in mindfulness practice, serving as a core template for engaging with moment-to-moment experience and fostering insight [1-8]. They are often described as the "heart" of individual mentoring and facilitate a person's experiential discovery [5].

Here's a breakdown of what makes these questions so valuable:

### 1. "What is happening right now?" (The "Recognize" aspect)

This question is a direct invitation to **bring attention to the present moment** and acknowledge whatever is arising in one's experience without judgment [1-4, 6, 7, 9, 10].

* **Anchoring Presence:** It prompts the practitioner to **scan the body for predominant sensations**, feel the breath, and notice moods and states of mind [4, 7-9, 11-14]. This helps to quiet and collect the mind, deepening embodied presence [15].
* **Unveiling Experience:** It encourages **curiosity and interest** in the direct, felt sense of what's happening internally, rather than getting lost in conceptual understanding or external distractions [7, 16-18]. This includes recognizing difficult emotions, physical pain, or thoughts [4, 7, 10, 19-21].
* **Waking from Trance:** By asking "What is happening right now?", one shifts from being absorbed or lost in thought to simply noticing thinking as a phenomenon, thereby **waking up from trance** and returning to full presence [11, 20-22].
* **Foundation for RAIN:** This question is the essence of the "Recognize" step in the RAIN practice (Recognize, Allow, Investigate, Nurture), which is a powerful tool for dealing with difficult experiences by acknowledging what is present [1-3, 6, 10, 23].

### 2. "Can I be with this?" (The "Allow" aspect)

This question cultivates an **attitude of allowing and acceptance** towards whatever has been recognized in the present moment, rather than resisting it or trying to change it [1-4, 6, 8, 10, 23-26].

* **Creating Space:** It encourages the practitioner to **pause and make space** for the experience, letting it be just as it is, even if it's unpleasant [4, 23-29]. Phrases like "This too," "Yes," or "I can hold this" can be used to facilitate this allowing [23, 24, 26, 30, 31].
* **Dissolving Resistance:** The act of allowing is incredibly powerful in **dissolving the resistance that keeps us hooked** to emotions and suffering [23, 26, 27, 32, 33]. When we stop saying "no" to reality, even painful reality, a deeper sense of presence can emerge [31, 32, 34].
* **Developing Resilience:** Consistently practicing "Can I be with this?" helps develop the **habit of staying with what we previously would have left or avoided** [25, 35]. This builds resilience and the capacity to meet life's challenges with more openness [36, 37].
* **Gateway to Freedom:** By engaging with experience in this way, one begins to **undo identity** that is hitched to suffering and discover a larger, more spacious sense of self, which is an expression of true nature and leads to freedom [38-43].

### Their Intertwined Relationship and Benefits:

Mindfulness and concentration are often taught as a **synergy**, where these questions play a central role [1-3, 6, 44].

* **Deepening Presence and Insight:** The initial question, "What is happening right now?", brings the mind into focused attention. The second question, "Can I be with this?", cultivates the relaxed, non-judgmental awareness necessary for that attention to deepen and for insights to arise [4, 8, 29].
* **Working with Difficulties:** These questions are particularly effective when working with "tangles" or suffering, as they create a pathway to directly engage with and process challenging experiences without being overwhelmed [5, 10, 16, 42, 45-48].
* **Empowerment:** By guiding individuals through these questions, teachers empower students to discover their own capacity for presence and freedom, rather than providing prescriptive solutions [5, 49]. This direct experience of shifting one's relationship to discomfort is profoundly transformative [42, 50-53].
* **Safety and Flexibility:** While encouraging deep inquiry, it's crucial for teachers to create a safe space and offer alternatives, allowing students to determine their "window of tolerance" and step back if an experience becomes too overwhelming [11, 36, 54-58]. The effectiveness lies in the gentle, inviting nature of the inquiry [59, 60].
