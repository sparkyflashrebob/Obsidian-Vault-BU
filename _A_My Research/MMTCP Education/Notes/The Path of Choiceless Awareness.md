**Choiceless awareness** is a core aspect of mindfulness practice that involves **not directing or controlling anything, but simply noticing and allowing whatever is predominant in your experience** [1-11].

Here are some key aspects of choiceless awareness:

* **Non-Direction and Non-Control**

  + In choiceless awareness, you are not manipulating or controlling your experience [1-9, 11, 12]. Instead of trying to make something happen or direct your attention in a specific way, you rest in an open, allowing presence [7-9, 13].
  + This "non-doing" presence is considered essential for fully embodying awareness itself, as any attempt to direct attention or focus on something specific can lead to contraction and prevent full awareness [7, 9, 14, 15].
  + The goal is to realize who you are beyond the meditator, to eliminate the sense of a "self" that is "doing" the meditation [14-18].
* **Open and Receptive Attention**

  + It involves **being aware of prominent experiences as they arise from moment to moment** [19]. This includes sounds, sensations, feelings (pleasant, unpleasant, neutral), thoughts, and emotions [11, 19-31].
  + You receive all domains of experience with a **mindful, open attention**, without judgment, resistance, or grasping [11, 26-48].
  + This state allows life to unfold naturally [7-9, 11, 49-52].
* **Relationship to Other Practices**

  + While choiceless awareness is a profound practice, it's acknowledged that it is not the *only* practice [53]. Many meditative strategies or "skillful means" are helpful for quieting the mind and supporting the journey towards natural presence [17, 18, 54-62].
  + These supports, like following the breath, help collect attention and deepen embodied presence, allowing the mind to settle [63-77]. However, one should not confuse these methods with the liberating presence itself [62].
  + It is valuable to dedicate a portion of each meditation sitting to simply resting in Natural Presence without any "doing" [13].
* **Revealing True Nature**

  + As you practice choiceless awareness, you learn to step outside the constantly flowing stream of thoughts and witness them, realizing that thoughts are insubstantial and dissolve like clouds [5, 6, 26-28, 78-89]. This process leads to resting in the field of mindful loving awareness rather than being carried away by thought [79, 84, 90, 91].
  + This practice reveals the truth of impermanence—that all experiences arise and pass away and are ungraspable [92-107]. It allows you to see that sensations and phenomena are just happening, not owned by anyone, leading to a loosening of identification with them and a realization of the emptiness of self [97, 107-112].
  + This shift in identity moves you from being fixated on a particular story or feeling to realizing that your deepest nature is vast awareness itself [11, 87, 107, 113-115]. This awareness is described as open, luminous, knowing, clear, transparent, and timeless [10, 116-121].
  + Resting in this awareness allows you to be **response-able** rather than reactive, offering a space where healing and freedom can emerge [122-125]. It fosters a sense of inner peace and freedom that is not dependent on external conditions [21, 61, 86, 87, 104-106, 126-135].
