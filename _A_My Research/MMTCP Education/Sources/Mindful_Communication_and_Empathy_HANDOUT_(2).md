#### Mindful Communication and Empathy

MINDFULNESS MEDITATION TEACHER CERTIFICATION PROGRAM
Empathy
Empathy is sensing, feeling, and understanding how it is for another person. We are hardwired for empathy (it’s key to flourishing of species)—and it takes attentiveness,
openness, and letting go of our preexisting narrative about a person (i.e., we need to establish “beginner’s mind”). Empathy arises from a presence to what is alive in a person in the present moment.
We are often unable to access or express empathy during stress or reactivity, during conflict, when we are self-conscious, and in long-term relationships (if we’ve become habituated). Empathy is blocked by judgment.
It is important to practice empathic attention to increase regular access and enhance its power.
We pick up information through a number of emotional and cognitive systems. They naturally weave together as we cultivate an empathetic understanding:

* Reading emotion in face and eyes. We evolved to communicate core emotions through universal
  facial expressions, and by paying close attention, you can perceive these emotional signals.
* Sense beneath the surface. Feel into “the being behind the eyes.” Look for the softer, younger,
  more vulnerable material beneath the defensive outer presentation.
* Go into your own body to sense the embodied state of the other person.
* Cognitive investigation. Knowing something of the history of the other person, you might
  actively imagine what you would be wanting deep down if you had that history yourself (i.e., make a good guess).
  One of the most powerful ways to build skill in empathy is to express your empathetic understanding!
  While there are many ways to express empathy, it can be particularly powerful to respectfully name the feelings you sense the other person is experiencing. It will both enhance your perceptual skills in emotional resonance and serve increasing understanding and connection with others.
