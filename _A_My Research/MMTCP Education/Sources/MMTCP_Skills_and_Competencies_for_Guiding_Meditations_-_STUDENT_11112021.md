MINDFULNESS MEDITATION TEACHER CERTIFICATION PROGRAM

##### Skills and Competencies for Guiding Mindfulness Meditation Practices

This document contains competencies for guiding mindfulness meditation practices, including walking and standing meditation. There are four areas of competence:

* Competence Area 1: Delivery of Guidance
* Competence Area 2: Content of Guidance
* Competence Area 3: Relational Skills
* Competence Area 4: Walking & Standing Meditation
  For each of these topics, there are specific competencies divided into two parts:
* Part One: Fundamentals
* Part Two: Additional Point(s)
  Competence Area 1: Delivery of Guidance
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Use language that is understandable, inclusive and accessible to all
* Make sure that your tone of voice, volume and pacing are appropriate
* Show that you are familiar with the content and are not just reading a script
* Make sure that the practice lasts for the allotted length of time i.e., not too short
  and not too long Part Two: Additional Points
* Construct the meditation practice with a beginning, the guidance, and an ending
* Use eye contact and nonverbal communication appropriately
  2
  Competence Area 2: Content of Guidance
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Make sure your guidance is consistent with MMTCP teachings and practices
* Offer guidance that is inclusive, nondiscriminatory, and appropriate for the
  audience
* Present guidance that is relevant to the topic
* Invite practitioners to be aware of their experience in the present moment
* Include guidance on where practitioners should place their attention, such as the
  body, and different sense experiences, as well as on the qualities of that attention e.g., gentle, soft, curious, nonjudgmental, etc.
  Part Two: Additional Points
* Balance invitational and directive guidance
* Include appropriate suggestions, alternatives, and examples, such as “Perhaps
  there’s a feeling of……or….or….” and, “Maybe there’s a sense of…..or….or….”
* Offer guidance on what to do when common meditation challenges arise,
  such as wandering mind, sleepiness, physical discomfort, and boredom
* Include guidance on posture and/or alternative meditation positions e.g.,
  sitting, walking, standing, and lying
  Competence Area 3: Relational Skills
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Be attuned to your audience and try to convey warmth, openness, compassion, respect, and appreciation
  Part Two: Additional Point
* If anything unexpected happens during the meditation e.g., interruptions, coughing fit, background sounds, Zoom glitch, cell phone goes off, power outage, extreme weather, try to work skillfully with it
  3
  Competence Area 4: Walking & Standing Meditation
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Make sure your guidance is consistent with MMTCP teachings and practices
* Distinguish between formal walking and/or standing meditation and simply taking
  a walk and/or ordinary standing. Describe the uses and benefits
* Guide practitioners on where they should place their attention. For walking
  meditation, this could include lifting and placing the feet, as well as shifting the weight. For standing meditation, this could include the shoulders, hands, feet, and the ground underneath the feet
* Offer guidance on the qualities of that attention e.g., gentle, soft, curious, nonjudgmental
* Include guidance on practicalities. For walking meditation, this could include having a pre-set path, speed, pausing, and turning. For standing meditation, this could include posture
* Show that you are familiar with walking and/or standing meditation
* Make sure that the guidance is inclusive and nondiscriminatory by offering
  alternatives for students for whom walking and/or standing are not possible Part Two: Additional Points
* Balance invitational and directive guidance
* Encourage practitioners to set intentions beforehand
* Describe possible challenges and ways of working with them. For walking
  meditation, this could include walking too fast or too slowly. For standing meditation, this could include physical discomfort and dizziness
