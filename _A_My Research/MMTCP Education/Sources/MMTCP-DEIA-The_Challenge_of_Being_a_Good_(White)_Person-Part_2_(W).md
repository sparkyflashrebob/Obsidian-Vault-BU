# I often bike to work. And I was also volunteering at a local high school that's between my home and the work. And so, I was stopping there for a meeting at the student health service, which was housed in a modular building on the school grounds. As usual, because I had a closet in my office at work to put my bike, I didn't bring my bike lock.

# So, when I arrived there, I left my bike by the front door and asked the African American woman who was the receptionist if I could bring my bike inside. And she said, "No. No bikes are allowed in buildings." And I said, "Well, could you get Kim? Because Kim said-- I know Kim, the director. And would you mind if I asked her?" And well, it was really clear that that was not going over well.

# Even as I sat in that meeting, I-- my heart -- it feels heavy-- my heart feels weighted as I tell you this story. I felt like I had done "power over" this woman, who was simply doing her job-- and an important job. Because the bikes, really, it's a hazard and an accessibility issue.
