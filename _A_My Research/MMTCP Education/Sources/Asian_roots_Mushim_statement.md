[avoid use of the words “sitting meditation”]. Please feel free to do whatever is most comfortable for you – you can sit upright on cushions on the floor, in a chair, on a sofa, and you can lie down if you have chronic illness, spinal injuries, or you’re exhausted as so many of us are during this pandemic. Go to the bathroom and drink water and move around whenever you like. Keep your eyes open a bit, or closed – whatever makes you feel safer. And I do hope you are safe enough at this moment. [a safe enough environment is a form of privilege] Mindfulness meditation is only one of hundreds if not thousands of different methods or techniques of meditation in many different spiritual traditions. There is Christian meditation, Jewish meditation, Muslim meditation, many forms of indigenous peoples’ meditation practices. Here we’re doing secular or non-religious mindfulness meditation. Mindfulness meditation is a technique that is exactly the same technique as what is called insight meditation or vipassana meditation in the Buddhist tradition in places like Thailand, Burma, and Sri Lanka today. It is probably at least 2,500 years old and is said to be only one of several different types of meditation taught by the historical Buddha. It’s been helpful to many people of all ages – however, diversity is present everywhere. Please give it a try if you like, and see if it’s useful to you. If it doesn’t work for you, no problem! You have many choices.*
