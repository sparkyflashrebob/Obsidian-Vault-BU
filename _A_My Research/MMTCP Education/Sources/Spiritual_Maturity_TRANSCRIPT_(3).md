JACK KORNFIELD: In this session, I'd like to talk about spiritual maturity, spiritual fulfillment,  spiritual well-being.
Thomas Merton writes, "[The rain] fills the woods with immense and confused sounds. It  covers the roof of the cabin"-- where he lived in the woods behind the monastery-- "with its  insistent rhythms. And I listen, because it reminds me again and again that the whole world  runs by rhythms I've not yet learned to recognize, rhythms that are not those of the engineer."  Or the scientist. Rhythms that are not those of the business person. They are the rhythms of  the universe. "The rain surrounds this cabin with a virginal myth, a whole world of meaning,  secrecy, of silence, of rumor. Think of it all that speech pouring down selling nothing, judging  nobody, drenching the thick mulch of the leaves, soaking the trees filling the gullies and  crannies with water.
What a thing it is to sit quietly among the trees, cherished by this wonderful, unintelligible,  perfectly innocent speech, the most comforting speech in the world, the talk that rain makes by  itself over the ridges and hills and the water courses. Nobody started it, nobody is going to  stop it. It will talk as long as it wants, this rain. And as long as it talks, I am going to listen."
And so the invitation again has been to step out of the fixation of our lives, of being in a certain  place at a certain time and doing things and checking things off. And as it said, the question is  not the future of humanity, but the presence of eternity. That we somehow allow ourselves to  open to a vastness and a rhythm and a possibility.
Rachel Carson, the great naturalist, she writes, "If I had influence over the good fairy who is  supposed to preside over the birth of all children, I would ask one thing-- that her gift to each  child in the world would be a sense of wonder so indestructible it would last through their  whole life." And that's a vision of beginner's mind of freshness, to see.
Now it's a little bit confusing, because awakening, or the different goals and possibilities, are  described in so many different ways. And not only that, people become territorial, and they put  their stamp on it, and "This is trademarked, and we have it and you don't," in all kinds of  traditions.
From the deepest place of awakening, consciousness itself, which is what we are and which  creates life in the universe, manifests like a crystal. That is to say, in its purest state,  consciousness is luminous and open and fertile and has all possibilities. When you shine light  through a crystal, you get all the different wavelengths or colors of the rainbow, of red, green,  violet, blue, and so forth. Consciousness has all these flavors when we turn toward awareness  or consciousness itself.
And so in the deepest places in people's spiritual life and practice, they have realizations that  everything is love. Or they have a realization that it's all perfect, that there is a realization of the  perfection of the world, or a realization of deep peace. That everything-- "all shall be well and  all manner of things shall be well," from St. Julian of Norwich.
Or a realization of emptiness. And emptiness is not a lack of things. It's a void, it's the fertile  darkness out of which all things are born. And it's extraordinary to have these realizations.
