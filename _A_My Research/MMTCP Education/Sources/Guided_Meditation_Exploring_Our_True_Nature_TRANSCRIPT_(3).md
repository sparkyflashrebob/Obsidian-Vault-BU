TARA BRACH: Just find a way to sit that's upright, relaxed. Taking a moment as you come into  stillness to gently sweep the awareness through the body, letting go of any areas of obvious  tightness or tension. Taking a few full breaths, letting go with the out breath, and letting the  intention be to let life be just as it is and allowing presence.
When thoughts arise, simply the noticing and relaxing back again, that backward step, and just  letting be. It can be helpful, this invitation from the Tibetan teachings, utterly awake, senses  wide open. Utterly open, non-fixating awareness.
Sensing in the foreground this changing stream of sound. Sensation, feeling. And then that  gentle inquiry. What is aware? Or maybe, what is listening?
Or who is aware? Who is turning back gently to sense what's true? Then letting go and just  being that, being what is seen.
If you inquire and thoughts arise, just to let that come and go. Relax back again, over and over,  just becoming that presence, letting that changing stream happen as it is. Sounds, sensations.
And if there's some fixation, thoughts, you can turn the attention again. Who's thinking? Who's  listening? There is awareness here. Turning their attention back towards awareness and then  letting go again into what is seen.
You are the unchanging awareness in which all activity takes place. It's not difficult to realize  this because it's your true nature. Simply inquire, who am I? And watch carefully. Without  effort, without stirring a thought, look within, approach with reverence, and stay as heart.
Letting be the foreground of experience, the changing river of sound, sensation, aliveness. And  sensing in the background that alert, inner stillness. The formless, illuminated presence that's  the very source of our being.
