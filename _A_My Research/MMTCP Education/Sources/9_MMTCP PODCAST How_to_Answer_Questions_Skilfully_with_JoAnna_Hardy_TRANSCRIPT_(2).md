It actually is quite a cutting-edge practice for us as teachers, because as students question into
or ask about what we're teaching, it really helps us fine-tune the way that we communicate. It
helps us fine-tune and ask ourselves what we actually do or do not know enough about to be

I think often, a mistake the teachers make, or that students make in regards to teachers, is
putting them on a pedestal or projecting an all-knowing experience. And one of the main things
that questions do is remind us of our wholeness in the practice together. It reminds us of the
integral and important part, that there is a teacher and there is a student, or there are teachers
and there are students. And at different times, we're in different positions, just because we're in
front of the room does not mean that we aren't a student or that we are learning.
So one of the things I've noticed throughout my years of teaching-- and have talked to many
teachers about this-- is, oftentimes, we can come in all excited and all intentional with this
prepared talk or these prepared meditation instructions. And we have our notes, and we have
our piece of paper, and we're ready to go, and we've been preparing for a week. And then we
get into a room, and it's like, uh-oh, there's people here. We have this running monologue going
that we want to perfect and have it right.
And a really important aspect of what we're even doing in the teacher seat is to teach. It is to
respond to the needs of our community, to respond to the needs of the people that are in the
room. And so, oftentimes, when we get set in one monologue or a way of teaching, we forget
who we're actually teaching to and learning with.
So the questions really help remind us. Even if we do get in this cadence where we start talking
and talking and talking and talking, trust that your students will remind you about what they
didn't understand, how we weren't actually skillful or good at describing or relaying what we're
trying to describe or relay. So I hold that as a really powerful and important teaching moment,
for me as a teacher, to really see who's in the room.
