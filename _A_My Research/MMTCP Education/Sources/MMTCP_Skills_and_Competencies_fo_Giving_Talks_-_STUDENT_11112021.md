MINDFULNESS MEDITATION TEACHER CERTIFICATION PROGRAM

##### Skills and Competencies for Giving Talks

This document contains important skills and competencies for giving talks.
There are three areas of competence:

* Competence Area 1: Delivery of the Talk
* Competence Area 2: Content of the Talk
* Competence Area 3: Relational Skills
  For each of these topics, there are specific competencies divided into two parts:
* Part One: Fundamentals
* Part Two: Additional Point(s)
  Competence Area 1: Delivery of the Talk
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Use language that is understandable, inclusive and accessible to all
* Make sure the delivery is engaging and shows that you are familiar with the
  content i.e., not just reading the talk
* Make sure that your voice is loud and clear enough for all to hear, and that the
  pacing is appropriate
* Make sure that the talk lasts for the allotted length of time i.e., not too short and
  not too long Part Two: Additional Points
* Construct your talk with an introduction, an exposition, and an ending
* Explain any acronyms and Pali/Sanskrit/Buddhist terms you use
* Use eye contact and nonverbal communication appropriately
  2
  Competence Area 2: Content of the Talk
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Make sure your talk is consistent with MMTCP teachings and practices
* Offer a talk that is inclusive, nondiscriminatory, and appropriate for the
  audience
* Make sure the content of the talk is relevant to the topic
* Present and explain no more than 3-4 core ideas
* Make sure that any quotes, poems, stories, and any authorities you reference,
  reflect diversity e.g., gender, race
* Help the audience to relate to their own experiences including specific
  thoughts, emotions, or sensations. Connect individual experiences and universal human experiences
  Part Two: Additional Points
* Include questions, brief experiential exercises, or other approaches to elicit the audience’s direct experience
* Include suggestions and practices for working with the topic
  Competence Area 3: Relational Skills Part One: Fundamentals
* Be attuned to the audience and convey warmth, openness, compassion, and appreciation
* Treat the audience as co-inquirers and do not talk down to them Part Two: Additional Points
* If anything unexpected happens during the talk e.g., interruptions, coughing fit, background sounds, Zoom glitch, cell phone goes off, power outage, extreme weather, try to work skillfully with it
