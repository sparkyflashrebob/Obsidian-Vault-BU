### Skills and Competencies

MINDFULNESS MEDITATION TEACHER CERTIFICATION PROGRAM

##### Skills and Competencies for Responding to Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

This document contains important skills and competencies for responding to frequently asked questions (FAQs).
There are three areas of competence:

* Competence Area 1: Listening & Understanding
* Competence Area 2: Responding
* Competence Area 3: Relational Skills
  For each of these topics, there are specific competencies divided into two parts:
* Part One: Fundamentals
* Part Two: Additional Point(s)
  Competence Area 1: Listening & Understanding
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Actively listen to questioners
* Only interrupt when it is appropriate e.g., if the questioner is over-
  intellectualizing nor discursive, being disruptive, or offensive to other members of the class
* Make sure you understand the question or ask a question to clarify your understanding
  Part Two: Additional Points
* You could repeat or summarize what you heard the questioner say
* Try to discern when it is not appropriate to work with a question in a class setting
  and suggest alternatives, such as talking one on one afterwards
  2
  Competence Area 2: Responding
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Make sure your response is consistent with MMTCP teachings and practices
* Use “wise speech” and language that is understandable, inclusive, and accessible
  to all
* Normalize questions by relating them to the class by generalizing them or
  focusing them, if appropriate
* Respond in a way that makes questions relevant to the class by generalizing them
  or focusing them, if appropriate
* Respond appropriately, based on your own knowledge and experience and by
  providing guidance and practice suggestions, such as RAIN
* Invite inquiry into the question through open-ended questions focusing on the
  questioner’s direct experience and their own understanding, and if appropriate, exploring what might have motivated them to ask the original question
  Part Two: Additional Points
* In your response, show that you are familiar with the subject of the question, or have a desire to co-inquire into it with the questioner
* Do not try to “fix” questioners or solve their problems for them
* Invite questioners to reflect on their assumptions, expectations, and beliefs about
  themselves and the world, when appropriate
* Try concluding your responses by checking back with the questioner, asking “Does
  this respond to your question?” or something similar
* Allow and invite follow-up questions, if there is time
  3
  Competence Area 3: Relational Skills
  Part One: Fundamentals
* Make sure you are attuned to questioners and their present moment experience
* Convey acceptance, patience, warmth, and compassion to questioners
* Be curious about questioners and their experience and also respectful of their
  vulnerabilities and boundaries
* Treat questioners as co-inquirers and do not talk down to them
  Part Two: Additional Points
* Try to maintain an awareness of the whole class, as you listen and respond to individual questioners
