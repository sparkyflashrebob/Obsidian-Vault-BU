Flava D's process for creating a liquid Drum & Bass (DnB) beat from scratch involves several key steps, focusing on layering sounds and utilizing effects to build a cohesive and energetic track [1].

Here's a breakdown of her workflow:

* **Starting Point:** Flava D typically begins with a **texture or a piano sound** to establish an initial vibe. She uses **Addictive Keys for pianos**, often turning down the velocity if the sound is too harsh [1].
* **Hats Integration:** She then adds **hats**, searching for good sounds and loading them into a drum rack. She shortens hat loops into **one-shots**, applies **subtle reverb**, and consistently **removes the low-end** from drum elements [1].
* **Shaker Loops:** She incorporates **shaker loops** for background texture, adding **chorus** and adjusting their **transients in Ableton** to make them more "stripped back" [1].
* **Plucky Bass/Riff:** For a plucky sound, she uses **Serum**, adding a filter to shape the sound. She further processes it with **convolution reverb** and a bit of **delay** to create a "bass riff" [1].
* **Snare/Clap and Breakbeat:** A **short and snappy snare clap** is added to get the main transient, which she then layers with a **breakbeat**, such as a "think break." She applies **transient adjustments** to the break, removes its **low-end** to prevent clashing, and adds **convolution** [1].
* **Additional Shakers and Compression:** More shakers are added, often panned widely to create a **"3D" sound**. She uses a **glue compressor** on the drum elements to make them sound more cohesive [1].
* **Sidechaining for Clarity:** A crucial step is **sidechaining** the pianos and plucky melodies to the kick drum. She uses a tool called "Kickstart" for this, syncing it to the kick so that the melodies "dip" in volume, preventing frequency clashes with the kick and bass [1].
* **Reese Bassline:** Finally, she adds a **Reese bassline**, ensuring the sidechaining is also applied to it to avoid clashing with lower frequencies [1].
* **Workflow Philosophy:** Flava D emphasizes that this structured approach allows her to build the "skeleton" of an idea. She filters down pianos for clarity and sometimes uses pre-made basslines or pads from previous projects to quickly capture melodies she has in her head, valuing workflow efficiency to avoid losing creative ideas [1].
