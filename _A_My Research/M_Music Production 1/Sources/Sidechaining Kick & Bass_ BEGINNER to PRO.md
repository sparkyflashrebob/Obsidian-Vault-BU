hits The traditional method is to load a compressor on your bass Then route your kick signal into the compressor's side chain input hence the name side chain compression often shortened to just side chaining So every time the kick hits the compressor's gain reduction turns the bass down And now with side chaining applied to this bass we can describe the result as tighter more controlled and more it comes to sidechaining you have two main options Using a compressor or a dedicated volume ducking plug-in A huge pro for using a compressor is that it's free You already have a side chain capable compressor in your door Something worth understanding is that a compressor reacts to the dynamics of the input So louder inputs will trigger more compression and vice versa This is why I use a compressor when I want ducking that reacts more when the trigger is louder and less when the trigger is quieter However compressors have some downsides First they have somewhat limited control compared to modern tools The compressor's attack is usually kept at minimum when ducking for the fastest response That leaves only the release and sometimes hold for shaping the curve And because the compression follows the length and shape of your kick it's tricky to create ducking that's shorter than your input signal Though you can overcome this limitation somewhat by highpass filtering the signal that the compressor is listening to Or you can use a silent ghost trigger Here's how to do it Set up a short sound like a click mute it in the mix and route the signal into your compressor's side chain input to trigger ducking Because the click trigger is short the compressor's release phase will engage much sooner giving you more control over the release And if you want your bass to pump over a kickless breakdown you can silence your actual kick but keep the trigger running and you can program side chain patterns that do not follow the kick Anyway back to compressor downsides A compressor is a multi-purpose tool with lots of parameters to dial in Its workflow isn't designed for side chaining only and compressors can create clicks and pops especially when side chaining bass Stay tuned and I'll show you how to avoid these clicks later in the video Now let's talk about dedicated side chaining tools A volume shaping plug-in uses an LFO curve to modulate the volume of your signal Just add it to your bass track and load a curve The default sync mode repeatedly triggers the LFO in sync with your door It's not reacting to the kick but instead ducks at a set rate This is kick signal into the plug-in's side chain input And audio mode follows the rhythm of your kick no matter the kick pattern And then you've got MIDI mode Root MIDI into the plug-in and the ducking will compressor a volume shaping tool works independently of your kick's dynamics It will turn down the bass exactly the same every time no matter the volume level of the trigger signal So it's generally
