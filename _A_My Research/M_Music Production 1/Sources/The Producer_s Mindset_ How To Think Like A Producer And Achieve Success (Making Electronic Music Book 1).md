# Part 1: The Foundation

#### Warning: This book demands that you think for yourself. It assumes you want to “make it” as an artist, a producer, or a DJ. If you want to make music for a living, or at the very least produce electronic music (and do it well), the suggestions is this ebook will benefit you.

#### It’s important to know that you’ll get the most out of this book by reading through from cover to cover and trying to process each section. Then, think through each part, each idea, and all the actionable tips. Think it through and come to your own conclusions.

#### 1 - Abandoning Negative Beliefs

#### The first and most crucial step to becoming a successful producer is getting rid of any negative beliefs you might have about yourself. It’s difficult to build good habits and create a healthy environment when you’re limiting your own success with faulty beliefs.

#### Below you’ll find some of the most common inaccurate beliefs. If you believe in any of these, write down the corresponding positive belief and set it as a phone alert daily, or write it on a sticky note and place it near your music workstation. Whatever you do, you absolutely must rid yourself of these falsehoods.

#### 1. I will not make any money from producing.

#### Positive Belief: Many producers make money from their art.

#### While it may be true that you can’t make as much money from album sales these days, money is certainly not a problem when you’re getting regular releases. Think of music sales as exposure. If you get any royalties from your releases, then it’s a bonus. But really it’s exposure to build a fanbase so you can play gigs and make money that way. Gigs will pay better and better the larger your fanbase is.

#### Also, it’s worth remembering that you won’t make any money from unfinished songs. Focus on the music now, worry about the money later. If money is your top priority anyway, you’re doing it for the wrong reasons.

#### 2. No one wants to hear my music

#### Positive Belief: There’s always an audience for even the narrowest of tastes.

#### This is an irrational fear. People love finding new artists and new songs. If you think no one is interested, it’s likely due to one of three reasons:

#### 1) You don’t think your music is good enough 2) You think there won’t be an audience for your style of music 3) You think fans don’t care about unknown artists

#### Here is why those three beliefs are not true. Fans and music enthusiasts LOVE discovering new artists. There’s even a word for young people that thrive on discovering artists before everyone else - hipsters. If your music sucks, not as many will listen to it, but there will always be someone.

#### Regarding the second point, there’s always an audience, no matter how niché the genre. There are billions of people on the planet, and millions of them listen to electronic music. They are all over the world. It may not ever be as mainstream as a Top 40 single, but there is certainly an audience. No matter how narrow your taste in music, there are others out there who like it.

#### Finally, in response to the third point, if you think no one cares about unknown artists, you’re wrong. Many electronic music enthusiasts, and even more DJs love the thrill of discovering new, unknown artists. There’s something awesome about playing tracks in a set that no one has ever heard before. There will be an audience for your songs, and if you get your material in front of the right eyes and ears, people will listen to you and start following you.

#### 3. I’m not good enough

#### Positive Belief: I can always improve, or hire a mastering house to mix and master my music.

#### This is the only belief where there may be some truth to it. But even if you are not great at producing, that is not an excuse to stop doing what you love. If anything, it’s a great reason to keep pushing forward.

#### 4. I don’t know what to create

#### Positive Belief: The music and inspiration will come.

#### Producing electronic music is a process. If you don’t feel inspired immediately, either take a break for a few hours or just start making something anyway and let the inspiration eventually flow through you.

#### Granted, I don’t often utilize affirmations, but many people make use of them. If you can get by without them, go for it. The point is that you need to abandon detrimental beliefs that will hold you back from progressing and achieving success. Once you’ve conquered these hindering beliefs, you can then start building habits for success.

#### 2 - Find Your Mood

#### If you’ve done away with limiting beliefs and you’ve got an open mind, the next step is to get yourself in the mood to make music. You can’t do calculus without simple, basic addition. Likewise, you can’t acquire the ideal mindset if you’re not in the mood to produce. So, that’s where we’ll start. The basic building block of getting in the mood.

#### If building an ideal mindset is a pyramid, then being ‘in the mood’ is the bottom layer. If you’re not in the mood to make music, you aren’t going to get very far. The goal then is to always be in the mood to produce. Unfortunately, that’s impossible. What is possible though is changing certain details so that you’re in the mood more often.

#### Everyone has different routines and practices, methods and approaches. Chances are you have yours too. If you’re brand new to making music, give it a couple months and you’ll find you’ve developed routines, perhaps without realizing it. Humans are creatures of comfort and habit, and when we find something that works, we tend to stick with it. The trick to more productive sessions is creating habits that are more efficient than what you’re currently doing.

#### In my experience, everyone has an ideal mood for making music. If you aren’t sure what yours is, then this chapter will help you tremendously.

#### To better explain what I mean, think of all your favorite artists. I’ve never met anyone who made a chart-topping track while they were pre-occupied with all the chores they had to do over the weekend. Or when they were busy thinking about the date they had planned the following week. Most producers are not caught up thinking about other things; they tend to be mentally prepared to make music when they enter the studio.

#### I have my own methods. I don’t sit down to write a track when I’m hungry or tired. I can’t focus when I’m hungry and I doze off when I’m sleepy regardless of how focused I am. Though you may not realize it, you likely have ideal mindsets where you work better too.

#### Try to recall the mental state you were in when you made what you consider to be your best songs. Ask yourself the following questions:

#### 

* #### When have I produced my best songs?
* #### Do I love to make music when I’m happy? Sad?
* #### Do I vent my anger and frustration out into my music?
* Do I feel more inspired when drunk, stoned, or on some other substance?

#### There is no correct answer. Everyone will have different ideals. Write down the answers to all the above questions and you’ll begin to see how and when you operate best. It could be that you can’t achieve proper mixdowns unless you’re sober. Or, perhaps you make very dark techno when you’re angry, but super melodic trance when you’re happy. Maybe you prefer sitting down in your studio only when you’ve got a giant mug of coffee and a pack of cigarettes. Whatever your ideal conditions, you’ll begin to discover what they are as you write down your answers.

#### 3 - A Productive Environment

#### So you’re in the mood, you’re hyped to make music, and you believe you can actually have a fighting chance at success. What’s next? A productive, positive environment. I mean this in the literal sense - a physical environment. Without a ‘safe haven’, a space dedicated explicitly for your music, you won’t be giving yourself a fighting chance.

#### A productive space is the single biggest contributor to an ideal mindset, outside of the mindset itself. There are three elements to a productive environment:

#### 

* #### Location
* #### Consistency
* Tools

#### Location: If you’re going to take producing seriously, you need to have some sort of dedicated space or area. This offers some key advantages, the main one being fewer distractions.

#### Depending on your life circumstances, it may be relatively easy or quite difficult to set yourself up with a personal music studio, however no matter how tough it can still be done. I’m an advocate that nothing can stop you if you’re determined enough.

#### Back when I was a college student, my studio was nothing more than a laptop and headphones. Hardly the ideal environment. But it never kept me from learning and growing. A laptop and headphones are better than nothing at all. I spent many afternoons in coffee shops and many nights locked in my bedroom making bad music.

#### Now, my bedroom serves as my studio. Though I don’t have a spare room to dedicate, I still have monitors, a computer, headphones, and many other resources in my bedroom. And it hasn’t stopped me from producing.

#### If you’re settled down with a home and a family, the basement or spare bedroom can serve as your studio space. If you’ve got some extra financial resources, build your own studio. Whatever your situation, it’s important to have a space, no matter how small. You’d be surprised how beneficial having a refuge can be for your producing.

#### Last but not least, don’t forget there’s always room to grow. Have a laptop and terrible headphones? Save up for decent headphones. Have a bedroom? Invest in monitors. Have monitors and a computer? Start treating your room for acoustics.

#### Initially, don’t concern yourself with which kinds of equipment to invest in (that will be covered in a later book), but rather focus on the importance of having some sort of studio space.

#### Consistency: The best and most successful people in any field all share one thing in common - dedication. According to Malcolm Gladwell’s research in his book Outliers, it takes at least 10,000 hours to master a skill. The Beatles had played over a thousand concerts, seven nights a week, eight hours a day prior to blowing up internationally. Bill Gates had practiced coding and programming for over 10,000 hours by the time he was in college.

#### Time takes time. It’s probably a phrase you’ve heard before. You won’t be as good as the best producers unless you put in the same amount of time that they have. And the only way to achieve that mammoth amount of practice is consistency. You have to focus on your craft day after day, month after month, year after year.

#### Unfortunately, many aspiring artists quit after not seeing the results they had hoped for. The results will come, but you need to stick around long enough to see them. Patience is key.

#### How do you achieve consistency? A solid starting point is by setting aside time each day for your music.

#### Time is the only thing every person runs out of. There will always be 24 hours to a day, 60 minutes to an hour, and 7 days to a week. The beautiful fact is that no one has more time than anyone else. Warren Buffett may never run out of money, but he’s on the same ticking clock as everyone else. Every successful producer knows this and devotes a large portion of their time to the one thing they truly love - their music.

#### If you’ve got a busy life and you’re not sure where to start, the first thing to do is determine how much flexibility you have in your schedule. If you have some degree of flexibility to your life, think about what your best time of day is. For example, I function best at night. Specifically the hours from 11pm through 4am, depending on my schedule. Some folks love getting out of bed at 5am to start their day early. Everyone has their own preference, and works best under different conditions. Figure out your ideal timeframe and try to produce during those hours.

#### If you don’t have room for schedule changes, then you should look at what you can cancel or reduce in your life. How will you hit that 10,000 hour point? If you keep a calendar, in either the physical or mental form, pencil in some free time where you will only focus on creating music. Yes, this might mean skipping the sports game, cutting back on dating, or staying up later after work. If you’re desperate enough to realize your dreams, you’ll find an opportunity to free up a few hours.

#### Many musicians I meet have a wife, kids, extended family, friends, day jobs, other hobbies, and more. Despite this, they make time to produce and release music. A few DJs like Claude VonStroke, Marcel Dettmann, Luciano, and others have wives, kids, record labels, and they tour extensively. Berlin DJ Rødhåd started his own label, began touring, and released new tracks all while having a day job. It’s not about if you are too busy, but how you will carve out the time for your music.

#### If you are dedicated to your art, any amount of time will suffice, at least when starting out. It doesn’t have to be 6 hours every day - even one or two hours an evening is a great beginning. Just get to it though, don’t procrastinate.

#### Tools: The last part to a productive environment is what tools you use. This point is rather simple, so I’ll keep it brief. If you don’t have any production tools, you obviously need some. An affordable beginner’s kit would include a computer with your preferred DAW (Ableton, Logic, FLStudio, Cubase, etc.), some headphones or cheap monitors, and only a couple plugins. I emphasize only needing a few plugins because it’s way too easy to drown in the indecision of finding new VSTs and plugins to use. You end up not mastering the ones you currently have.

#### If you already have a setup that gets the job done, then all you need to worry about is being able to practice. You don’t need the newest analog synthesizer, or the latest plugin bundle. Stick with what you have until you can make amazing music. If you get distracted by all the shiny new toys you could create music with, you’ll end up veering off the path to success. Having the latest Moog synth doesn’t matter if your music still sounds piss-poor. On the other hand, if you’re making incredible songs, you can do it with even the worst synths and effects.

#### \*\*\*

#### If you’ve got a productive environment and all your puzzle pieces are aligned, it’s time to focus on building daily habits and routines.

#### Sit down whenever your new, modified schedule allows, and practice. Make a track. Try a new bass line. Experiment with compression. Do something everyday. Even at 30 minutes each day, you’ll be much happier and way more productive after a few weeks.

#### Forming habits are the best way to steadily progress towards your goals. TO many they may seem daunting, so here’s a simple way to look at building new habits:

#### 1. Imagine whatever it is you want to add to your life. Maybe it’s mastering the art of EQing, or perhaps it’s creating a song every single day.

#### 2. With a specific goal in mind, set aside at least 15 minutes every single day to practice working toward your goal.

#### 3. Don’t worry about whether you will achieve the goal. Instead, focus on what you need to do today. Sit down and complete your tasks for today, and then don’t think about it until tomorrow.

#### Essentially, take it one day at a time. Before long you’ll look up six months down the road and see how far you’ve traveled.

# Part 2: Thinking Like A Producer

#### 4 - Managing Time

#### If you’ve followed the advice in this book so far, then it’s time to learn the concepts, principles, and ideas behind a healthy mindset. An outlook designed for success is your best weapon in the music universe. The rest of this book discusses at length how to maintain your very own ideal mindset as a producer.

#### As mentioned in the last chapter, time is an important gear in your music-making machine. It’s always slipping by, and there’s nothing anyone can do about it. So, the objective becomes how you can best optimize your life so as to have studio time on a regular basis.

#### For those just starting out, the best thing to do is to forget about tutorials, videos, and books (yes, even this book) and instead practice making music every day. Learning about advanced production techniques isn’t going to help if you can’t find time to sit down and actually practice those techniques.

#### The easiest way to begin is literally to start making music. Sit down whenever you’ve got free time and start building a loop or something. Create sounds for just fifteen minutes today. If you can set aside more time, awesome. If not, at least you’ve got fifteen minutes down. Now, do it again tomorrow and every day after that. Once you get that habit formed, add more time. Force yourself to sit down for an hour at a time.

#### Having trouble finding time? Here are a few things you can probably do less often or not at all:

#### 

* #### Social networking (Facebook, Snapchat, Instagram, Twitter, etc.)
* #### Checking email constantly
* #### Watch TV or movies
* Going out to bars or hanging with friends, girlfriends, boyfriends, lovers, etc.

#### I’m not saying the above things need to be eliminated from your life, but there is always something you can cut back on to make time for your music.

#### 5 - Reducing Expectations

#### Unrealistic expectations will get you into trouble. Believe me, I speak from experience. Expectations can persuade you that you are never achieving enough. They will make you feel inferior to others and they will ruin your motivation.

#### The key is to acknowledge your expectations and let them be. If you’re struggling to write a song, don’t get discouraged because it doesn’t sound how you think it should. When you expect your tracks to sound as good as a finished, mastered song by one of your favorite artists, don’t get upset when you’re let down.

#### The problem is usually that we all have expectations. All humans do. I expect the sun to rise tomorrow morning and I expect to wake up tomorrow. Those are natural expectations. But sometimes we have unrealistic expectations, like when I expected my music to sound professional even though I had only been producing for a few months. The trick is not to rid yourself completely of expectations because that is impossible, but rather lower them and watch out for poor reactions. It’s okay to fall short of your expectations because you’re not perfect. Just make sure not to get down on yourself.

#### Some years ago I remember feeling very satisfied with a song I had just completed and it resulted in an epiphany. I realized my satisfaction was due to the fact that I had kept an open mind the entire time. I was not trying to emulate a certain style, mimic a specific artist, or achieve a new effect. I simply wanted to make whatever was flowing through my head at the time. And sure enough, devoid of ridiculously high expectations, I was pleased with my work.

#### An open mind allows for several positive benefits:

#### 

* #### There is no pressure to achieve a certain outcome or result
* #### Reduced expectations leads to lower stress levels, and that leads to better channeling of creative energy
* There is a better chance of “going with the flow”

#### The last bullet point is worth discussing further. In my past, I would find myself trying to make a certain sound or emulate a specific idea. Creativity goes its own way though, and this would occasionally cause a conflict because I was trying to force a certain outcome, yet my creativity and inspiration was naturally heading in a different direction.

#### By allowing yourself to change directions when it feels right, the outcome is often better than expected. It just so happens that changing directions is only possible with an open mind.

#### Thus, this section is about managing expectations. Though this applies to all areas of life, I’m only referring to expectations as they pertain to music and producing. There will always be expectations, and they will always be higher for yourself. We tend to be our own worst critics. Knowing this much is a solid starting point, but you won’t make it far out of the gate unless you can learn to acknowledge your standards for what they are and set them aside for the moment.

#### If you expect to be the next Porter Robinson or Deadmau5, you may find yourself disappointed. Understand your expectation, but set it aside, otherwise you will be let down. The best producers and DJs are honing their craft because it’s what they live for, not because they want to be another Laidback Luke, Skrillex, David Guetta, or DJ Tiesto.

#### 6 - The ‘Blank Slate’

#### For some producers, creating with a blank slate is the ideal. It’s a mindset where there is no persuasion, no assumptions, no expectations, and no pre-determined paths. You’ve got a canvas, and anything goes. WRite what you’re moved to write, design what flows from your mind.

#### Obtaining that ‘blank slate’ is certainly attractive. But just how do you obtain it?

#### If you’re serious about expanding your creative limits and are interested in obtaining that ‘blank slate’ mentality, there are several options. Meditations and breathing exercises are a great natural way to achieve your desired state. Centering yourself in the morning or at night can be tremendously beneficial, not only for your music but also your life in general. Another option is the recreational use of substances. Many artists feel it helps their creative output. I have experimented with both methods, and concluded that meditation seems to work better in the long run, especially for those who might have addictive personalities.

#### For those interested in trying substances, you might find that stimulants (nicotine, amphetamines, even caffeine…) will help you focus. It can be an easy way to attain that feeling of being ‘in the zone’. For others, a mellow high from marijuana leads to creative bursts and deep thoughts. Maybe hallucinogens will open your mind and allow you to explore uncharted territory. Whatever you decide, just know that I don’t condone any of it. If you do choose to indulge in mind-altering substances, please remember that moderation is best.

#### Keep in mind that while you may feel more creative on a substance, the level of skill required in mixing down your tracks often demands a sober state of mind. Knowing what needs fixing, where your problem spots are, and how levels need to be adjusted is not easily done with lowered inhibitions.

#### The other route, as mentioned above, involves meditation and breathing exercises. Centering yourself in the morning or just before a studio session can really clear your mind and allow you to become unblocked. With no mental obstacles, tapping into a pure creative zone is easier and leads to more fruitful results. If you’re interested in trying out meditation, order a few books on the art. While you wait for them to show up in your mailbox, you can focus on taking slow, deep breaths every morning. It’s quick and easy, and will yield noticeable effects.

#### 7 - Track Your Producing

#### In order to stay productive, you can make use of time-tracking and productivity apps. Though they’re typically used for business-related activities, they can be very useful with making music.

#### I find that sitting down whenever I have the opportunity is what works best for me. A three hour session is great, but so is a 35 minute one before leaving for work too. Anytime inspiration hits, I try to map it out if possible.

#### Every producer has a different operating method though, and what works for me may not be good for you. Here are some things you can do to ensure that you’re devoting enough time to producing:

#### 

* #### Pomodoro technique - Using the Pomodoro technique, you can sit down to write music for a set number of minutes before taking a five minute break. The typical length is 25 minutes, though if you’re anything like me, you’ll find yourself setting the work period for an hour or longer before taking a break. Also, if you’re in ‘the zone’, you may end up skipping the breaks altogether and working right through. I’ve had some pretty inspired 6 hour sessions before!
* #### Record your sessions - No, I don’t mean a recorded studio session. Instead, write down your progress every day or every time you produce. Jot down the name of the track, the genre, the time and date, how far you progressed (loop, arrangement, mixdown, etc.), and if there was a specific technique or trick you were trying. Having a log of your production sessions will help you see what to work on and show you how much time you’re actually spending.

#### You don’t just have to track your time though. Track your progress and better visualize what it is you want to accomplish through basic tools like mindmaps and to-do lists. Here are a few tools that will help you write down goals and track your progress:

#### 

* #### Easy To-Do Lists - WorkFlowy
* #### Keep Notes and Sources of Inspiration - Evernote
* #### Free MindMaps - Mind42
* Manage Projects - Basecamp

#### Each of the above tools will help you in various ways. Evernote and Basecamp are great for saving various things you find on the internet. Whether it’s blog posts on producing, websites, record labels, free acapella sites, or anything else, you can save it to Evernote and instantly access it later. Basecamp offers a really easy way to start projects, keep yourself reminded of goals, and remember what is due when (if you’ve got remixes and releases to finish).

#### Workflowy offers free to-do lists in a very smart and intuitive user interface. Mind42 allows you to create free workflows. Many artists are visual in nature, and tools like Mind42 and Workflowy offer a visual aspect to staying productive.

#### Not sure how you would use these tools? Here are some simple examples:

#### 

* #### To save an informative article on parallel compression or using effects
* #### To save an image showing the signal path of a particular synthesizer
* #### Saving songs that inspire you
* #### Writing out your mixing process so you never forget a crucial step
* Keeping a list of all your favorite plugins and sample libraries

#### 8 - Build That Habit

#### If you ask anybody who is amazing at what they do how long they’ve been doing that thing, chances are they’ll tell you they’ve been at it for many years. Body builders don’t get huge because they worked out once. Famous authors didn’t just write one book. Think about how many titles Stephen King has published. Even better, how many tracks have your favorite producers released? Or how many albums? How many gigs do they play?

#### The theme here is consistency and rigorous practice. It’s all a habit. You’re either in the habit of making music or you aren’t. Which will you choose?

#### While you may wish you were further along or better at what you do, it’s crucial to realize that you’ll only get better by making habits. You will never be amazing if you don’t routinely practice.

#### Here’s a crash course on building the music-making habit: Make a song every day. It doesn’t have to be great. In fact, it’s better if it’s a terrible song. Sit down, write a track, and be done with it. Do it all over again tomorrow. You’ll never get better unless you can keep chugging along.

#### Many of you might be doubting this advice. Who wants to make poor quality music? No one. But everyone sucks until they don’t anymore. And it just so happens that you will suck until you practice enough to get good.

#### Remember, the goal is not to make bad music and sell it, but rather to merely make music consistently. If it’s bad, that’s totally fine. It’ll get better. No one is asking you to share it with others. Just get used to the process of outputting music regularly. If you do it long enough, eventually you’ll be cranking out amazing tracks constantly.

#### If you decide to produce daily, the logical next question then becomes how do you create a song so quickly? That is where having a workflow comes into play. Don’t have a workflow? It’s alright. You’ll develop one as you continue to make music every day. If you’re focused strictly on making a song every day, you’ll build a workflow without even realizing it.

#### Of course, habits are useful not just for producing regularly, but many other things as well. If you’re the kind of person that makes incredible 4 bar loops but can’t turn it into an actual song, then right there that’s another habit waiting to be formed. Loops are great, but they don’t equal finished songs. And not having finished songs means no published music which results in no shows and no pay. If you struggle with finishing songs, get into the habit of creating your arrangement for every loop you make. Even if you don’t like the song, make it from start to finish anyway.

#### But can it be that easy?

#### Well, yes and no. Forming a new habit is literally as simple as doing an action over and over again until it sticks and you no longer have to think about it. The difficult part arises when you have the opportunity to take action and laziness sets in. Or perhaps it’s not even being lazy, but just that you have higher priorities. Both are terminal illnesses for new habits.

#### If you’re not feeling motivated to act on your new habit (e.g. sitting down to make that new track for the day), do it anyway. You absolutely, positively must act on it. Otherwise, the habit will die and you will find yourself making excuses when you don’t want to produce. And that is the death of your music career before it even starts.

#### So, what are you waiting for? Get started today and begin making music. Begin using that effect you never really understood. Start finishing your songs. Keep at it.

#### 9 - Set Effective Goals

#### If you’re unsure of what habits to begin building, you should think about setting some goals.

#### Similar to tracking your time and attempting to hit a specific number of hours each week, having other goals is beneficial too. The point behind setting goals is so that you’re always pushing yourself to new heights. Without a challenge, you’re not going to grow to your potential, nor will you ever see what your potential is.

#### Once you’re in the habit of making music daily (or at least regularly), you can begin pursuing more specific things like mastering the use of equalization or compression.

#### Of course, your goals will be different based on how far along you are, but it is always good to have them. A manageable way to break down your goals is to start at the top of the mountain. What do you want to achieve? What is the end goal? When you start at the top and work your way down, you begin to see the specific objectives that will eventually land you at your destination.

#### Let’s say your ultimate goal is DJing across the world. The art of DJing was always your primary love. But how do you get to be a DJ? The tried and true way is by producing and getting tracks signed to labels. With recognizable tunes, people will know your name and want to book you. You now know the end goal, and the avenue you’ll use to get there, as well as what it takes to travel that avenue. Now ask yourself: “How do I make recognizable tunes”? The answer to that is simple: You’ve got to have your own distinct sound. You’ve also got to be an amazing artist.

#### That can still be broken down further. What defines an amazing artist and a distinct sound, and how do you grow into both?

#### The secret to setting effective goals is to ask yourself, “How will I do that?” at every level. Once you can no longer break the goal down any further, you will have a reasonable objective to attack.

#### 10 - Give It 110%

#### As previously stated, unless you’re a rare gem, you’re going to suck at the beginning. Probably you’ll suck for a while after that too. This is good though, as it provides a natural barrier to entry with producing electronic music.

#### The key is not getting discouraged, and that’s hard not to do. But it’s mandatory, and it’s part of everyone’s journey. You take your first crack at producing and you realize it’s terrible. You try some more and you realize you still are wholly incompetent, but at least you’re beginning to have some fun with it. Finally, you start seeing results.

#### The details are different for everyone, but the process is identical. How many times are you willing to fail before you succeed? The only proper response is an infinite number of times. Are you willing to make five thousand terrible songs before you make a good one? Are you willing to release a hundred EPs before you begin gaining significant traction? Are you willing to trainwreck a thousand gigs before you get the hang of the art of DJing? Hopefully it doesn’t take that long, but you get the idea.

#### The determining factor in success is understanding that you will not receive instant gratification. Sure, making new music is exciting, but the real results don’t start for a while.

#### Is there an idea you just can’t grasp? Are you willing to try 100 times before you own the concept?

#### Here’s how this suggestion translates into a real world application:

#### 

* #### If you’re only familiar with a few synths or VSTs, limit yourself to using only a new synth and use it 100 times before you use anything else.
* #### If you suck at breakdowns, make only breakdowns 100 times before doing anything else
* #### If you are experimenting with a producing a new genre, give it a hundred times before you judge whether you’re good at it or not

#### In this way, you can begin actively improving your weaknesses.

#### 11 - I Can’t Get That Sound

#### Have you ever had a crazy idea in your head that you just had to try in your music? Or a sound that you think would make for an awesome riff in your next track? But then when you sit down to actually replicate the sound in your DAW, it’s nothing like what you’d hoped?

#### Welcome to every musician’s plight. If you’re like me, you’ll find yourself still trying to design that particular sound four hours later. You skipped dinner or stayed up too late, and only if you’re lucky did you nail it. More often though it just didn’t ever come close to your original idea.

#### If you can relate, it’s time to change your outlook. Forget about perfectly reconstructing that sound or idea. Instead, sit down and begin to work on it, but allow yourself to embrace whatever the results are. By exclusively trying to recreate a certain sound, you’re limiting your creative output. You’re shutting everything else out and deliberately putting on blinders. This is inefficient and tends to be a waste of your time.

#### Music is the guiding force here. It exists in a universe of its own, and you’re the instrument that will communicate it. Yes, that’s a very hippy-ish way of looking at producing, but I find it to be true. This is no different than when people talk of going with the flow.

#### So how does one go with the flow and let the music drive the person? The best advice I can offer is to sit down, attempt to get what’s in your head out and onto your computer, and then let it transform into what it wants to. Whatever direction it takes, run with it and sculpt it. If your ideas are based off of a song, genre, or style you recently heard, try this:

#### 

* #### Zoom out. Inspired by an arpeggiated bass line? What was so inspiring about it?
* #### Focus on the mood, energy, and tone the particular sound conveyed. Was the arpeggiated line dark? Bright? Was there a change in key or scale?
* #### Write these conclusions down. Describe the sound or song that inspired you. Describe the idea that’s bouncing around in your head.

#### You will most likely find that your best music originates when you let the music drive your idea, rather than letting the idea drive your music.

#### 12 - One At A Time

#### What I’m about to explain is a great philosophy for all areas of your life, but it happens to be especially relevant to musical and artistic endeavors. The concept of one at a time is crucial for prolonged success. When building habits, when quitting something, when making gigantic life changes - breaking your timeline into manageable chunks is much less overwhelming.

#### I’ll start with a phrase you’ve probably heard before at least once in your life - one day at a time. Put simply, this means you ought to not worry about your future and instead focus on today. What needs to be done today? When you want success, projecting into the future won’t do you any good.

#### Keep in mind, projecting future outcomes is not the same as planning for your future. Go ahead and plan, it’d be silly not to. But don’t plan the outcomes, because that won’t get you far at all. Plan for what you want and work towards getting it one day at a time. Figure out what needs to be accomplished today and do just that. Worry about tomorrow tomorrow.

#### Keeping with the “one at a time” theme, if you’re struggling with a particular area of music production, don’t let everything overwhelm you. There are so many areas to understand - compression, EQ, effects, stereo image, sound design, mixing, mastering, etc. Pick one and learn it. Study it. Own it. Then move on to another weakness.

#### This principle also applies to practicing regularly. To begin building habits, start today and don’t think about tomorrow. Devote an hour today to music. Tomorrow, do it again. But don’t think about the next day until it arrives.

#### Likewise, this works with finishing tracks. Many people fall into the notion that you should be working on many projects at once if you’re a real artist. If you’re dedicated, you’ll have a million projects in the works. While this might be fine, if you struggle with finishing songs, delete everything except your current tune. If you get distracted, take a break, but don’t start another song until you finish the current one.

#### Apply this “one at a time” rule to your productions and you’ll be surprised to find that you’ll see results sooner. You’ll also forget about the pressure of “making it” because you’ll be focused on one day only. If you keep your head down and practice, you’ll be pleasantly surprised when you look up months later to newfound success.

#### 13 - Are You Good Enough?

#### Many artists have experienced a feeling of inferiority before. You know the feeling. You hear an amazingly well produced track and you think to yourself you’ll never be able to cultivate such talent. Or you see someone else start to get popular and blow up, and you feel left in the dust. Or maybe you just can’t get motivated to finish a song and you’ve been feeling this way for weeks.

#### These things happen to the best of us. All we can do is push through it and work around it. Life happens in cycles, and your creativity and drive are not exceptions. Fortunately, there are things you can do to push past the valleys of reduced motivation.

#### If you haven’t ever been in such a spot, it’s unlikely you’ve been accomplishing a lot. At the very least you’re not pouring your heart and soul into your music. If you’ve got nothing on the line, there’s no reason to feel like you’re not good enough. Feeling inferior is a by-product of actually caring. It’s easy not to feel that way if you aren’t invested.

#### So, if you are invested in your material, there are some actions you can take to get out of whatever funk you find yourself in.

#### If you’re concerned that you aren’t good enough and you feel discouraged, the most important thing you can do is to not stop. The feeling can arise over anything. Maybe you hear an amazing song and think that you’ll never be that good. Or maybe you go on vacation and upon returning you realize your ambition has been replaced with laziness.

#### When you feel like you’re not making enough progress, or you’re on that ever-so-common artistic plateau where you’re not learning much, the solution is to continue. If you stop during those periods you definitely won’t improve. But if you push through and continue, you’ll find the plateau you’re on eventually ends and you continue going uphill again.

#### While pushing forward is a solid first step, there is more to it than that. There are other steps you can take to break through any monotonous or discouraging periods. If you’re driving forward, yet you find yourself growing tired of the same old routine, switch things up. Identify something you’ve consistently been doing and flip it upside down. I find myself regularly sticking with what I think works well from a production standpoint only to realize I can grow by leaps and bounds when I toss my hesitation out and jump into unknown territory. Are you afraid of using compression because you’re unfamiliar with it? That’s the perfect reason to dive in. Have you been using reverb to spice up your hi-hats? Get rid of it and try something else like a short delay.

#### A lot of new heights are reached when you’re not afraid to try things that might not sound right or are traditionally frowned upon. Take what you’ve learned and try the opposite.

#### Occasionally, no matter what you do, you’ll get tired of your music. It’s boring, it all sounds the same, or you always get the similar results. You feel like you’re struggling to push the limits. When you’re at a spot like this, one thing that never fails is working with someone else.

#### Finding another producer to collaborate with is beneficial for two reasons: You will either have chemistry and produce amazing tracks, or you won’t have chemistry in which case you can at least learn their workflow and incorporate their techniques with your own.

#### Sometimes, no matter how hard you push through, you still feel like quitting. There will be moments when, despite having changed up your methods and mindset, at the end of the day you still feel burnt out, uninspired, and unable to offer 100%. When these waves crash over you, it may be that the best answer is to actually give your music a break.

#### A break can be a healthy thing as long as you’re not using it to procrastinate. The difference lies in your motive, and you need to be honest with yourself about what that truly is. It’s quite common for artists to put off making music because they’re afraid of failure. If that’s the case with you, a break probably isn’t ideal. But if you’re truly exhausted and uninspired, then walking away from your music for a bit will help. It can clear your mind, allowing you to re-energize and acquire a fresh new perspective.

#### However far off the beam you may be, the ultimate goal is to get back on track and continue your musical journey. Sometimes it requires trudging forward, other times it means changing routines and breaking out of comfort zones. The point is to keep the forward momentum.

#### 14 - Producing As A Life-Long Journey

#### Producing can definitely be a life-long endeavor, and the suggestions in this book will help you transform from occasional producing into a daily, regular habit. And while you don’t have to produce every day, you do want to begin setting goals each week. By continually practicing your musical abilities, you can make a habit out of producing much like you would brushing your teeth or working out.

#### You may find yourself sacrificing some things in your life, but that is okay. The longer you continue to make music, the more natural it will become, and the better you will be at producing.

#### Some other tips you might find helpful:

#### 

* #### Call yourself a musician or a producer. This helps solidify the image you have of yourself. If you view yourself as a producer, it becomes easier to make it a part of your life.
* #### Don’t let criticism and feedback on your music keep your from doing what you love. Even if someone doesn’t like your music, don’t let that be an excuse to quit.
* #### Understand that it will take years before you are at the point that the masters are at. Time takes time. Have fun with the journey and you will find yourself quickly approaching a level you never thought possible.

#### If your top priority is to enjoy the process of making music rather than earning an income, you will have a blast and experience success.
