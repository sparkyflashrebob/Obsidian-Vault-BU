A fun way to learn about strategy, tactics and social movement history.
MATERIALS One suit of cards: Tactics first ● Timer ● Pen & paper to keep score
PLAY 1. Team-up. 3+ people per team. If < 6 people, play as one team. 2. Guess! One person in your team draws a card from the deck and gives clues
about the card to the team. For example: If the card is “Use the Spectrum of Allies,” you might say, “an activity for identifying your friends and enemies”. Once your team guesses the card title, pick another and keep going. If your team can’t guess a card, set aside, and place back in deck at end of turn . If you give a clue that breaks this rule, place the card back into the deck and move on to the next card. If the timer goes off before the team has guessed the last card, put it back into the deck so another team can guess it. Your team has 2 minutes to guess as many cards as possible. Hold onto correctly guessed cards. Put discards back in deck. Next team goes. Round continues until deck is done.
3. Four Rounds Pay attention. While one team is playing, the other team(s) should be watching and keeping note of the cards at play, as they will reemerge in the following rounds. Rotate clue-givers so each person has a chance to get their team to guess (er, make a fool of themselves) in front of their comrades. Round 1: You can say anything except the words in the title. Round 2: Only one word (or sound). Round 3: Use charades (motions without sound) to give clues. Optional Round 4: Use only face charades! Or try using only a sound to get your team to guess the card. Tally score at the end of each round (one point for each card guessed correctly), put all the cards back in the deck, shuffle, and go on to the next round.
4. Win. Add points from each round & declare winners!
*OPTIONS ● Swap order of Rounds 2 & 3. Some people prefer to do charades before*
one word. You may, too. ● Challenge. In round one, the clue cannot include the category of the card
(for example, you can’t say “it’s a tactic”).
● Double challenge. Don’t say any of the words on the card.
TIPS FOR FACILITATORS ● Radical Charades is a fun way to introduce students to the deck. At any point
in the game, if players become interested in a particular card, you can stop play (and the timer) and have the player read the card out loud, or even use their smartphone camera to follow the pop-up code to the online toolbox and explore further. Set a time limit for exploring the card, so the game can continue.
● Some participants may be familiar with the game Celebrity, which is similar to this game and may provide helpful orientation.
