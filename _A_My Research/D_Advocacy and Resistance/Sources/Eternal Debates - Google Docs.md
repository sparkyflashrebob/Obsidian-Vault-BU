*Some debates are not right v. wrong but rather express two sides of an important question that must constantly be wrestled with.*
Materials: 2 piles of cards: Debates, Stories
*Play: 1. Pick a pair of Debate cards (e.g. Debate 1, Side A & B) and read aloud. 2. Pick a Story from the pile and read aloud. 3. Debate which side the Story supports. 4. Continue flipping over story cards and debating.*
*OPTIONS ● Play in teams. ● Also use Principles. ● Instead of choosing a debate, pick one randomly. ● Use a timer and give each team a few minutes to prepare their arguments,*
*then… debate! ● Take up any of the questions in the Beautiful Trouble Study Guide . ● Do it in public, with a soapbox of course.*
*TIPS FOR FACILITATORS ● Add Tactics, for example, argue the everyone is an artist with Flashmob or*
Distributed Action . ● Use the debate cards to reality-test your proposed action, to interrogate the
values behind actions created in Design an Action , or to evaluate a past action.
● Each team gets a hand of cards (3 each of tactics, principles, theories) to use in making their case. Team can choose which cards contribute to their best case. After one team makes their case, the other team can cross-examine by using the cards to make a case for THEIR side, thus stealing the card from the other team.
● Specify the number of people in teams - or you can discuss as whole group or in small groups and have groups report back.
*● Good conversation for road trips or family gatherings! ● Follow the pop-up code on the Story (or Principle ) to help your team prepare*
for their debate. ● Do a debate on a train. Do a debate to feel less vain. Even use a repeating
refrain! Drive your friends insane! You can debate while driving on the road.
You can debate through a coursework load. What to bring along to ruin a perfectly good date: A debate!
