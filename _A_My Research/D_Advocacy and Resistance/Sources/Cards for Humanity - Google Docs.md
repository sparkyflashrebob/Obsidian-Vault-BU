You love Cards Against Humanity® but you’re actually for humanity.
MATERIALS Shuffled cards • Hat • Pens & scrap paper
PLAY 1. Create absurd scenarios. Each player writes 3 sticky situations (“James Bond
comes to dinner...” “Your water-supply is clogged with sparkly unicorns...”) on 3 scraps of paper. Put all scenarios in a hat.
2. Deal 5 cards to each player. 3. Play. One person starts as Judge, picks a scenario from hat, reads aloud. Each
player passes face-down to Judge the card most likely to fix the absurd scenario (and offers them the best chance for a win!).
4. Judge decides democratically/dictatorially/quirkily which card is best (as players try to win them over). Winning player keeps the scenario paper. Discard used cards.
5. Next round. Judge role rotates clockwise. Each player draws 1 card (to maintain 5 cards in hand). New Judge selects new scenario.
6. Win. Player with most scenario papers at end of the hour wins.
OPTIONS ● Use sticky historical situations (“You’re part of Gandhi’s Salt March and you
just got word that the Raj will come out and could maim and kill protesters.” “You’re trying to decide whether to join the conspiracy to assassinate Julius Caesar.” “You’re an autoworker in 1937 and you just sat down in GM’s Flint plant.”) or movie situations (“You’re the Black Panther and you need to decide if you will use your resources from Wakanda to help family in Los Angeles.”)
● Speed round . Give the Judge just 3 minutes to select winning card. Or, if you are playing with a lot of people, have the judge just read their top 3 pics instead of reading all cards aloud.
● Remove Methodology and Wild Cards from your deck (as they can be harder to play.)
● Have the Judge set their own criteria for what kind of response they want to receive (“totally absurd,” say, or “most militant,” or “most do-able in real life.”)
*● Experiment with how you want to judge . Democratically? Dictatorially? One way to judge democratically is to have players close their eyes and raise their hands to vote for the card of their choice. Or choose winning cards democratically, inviting all players to make their case and vote. ( Principle: Down with hierarchy! Up with humanity! )*
*TIPS FOR FACILITATORS ● Never played Apples to Apples ® or Cards Against Humanity ® ? Don’t worry!*
This is a fun way to get your group’s creative juices going that might also generate some useful ideas, and no prior knowledge is required.
*● Make scenario cards ahead of time. ● Justify your whimsy. Unlike in Cards Against Humanity™ , in which cards are*
submitted anonymously, the Judge may choose to have players share why they chose the card, and encourage creative presentations of their ideas (act it out, sing about it, body-sculpt it, etc.) to “win over” the Judge’s favor. Here’s where your creative persuasion skills can come into play.
*● Science Fiction & Social Movements. Use this activity as part of a curriculum on the creative role sci-fi can play in helping social movements imagine (and prepare for) possible futures. (You might assign readings from Octavia’s Brood (Ed. adrienne maree brown and Walidah Imarisha ).*
● Encourage participants to learn more. Each card includes a unique “pop-up code.” Turn your smartphone camera on and hover over the card. See what happens?! Your smartphone automatically pops up a link to the full version of that card in the Beautiful Trouble online toolbox.
● Don’t forget to capture your best ideas. For all of these activities, you may want to designate a notetaker who can write down — or quickly take pictures with their phone — any ideas your team generates during the game.
