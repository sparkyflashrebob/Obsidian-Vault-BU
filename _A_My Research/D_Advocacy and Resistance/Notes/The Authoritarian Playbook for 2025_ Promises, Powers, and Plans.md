"The Authoritarian Playbook for 2025" is a report authored by United to Protect Democracy that aims to delineate how a potential authoritarian president would dismantle American democracy and what measures can be taken to protect it [1, 2]. It builds upon Protect Democracy's earlier "The Authoritarian Playbook," which provided a framework for understanding how modern strongmen consolidate power globally [3, 4].

The report's primary goal is to clearly show how Donald Trump would fulfill his most extreme anti-democratic pledges in a second term and to offer expert recommendations for mitigating these dangers [2]. It focuses specifically on **Promises, Powers, and Plans** [2]:

* **Promises**: The report compiles Donald Trump's stated intentions for a second term, situating them within the context of a global resurgence of similar authoritarian figures whom Trump has openly admired and emulated [2].
* **Powers**: It analyzes the powers of the presidency and how they could be leveraged to implement these promises, detailing the legal mechanisms involved and assessing the strength of existing safeguards [5].
* **Plans**: The report explores the specific plans developed by Trump and his allies to bypass or override the checks and balances within the American system, describing the practical implications and potential negative impacts on American life upon implementation [5].

The report highlights **six major threats** or categories of actions that an authoritarian leader would employ to dismantle democracy, which are consistent with a global "authoritarian playbook" [6, 7]:

1. **Pardons to License Lawbreaking**

   * **Promise**: Trump has pledged to abuse the pardon power to enable future violence and lawbreaking, reward criminality, serve his self-interest, and shield himself and his allies from accountability [8-11]. He specifically indicated he would pardon a "large portion" of those convicted for federal offenses related to the January 6th attack [9].
   * **Playbook Tactic**: This furthers the authoritarian tactics of aggrandizing executive power, targeting marginalized communities, and stoking violence [9].
2. **Directing Investigations Against Critics and Rivals**

   * **Promise**: Trump's campaign is heavily focused on retribution, with plans to dismantle the Department of Justice's (DOJ) traditional prosecutorial independence. This would grant him personal control to direct law enforcement against perceived opponents and protect himself from criminal exposure [8, 12]. Specific targets mentioned include President Joe Biden, former Chief of Staff John Kelly, former Attorney General William Barr, and former Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman General Mark Milley [13].
   * **Playbook Tactic**: This is a classic example of politicizing independent institutions, allowing the targeting of vulnerable communities and the quashing of dissent [12].
3. **Regulatory Retaliation**

   * **Promise**: Trump has vowed to centralize and use federal regulatory power to reward political loyalty and penalize critics, particularly within the media [14, 15]. This involves making government aid, contracts, licenses, and other administrative tools contingent on personal loyalty [16]. It would also involve reissuing the "Schedule F" executive order from October 2020, which aimed to strip job protections from tens to hundreds of thousands of career civil service employees and replace them with loyalists [17].
   * **Playbook Tactic**: These plans mirror measures used by leaders like Viktor Orbán in Hungary to transform government power to serve the ruler's interests [18, 19].
4. **Federal Law Enforcement Overreach**

   * **Promise**: Trump intends to expand the reach of federal law enforcement by consolidating resources under the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), primarily for aggressive immigration enforcement [14, 20]. This includes plans to invoke parts of the Alien and Sedition Acts for large-scale arrests and the establishment of mass detention camps, reminiscent of World War II internment camps [20-22]. He has also threatened to cut federal funding to cities that refuse to cooperate [23].
   * **Playbook Tactic**: This directly follows authoritarian tactics of aggrandizing executive power and using it to target vulnerable communities, often by dehumanizing them as "vermin" [20, 22, 23].
5. **Domestic Deployment of the Military**

   * **Promise**: Trump has promised and planned to deploy military force domestically to suppress dissent, target vulnerable communities, and potentially interfere with elections by invoking the Insurrection Act [14, 24]. This could involve deploying National Guard troops and Marines to American cities, including Los Angeles, without local or state authorization, and potentially using military action against Mexican drug cartels within the United States [24-27].
   * **Playbook Tactic**: Politicizing institutions like the military is a key authoritarian tactic designed to silence opposition and coerce loyalty to the autocratic leader [28].
6. **The Autocrat Won’t Leave**

   * **Threat**: The report addresses Trump's repeated suggestions about staying in office beyond a second term, a common behavior among autocrats who ignore or eliminate legal term limits, such as Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping [29-32]. Trump's public statements, including invoking monarchic imagery and declining to rule out a third term, indicate a willingness to undermine the peaceful transfer of power [6, 31, 33].
   * **Playbook Tactic**: In competitive authoritarian systems, elections can become a tool to reinforce autocratic control rather than challenge it, with leaders using law enforcement and media control to influence outcomes [34].

The report concludes with **ten recommendations** for a pro-democracy coalition to prepare for and mitigate the threat of an autocrat returning to the Oval Office, emphasizing lessons learned from past successes in combating authoritarianism during Trump's first term [29, 35-37]. It highlights that collective action, such as community resistance gatherings, is essential to counteract the isolation that authoritarians seek to impose [38, 39].
