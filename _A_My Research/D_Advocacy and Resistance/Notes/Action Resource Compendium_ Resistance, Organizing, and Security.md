Here is a list of resources available by type and subject, drawing information from the provided sources:

**I. Nonviolent Action & Strategic Resistance Frameworks**

* **Books/Publications:**

  + **Gene Sharp's *The Politics of Nonviolent Action* (3 Vols.)** [1, 2]: A seminal work cataloging 198 methods of nonviolent action and providing historical examples. It includes chapters detailing methods of nonviolent protest and persuasion [2, 3], social noncooperation [2, 4], economic noncooperation (boycotts and strikes) [2, 5, 6], political noncooperation [2, 7], psychological intervention [2, 8], and physical intervention [2, 9]. It also covers the nature and control of political power and dynamics of resistance [2, 10-13]. An appendix and bibliography are also available [14].
  + ***Beautiful Trouble: A Creative Activist's Toolbox*** [15-17]: Described as a "pattern language" or "matrix of flexible, interlinked tools" for creative activism, rather than a strict cookbook [15, 18]. It's a modular toolkit written by over 175 frontline activists, available as a book, web toolbox, and strategy card deck [17, 19]. It covers Tactics, Principles, Theories, Case studies, and Practitioners [15, 18, 20-23]. Its modular format allows for endless expansion and submissions of new insights [24-28].
  + ***Beautiful Rising: Tactics for Creative Resistance*** [29, 30]: A publication and common platform compiling effective approaches and innovations in creative activism from the Global South [19, 31, 32]. It includes stories, tactics, principles, theories, and methodologies [18, 32, 33]. The book is a subset of a larger online toolkit at **beautifulrising.org** [32, 34].
  + ***Beautiful Solutions: A Toolbox for Liberation*** [16, 23, 35, 36]: A book and online resource focusing on community-led solutions and building a more just world [23, 37]. It contains stories, principles, and questions [37].
  + ***Why Civil Resistance Works*** (Columbia Studies in Terrorism and Irregular Warfare) [38]: An academic book on the outcomes of nonviolent and violent resistance campaigns. It references a comprehensive bibliography on nonviolent civil resistance by April Carter, Howard Clark, and Michael Randle (2006) [39-41].
* **Toolkits & Guides:**

  + **BATMo! (Beautiful Action Trainer Modules)** [42, 43]: An innovative card deck-based toolkit with 11-12 modules for Nonviolent Action Trainers & Facilitators [42, 44-46]. It helps NVA trainers quickly produce agendas and identify training activities [44, 47]. It includes 7 Nonviolent Action Modules and 5 Process Modules [44, 46], each with specific activities and instructions available online via QR codes on printable prompt cards [46, 48-50]. Modules include:
    - Nonviolent Action History [48]
    - Vision & Values [51]
    - Assessment & Analysis [51]
    - Strategic Implementation [52]
    - Developing Implementation Plans [52]
    - Building Resilience: Self-care and community care [50]
  + **Beautiful Trouble Strategy Cards** [30, 36, 53, 54]: A deck of 100+ cards for creative action design, social movement history, and organizing [53, 55-57]. It features 5 suits (Tactics, Principles, Theories, Stories, Methodologies) plus wild cards, eternal debate cards, and game-play cards [55-58]. Each card has a QR code linking to the online toolbox or further resources [59, 60]. Includes games like "Cards for Humanity," "Cardstorm!", "Radical Charades," "Design a Creative Action," "Divine the Future," and "Eternal Debates" [58, 59, 61-64].
  + **"198 Methods of Nonviolent Action" Handouts/Activity** [65]: A dynamic resource providing a framework for facilitators to lead an experiential activity. It includes 32 unique, double-sided handouts, each with a category of nonviolent action (from Gene Sharp's methods), specific methods, a real-world example, and a discussion question [65, 66]. The backside features a checklist of the 32 categories [67].
  + **The Authoritarian Playbook for 2025** [68, 69]: A report that puts the current authoritarian threat into context and lays out specific autocratic actions to expect in 2025 and beyond. It also includes ten strategies for the broader pro-democracy coalition to mitigate the threat [68].
  + **New Pan-Afrikanism Toolkit: Wisdom for Modern Struggle** [70, 71]: Available in English, Arabic, French, and Portuguese versions [70, 71].
  + **"Tactic Typology" by Daniel Hunter, Training for Change** [72]: A typology of tactics categorized into Symbolic Protest, Noncooperation (economic and political), and Alternative Cooperation [72].
  + **The Movement Compass** [73]: Builds upon Bill Moyer's Movement Action Plan and Movement Netlab's Movement Cycle Matrix. More information is available at **beautifultrouble.org/toolbox/tool/the-movement-cycle** [73].
  + **A Diplomat’s Handbook for Democracy Development Support** [74]: Offers an extensive catalog of resources and assets that diplomats can draw from to support civil society and individuals/groups committed to nonviolent change [74].
* **Organizations/Platforms:**

  + **Beautiful Trouble (beautifultrouble.org)** [17, 19, 21, 30, 35, 36, 42, 43, 45, 49, 53, 54, 58, 60, 73, 75-82]: A global network and online toolbox of creative activism resources, including books, strategy cards, and training modules. It curates collective wisdom of diverse practices across many realms and regions [19, 83]. It is multilingual and movement-crowdsourced [19, 84].
  + **Beautiful Rising (beautifulrising.org)** [19, 32, 34]: An online platform and book focused on creative activism from the Global South, offering a common platform to document effective approaches and innovations [31, 32].
  + **Waging Nonviolence (wagingnonviolence.org)** [85-87]: A source for original news and analysis about struggles for justice and peace around the globe [86]. It partners with Choose Democracy to provide resources on how to stem the rising authoritarian tide [88].
  + **The Commons Library (commonslibrary.org)** [89]: A platform providing resources related to protests, including information on organizing, strategy, rights, safety, and archiving [90-92].
  + **The TESA Collective (The Toolbox for Education and Social Action)** [77]: Works with organizations and groups to build tools, games, and programs for their cause [77].
  + **Protect Democracy (protectdemocracy.org)** [68, 93-97]: An organization focused on defending the rule of law and providing resources related to elections, authoritarian threats, and federal workers' rights [68, 94, 96-100].
  + **Mobilize (Mobilize.us)** [101-106]: A platform for registering and managing events, tracking RSVPs, and communicating with guests for large-scale organizing efforts [101, 104, 107]. Events can be set to public or private [104].

**II. Community Organizing & Events**

* **Toolkits & Guides:**

  + **One Million Rising, Resistance Gathering Host Toolkit** [105, 108]: A comprehensive toolkit for planning community gatherings, including a checklist (before, during, after the gathering) and a guide for hosting effective meetings [105]. It details necessary materials and provides recommendations for roles like a "Safety Lead" or "De-Escalation Lead" [108].
  + **Resist Trump - Hosting a Get-Together Toolkit** [109]: A toolkit for hosting a get-together, covering planning, location, timing, materials, advertising, invitations, themes, activities, and general hosting tips. It suggests using Mobilize for event advertising and RSVP tracking [106, 109]. Both PDF and video versions are available [106].
  + **"How to Create and Run an Event on Mobilize" (YouTube Tutorial)** [104]: A video guiding users through setting up events on the Mobilize platform, including event details, visibility settings (public/private), location (in-person/virtual), date/time, capacity, contact information, language, and accessibility [104, 110]. It also covers managing events, sending communications to attendees, and tracking attendance/feedback [107, 111, 112].
  + **Mayday Strong Labor Day Toolkit** [103]: A toolkit available at **mayday.org** (or bit.ly/Laborday\_signup) to guide individuals in hosting a day of action [103].
  + **"How to Host an Effective Meeting Guide"** [105]: Part of the One Million Rising Host Toolkit, it provides guidance for running successful meetings.
* **Conceptual Resources:**

  + ***People, Power, Change: Organizing for Democratic Renewal* by Marshall Ganz** [113]: A book that delves into community organization, power dynamics, and change. It covers concepts such as power theory of change, resource mobilization, and distinguishing between mobilizing and organizing [113-132]. It outlines five leadership practices: Relationships, Storytelling, Strategizing, Action, and Structure [115].

  + **Mediawise’s resources** [68]: Recommended for media literacy and information diet improvement.

**V. Immigrant Rights & Community Defense**

* **Hotlines/Networks:**

  + **Marin Rapid Response Hotline** [138-140]: A 24/7 hotline with bilingual dispatchers for immigrant rights activists in Marin, offering immediate legal connection for those detained by ICE [139]. It also serves as a hub for real-time alerts and information verification [139, 141].
  + **Rapid Response Network phone numbers** [142, 143]: A list of phone numbers for various Rapid Response Networks across Northern California [142, 143].
* **Toolkits & Guides:**

  + **Red Cards** [140-142, 144]: Flyers available in Spanish and English to inform individuals of their constitutional rights when encountering immigration authorities (e.g., don't open the door, don't sign anything, right to a lawyer) [141-143]. These are widely available for purchase online [144].
  + **Preparedness Plan** [142, 143]: A family preparedness plan available in English, Spanish, and Chinese for possible encounters with immigration authorities [142, 143].
  + **Employer Training** [145, 146]: Training offered to employers to understand their role and rights during ICE encounters, including internal protocols and staff communication plans for physical and paper raids [145, 146].
  + **Legal Observer Training** [138, 140, 147]: Training for volunteers to document ICE activity, which includes information on tactics, strategy, and role-playing scenarios [147].
  + **AROS (Alliance to Reclaim Our Schools) Toolkit** [148]: A toolkit to easily contact members of Congress during recess, visit offices, and send emails related to education issues [148].
  + **Florida 7-Day Collective Action Toolkit** [148]: A social media toolkit created in Florida for collective action leading up to Labor Day, focusing on issues like public education funding [148].
* **Organizations:**

  + **CIPC (California Immigrant Policy Center)** [140]: Conducts statewide calls and provides updates on legislation and budget impacts related to ICE actions and state reactions [140].
  + **Road of Shalom** [149]: A faith congregation mentioned for its accompaniment support for those impacted by ICE [149].
