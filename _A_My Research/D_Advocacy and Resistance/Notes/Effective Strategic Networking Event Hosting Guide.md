Hosting your own networking event, particularly a community gathering focused on strategic action, involves several key recommendations to ensure it is effective, welcoming, and safe.

Here's a guide to hosting your own networking event:

**I. Strategic Purpose and Mindset**

* **Define Your Goal** [1]: Clarify the purpose of your gathering. Do you want to bring like-minded people together to "let off steam," to start a regular meeting group, to respond to a local or global event, or to build an existing group [1]? For strategic gatherings, the goal is to **build connections, share knowledge, and foster readiness and resilience** around fighting authoritarianism and participating in mass defiance and strategic non-cooperation [2-4].
* **Embrace Smaller, Curated Groups**: Instead of large, noisy, undifferentiated masses of people, aim for **smaller groups in quieter environments** where you know who is coming [5]. This allows for more targeted and efficient interactions, optimizing the environment for meaningful relationship building [5].
* **Counteract Isolation**: Authoritarians thrive on division and making people feel alone and scared [6, 7]. Hosting an event actively **breaks this isolation** by connecting people who share concerns and are ready to act [6, 8].
* **Be Authentic and Inclusive**: Be yourself, speaking genuinely from your values and experience [9, 10]. Acknowledge that attendees might be feeling stressed, mad, or grieving, and create a space where opinions and feelings can be shared respectfully, even if there's disagreement [11].
* **Focus on Trust**: Build trusted, private relationships. This "speed of trust" is critical for successful and strategic change [12]. Affinity groups, composed of people who share connections and trust, are a good model for this [8].

**II. Planning Your Event**

* **Choose a Time, Date, and Location** [13]:
  + **Flexibility**: Consider what works best for your invited guests, including evenings, weekends, or daytime hours, as people have varying schedules [14].
  + **Comfortable Space**: Your living room, backyard, a community center, library, public park, café patio, or union hall can all work [3, 15, 16]. Ensure you have permission if using a public space [15].
  + **Distraction-Free**: Choose a location quiet enough for people to hear each other and minimize distractions [17, 18].
  + **Accessibility**: Consider parking, public transportation, and physical accessibility [19].
  + **Privacy**: If hosting at your home, **do NOT invite strangers** (people you are not socially connected with) [13, 20]. If casting a wider net to the public, use a public location [20, 21].
* **Recruit Attendees** [17]:
  + **Start with Your Network**: Begin by inviting people you know well—friends, family, co-workers, and neighbors [21, 22]. Personal invitations work best [21].
  + **Expand Carefully**: If interested, you can invite friends of friends, or post a private link in like-minded group chats or closed social media groups [21]. Avoid public blasting for private events [13].
  + **Set a Goal**: Aim for around 10 attendees, but understand that drop-off is normal. Plan for **twice the number of RSVPs** you desire for actual attendance (e.g., 20 RSVPs for 10 attendees) [17, 23, 24]. Powerful gatherings can happen with just a few motivated people [17].
  + **Recruit Helpers**: Delegate tasks like bringing snacks, setting up chairs, or leading a short icebreaker. This lightens your load and increases turnout [21]. Consider having a "Safety Lead" or "De-Escalation Lead" [18].
* **Register Your Event**: Use a platform like Mobilize.us to register your event. This helps track progress, provides host resources, and offers tools for planning and communicating with guests [25-27].
  + **Private Visibility**: Set your event visibility to "private" so only those with the direct link can see it [20, 28].
  + **Location Privacy**: For home events, ensure the location is private and non-archivable on the platform [29].
  + **Communication**: Use the platform's features (like "Email Attendees" on Mobilize) to send reminders, provide location details, and share important information [28, 30].
* **Prepare Materials and Space** [18, 31]:
  + **Organizational**: Have a sign-in sheet, pens, sticky notes, and name tags. Large paper/butcher paper and markers can be useful for group brainstorming [18].
  + **Content**: Have a printed agenda and discussion script for yourself, and copies for attendees or a way to project it [18, 20].
  + **Refreshments**: Offer drinks and snacks, possibly potluck style, and inform guests about what to expect [18, 31].
  + **Atmosphere**: Arrange chairs in a circle for better visibility and hearing [18]. Have a small welcome sign or symbol at your door/mailbox [18].
  + **Tech Check**: For virtual events, test your audio and Wi-Fi [18].
* **Confirm Guests**: In the 24 hours before your gathering, personally call or text everyone who RSVP'd. This can boost turnout significantly [32].

**III. During the Event**

* **Set Up Early**: Arrive and set up at least 30 minutes before an in-person event, or 15 minutes for a virtual one [32].
* **Welcome and Ground**: Greet attendees as they arrive. Start by welcoming everyone and grounding them in the "why" of the gathering [33].
* **Facilitate the Discussion**:
  + **Set Expectations**: Clearly state that the purpose is to explore ideas and build community, not necessarily to make group decisions or agree on every point [11]. You can use a phrase like, "We might not agree, but we won't be disrespectful to one another" [11, 34].
  + **Guide, Don't Dictate**: Guide the discussion using the provided agenda and script [3, 20, 35]. Validate people's thoughts and feelings, but keep the group on track with the topic [36].
  + **Encourage Stories**: Ask open-ended questions that invite personal stories and experiences, rather than just opinions [10].
  + **Break into Small Groups**: If the group is large, break into smaller groups to facilitate more focused discussion and allow different thinking styles to flourish [37].
  + **Model Courage**: Your presence and willingness to host inspires others to be more courageous [11].
  + **Document Ideas**: Designate a notetaker to capture ideas and insights generated during the discussion [38, 39].
* **Safety and Disruption**:
  + **Stay Calm**: If someone attempts to disrupt the gathering, remain calm and non-confrontational [40].
  + **Politely Ask to Leave**: Request disruptors to leave without engaging in debate [40].
  + **Prioritize Safety**: If the situation feels unsafe, end the gathering and move everyone to a safe location [40]. In an emergency, call 911 [40].
  + **Report Incidents**: Afterward, email the organizing team with a brief incident report [40].

**IV. After the Event**

* **Clean Up**: Enlist volunteers to help clean the space [33].
* **Mark Attendance**: Update your Mobilize Host Dashboard to mark who attended. This is crucial for tracking overall goals [15, 41].
* **Share Photos**: Send photos of your event to the organizing team [15].
* **Send Thank Yous**: Send a brief thank you note to attendees, including any follow-up asks or next steps [15, 42].
* **Gather Feedback**: Be open to receiving feedback, both positive and negative, as an opportunity to learn and grow as a host [42, 43]. Mobilize can provide automatic feedback mechanisms [42].
* **Breathe and Celebrate**: Acknowledge your effort and success in hosting the gathering [15, 43].
