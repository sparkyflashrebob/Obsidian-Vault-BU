To resist authoritarianism, a multifaceted approach centered on **civil resistance** and **nonviolent action** is advocated, drawing on historical successes and strategic planning [1, 2]. These strategies aim to disrupt the authoritarian regime's ability to consolidate power and to foster a resilient, democratic society [3-5].

Key strategies for resistance include:

* **Building a Powerful and Broad Civil Resistance Movement**

  + This involves **mass defiance** across society, engaging diverse groups of people, and resisting out loud through various actions [1, 4]. Research indicates that civil resistance movements significantly increase the probability of success against democratic backsliding, with over half of such movements succeeding, often making the country *more* democratic than before [1].
  + It requires **mass participation**, drawing on a broad range of communities and constituencies, including millions of workers, teachers, lawyers, truck drivers, farmers, merchants, and students [6]. A notable statistic suggests that no regime has stayed in power when 3.5% of the population engages in active protest, which equates to about 11 million Americans [6].
  + This includes finding common cause across ideological and sectoral lines to stop tyranny [6].
* **Employing a Wide Repertoire of Nonviolent Tactics**

       - **Specific Methods**: Public speeches, letters of opposition/support, declarations by organizations, signed public statements, petitions, slogans, caricatures, symbols, banners, posters, leaflets, pamphlets, books, newspapers, journals, records, radio, television, skywriting, earthwriting, deputations, mock awards, group lobbying, picketing, mock elections, displays of flags/symbolic colors, wearing symbols, prayer, worship, delivering symbolic objects, protest disrobings, destruction of own property, symbolic lights, displays of portraits, paint as protest, new signs and names, symbolic sounds, symbolic reclamations, rude gestures, haunting officials, taunting officials, fraternization, vigils, humorous skits and pranks, performances of plays and music, singing, marches, parades, religious processions, pilgrimages, motorcades, political mourning, mock funerals, demonstrative funerals, homage at burial places, assemblies of protest or support, protest meetings, camouflaged meetings of protest, and teach-ins [7-28].
       - **Examples**: Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech at the March on Washington [7], a 17-year-old trolling Donald Trump by reserving rally tickets and not showing up [9], a silent protest by Portland Timbers and Seattle Sounders fans against a political signage ban [13], scientists staging a mock Victorian funeral to protest science funding policy [21], impromptu protests against Boris Johnson's parliament suspension [23], and dancers holding an open-air ballet class in solidarity against bullying [17].

* **Building Alternatives and Support Structures**

  + These tactics aim to strengthen the capacity and self-reliance of a non-violent movement and undermine popular support for institutions under authoritarian control [29].
  + Examples include mutual aid groups, alternative communications and transportation systems, food cooperatives, underground clinics, and groups that provide a positive vision for what democracy could be [29, 66]. This also involves setting up strike funds to support striking workers [29].
* **Strategic Planning and Learning**

  + **Pro-democracy coalitions** should be created *before* a crisis fully arrives [67, 68].
  + **Anti-democratic ideas and promises** must be taken seriously [67, 68].
  + Movement development tools like "Beautiful Trouble" offer methodologies and exercises to assess situations, plan campaigns, and continuously innovate strategy [69-74]. This includes identifying strengths, weaknesses, threats, and opportunities, and choosing tactics that support a chosen strategy [70, 73].
  + Learning from history is crucial, understanding past successes and failures in nonviolent struggle [75-78].
  + The importance of **"Block, Bridge, Build"**: blocking harms, bridging connections across movements, and building solutions for a better future [79, 80].
* **Targeting Pillars of Support**

  + Authoritarian regimes rely on the active and passive support of ordinary people organized in key institutions in society, which are referred to as "pillars of support" (e.g., business, labor, faith organizations, education, civil servants, security forces) [6, 30, 81, 82].
  + A critical strategy is to induce **defections and loyalty shifts** within these pillars [6, 30]. When members of these pillars stop cooperating, it becomes difficult for autocrats to maintain power [30].
  + This can involve pressuring these pillars through various tactics, as well as "good old-fashioned organizing" and meeting with members to shift their incentives [53, 56, 58, 83].
* **Fostering Courage and Community**

  + It is crucial to **counter the sense of isolation** that authoritarians seek to impose [84].
  + **Community resistance gatherings** are promoted to build connections, share knowledge, and enhance readiness and resilience in fighting authoritarianism [84-86]. These gatherings provide a space for people to discuss how to non-comply with orders and support each other in taking greater risks [85, 87].
  + Supporting those who take risks and encouraging new people to join the movement is vital [4, 87, 88].
* **Maintaining a Positive Vision**

  + Resistance efforts should be driven by a clear positive vision of what is being fought *for*, such as a society where everyone can be free and flourish, with access to basic needs like healthcare, housing, food, clean air, and water [89].
  + This positive vision helps sustain energy and momentum for non-cooperation and resistance [89].
* **Upholding Democratic Norms**

  + Strategies include rallying around non-partisan, independent public servants and upholding the rule of law and institutions, while always repudiating violence [67, 68].
  + Protecting the first targets of authoritarianism and advocating for the most vulnerable is also a key strategy [67, 68].
