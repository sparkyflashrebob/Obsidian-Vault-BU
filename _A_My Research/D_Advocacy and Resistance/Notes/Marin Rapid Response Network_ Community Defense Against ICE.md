The "Introduction to Marin Rapid Response Network" meeting began with Susan welcoming attendees and introducing **Lisa Bennett, the executive director of the Multicultural Center of Marin and founder of Indivisible Sausalito** [1]. Susan highlighted Lisa's extensive knowledge and engagement in racial justice and immigrant rights, expressing gratitude for her time [1].

Lisa expressed her pleasure at reaching a large audience and noted that **this is not the first instance of challenges posed by Mr. Trump** [1]. The meeting's purpose is to **learn more about the Marin Rapid Response Network (MRRN), the Marin Rapid Response Hotline, ICE activities in Marin, general ICE trends, and what actions can be taken in response** [1]. The MRRN is a program of the Multicultural Center of Marin, revived after being dormant during the Biden years, and was originally started by Olivia Beltran in 2018 [1]. Attendees were informed that follow-up links to interesting information or resources shared during the presentation would be sent via email [2].

The meeting outlined a **community defense model** adopted by immigrant rights activists nationwide, which includes [2]:

* A **24/7 hotline** (which Marin has) [2].
* **Training legal observers to document ICE activities** [2].
* Offering **"Know Your Rights" trainings for individuals and employers** [2].

Further needs for Marin specifically were identified, such as [3]:

* **Financial and legal support** for those impacted by ICE [2, 3].
* **Advocacy and power-building from within the community** [2].
* The establishment of a **mutual aid system** [2].
* **Policy change directed by impacted community members** [2].
* Outreach to day laborers to inform them of their rights and the hotline [3].
* People to accompany individuals to court or ICE appointments where they might be detained [3].
* Pro bono or low/no-cost immigration attorneys [3].

The **Marin Rapid Response Hotline (number provided in the meeting)** allows people to text photographs or call to report sightings, verify or debunk rumors, and connect detained individuals to immigration attorneys [3]. Bilingual dispatchers are available 24/7 [3]. The MRRN prioritizes training legal observers, with current efforts to onboard new coordinators [4]. They also plan to **increase outreach and training to employers** due to concerns about workplace raids, defining a "raid" as 10 individual arrests in a county in a single day or a workplace raid [4]. A **mass alert system** has been implemented, and the next step is to make it easy for people to sign up for alerts [4]. The meeting emphasized that any data, images, or videos gathered by legal observers are **never put on social media** but are given to the impacted family first [5].

A key point of caution was raised regarding certain **apps (like Ice Block) which are deemed unhelpful** because they only notify users within a five-mile radius, lack detailed information, and cannot have false reports removed, leading to unnecessary fear [6]. Helpful information for the hotline includes the size of activity, actions taken, location, unit identification (ICE, DHS, HSI, etc.), time of incident, and equipment seen (using the **SALUTE acronym**) [7].
