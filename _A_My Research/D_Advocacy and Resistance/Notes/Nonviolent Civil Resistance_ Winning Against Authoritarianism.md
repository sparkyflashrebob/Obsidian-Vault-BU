"Ep 1 - How We Win: Nonviolent Civil Resistance" is the first episode in the "How to Become a Leader in the Trump Resistance" leadership development series. This episode, moderated by Nevin K. with co-moderator Tracy, aims to introduce participants to the foundational concepts of nonviolent civil resistance and how authoritarianism impacts society [1].

The key elements spelled out in this episode include:

* **Resist Trump Campaign Core Principles**: The campaign's vision is **a future without Trump and his enablers in power**, its mission is **lawful non-violent civil resistance of Trump and his enablers**, and its values are **collaboration, integrity, nonpartisanship, and professionalism** [1].
* **Relevance of the First Amendment**: The session emphasizes the use of **First Amendment rights**, specifically freedom of speech, the right to peaceably assemble, and the right to petition the government for a redress of grievances, as the basis for lawful nonviolent civil resistance [1-3]. This is presented as constitutional protection for the activities undertaken [3].
* **Series Structure Overview**: It clarifies that the overall series is a **12-episode, drop-in program** structured into three modules (Level 1, 2, and 3), each culminating in a major assignment [2-4]. Episode 1 is the first "yellow block" in Level 1 [5].
  + **Module 1 (Level 1)**: Focuses on preparing participants to **host a get-together**, with a soft deadline of July 20th [2, 3]. Subsequent episodes in this module cover organizing basics, digital hubs, and planning for the get-together [5, 6].
  + **Module 2 (Level 2)**: Aims to prepare participants to **run a lawful protest**. Topics include "knowing your rights," "using social media," "staying safe" (de-escalation and digital security), and "taking action" [3-6].
  + **Module 3 (Level 3)**: The goal is to prepare participants to **start a "resist pod"**, defined as a group of two or three people committed to regular civil resistance actions [2, 4, 6]. This module covers advanced media techniques, fundraising, and advanced organizing [5, 6].
* **Episode 1 Specific Agenda**:
  + **How Authoritarianism Affects Us**: This segment discusses "what we lose and what's at stake," asserting that the US has traditionally been a beacon of democracy but is at risk of losing that status [7, 8]. It draws comparisons to other countries where authoritarianism has impacted free speech, elections, civic participation, and public services [8]. Daniel Hunter also highlights that authoritarian regimes consolidate power through means like polygraph testing, tariffs, and even floating ideas of extended presidencies [9].
  + **Breakout Group Discussion**: Participants discuss one institution in their community that upholds the Trump administration and how nonviolent action might shift or weaken its support [10].
  + **Mentimeter Exercise**: An interactive poll where participants share thoughts on how non-violent action can weaken such institutions [11].
  + **What is Nonviolent Civil Resistance**: This segment, led by guest speaker Brian Register, explores how nonviolent campaigns succeed by encouraging more people to make their own decisions, thereby fracturing overall power structures. This concept is linked to the idea of "pillars of support" for a regime, such as media, police, organized religion, and education [10, 12]. Daniel Hunter mentions research indicating that civil resistance movements are successful over 50% of the time, leading to more democratic outcomes, while campaigns without such movements are rarely successful (7.5%) [13].
  + **Q&A and Second Breakout Group**: Brian Register answers questions, including a discussion of "kettling" tactics by police [14]. The second breakout session asks participants to reflect on a nonviolent tactic that stood out to them and whether it could work in their community [15].
  + **Second Mentimeter Exercise**: Another interactive poll gathering responses on nonviolent tactics [15].
* **Learning Tools and Next Steps**: The episode encourages participants to engage with the **Google Classroom** (for content, assignments, and reflection), the **Leadership Lab** (for weekly assignment reviews and collaborative planning), and **Discord** (for 24/7 community connection and "learning pods") [11, 16-20]. It announces the next episode will cover "How We Fight: Organizing and Resistance" [6, 21, 22].
