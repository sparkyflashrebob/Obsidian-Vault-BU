"Ep 0 - Orientation" of the "How to Become a Leader in the Trump Resistance" series, led by Nevin K from the Resist Trump campaign, spells out the foundational elements and structure of the leadership development program [1, 2].

Key aspects spelled out in this orientation include:

* **Resist Trump Campaign Core Principles** [2, 3]:
  + **Vision**: A future without Trump and his enablers in power [2, 3].
  + **Mission**: Lawful, nonviolent civil resistance of Trump and his enablers [2, 3].
  + **Values**: Collaboration, integrity, nonpartisanship, and professionalism [2, 3].
* **Relevance of the First Amendment**: The program emphasizes the exercise of First Amendment rights, including freedom of speech, the right to peaceably assemble, and to petition the government for a redress of grievances, as the basis for lawful non-violent civil resistance [2-6].
* **Series Structure (12 episodes over 12-13 weeks)** [7-9]: The program is divided into three modules, each culminating in a major assignment designed to build leadership skills:
  + **Module 1 (Level 1): Host a Get-Together** [8, 9]:
    - Initial episodes cover the basics of nonviolent civil resistance, organizing, resistance, and using digital hubs like Discord to cultivate community [8, 9].
    - The assignment encourages hosting an in-person gathering with friends, family, or neighbors, with a suggested deadline of July 22nd [5, 8, 10]. A toolkit and Mobilize platform are provided for event registration and promotion [5, 10].
  + **Module 2 (Level 2): Lead a Lawful Protest** [9, 11]:
    - This module prepares participants for more complex actions by covering topics such as "knowing your rights" (legal basics for activists), "using social media" for mobilization, and "staying safe" through de-escalation and digital security [9, 11, 12].
    - The assignment involves running a lawful protest [9, 11].
  + **Module 3 (Level 3): Form a Resist Pod** [11, 12]:
    - Focuses on advanced techniques, including media strategies, fundraising, and advanced organizing [11, 12].
    - The final assignment is to form a "resist pod," a small group (2-3 people) committed to regular civil resistance actions [11-13].
* **Learning Approach**: The series is designed around the **70-20-10 rule for leadership development**, where 70% of learning comes from experiences, 20% from social learning (peer collaboration), and 10% from formal training (webinars, readings) [14].
* **Engagement Tools and Community Building** [15-18]:
  + **Webinars**: Weekly 90-minute interactive sessions with breakout rooms and expert speakers [16].
  + **Google Classroom**: A hub for accessing content, completing small reflection assignments, and finding an assignments checklist [15, 16, 19, 20].
  + **Leadership Lab**: Weekly Tuesday night sessions for reviewing assignments, discussing responses, and collaborative planning [17].
  + **Discord**: An online platform for 24/7 community connection, dialogue, updates, and resources, including "learning pods" [17, 18].
  + **Learning Pods**: Peer-based groups, often regional, for weekly check-ins and discussions related to course content [13, 18].
* **Call to Action**: Participants are encouraged to register for the Google Classroom, attend the leadership lab, join Discord, and start a learning pod immediately, with particular emphasis on sharing the program widely [21-23].

A potential avenue for further analysis could be to examine the specific content and exercises provided within each module, particularly focusing on how the "70-20-10 rule" is practically implemented through the assignments and community engagement tools.
