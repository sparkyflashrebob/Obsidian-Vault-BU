**Slide 1: The Power of Nonviolent Action**

* **Nonviolent action** is a dynamic resource inspired by Gene Sharp's 198 Methods of Nonviolent Action [1].
* It serves as an active technique of struggle, involving deliberate withdrawal of cooperation and defiance [2, 3].
* This approach is used to challenge opposition, learn different strategies, and connect nonviolence theory to real-world examples [1].
* Nonviolent action can lead to shifts in the balance of power and can be continuously refined to better meet human needs [4, 5].

**Slide 2: Three Main Categories of Nonviolent Action**

* Nonviolent action methods are broadly categorized into three main types [6, 7]:
  1. **Protest and Persuasion Tactics**: Actions used to express disapproval, promote solidarity, and signal unjust situations [6, 7].
  2. **Non-cooperation Tactics**: Intentionally disrupting the status quo by refusing to comply with demands or withdrawing support [8, 9].
  3. **Building Alternatives**: Strengthening the capacity and self-reliance of a nonviolent movement and undermining support for authoritarian institutions [8].
* These categories encompass a wide repertoire of tactics, including the 198 methods identified by Gene Sharp [1, 10-12].
* *Visual idea: A three-part infographic or Venn diagram showing the interconnectedness of these categories, perhaps with Gene Sharp's name/image.*

**Slide 3: Protest and Persuasion Tactics in Detail**

* These tactics help people overcome fear, apathy, and acquiescence, promoting solidarity [6].
* **Methods include**:
  + **Formal Statements**: Public speeches (e.g., Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech) [7, 9, 13].
  + **Communications with a Wider Audience**: Slogans, banners, leaflets, and digital campaigns (e.g., teen trolling Donald Trump's rally tickets) [7, 9, 14].
  + **Symbolic Public Acts**: Displays of symbols, protest disrobings, or silent protests (e.g., Portland Timbers/Seattle Sounders fans protesting signage ban) [7, 9, 15].
  + **Drama and Music**: Humorous skits, performances, and singing (e.g., dancers' open-air ballet class) [7, 9, 16].
  + **Processions and Public Assemblies**: Marches, parades, vigils, and protest meetings (e.g., protests against Brazil's president, UK protests against parliament suspension) [7, 9, 17, 18].
* *Visual idea: Icons representing speeches, banners, music notes, protest signs, and marching feet.*

**Slide 4: Strategic Non-Cooperation Tactics**

* **Non-cooperation** is about intentional strategic stubbornness, directly removing an opponent's sources of social, political, and economic power [8].
* **Methods include**:
  + **Ostracism of Persons**: Social boycotts and excommunication (e.g., Turkish bar associations boycotting judicial ceremony) [8, 19, 20].
  + **Noncooperation with Social Events, Customs, and Institutions**: Student strikes and withdrawal from social institutions [8, 21].
  + **Action by Workers, Producers, and Middlemen**: Boycotts and strikes (e.g., Indian restaurants boycotting food-tech apps, Swiss women's pay protest, Irish farmers refusing cattle) [8, 22-24].
  + **Action by Owners and Holders of Financial Resources**: Refusal to sell property, withdrawal of bank deposits, and refusal to pay debts (e.g., VF Corp stopping Brazilian leather use) [8, 25, 26].
  + **Citizens’ Noncooperation with Government**: Boycotts of legislative bodies or elections [8, 27].
  + **Citizens’ Alternatives to Obedience**: Civil disobedience of "illegitimate" laws (e.g., Colin Kaepernick kneeling during the anthem) [28, 29].
* When institutional "pillars" (e.g., workers, businesses) engage in organized non-cooperation, the chances of stopping democratic backsliding significantly increase [30].
* *Visual idea: A broken chain, a fist, or a visual representing withdrawal of support.*

**Slide 5: Building Alternatives for Resilience**

* The purpose of **alternative institutions** is to strengthen a non-violent movement's capacity and self-reliance [8].
* They also undermine popular support for institutions under authoritarian control and can replace harmful or repressive structures [8].
* **Examples include**:
  + **Mutual aid groups** and food cooperatives [8].
  + **Alternative communication systems** (e.g., Naval Academy grads crowdfunding to replace removed books) [8, 30].
  + **Alternative transportation systems** and underground clinics [8].
  + **Systems to accompany targeted populations** like immigrants [8].
* These actions are crucial for sustaining non-cooperation and providing a vision for the democracy being fought for [8].
* *Visual idea: Interconnected hands, a growing plant, or a community network symbol.*

**Slide 6: Principles of Successful Nonviolent Campaigns**

* **Mass Participation**: Successful movements build and sustain participation from a broad range of communities and constituencies [10]. No regime has stayed in power when 3.5% of the population engages in active protest (approximately 11 million Americans) [10].
* **Diverse Tactics**: Drawing on a wide repertoire of non-violent tactics, shaking things up and generating pressure from all sides, creates more opportunities for involvement and leverage [10].
* **Defections and Loyalty Shifts**: Success often involves key institutional pillars (e.g., workers, faith communities, civil servants, businesses, security forces) withdrawing support from authoritarian systems [6, 10].
* **Maintain Nonviolent Discipline**: Staying nonviolent is crucial for effectiveness and maintaining the moral high ground [31-33]. Violence can bolster the legitimacy of the state [32].
* *Visual idea: A large, diverse crowd, a chess board with pieces shifting, or a "shield" representing nonviolent discipline.*

**Slide 7: Collective Action for Democracy**

* Fighting authoritarianism requires building and activating a powerful, cross-society opposition with **mass defiance** [34].
* Organizing with people you know – friends, family, co-workers, neighbors – is a key first step [35, 36].
* Community gatherings foster connections, shared knowledge, and resilience, supporting individuals to take greater risks [37, 38].
* The more people who connect and defy, the safer and stronger the movement becomes [39].
* **We can achieve victory** by acting collectively and committing to strategic non-cooperation [40, 41].
