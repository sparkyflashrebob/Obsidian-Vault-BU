The One Million Rising initiative espouses methods primarily focused on **strategic non-cooperation and mass defiance** to combat authoritarianism [1]. This approach is rooted in academic research and analysis of successful pro-democracy movements worldwide [2-4].

Key methods and guiding principles promoted by One Million Rising include:

* **Building Mass Participation**: The initiative aims to mobilize large numbers of people, striving to reach one million individuals who are prepared to participate in the movement. This goal is informed by research suggesting that no regime has remained in power when 3.5% of the population engages in active protest, which translates to approximately 11 million Americans [3-5]. Achieving this requires "good old-fashioned organizing" to build broad and diverse coalitions that span various ideologies and sectors, finding common ground against tyranny [3].
* **Utilizing a Wide Repertoire of Nonviolent Tactics**: One Million Rising encourages diverse tactics to generate broad pressure [3]. These tactics are categorized into three main areas based on Gene Sharp's work [6-8]:
  + **Protest and Persuasion**: Actions designed to express disapproval, such as marches, rallies, demonstrations, wearing symbols, projecting messages, and educating or recruiting others. These methods help participants overcome fear and apathy, foster solidarity, and signal the presence of an unjust situation [6].
  + **Building Alternatives**: Tactics focused on strengthening the movement's capacity and self-reliance, while simultaneously undermining popular support for institutions under authoritarian control [6].
  + **Non-cooperation**: Considered the most powerful set of tactics, this involves directly removing sources of power from an authoritarian regime by refusing to cooperate or comply with demands or orders [6, 9]. This can manifest at different levels:
    - **Individual and Small Group Non-cooperation**: Most non-cooperation begins on a small scale [10]. Examples include a teacher refusing to remove an "everyone is welcome" poster, a lawyer condemning a law firm's capitulation and initiating a petition, or an individual warning neighbors about immigration enforcement activities [10]. Librarians setting up "band books" stations is another example [11].
    - **Collective and Mass Non-cooperation**: Scaling up individual acts into coordinated efforts across society, leading to significant impacts such as general strikes or large-scale economic boycotts [10, 12].
* **Targeting Institutional Pillars for Defection**: A critical strategy involves inducing loyalty shifts and defections within the key institutional pillars that enable an authoritarian regime [3, 6, 9]. These pillars include workers, faith communities, civil servants, businesses, and security forces [3]. By withholding their knowledge, skills, or backing, individuals within these pillars can make it difficult or impossible for autocrats to maintain power [9, 13]. Examples of this include:
  + **Business Sector**: Business owners refusing to cooperate with authorities (e.g., Dodger Stadium's refusal to allow ICE on its premises) [14]. Consumer boycotts are also crucial, such as the successful boycotts against AeIo Airlines due to its contracts with ICE, the Tesla takedown boycott, and the Target boycott over anti-DEI policies [14, 15]. Activists also encourage local businesses to display "no kings, no ICE" signs [14].
  + **Education Sector**: Refusal of academic institutions to comply with certain orders (e.g., Harvard's initial stance against Trump's directives) and letter campaigns organized by alumni to pressure school administrations [14].
  + **Media Sector**: Collective actions like marches and denunciations targeting media headquarters to protest perceived retaliation or influence content [11].
  + **Economic Pressure**: Utilizing economic leverage against enablers of authoritarianism, such as campaigns against T-Mobile due to its association with Elon Musk's Starlink [11].
* **Building Community Resilience and Connection**: One Million Rising actively counters the authoritarian tactic of isolating people [16, 17]. It emphasizes fostering connections, building community resilience, and supporting individuals in taking greater risks and navigating difficult situations [16, 18]. Hosting community resistance gatherings is a central component, enabling participants to discuss ideas, learn about mass defiance and non-cooperation, and identify local opportunities for action [4, 19, 20].
* **Strategic Planning and Continuous Innovation**: The movement advocates for a "block and build" strategy, which means actively resisting harmful authoritarian actions while simultaneously building relationships, skills, and collective power for a desired democratic future [21, 22]. It acknowledges that not every action will succeed and some may even face backlash, emphasizing the importance of adaptability and ongoing assessment of campaign strategies [23, 24]. The goal is to move from simply raising "awareness" to inspiring tangible "action" through clear objectives and well-defined strategies [25-27].
