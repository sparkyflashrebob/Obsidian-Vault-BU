### Work Plan: Marin DSA Prison and Policing Abolition Committee

Mission Statement: The Marin DSA Prison and Policing Abolition Committee aims to dismantle systems of carceral control and policing, advocating for community-based solutions, transformative justice, and the reallocation of resources towards human needs in Marin County. We believe in a future free from cages and state violence.

##### I. Overarching Goals (2025-2026)

1. Educate and Mobilize: Increase public understanding of abolitionist principles and foster community engagement around alternatives to policing and incarceration in Marin.
2. Advocate for Policy Change: Publicly pressure local government and institutions to adopt policies that reduce reliance on carceral systems and invest in community well-being.
3. Build Coalitions: Strengthen alliances with affected communities, advocacy groups, and progressive organizations working towards similar goals.
4. Support Affected Communities: Provide solidarity and tangible support to individuals and families impacted by the policing and carceral system through advocacy, mutual aid programs and public safety alternatives to policing.

##### II. Strategic Pillars & Key Activities

Pillar 1: Education & Outreach
● Objective: Demystify abolition, challenge carceral narratives, and build a broad base of support.
● Activities: ○ Workshops & Teach-Ins: Organize monthly online/in-person sessions (possibly
in collaboration with Poli Ed Comm) covering topics like: ■ History of policing and incarceration in the US and Marin. ■ Understanding abolitionist frameworks ("defund," "abolish,"
"invest/divest"). ■ Restorative justice and transformative justice principles. ■ Community safety models without police.
○ Reading Groups: Facilitate discussions around key texts on abolition, critical race theory, and prison industrial complex within one agenda item per month.
○ Digital Content Creation: Develop accessible infographics, social media campaigns, and blog posts explaining committee positions and abolitionist concepts.
○ Community Forums: Host open forums to gather input and address community concerns related to policing, public safety, and mutual aid opportunities.
Pillar 2: Policy Advocacy & Campaigning
● Objective: Drive concrete policy changes at the county and municipal levels that attempt to divest from carceral systems and invest in community-led solutions.
● Activities: ○ Research & Policy Proposals: Identify specific local policies (e.g., police
budget allocation, school resource officers, jail expansion plans) ripe for intervention. Develop research-backed proposals for alternatives.
○ Public Comment & Testimony: Regularly attend and provide public comment at County Board of Supervisors, city/town council, school board, and other relevant public meetings.
○ Campaign Development: Launch targeted campaigns around specific demands (e.g., reallocating a percentage of the MCSO/PD budgets to mental health services, ending ICE cooperation, advocating for diversion programs).
○ Candidate Engagement: Engage with local political candidates to assess their stances on abolitionist issues and educate them on committee priorities.
○ "Know Your Rights" Materials: Disseminate information on legal rights when interacting with law enforcement, including immigration officials
Pillar 3: Coalition Building & Solidarity
● Objective: Forge strong, collaborative relationships with allied organizations and communities to amplify impact.
● Activities: ○ Community Trust Building: Seed, nurture and grow foundational relationships
in the Marin communities that have been historically most adversely affected by the policing and prison system.
○ Regular Check-ins: Establish consistent communication and joint planning with allied established efforts such as ACLU of Northern California, Marin/Bay Area racial justice groups, mental health advocates, NLG-SF and housing rights/food justice organizations.
○ Joint Actions: Participate in and co-organize rallies, demonstrations, and public events with coalition partners.
○ Mutual Aid Exploration: Investigate and support local mutual aid efforts that address community needs outside of state intervention.
○ Inter-Committee Collaboration: Coordinate with other Marin DSA committees on intersecting issues.
Pillar 4: Support & Awareness for Affected Communities
● Objective: Center the experiences of those most impacted by the carceral system and provide tangible support.
● Activities:
○ Storytelling Initiatives: Create platforms (e.g., testimonials, written narratives) for community members to share their experiences with policing and incarceration.
○ Bail Fund Support: Explore ways to support local bail funds or initiatives aimed at pretrial freedom.
○ Resource Sharing: Compile and disseminate information on legal aid, re-entry services, and other support resources for formerly incarcerated individuals and their families.
○ Visibility Campaigns: Organize events or campaigns to highlight the disproportionate impact of policing on specific communities in Marin.

##### III. Committee Structure & Operations

● Meetings: Weekly committee meetings, Thursday at 7:30pm to plan, coordinate, and debrief activities.
● Roles/Subcommittees (as needed): ○ Research & Policy Subcommittee: Focus on data collection, policy analysis,
and proposal drafting. ○ Outreach & Education Subcommittee: Develop content, organize events, and
manage communications. ○ Campaigns & Direct Action Subcommittee: Plan and execute advocacy
campaigns and public actions. ● Communication: Utilize a dedicated committee communication channel for ongoing
discussion and coordination. ● Reporting: Regular updates to the Marin DSA Coordinating Committee.

##### IV. Timeline & Key Milestones

Q3 2025 (July - September): Foundation & Education Focus
● Recruit new committee members, those already engaged in or drawn to abolition work and study as well those from communities most adversely impacted by the harms of the carceral system who also are currently underrepresented in Marin DSA.
● Conduct internal committee training on abolitionist principles. ● Host first public "Abolition 101" workshop. ● Gather Data: Budgets, Stops/Arrests, Referred to prosecution/Convictions, Clearance
Rates, “Crime” data, Call Logs ● Identify 1-2 key local policies for initial focus. ● Begin mapping local allied organizations and mutual aid support opportunities
Q4 2025 (October - December): Policy & Coalition Building
● Launch first targeted campaign ● Look to fill gaps in current mutual aid efforts with new project ● Present initial policy proposals at a public meeting
● Establish formal communication channels with 2-3 key coalition partners. ● Host a community forum on public safety alternatives.
Q1 2026 (January - March): Sustained Advocacy & Support
● Organize a joint action with a coalition partner. ● Host a "Know Your Rights" workshop. ● Monitor progress on targeted policy changes. ● Begin collecting community impact stories. ● Prepare for 2026 budget season and seed through comments at public meetings
Q2 2026 (April - June): Evaluation & Expansion
● Launch carceral budget reduction campaign ● Start to evaluate effectiveness of campaigns and educational efforts. ● Identify new policy targets or deepen existing campaigns. ● Expand outreach to new communities. ● Plan for year two initiatives.

##### V. Resources Needed

● Volunteers: Dedicated committee members for research, organizing, outreach, and advocacy.
● Funding (minimal): For event space, printing materials, digital tools ● Expertise: Potential need for legal advice, policy experts, or community organizers for
guidance.

##### VI. Metrics for Success

● Number of attendees at educational events. ● Engagement metrics on digital content (shares, views). ● Number of public comments/testimonies delivered. ● Formation of new coalition partnerships. ● Tangible policy changes or shifts in public discourse. ● Qualitative feedback from community members and partners.
This work plan is a living document and will be reviewed and updated regularly based on committee capacity, local political developments, and community needs.
