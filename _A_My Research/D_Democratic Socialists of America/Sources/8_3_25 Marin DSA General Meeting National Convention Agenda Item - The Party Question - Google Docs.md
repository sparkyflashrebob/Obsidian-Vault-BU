Introduction ● It’s been a longtime strategic imperative of socialist movements all over the world to
develop labor parties or socialist parties in their respective countries as vehicles for developing class consciousness and delivering reforms that materially benefit workers, which in turn help to raise working-class expectations, shift power from capital to labor, and expand the socialist movement’s organized base of support. These are all essential conditions for achieving socialism.
● The US left is at a fundamental political disadvantage in that it does not have, nor has it ever had, a socialist or labor party. Instead, we have been stuck with the Democrats.
● The Democratic Party, while changing significantly for both good and ill over the last century, is a capitalist party, representing the interests of broad sections of the capitalist class, including finance, tech, healthcare, real estate, defense, and more. While it contains many progressive and working-class constituencies within its coalition (union workers, feminists, environmentalists, etc), it routinely favors the priorities of its capitalist funders over and above its voter base.
○ And in recent years, the party has proactively sought to reengineer its voting base to trade away lower-income and less educated working-class voters (its historical New Deal base) for more affluent and highly-educated professional voters from the suburbs.
● This all has led many socialists and leftists to conclude that we need an independent working-class party of our own.
● The problem is that the US electoral system is structured to prevent new or minor parties from ever challenging the two major established parties.
Barriers to Entry ● First, you have the ballot line issue. In the US, it is extremely difficult for a minor party to
qualify to appear on the ballot. In most democracies, this process is as simple as filing some paperwork, paying a modest deposit, and/or gathering maybe a few hundred signatures. In the US, it often means gathering tens of thousands of signatures for each
*candidate in each election, sometimes within a narrow window of time, then defending your ballot line access from legal challenges. And then even if you do qualify to get on the ballot, the election rules can be changed at the drop of a hat by state legislators from the two major parties who have no interest in seeing new parties become competitive.*
*● Second, we have the spoiler issue. In the US, we have first-past-the-post, winner-take-all elections , instead of some form of proportional representation. What that means is that whatever candidate wins an outright majority or plurality in any given election will be the sole representative of that district or state. Minor parties that win 2%, 5%, or even 10-20% of the vote win absolutely no representation in their legislative body. Instead, they end up taking votes away from the major party more closely aligned with them and helping the major party they most oppose. This is what we call the spoiler effect, and it's a structural feature of the US electoral system.*
Realignment vs. Clean Break ● These structural barriers to forming a new party have led many leftists to pursue a
strategy of realignment , that is, working within the Democratic Party to transform or takeover the party so that it becomes the worker’s party we need.
○ Realignment proponents’ greatest victory was pushing out the racist southern Dixiecrats from the party following the civil rights era. But despite this historic accomplishment, progressives and socialists within the party have never gotten close to transforming the Democrats into a true workers party. In fact, since the 1990s and the emergence of Clinton-era so-called “New Democrats,” the party establishment has consistently moved further and further to the right, becoming increasingly dependent on and responsive to Wall Street, Big Pharma, Big Tech, and other segments of the capitalist class.
● The historical alternative to an electoral strategy of realignment has been called the clean break strategy. Advocates of a clean break call for socialists and other leftists to completely and immediately break with the Democrats, refusing to support their candidates, and instead running our own candidates on our ballot line.
○ The problem with the clean break is that the structural barriers to competing in our electoral system as a new party inevitably consign these parties to electoral irrelevance. Third parties like the Green Party spend nearly all their organizing capacity working to get on the ballot and combatting challenges to their ballot access in the courts, only to win 1-2% of the vote and get blamed as spoilers.
The Dirty Break ● Luckily, there is a third option that avoids the strategic pitfalls of both the realignment and
clean break strategies. This is the dirty break strategy. ● The dirty break strategy calls for strategically and opportunistically using the Democratic
Party ballot line to run democratic socialists in Democratic primaries in order to wage class-struggle campaigns and elect socialists into office.
*○ But while these campaigns run within Democratic primaries and their candidates therefore run as Democrats, the goal is not to build or strengthen the Democratic Party. Rather class-struggle campaigns are designed to expose and heighten the*
contradictions between the party’s capitalist donors and its working-class base and, perhaps most importantly, to build independent working-class organization and electoral infrastructure that can be used on future races to further grow our strength.
● The idea is that we could then amass an army of socialist elected officials, backed by a powerful, independent electoral organization with a mass working-class base. At that point, we could definitively break with the Democrats and realistically run on our ballot line without acting as a spoiler or succumbing to ballot eligibility barriers. Or perhaps just as likely, we may get pushed out of the Democratic Party and be forced to run on our ballot line once we pose too great a threat to capitalist interests within the party.
● DISCUSSION: ○ Why does the working class need a party of its own? ○ Do you agree that the realignment and clean break strategies won’t work? ○ What are some challenges in implementing the dirty break?
DSA as the Proto-Party ● This all raises the question: What is the role of DSA in the dirty break strategy? Are
we seeking to become the workers party itself? Or would we be a central player in a broader formation that becomes the workers party?
● Many in DSA argue that we should play the role of a party surrogate , an independent party-like organization that shares similar structures and fulfills similar functions as those of a traditional political party. DSA already recruits and runs candidates for office; we do political education; we have a democratically determined platform and a democratically elected leadership. What we lack is a ballot line of our own and a mass working-class base.
● This then brings us to the central party question now facing DSA: How do we move towards the formation of a mass-based independent workers party? There are three different resolutions being presented at our national convention that articulate three different answers to this question.
○ R04: DSA launches a new socialist party in the next four years ○ R07: DSA codifies its role as a the party-surrogate while planning to become a
true socialist party in the future ○ R33: DSA coheres a broad Left-Labor coalition to run a socialist for president in
2028 and lay the foundations for a future workers party
Convention Resolutions ● R04: For a Socialist Party in Years, Not Decades
*○ Author Summary: This resolution sets DSA on a course to build a socialist party through constructing and strengthening local pre-party formations, developing the left wing of the labor movement, launching a socialist slate of candidates, and bringing together aligned organizations in shared struggle.*
*● R07: Principles for Party-Building ○ Author Summary: Principles for Party-Building are a set of guidelines for the task*
set out by the 2025 convention’s theme - preparing for a decade of party building.
*Almost everyone in DSA says they want a party, but most of us mean different things. These ten principles define DSA’s approach as a party surrogate with real political independence, and the values we must hold in establishing a mass socialist party.*
*● R33: Unite Labor & the Left to Run a Socialist For President and Build the Party ○ Author Summary: DSA needs to bring together labor unions and other mass*
*organizations into a left-labor coalition to run a Presidential candidate in the 2028 Democratic primary. This coalition should also run a national slate of down-ballot candidates in the 2026 and 2028 elections on a common pro-labor platform. This coalition can lay the foundations for a future worker’s party.*
● DISCUSSION: ○ How do these resolutions differ in their approach to building a mass independent
party? Do they all share this end goal? ○ What is the difference between calling for a socialist party vs. a left-labor or
working-class party? ○ How do the resolutions relate to the question of the ballot line?
Addendum: A Note on US Political Parties ● It’s important to note that the US does not have real political parties as they are typically
understood in other advanced capitalist democracies across the world. ● In most democracies, parties consist of dues-paying members, who democratically
determine the party’s platform and then recruit candidates who are accountable to party members to run on and implement that platform.
● In the US, our parties have no members at all, their platforms are entirely determined by their presidential nominee and have little bearing on how they govern, and their candidates are typically self-selecting.
● In other words, US parties act more like shallow fundraising and consulting networks for candidates, not ideological or democratic membership organizations.
