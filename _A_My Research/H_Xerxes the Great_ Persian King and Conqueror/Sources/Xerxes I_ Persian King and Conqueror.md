King Xerxes I of Persia, also known as Xerxes the Great, was the fourth king of the Achaemenid Empire from 486 to 465 BC. Son of Darius I and Atossa, he is renowned for his massive, though ultimately unsuccessful, invasion of Greece in 480 BC. His reign was also marked by large-scale building projects, such as at Persepolis, and internal rebellions in Egypt and Babylon. Xerxes was assassinated by members of his court in 465 BC, succeeded by his son, Artaxerxes I. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]

Key Facts [1, 2, 3, 4]

• Reign: 486–465 BC

• Predecessor: Darius I

• Successor: Artaxerxes I

• Family: Son of Darius I and Atossa, daughter of Cyrus the Great

• Title: King of Kings (Shahanshah) of the Achaemenid Empire

Major Events and Accomplishments

• Second Persian invasion of Greece (480 BC): Xerxes led a massive invasion of Greece in an attempt to conquer it, but his forces were defeated at battles like Salamis and Plataea, leading to the Persian withdrawal. [4, 5, 7, 8]

• Suppression of revolts: Upon becoming king, he put down revolts in Egypt and Babylon. [6]

• Building projects: He continued extensive building at Persepolis, including finishing his father's palace and undertaking the construction of the Hall of a Hundred Columns. [7, 9]

• Religious centralization: His reign saw efforts toward religious centralization, enforcing the worship of Ahura Mazda and the rejection of "Daivas" (false gods or demons). [6, 10]

End of Reign

• Xerxes I was assassinated by members of his court, including his guard commander, Artabanus, in 465 BC. [6, 9]

• His son, Artaxerxes I, then ascended to the throne. [2]
