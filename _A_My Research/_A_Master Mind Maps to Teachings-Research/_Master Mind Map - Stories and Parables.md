  * *Nekkhamma* (Renunciation - *Hatthipāla Jātaka*)

  * *Viriya* (Energy - *Mahājanaka Jātaka*)

  * *Sacca* (Truthfulness - *Mahāsutasoma Jātaka*)

  * *Mettā* (Loving-Kindness - *Ekarāja Jātaka*)

## 3. Dhammapada Origin Narratives (*Dhp-A*)
* **Kisāgotamī & The Mustard Seed**: Awakening to the universal nature of mortality.
* **Paṭācārā**: Moving from psychotic bereavement to Arahantship through direct grounding.
* **Cakkhupāla**: The blind monk whose physical eyes collapsed from refusing to lie down during meditation, while his spiritual eyes opened into liberation.

## 4. Living Forest Tradition Parables & Similes of Luang Por Chah
* **Dedicated Master Compendium Note**: [[_Ajahn Chah stories_|Stories, Parables, and Recollections of Ajahn Chah (28 Master Stories) ↗]]
* **Direct Historical Dharma Talk Reports**:
  * [[Report - Ajahn Chah - Evening Dharma Talk (Insight Meditation Society 1979-06-16)|Evening Dharma Talk at IMS (1979-06-16) — The Bell, the Water Glass, and Disturbing the Car ↗]]
  * [[Report - Ajahn Chah - Discussion and QA (Insight Meditation Society 1979-06-16)|Discussion & Q&A at IMS (1979-06-16) — Dying Before Dying & Taming the Stubborn Horse ↗]]

### Core Living Similes of Luang Por Chah
1. **The Glass is Already Broken (*Anicca / Non-Attachment*)**: Holding things with precious care while knowing their inherent dissolution; when the glass shatters, the heart says *"Of course."*
2. **Is That Boulder Heavy? (*Upādāna / Letting Go*)**: Looking at a giant jungle rock: *"Is it heavy? Not if you don't pick it up."*
3. **Still, Flowing Water (*Samatha & Vipassanā Together*)**: Stillness within, yet fully cognizant of changing conditions without getting swept away.
4. **Disturbing the Car (*Sensory Contact & Projection*)**: The car traveling on the road is just being a car; it is our ear and mind reaching out to attack the sound that creates suffering. Let cars be cars, planes be planes, and ourselves be ourselves.
5. **The Bell, the Glass & the Alms Bowl (*Paññatti & Relative Designation*)**: An object is neither big nor small in its own nature (*sabhāva*); our preferences and comparative framing construct the illusion of magnitude.
6. **Bailing the Water Drum (*Steady Purification*)**: Scooping out dirty water with a mindful ladle each day, while ensuring we do not pour buckets of sensual craving back in through heedlessness.
7. **Taming the Stubborn Horse (*Damma & Internal Refuge*)**: Withholding the sweet grass of comfort when the mind rebels; cultivating internal spiritual power (*bala*) rather than living on borrowed inspiration from teachers or retreats.
8. **Dying Before Dying (*Dissolution of Doubt & Identity-View*)**: True death is not the biological heart stopping, but the complete cessation of doubt (*vicikicchā*) and identity-view (*sakkāya-diṭṭhi*), seeing the five aggregates as an ownerless, empty flow.
