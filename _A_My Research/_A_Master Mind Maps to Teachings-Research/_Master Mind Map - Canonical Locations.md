## 1. Sāvatthī (Kosala Capital)
* **Jetavana Grove**: Purchased by Anāthapiṇḍika by layering gold coins across the soil; the primary monsoon residence where the Buddha spent 19 rainy seasons.
* **Pubbārāma**: The "Eastern Park" with its Migāramātā Palace, gifted by Visākhā for large monastic assemblies.
* **Andhavana**: The dense "Dark Forest" outside the city where monks and nuns sought solitary day-retreats and meditative seclusion.

---

## 2. Rājagaha (Magadha Capital)
* **Veḷuvana (Bamboo Grove)**: Gifted by King Bimbisāra; protected by natural hills and shaded by towering bamboo.
* **Gijjhakūṭa (Vulture's Peak)**: The rocky precipice where the Buddha retreated for deep stillness; the setting of Devadatta's boulder assassination attempt.
* **Jīvakambavana**: The royal physician Jīvaka's tranquil mango grove used for medical convalescence and ethical discourses (*MN 55*).

---

## 3. Vesālī (Vajji Confederacy)
* **Mahāvana Kūṭāgārasālā**: The "Peaked Hall" monastery situated in the great natural forest extending toward the Himalayas.
* **Historical Significance**: The site where Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī arrived on foot with swollen feet to request monastic ordination for women.

---

## 4. Kosambī (Vatsa Kingdom)
* **Ghositārāma**: Donated by the wealthy banker Ghosita.
* **The Reconciliation Container**: The historical site of the heated monastic dispute over minor vinaya infractions; the Buddha walked away into the forest to live with the solitary elephant, teaching that solitude is superior to living with stubborn disputants (`[[Kosambiya_Sutta_MN_48_Discourse_at_Kosambi]]`).

---

## 5. Nādika & Gosiṅga Sal Wood
* **The Archetype of Concord**: The forest park where Anuruddha, Nandiya, and Kimbila lived "different in body, but one in mind," laying down personal self-will to support one another (`[[Culagosinga_Sutta_MN_31_Living_in_Concord_Blending_Like_Milk_and_Water]]`).
