## 1. The 3 Universal Truths (*Tilakkhaṇa*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: SN 22.59 (*Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta*), Dhp 277–279, SN 22.15 (*Yadanicca Sutta*).
* **Dedicated In-Depth Research Reports**:
  * 🌊 **[[Universal_Truth_1_Anicca_Impermanence_and_the_Flow_of_Experience|Universal Truth 1: Anicca — Impermanence & the Flow of Experience]]**
  * 🎯 **[[Universal_Truth_2_Dukkha_The_Architecture_of_Stress_and_Unsatisfactoriness|Universal Truth 2: Dukkha — The Architecture of Stress & the Two Arrows]]**
  * 🕊️ **[[Universal_Truth_3_Anatta_Non_Self_and_the_Release_of_Ownership|Universal Truth 3: Anattā — Non-Self & the Release of Ownership]]**
* **1. Impermanence (*Anicca*)**: Every conditioned physical and mental phenomenon rises, shifts, and dissolves (*uppāda-vaya*). Reaching non-existence after having come to be.
* **2. Unsatisfactoriness / Stress (*Dukkha*)**: The three tiers (*dukkha-dukkha*, *vipariṇāma-dukkha*, *saṅkhāra-dukkha*). Sensory contact is the First Arrow; added aversion and storytelling is the Second Arrow (*dukkha* proper).
* **3. Not-Self / Insubstantiality (*Anattā*)**: Insusceptibility to absolute mastery (*avasavattana*). What is impermanent and subject to stress cannot rightfully be clutched as *"This is mine, this I am, this is my self."*
* **Somatic Inquiry**: Directly observing changing sensations in the body (tightness in the belly, breath rhythm) as impersonal biological processes rather than personal failure.

## 2. The Four Noble Truths (*Cattāri Ariyasaccāni*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: SN 56.11 (*Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*), MN 141 (*Saccavibhaṅga Sutta*).
* **The Four Active Duties**:
  * **Truth 1: Dukkha (Suffering / Friction)** $\rightarrow$ **To be fully understood** (*Pariññeyya*). Met with open somatic inquiry rather than denial or avoidance.
  * **Truth 2: Samudaya (Origin: Craving / Taṇhā)** $\rightarrow$ **To be abandoned** (*Pahātabba*). Relinquishing the compulsive thirst for sensuality (*kāma*), becoming (*bhava*), and non-becoming (*vibhava*).
  * **Truth 3: Nirodha (Cessation: Nibbāna)** $\rightarrow$ **To be realized** (*Sacchikātabba*). Experiencing the unconditioned stillness when craving ceases.
  * **Truth 4: Magga (The Eightfold Path)** $\rightarrow$ **To be cultivated** (*Bhāvetabba*). Living the integrated middle path.

---

## 3. The Eightfold Path (*Ariya Aṭṭhaṅgika Magga*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: MN 117 (*Mahācattārīsaka Sutta*), DN 22 (*Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta*).
* **The Threefold Training (*Tisikkhā*)**:
  * **Wisdom (*Paññā*)**: Right View (*Sammā-diṭṭhi*), Right Intention (*Sammā-saṅkappa*).
  * **Ethics (*Sīla*)**: Right Speech (*Sammā-vācā*), Right Action (*Sammā-kammanta*), Right Livelihood (*Sammā-ājīva*).
  * **Stillness (*Samādhi*)**: Right Effort (*Sammā-vāyāma*), Right Mindfulness (*Sammā-sati*), Right Concentration (*Sammā-samādhi*).

---

## 4. Precepts & Vinaya (*Sīla & Saṃvara*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: AN 8.39 (*Abhisanda Sutta*), Vinaya Suttavibhaṅga.
* **The Ethical Container**:
  * **Five Precepts (*Pañcasīla*)**: Non-harming, non-stealing, sexual integrity, truthful speech, sobriety.
  * **Eight Precepts (*Aṭṭhaṅgasīla*)**: Monastic simplicity, sense-moderation on Uposatha days.
  * **Vinaya Code (*Pātimokkha*)**: Monastic community covenants safeguarding concord and peace.
* **Contemplative Harvest**: Ethical clarity produces **non-remorse (*avippaṭisāra*)**, settling bodily agitation and allowing meditation to flourish.

---

## 5. The 3 Poisons (*Tīṇi Akusalamūlāni*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: AN 3.68 (*Mūla Sutta*), MN 7 (*Vatthūpama Sutta*).
* **The Root Defilements & Antidotes**:
  * **Greed / Passion (*Lobha / Rāga*)**: The pull to consume and possess. *Antidotes*: Generosity (*dāna*), contentment (*santuṭṭhi*), contemplation of impermanence.
  * **Hatred / Aversion (*Dosa*)**: The push away, irritation, hostility. *Antidotes*: Loving-kindness (*mettā*), compassion (*karuṇā*), patience (*khanti*).
  * **Delusion / Ignorance (*Moha / Avijjā*)**: Lack of clear awareness. *Antidotes*: Wise attention (*yoniso manasikāra*), penetrative inquiry (*paññā*).

---

## 6. Karma & Volition (*Kamma & Vipāka*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: AN 6.63 (*"Intention, monks, is what I call karma"*), MN 135.
* **Core Principles**:
  * **Intention as Action (*Cetanā*)**: Volitional choices plant seeds in consciousness that ripen into experiential fruit (*vipāka*).
  * **Non-Fatalism**: Past momentum conditions the present, but present awareness determines current action.
  * **The Two Arrows (*Sallatha Sutta*, SN 36.6)**: Experiencing unavoidable raw sensory impact (First Arrow) without shooting the reactive arrow of blame or despair (Second Arrow).

---

## 7. Meditation (*Bhāvanā & Samādhi*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: MN 10 (*Satipaṭṭhāna*), MN 118 (*Ānāpānasati*), MN 128 (*Upakkilesa*).
* **The Core Meditative Arena**:
  * **Samatha & Vipassanā**: Gathering stillness to unify the mind; deploying insight to dissolve clinging.
  * **The 4 Foundations of Mindfulness**: Body (*Kāya*), Feelings (*Vedanā*), Mind-States (*Citta*), Reality Principles (*Dhammā*).
  * **The 5 Hindrances (*Nīvaraṇas*)**: Sensual desire, ill-will, dullness/sloth, restlessness/remorse, doubt.
  * **The 4 Jhānas**: Progressive meditative absorptions culminating in unified, luminous equanimity.

---

## 8. The Brahmavihāras (*Cattāro Brahmavihārā*)
* **Canonical Foundation**: MN 55 (*Jīvaka Sutta*), DN 13 (*Tevijja Sutta*).
* **The Four Boundless Hearts**:
  * **1. Mettā (Loving-Kindness)**: Universal goodwill wishing genuine welfare for all beings.
  * **2. Karuṇā (Compassion)**: The tender quivering of the heart when meeting suffering.
  * **3. Muditā (Appreciative Joy)**: Unjealous delight in the happiness and virtue of others.
  * **4. Upekkhā (Equanimity)**: Calm, balanced neutrality rooted in understanding conditionality.

---

## 9. The 10 Fetters (*Dasa Saṃyojanāni*) & Stages of Awakening
* **Canonical Foundation**: MN 64 (*Mahāmāluṅkya Sutta*), SN 45.179.
* **The Progressive Stages of Awakening**:
  * **Lower 5 Fetters (*Orambhāgiya*)**:
    1. *Sakkāya-diṭṭhi* (Personality belief / separate self)
    2. *Vicikicchā* (Skeptical doubt)
    3. *Sīlabbata-parāmāsa* (Attachment to rites and rituals)
    4. *Kāmarāga* (Sensual craving)
    5. *Byāpāda* (Ill-will / resentment)
    * **Stream-Entry (*Sotāpanna*)**: Completely severs 1–3.
    * **Once-Returner (*Sakadāgāmī*)**: Significantly weakens 4–5.
    * **Non-Returner (*Anāgāmī*)**: Completely severs 4–5.
  * **Higher 5 Fetters (*Uddhambhāgiya*)**:
    6. *Rūparāga* (Craving for fine-material jhānas/rebirth)
    7. *Arūparāga* (Craving for formless realms)
    8. *Māna* (Conceit / comparison)
    9. *Uddhacca* (Subtle restlessness)
    10. *Avijjā* (Fundamental ignorance)
    * **Arahantship**: Fully destroys 6–10, realizing unshakeable liberation (*Akuppā Cetovimutti*).
