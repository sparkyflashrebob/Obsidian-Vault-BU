Byzantine Empire

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronoia#cite\_note-fhwgr-1

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism
of the same period.
The institution
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pronoia&action=edit&section=1
was a grant that temporarily transferred imperial fiscal rights to an individual or institution. These rights were most commonly taxes or incomes from cultivated lands, but they could also be other income streams such as water and fishing rights, customs collection, etc. and the various rights to a specific piece of geography could be granted to separate individuals. Grants were for a set period, usually lifetime, and revokable at will by the emperor. When institutions, usually monasteries, received grants they were effectively in perpetuity since the institutions were ongoing. Grants were not transferable or (excluding certain exceptional cases late in the institution) hereditary; a
gave the grantee possession, not ownership, which remained imperial.

By the 11th century, Byzantine

In the late 11th century
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius\_I

Constantinople

granted by Alexius, however, were to members of his own (
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comnenus

A praktika also recorded the duties owed by the pronoiar to the emperor. If necessary, the emperor could request military service, although the pronoiar could not force his taxpayers to join him. Pronoiars were often reluctant to give military service if they lived a prosperous life on their grant, and they had some autonomy if they chose not to serve. If they could gain the support of their taxpayers, they could lead rebellions against the empire, but these were not as dangerous as rebellions in the capital, which Alexius' system could now more successfully avoid. Recruiting pronoiars to form an army helped unite the remnants of the empire after 1261. However, by this time, there were only a few thousand pronoiars, and although they paid for their own expenses, the emperors could not afford a full army or navy to strengthen the empire's defenses. The impoverished empire had very little tax revenue, and pronoiars began to extract rents from the paroikoi, turning back to the old Thema system.
