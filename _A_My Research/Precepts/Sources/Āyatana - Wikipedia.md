In [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), *āyatana* ( [Pāli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81li); [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit): आयतन) is a "center of experience" or "mental home," [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20212-1) which create one's experience. The related term *saḷāyatana* (Pāli; Skt. *ṣaḍāyatana* ) refers to six cognitive functions, namely sight, hearing, smelling, tasting, body-cognition, and mind-cognition. [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20212-1)
*Āyatana* may refer to both ordinary experience and the chain of processes leading to bondage, as to awakened experience centered in detachment and meditative accomplishment. The Buddhist path aims to relocate one from the ordinary, sensual centers of experience to the "mental home" of the purified, liberated awareness of the [jhanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhyana_in_Buddhism). [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20213-2)
Traditionally, the term *āyatana* is translated as "sense base", "sense-media" or "sense sphere," [\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-sense-base-3) due to the influence of later commentators like [Buddhaghosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhaghosa). [\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20205-4) The *saḷāyatana* are traditionally understood as referring to the five senses and the mind. [\[b\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-mano-5)

#### Etymology

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=1)]
*Āyatana* ( [Pāli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81li); [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit): आयतन) is a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism) term that does not have a single definition or meaning. [\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAungRhys_Davids1910183-6) The standard PTS Pāli-English Dictionary by Davids & Stede (1921) gives the following meanings of *āyatana* : [\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20201-7)
1. . stretch, extent, reach, compass, region; sphere, locus, place, spot; position, occasion
1. . exertion, doing, working, practice, performance
1. . sphere of perception or sense in general, object of thought, sense-organ & object; relation, order
While *āyatana* is usually translated as "base" or "sphere," [\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20211-8) or more specifically as "sense field," [\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20211-8) "sense base", "sense-media" or "sense sphere," [\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-sense-base-3) according to Ellis, "these are inadequate translations because they are based on later Buddhist traditions and commentarial literature and not on an historical understanding of the term." [\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20211-8)
In Vedic literature *āyatana* is "used for a regular place, position, etc. occupied by a person." [\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20211-8) In some Upanishads it refers to a "dwelling place" or "resort," or a "resting place for the mind," indicating that *āyatana* means "the place in which experience happens" or a "center of experience." According to Ellis, "center of experience" or "mental home" is a more adequate interpretation than "base" or "sphere." [\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20212-3-9)
Ellis notes that *āyatana* in the suttas most commonly appears in compound form, namely *saḷāyatana* or *cha phassāyatanā* , the "six āyatanas of sensual experience." According to Ellis, "This context is so dominant that translators like Bodhi and Walshe translate 'sense bases' even if the Pāli texts only mentions *āyatana* , and not *saḷāyatana* ." [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20212-1)
Ellis further notes that *saḷāyatana* is traditionally interpreted anatomically, and understood as referring to the five senses and the mind. Yet, according to Olivelle, *saḷāyatana* refers instead to cognitive functions, [\[c\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-Olivelle-10) and is therefore understood by Ellis as referring to sight, hearing, smelling, tasting, body-cognition, and mind-cognition. [\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20212-1)

#### In the Pali Canon

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=2)]
Throughout the [Pali Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali_Canon), the *saḷāyatana* are referenced in hundreds of discourses. [\[d\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-11) In these diverse discourses, the sense bases are integrated in various mnemonic lists.

##### Internal and external *āyatana*

The *āyatana* are further refined as six internal *āyatana* [\[e\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-13) and six corresponding external *āyatana* . [\[f\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-15) Together they form: [\[g\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-Sa%E1%B8%B7%C4%81yatana-16)
\* sight ('eye') and [visible objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupa_(Buddhism)) [\[h\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-17)
\* hearing ('ear') and sound
\* smelling ('nose') and odor
\* tasting ('tongue') and taste
\* body-cognition ('body') and touch
\* mind-cognition ('mind') [\[b\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-mano-5) and [dharmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma_(Buddhism)) (mental objects) [\[i\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-18)
The *saḷāyatana* are related to the *indriya* , the five senses and the mind; the *indriya* become *saḷāyatana* when they are distorted by a defiled mind. [\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-19) [ *bettersourceneeded* ] *Indriya* also refers to the five spiritual facultues, which contribute to an awakened state of mind.

##### Five skandhas

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=4)]
Based on these six pairs of *āyatana* , a number of mental factors arise, as described in the [five skandhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_skandhas). Thus, for instance, when the auditive cognitive function ('the ear') is triggered by sound, the associated consciousness (Pali: *viññāṇa* ) arises. With the presence of these three elements ( *dhātu* ) – hearing function, sound and hearing function-related consciousness – "contact" ( *phassa* ) arises, which in turn is apprehended as a pleasant or unpleasant or neutral "feeling" or "sensation" ( *vedanā* ). With feeling, "craving" ( *taṇhā* ) (or aversion) arises. (See Figure 1.)
Such an enumeration can be found, for instance, in the "Six Sextets" discourse ( *Chachakka Sutta* , [MN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majjhima_Nikaya) 148), where the "six sextets" (six sense organs, six sense objects, six sense-specific types of consciousness, six sense-specific types of contact, six sense-specific types of sensation and six sense-specific types of craving) are examined and found to be [empty of self](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatta). [\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-20)

The *saḷāyatana* are included in the [Twelve Nidanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Nidanas), a list compiled of several sublists including the five skandhas, which describes the process of becoming. [\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-21)

##### "The All"

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=5)]
In a discourse entitled, "The All" ( [SN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyutta_Nikaya) 35.23), the Buddha states that there is no "all" outside of the six pairs of the *saḷāyatana* . [\[j\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-22) In the next codified discourse (SN 35.24), the Buddha elaborates that the All includes the first five aforementioned sextets (sense organs, objects, consciousness, contact and sensations). [\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-23) References to the All can be found in a number of subsequent discourses. [\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-24) In addition, the [Abhidhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhidhamma) and post-canonical Pali literature further conceptualize the *saḷāyatana* as a means for classifying *all* factors of existence. [\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-25)

##### "Aflame with lust, hate and delusion"

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=6)]
In "The Vipers" discourse ( *Asivisa Sutta* , [SN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyutta_Nikaya) 35.197), [the Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buddha) likens the internal *saḷāyatana* to an "empty village" and the external *saḷāyatana* to "village-plundering bandits." Using this metaphor, the Buddha characterizes the "empty" [\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-26) sense organs as being "attacked by agreeable & disagreeable" sense objects. [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-27)
the contact between the senses and the sense objects
gives rise to fleeting perceptions of happiness and distress.
Bhagavad Gita 2 : 14 [\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-28)
Elsewhere in the same collection of discourses ( [SN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyutta_Nikaya) 35.191), the Buddha's Great [Disciple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sravaka) [Sariputta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sariputta) clarifies that the actual [suffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukkha) associated with sense organs and sense objects is not *inherent* to these *saḷāyatana* but is due to the ["fetters"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetter_(Buddhism)) (here identified as "desire and lust") that arise when there is contact between a sense organ and sense object. [\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-29)
In the " [Fire Sermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Sermon)" ( *Adittapariyaya Sutta* , [SN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyutta_Nikaya) 35.28), delivered several months after the Buddha's [awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi), the Buddha describes all *saḷāyatana* and related mental processes in the following manner:
"Monks, the All is aflame. What All is aflame? The eye is aflame. [Forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupa_(Buddhism)) are aflame. [Consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnana) at the eye is aflame. [Contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phassa) at the eye is aflame. And whatever there is that arises in dependence on contact at the eye – experienced as [pleasure, pain or neither-pleasure-nor-pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedana) – that too is aflame. Aflame with what? Aflame with the fire of [passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilesa), the fire of aversion, the fire of delusion. Aflame, I tell you, with [birth, aging & death, with sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukkha)." [\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-30)

##### Liberation

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=7)]
The Buddha taught that, in order to escape the dangers of the *saḷāyatana* , one must be able to apprehend the *saḷāyatana* without [defilement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilesa). In "Abandoning the Fetters" ( [SN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyutta_Nikaya) 35.54), the Buddha states that one abandons the fetters "when one knows and sees ... as [impermanent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impermanence)" (Pali: *anicca* ) the [ *saḷāyatana* ], objects, sense- [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnana), [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spar%C5%9Ba) and [sensations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedana). [\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-31) Similarly, in "Uprooting the Fetters" (SN 35.55), the Buddha states that one uproots the fetters "when one knows and sees ... as [nonself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatta)" ( *anatta* ) the aforementioned five sextets. [\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-32)
To foster this type of penetrative knowing and seeing and the resultant release from suffering, in the [Satipatthana Sutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satipatthana_Sutta) ( [MN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majjhima_Nikaya) 10) the Buddha instructs monks to meditate on the *saḷāyatana* and the dependently arising fetters as follows:
"How, O [bhikkhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhikkhus), does a bhikkhu live contemplating mental object in the mental objects of the six internal and the six external sense-bases?
"Here, O bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands the eye and material forms and the fetter that arises dependent on both (eye and forms); he understands how the arising of the non-arisen fetter comes to be; he understands how the abandoning of the arisen fetter comes to be; and he understands how the non-arising in the future of the abandoned fetter comes to be. [ *In a similar manner:* ] He understands the ear and sounds ... the organ of smell and odors ... the organ of taste and flavors ... the organ of touch and tactual objects ... the consciousness and mental objects....
"Thus he lives contemplating mental object in mental objects ... and [clings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upadana) to naught in the world." [\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-33)
In the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths), one of many summaries of the [Buddhist path to liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_path_to_liberation), *dukkha* ('suffering') is observed to arise with craving (Pali: *taṇhā* ; Skt.: *tṛṣṇā* , lit. 'thirst'). In the chain of [Dependent Origination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Nidanas), craving arises with [sensations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedana) when the *saḷāyatana* is activated by [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spar%C5%9Ba). To detach from *tanha* and *dukkha* , one should develop awareness ( *sati* (mindfullness) and *sampajañña* (clear comprehension)) of the chain of events triggered by the *saḷāyatana* , and practice restraint and detachment ( *sammā-vāyāma* ( [right effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Right_Exertions)) and *dhyana* ('meditation')). [\[k\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-34)
Ellis notes that *āyatana* may also refer to the various stages of meditation ( *jhana* ), and "even the state of liberated Buddhist masters is termed *āyatana* ."' [\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20212-3-9) As such, they are also a "center of experience" or "mental home," in which our normal states of mind are abandoned and one relocates in the purified, liberated awareness of the jhanas. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllis20213-2)

#### In post-canonical Pali texts

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=8)]
The *Vimuttimagga* , the *Visuddhimagga* , and associated [Pali commentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atthakatha) [\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-35) and [subcommentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcommentaries,_Theravada) all contribute to traditional knowledge about the *saḷāyatana* .

##### Understanding sense organs

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=9)]
When the Buddha speaks of "understanding" the eye, ear, nose, tongue and body, what is meant?
According to the first-century CE Sinhalese meditation manual, *Vimuttimagga* , the sense organs can be understood in terms of the object sensed, the consciousness aroused, the underlying "sensory matter," and an associated [primary or derived element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabhuta) that is present "in excess." [\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-36) These characteristics are summarized in the table below.

| sense organ | sense object | sense consciousness | sensory matter | element in excess |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| eye | visual objects | visual consciousness | "...the three small fleshy discs round the pupil, and the white and black of the [eye-ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_eye) that is in five layers of flesh, blood, wind, phlegm and serum, is half a poppy-seed in size, is like the head of a [louseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louse)...." | Earth |
| ear | sound waves | auditory consciousness | "...in the interior of the two ear-holes, is fringed by tawny hair, is dependent on the membrane, is like the stem of a blue-green bean...." | Sounds |
| nose | odors | olfactory consciousness | "...in the interior of the nose, where the three meet, is dependent on one small opening, is like a *Koviḷāra* (flower in shape)...." | air |
| tongue | tastes | gustatory consciousness | "...two-finger breadths in size, is in shape like a blue lotus, is located in the flesh of the tongue...." | water |
| body | tangibles | tactual consciousness | "...in the entire body, excepting the hair of the body and the head, nails teeth and other insensitive parts...." | Heat (or lack thereof) |
| Table 1. The *Vimuttimagga's* characterization of sense organs. [\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-37) |  |  |  |  |

The compendious fifth-century CE *Visuddhimagga* provides similar descriptors, such as "the size of a mere louse's head" for the location of the eye's "sensitivity" (Pali: *pasāda* ; also known as, "sentient organ, sense agency, sensitive surface"), [\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-38) and "in the place shaped like a goat's hoof" regarding the nose sensitivity ( [Vsm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visuddhimagga). XIV, 47–52). [\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-39) In addition, the Visuddhimagga describes the sense organs in terms of the following four factors:
\* characteristic or sign ( *lakkha ṇ a* )
\* function or "taste" ( *rasa* )
\* manifestation ( *paccupa ṭṭ hāna* )
\* proximate cause ( *pada ṭṭ hāna* )
Thus, for instance, it describes the eye as follows:
Herein, the eye's characteristic is sensitivity of [primary elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabhuta) that is ready for the impact of visible data; or its characteristic is sensitivity of primary elements originated by [kamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma) sourcing from desire to see. Its function is to pick up [an object] among visible data. It is manifested as the footing of eye- [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijnana). Its proximate cause is primary elements born of kamma sourcing from desire to see. [\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-40)
In regards to the sixth internal *āyatana* of mind ( *mano* ), Pali [subcommentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcommentaries,_Theravada) (attributed to Dhammapāla Thera) distinguish between consciousness arising from the five physical *saḷāyatana* and that arising from the primarily post- [canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali_Canon) notion of a "life-continuum" or "unconscious mind" ( *bhava ṅ ga-mana* ): [\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-41)
"Of the consciousness or mind aggregate included in a course of cognition of eye-consciousness, just the eye-base [not the mind-base] is the 'door' of origin, and the [external sense] base of the material form is the visible object. So it is in the case of the others [that is, the ear, nose, tongue and body sense bases]. But of the sixth sense-base the part of the mind base called the life-continuum, the unconscious mind, is the 'door' of origin...." [\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-42)

##### The roots of wisdom

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=10)]
In the fifth-century CE exegetical *Visuddhimagga* , [Buddhaghosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhaghosa) identifies knowing about the *saḷāyatana* as part of the "soil" of liberating [wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_in_Buddhism). Other components of this "soil" include the [aggregates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skandha), the [faculties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indriya), the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths) and [Dependent Origination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_Origination). [\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-43)

#### Related Buddhist concepts

[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%C4%80yatana&action=edit&section=11)]
\* **Aggregates** (Pali, *khandha* ; Skt., *skandha* ): In a variety of suttas, the aggregates, elements ( *see below* ) and *saḷāyatana* are identified as the "soil" in which [craving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%E1%B9%87h%C4%81) and [clinging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upadana) grow. [\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-44) In general, in the [Pali Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali_Canon), the aggregate of material form includes the five material sense organs (eye, ear, nose, tongue and body) and associated sense objects (visible forms, sounds, odors, tastes and tactile objects); the aggregate of consciousness is associated with the sense organ of mind; and, the mental aggregates (sensation, perception, mental formations) are mental sense objects. [\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-45) Both the aggregates and the *saḷāyatana* are identified as objects of [mindfulness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sati_(Buddhism)) [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_meditation) in the [Satipatthana Sutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satipatthana_Sutta). In terms of pursuing liberation, meditating on the aggregates eradicates self-doctrine and wrong-view [clinging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upadana) while meditating on the *saḷāyatana* eradicates sense-pleasure clinging. [\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-46)
\* **Dependent Origination** (Pali: *paṭicca-samuppāda* ; Skt.: *pratitya-samutpada* ): As indicated in Figure 2 above, the six *saḷāyatana* (Pali; Skt.: *ṣaḍāyatana* ) are the fifth link in the [Twelve Causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Nidanas) ( *nidāna* ) of the chain of Dependent Origination and thus likewise are the fifth position on the [Wheel of Becoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavacakra) ( *bhavacakra* ). The arising of the six *saḷāyatana* is dependent on the arising of [material and mental objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namarupa) (Pali, Skt.: *nāmarūpa* ); and, the arising of the six *saḷāyatana* leads to the arising of " [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spar%C5%9Ba)" (Pali: *phassa* ; Skt.: *sparśa* ) between the *saḷāyatana* and [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijnana) (Pali: *viññāṇa* ; Skt.: *visjñāna* ) which results in pleasant, unpleasant and neutral [feelings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedana) (Pali, Skt.: *vedanā* ).
\* **Elements** (Pali, Skt.: *dhātu* ): [\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-47) The eighteen elements include the twelve *saḷāyatana* . The eighteen elements are six triads of elements where each triad is composed of a sense object (the external *saḷāyatana* ), a sense organ (the internal *saḷāyatana* ) and the associated sense-organ-consciousness ( *viññāṇa* ). [\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-48) In other words, the eighteen elements are made up of the twelve *saḷāyatana* and the six related sense-consciousnesses.
\* **Karma** (Skt.; Pali: *kamma* ): In a [Samyutta Nikaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyutta_Nikaya) discourse, the Buddha declares that the six internal senses bases (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body and mind) are "old kamma, to be seen as generated and fashioned by volition, as something to be felt." [\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#cite_note-49) In this discourse, "new kamma" is described as "whatever action one does now by body, speech, or mind." In this way, the internal *saḷāyatana* provide a link between our volitional actions and subsequent perceptions.

\* The Pāli word translated here as "mind" is *mano* . Other common translations include "intellect" [(e.g., Thanissaro, 2001a)](http://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn35/sn35.023.than.html) and "consciousness" [(e.g., Soma, 1999).](http://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.soma.html) In the [Suttapitaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suttapitaka), *mano* does not necessarily refer to all mental processing. Other oft-mentioned complementary mental processes include "consciousness" ( *viññāṇa* ) and "mental states" ( *citta* ). Nonetheless, in the [Abhidhamma Pitaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhidhamma_Pitaka) and later texts, these terms are at times used synonymously.
\* Buddhism and other Indian epistemologies identify six 'centers of experience', conventionally interpreted as "senses"; as such the number differs from the Western identification of five senses. In Buddhism, "mind" denotes a 'center of experience', conventionally interpreted as an 'internal sense organ', which interacts with sense objects, including [sense impressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phassa), [feelings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedana), [perceptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B1%C3%B1a) and [volition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81ra), all belonging to the five skandhas. See, for instance, [Bodhi (2000a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#CITEREFBodhi2000a), p. 288), and Hamilton (2001), p. 53: "... six senses, including one relating to non-sensory mental activity, are recognized in Buddhism and other Indian schools of thought." [Pine (2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80yatana#CITEREFPine2004), p. 101) argues that this scheme probably predates Buddhism, because it has ten external members (ear, sound, nose, odor, tongue, taste, body, touch) corresponding to the single external skandha (form), and only two internal members (mind and thought) corresponding to the four internal skandhas.

In terms of the Buddhist notion of the *saḷāyatana* , rūpa refers to *visual* objects (or objects knowable by the eye through light). This should not be confused with the use of the word rūpa in terms of the Buddhist notion of [aggregates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skandha) where rūpa refers to *all* material objects, both of the world and the body. Thus, when comparing these two uses of rūpa, the rūpa aggregate ( *rūpakkhandha* ) includes the rūpa sense-object ( *rūpāyatana* ) as well as the four other material sense-objects (sound, odor, taste and touch).
