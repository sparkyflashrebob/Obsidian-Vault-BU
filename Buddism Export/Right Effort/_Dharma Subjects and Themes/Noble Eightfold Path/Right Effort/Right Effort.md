[[Unsorted Notes/Right Effort]] is a [[mental discipline]] in Buddhism that involves developing [[wholesome]] qualities and releasing [[unwholesome]] qualities. It is the effort to cleanse the mind of impurities through self-discipline. The Buddha recommended that his disciples make the “right effort” to rid themselves of unwholesome thoughts, words, and actions. 

Right effort is part of the Eightfold Path, along with right mindfulness and right concentration. The four right efforts are: 

- Discarding evil states that have arisen
- Preventing the arising of unarisen evil states
- Developing unarisen wholesome states
- Augmenting arisen wholesome states

### Right effort 
The compassionate stewardship of the mind that steers us to liberation. It is the effort to keep the mind focused on the object. With repeated practice, the mind becomes gradually stilled and tranquil.

### Preventing Unwholesome mental states
- #### stem the hindrances
	- 1._Sensual desire_ means lust for pleasurable states: sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches. Or for wealth, power, position, fame, and their accompanying pleasures.
	  
	  2. _Ill will_ means hatred, anger, resentment, and repulsion.
	  
	  3. _Dullness_ means mental inertia and accompanying drowsiness or mental sinking into heaviness of the mind.
	  
	  4. _Restlessness or worry_ refers to states of agitation, excitement, frenzy, or stress, which keep the mind from focusing.
	  
	  5. _Doubt_ means a lack of resolution and indecisiveness, which hinders right effort.
