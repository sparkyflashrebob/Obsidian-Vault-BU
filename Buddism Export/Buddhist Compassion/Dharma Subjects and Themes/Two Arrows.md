The "Two Arrows" (or *Sallatha Sutta*) is a foundational teaching in Buddhism that distinguishes between the pain we cannot avoid and the suffering we create for ourselves.

It comes from the **Sallatha Sutta (SN 36.6)**, often translated as "The Arrow" or "The Dart." The Buddha uses this metaphor to explain how a wise person relates to pain differently than an untrained person.

### 1. The First Arrow: Unavoidable Pain
The first arrow represents the raw, inevitable difficulties of human existence. This includes physical pain, illness, loss, aging, and the general bumps of life.
* **The Nature of the Arrow:** It is shot by life itself.
* **Key Characteristic:** It is **outside your control**.
* **The Experience:** When this arrow hits, it hurts. The Buddha teaches that even an enlightened being (an Arahant) feels the physical sensation of this arrow. If you stub your toe or lose a job, the immediate shock and pain are real.

### 2. The Second Arrow: Optional Suffering
The second arrow represents our **reaction** to the first arrow. This is the mental resistance, storytelling, and emotional proliferation (*papañca*) that follows the initial event.
* **The Nature of the Arrow:** It is shot by **you** at yourself.
* **Key Characteristic:** It is **optional** and within your sphere of influence.
* **The Experience:** This is the "Why is this happening to me?" "I can't handle this," or "This will never end." It includes anger, resentment, self-pity, and anxiety about the future.

### The Core Teaching: Pain vs. Suffering
The Buddha essentially asks: *If a man were struck by an arrow, would he then pick up a second arrow and shoot himself in the same spot?*

He explains that the untrained person feels **two** pains: physical and mental. By obsessing over the pain, resisting it, or creating a narrative of victimhood, they multiply their suffering.

The wise practitioner, however, feels only **one** pain: the physical. They acknowledge the sensation of the first arrow ("There is pain here") but do not add the layer of mental anguish on top of it. They remain present with the sensation without adding the "story" of the pain.

### Summary
* **Pain (First Arrow):** Inevitable.
* **Suffering (Second Arrow):** Optional.

The practice of mindfulness is often described as the art of noticing the first arrow so that you can refrain from shooting the second one.
