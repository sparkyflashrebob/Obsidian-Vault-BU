**Key Points of the Satipatthana Sutta**

The [[Satipatthana Sutta]], often referred to as the "Great Frames of Reference," is a foundational scripture in Theravada Buddhism outlining the practice of mindfulness.

Here are some key points of the sutta:

1. Four Foundations of Mindfulness:

- Body (kāya): Contemplating the body in various ways, including the breath, postures, and bodily sensations.
    
- Feelings (vedanā): Observing and acknowledging feelings without judgment, noting whether they are pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral.
    
- Mind (citta): Observing the mind's activities, such as thoughts, emotions, and intentions, without clinging to them.
    
- Mental objects (dhammā): Contemplating the five hindrances (sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and remorse, and doubt), the five aggregates (form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness), and the four noble truths (suffering, its cause, its cessation, and the path to its cessation).
    

2. Mindfulness Arousings:

- Arising and passing away: Observing phenomena as they arise, change, and pass away.
    
- Comprehension: Understanding the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and impersonal nature of phenomena.
    
- Relinquishment: Letting go of clinging and attachment to phenomena.
    
- Non-identification: Seeing phenomena as they are, without clinging to a self or personality.
    

3. Mindfulness of Activities:

- Bringing mindfulness to everyday activities, such as walking, sitting, eating, and working.
    
- Observing bodily sensations, mental states, and external objects with detached awareness.
    

4. The Five Hindrances:

- Sensual desire (kāma-chanda)
    
- Ill will (vyāpāda)
    
- Sloth and torpor (thīna-middha)
    
- Restlessness and remorse (uddhacca-kukkucca)
    
- Doubt (vicikicchā)
    

5. Benefits of Mindfulness:

- Increased awareness and understanding of oneself and the world
    
- Reduced stress, anxiety, and negativity
    
- Improved concentration and focus
    
- Greater sense of well-being and happiness
    
- Ultimately, liberation from suffering
