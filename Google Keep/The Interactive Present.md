In part 1, present moment filled with conflicting objects.  No formula for letting them go.  
Ideally we should be able to let the mind settle down and deal with the difficulties from a sense of calmness.  But with many of us we have to deal with some difficult issues first.
Discernment requires concentration and concentration requires discernment.  How to bypass whatever issues you can bypass and deal with the issues in front of you.

What is going on in the present moment? Anger, fear, pain, lust. Or are we playing along with our distractions.  Using our discernment to investigate what's going on.  What can we do to relieve the problem?  Are we keeping it going?  

We are participants in our experience. An element of our intentions are shaping it.

We look at things this way not only for pains, we can turn this investigation to the mind.  Is that lust or anger just some mental habit? Are we really enjoying it? Are we repressing our truth?  Are we identifying with our lust, with our anger?
And finally, how about others emotions such as fear and greed?

Background
Cultivation of benefitial mind states. 
Opening up to the Deathless.  The Unfabricated.  To the Ultimate Truth here and now.  Liberation from the Wheel of Rebirth.  Nibanna.  

At the time of the Buddha, Hinduism referrred to a timeless state realized at the time of liberation.

"When one thing is practiced & pursued, ignorance is abandoned, clear knowing arises, the conceit 'I am' is abandoned, obsessions are uprooted, fetters are abandoned. Which one thing? Mindfulness immersed in the body." 

Together with the Deathless, the other common term for the ultimate goal is nirvana (Pali: nibbāna). It is clear that nirvana is reached through the complete ceasing of clinging: “A bhikkhu without clinging attains Nibbāna” (MN 106.12).

"When this is, that is." Dependent Origination

Everything is interconnected. Everything affects everything else. Everything that is, is because other things are. This is the teaching of Dependent Origination.
