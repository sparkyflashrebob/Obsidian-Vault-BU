As a part of today's topic I'd like us take practice in two positions of during our meditation. We will begin in the standing position.
Please find a place nearby where you can hear me, and easily transition to the sitting position.  You may find that just standing up where you are will work.
The idea is to be able to transition from the standing position to the sitting position with mindfulness intact.
So when you find your standing position you following the same general guidelines as the sitting position. 
It might be helpful to place your feet at shoulder width, with your knees very slightly bent so the back straightens.  
For me, this does not work so well so I slightly widen my stance so my and lock my knees so that my base is firmly established from the feet to the hips. 
You can adjust the angle of your feet to provide the most stability and allow you to adjust them slightly as needed. 
As you stand,  feel free to adjust your position as needed until coming to a place where you are relaxed and alert as in the sitting position.
Once there achieving the right balance also involves the placement of the hands.  
Sometimes I find having my arms to my side quite comfortable for long periods of time.  
Alternatively, you can place the hands in lotus position either at your navel or higher on the breastplate.  
The position of the arms and hands are also of concern during walking meditation.
Once you have your hands in position return your awareness to the whole of the body standing. Relaxing the body.....Letting go of any obvious tension by shaking one or both shoulders, one or both legs to release any tension.
It is recommended that you do a quick body scan as we went over last week - not necessarily becoming aware of sensation but for releasing any tension.  
And finally letting the awareness of the body breathing come to the forefront.  
Full awareness of the body breathing.  
Noticing the in-breath...... and noticing the out-breath.  And noticing both the in-breath and the out-breath. 
Using the breath to fill your entire body.
Resting in this position for the next few minutes.
Feel free to adjust your position slightly but when you do so know that you are doing so. Being aware of the body moving and then coming to rest.  Keeping your attention within the body.

In a minute or so we will transition to the sitting position. 
Keeping this mindfulness of the body and the body as it moves. Being aware of the mind as it intends to move, aware of the body moving, aware of it resting on the seated position, aware of it as it adjusts to its new position, mindful of your awareness of this movement. Awareness of the cessing of this movement. Awareness of the body in the seated position. Awareness of the body breathing. Mindful as the body breaths in - mindful as the body breaths out.    
Aware of the whole body breathing.

   

There’s no difference between standing and sitting on the breathing tips, but generally to breathe properly, your shoulders should not be moving up and down. This is called diaphragmatic breathing. This process cleans toxins from the body.
