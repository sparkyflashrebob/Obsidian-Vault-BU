So let's start for a moment with just a little practice arriving where we are. Find a way to sit just for the next three or four minutes in a comfortable and dignified posture. You can sit on a chair. You can sit on the floor, on a cushion or a bench, whatever way your body is comfortable and at ease on the earth. 
And if, as we go through these meditations and trainings, you find your body uncomfortable, you can notice that and work with it mindfully for a time and then feel free to change posture or move. Just do so mindfully.

So keeping this invitation for presence now, allow yourself first to look around where you are, opening mindfulness awareness to the body and senses. See the colors around you in the room, the computer screen, the windows and the light or darkness. And as you look around, relax and let yourself just take in the environment.

And when you're ready, then bring your attention back to yourself seated here and allow your eyes to close and relax more fully. Let the eyes and face be soft. Loosen the jaw. Let the  shoulders relax and the arms and hands rest easily so you're seated with a sense of dignity and alertness, and at the same time, a deep invitation of presence and relaxation.

And begin to notice the play of experience as you sit quietly. Notice the sounds that come and go. You can notice also if there's any sense of smell or taste, odors from the roses on the table nearby or someone cooking down the street, or even the words that I'm using which evoke the sense.

You can notice the body sensations, areas of ease or tightness, warmth or cool, vibration or stillness, pleasure or pain, the feel of this body received with a kind attention. You can notice the state of the heart as well. Is there interest, or gratitude, or sadness and grief, or excitement, or tenderness?
Does the heart feel open or closed? Without any judgment, without carrying a gun, just noticing.

And in the same way that you notice what Emily Dickinson called "the mob within the heart," you can notice the states of mind that's present now-- planning, excitement, relaxed, doubting or questioning, remembering, curious, obsessed.

And finally, again, feel your body seated here halfway between heaven and earth with presence and dignity, and realize that you can notice the play of experiences you have with a spacious and kind attention, that you've come more fully into the present.
