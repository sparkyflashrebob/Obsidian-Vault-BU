The Kenyan-born poet
Warsan Shire says, "What they did yesterday afternoon-- they set my aunt's house on fire.
I cried the way women on TV do, folding at the middle like a five pound note. I called the boy
who used to love me, tried to OK my voice. I said hello. He said, 'Warsan, what's wrong? What
happened?' I've been praying, and these are what my prayers look like.
Dear God, I come from two countries. One is thirsty. The other is on fire. Both need water. Later
that night, I held an atlas in my lap, ran my fingers across the whole world and whispered,
'Where does it hurt?' It answered, 'Everywhere, everywhere, everywhere.'"
