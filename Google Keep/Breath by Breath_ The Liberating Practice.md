1.  While breathing in long, one knows: “I breathe in long.” While breathing out long, one knows: “I breathe out long.” 2.  While breathing in short, one knows: “I breathe in short.” While breathing out short, one knows: “I breathe out short.”

Most commentators agree that the Buddha meant more than long and short here; he was talking about all the qualities of the breath.

One of the things you learn about this whole process—the conjunction of mind and body, with the breath as the meeting place—is that awareness has an extremely powerful effect on it.

you shouldn’t start out with some idea of gaining.

When we follow the breathing in the hope of becoming something better, we are compromising our connection to the present, which is all we ever have.

The first law of Buddhism is that everything is constantly changing.

However your practice seems to you, cherish it just the way it is.

Actually, the whole process is meditation: being with the breathing, drifting away, seeing that we’ve drifted away, gently coming back.

a seed that increases the likelihood of such moments in the future.

The object of these first two contemplations isn’t to make your breathing perfect. It’s to see how your breathing really is.
