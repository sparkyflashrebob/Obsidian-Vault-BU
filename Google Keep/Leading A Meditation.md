Here are some additional recommendations for leading a meditation.
These are suggestions, not rigid instructions. 

Stagecraft 
Tend the space, the light, temperature, sound, and chairs 

Comfort 
Make sure they are comfortable in the body grounded, steady, at ease. 

Voice 
Talk slowly and clearly, with appropriate pauses. 

Orienting 
First, direct them to open their attention to gently note their surroundings and then 
draw the attention back to themselves. 

Invitation 
Invite them to now use their breath to quiet their mind and tend their body and heart. 

Time 
Together, set an intention to sit for 5, 1 0, or 20 minutes. 

Eyes 
Suggest they close their eyes or leave them half open. 

Your Eyes 
Keep your eyes open and pay attention to both inner and outer at the same time 

Sense the Body 
Have them first sense their body seated as they are. 

Start Where You Are 
Remind them to start exactly where they are and feel the breath/anchor in the midst of it all. 

A Few Deep Breaths 
Start with a few deeper, cleansing breaths. 

Next 
After the deep breaths have ended, have them notice how the breath breathes itself. 

Notice 
Whereverthe breath is most clear nose,throat, chest, belly, orfull body. 

Anchors 
Acknowledge that some may be using alternative anchors. 

Adding Phrases Qr Numbers 
For those who wish, they can softly note in the background "calm" or "ease" with each breath, or note an anchor. They can also whisper to themselves, "Count one 
through ten." 

Returning to the Breath 
Remind them that returning is the key. Return to the breath/anchor I 000 
times gently and directly like training the puppy 

Distractions 
Let other experiences rise and fall like waves of the ocean around the breath/anchor. 
Whether sounds, sensations, thoughts, plans, emotions, or anything else, for now let them be in the background and steadily feel the breath breathe itself or the anchor in the midst of it all. 

Noticing Calm and Steadiness 
Have them look for any positive moments of calm, steadiness, ease, concentration, or peace that arise. Have them inhabit/rest/dwell in these while they are present. 

Refining Attention 
Further instruction can invite them to notice the beginning, middle, and end of each breath. 

Another Refinement 
You can invite them to notice and rest in the space between the breaths. 

Ending Announcement 
Sometimes it helps to say, "One more minute."
