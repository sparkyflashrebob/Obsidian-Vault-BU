Stephen Procter
Fixed Concentration - meditation object - to know  this - follow ignore distractions
with effort concentration will develop - Buddha called this applying and sustained attention
we are to remember this - apply attention - be aware of what is going on and keep reapplying that attention to our object
the effort is to keep putting our attention back
Notice that there are a whole lot of little forgettings
again and again and again
Buddha described this process as starting a fire with two sticks
need to generate enough heat - sustained effort - but if too much then we are creating too much stress
This effort is gentle
The practice is not an aggressive practice
towards gentleness.

In our culture we worship effort
we say what have you been doing? oh, I've been busy - Good!!!  we worship this

So when we come to the meditation we try to put too much effort into this.
effort creates energy - too much energy creates restlessness
if we put too much effort we will end up thinking all the time
or you can keep your body still

But we need to initially build this concentration, concentration on one thing.
So we start off with heaviness of the body, because it is easier to start off.
This will help some concentration to build. 
Then we go to the feeling of touch. This draws the energy away from our head - away from our thinking.
The feeling of touch is easy to feel, it is easy to remember. As we continue to feel then we will start to experience some concentration.  Is this your experience?
Then we go into the body and we relax in with our breath. Why, because we have to relax into our effort. So relaxing first physically and then relaxing mentally is relaxing into this effort to do. We have this strong urge to do.

If you are sure of what this urge is once this class is over, go home, sit on a lounge, and do nothing. Don't look at anything. Don't meditate. Don't listen to the radio. Don't read. Don't sit there thinking lofty thoughts. Just do nothing and see how hard it is to not do. 
That softening into is learning how to relax into "not to do". How to not to react. 

This is the path in which concentration develops.  It is very gentle.

Then we move onto the breath. Remember each breath as it comes in and out.  This is more difficult so we use a label to help remember.  "IN" when the breath is coming in and "OUT" when the breath is coming out. 

Notice that we are not looking at any detail of anything.  We are not even investigating. What we are just doing is remembering we are aware of this. That is all we need to do to develop this fixed concentration. 

Fixed Concentration is very helpful because what it does is it suppresses the hindrances to meditation. Particularly when we reach what is called Access Concentration. We become one-pointed with whatever we are following. 
At this point you will notice that you do not have to place any effort to sustain the awareness on the concentration object.  It will stay there itself.

The Hindrances are sensual desire, the desire to experience or aversion, the desire to not experience. Restlessness. Mental Sluggishness. And doubt.
They are all held back by the power of the concentration. This is important.

When we come in from our everyday life we are carrying these five hindrances to meditation - cause this is what we live our life thru, isn't it?
So if we are going to do the practice, we are going to have to hold them back temporarily. Until we learn how to overcome them.

So we follow our meditation object, we remember it. We ignore distraction.  We ignore it. Wander off on a sound, we come back.  Wander off with a thought, we come back. But don't investigate at this point. 

Once your mind has settled you will know because your attention will be able to follow the breath easily. It is beautiful.  A real pleasure will come up. Peace, pleasure, spaceousness, clarity comes up. 

At this point we do not want to continue building fixed concentration. You can do that if you want to build up tranquility meditation.  That is perfectly viable.  But once you get to this point, the sense doors will start shutting down. Which means you will know your meditation object, but you will not know sounds, the body will disappear, feel like you are floating.  All these sorts of things begin to happen. 
If these sense doors shut down, then you cannot investigate our experience of the world or the senses doors in regards to the world. We cannot experience to how we are relating to what we hear, and what we think, smell, taste, feel with our body.  We cannot investigate that if we cannot experience it.  
So we want just enough concentration so the hindrances are suppressed but not so much that what we are experiencing becomes very, very narrow. 
At that point, that is the time we change the type of concentration that we are developing.  
So at this point we change the way we watch the breath. Instead of knowing each breath as it goes in and out, we pay attention to the sensations in the breath. At the beginning of the in-breath you feel a coolness and then a pressure, then movement and an expanding feeling. Even in the expanding it is a completely different experience all the way through. Even in the expanding it is a whole lot of little experiences.
