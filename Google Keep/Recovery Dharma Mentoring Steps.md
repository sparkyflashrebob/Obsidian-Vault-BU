
Get to Know Each Other
Step 1 of 7
Welcome to Mentorship! Get started by introducing yourselves, then learn more
about each other and why you're both here.
Discuss 
You might talk about:
· Their current situation and why they're
interested in mentorship.
What kind of support they need.
Areas where you think you may be able to help them.

Tips:
• Your goal right now is to get to know each other.
Saying a bit about yourself and your own journey will help get the conversation going.

Make a Plan
Step 2 of 7
Have you agreed on what you'll be working on? Once you do, you're ready to come up with a plan...

Discuss

You might talk about:
What they're hoping to make progress on.
• What the goal could be for your time together - a goal that's specific, realistic, and
something they have control over.
How they're feeling about getting started.

Tips:
• Getting started can be hard. Reassure your partner that you're here to support them, and try to agree on one step they'll take toward their goal this week, even if it's small.
Consider sharing an example of something you used to struggle with and how you were able to change that.

Check In
Step 3 of 7
Does the plan you made for this mentorship need an adjustment or two? This is a great time to review the plan and make changes if needed.

Discuss

You might talk about:
If they were able to take a step toward their goal and how that felt.
• If there's a specific issue they'd like to talk through
What step they could take within the next week.

Tips:
It's OK to adjust the goal or the plan if that makes sense. Help them come up with a
short list of specific actions they can take. 
Suggest other places they might find support such as books, online sources, and
people in their life.

Take Care of Yourself
Step 4 of 7
When taking on a challenge, it's important to remember to take care of yourself. Keeping healthy habits will make it easier to reach your goal.

Discuss
You might talk about:
Something they could do this week that's just for them.
If there are any tasks or responsibilities they could get help with.
Other ways to reduce stress.

Tips:
• Help them brainstorm activities that make them happy - even if it's taking an hour to
read, catch up with a friend by phone or spend time outside.
Putting your own needs first can seem selfish or awkward, but you can remind them
that they'll be more effective if they're feeling their best.

Keep It Going
Step 5 of 7
Check in on progress (every little bit counts!) and where you might take things from here...

Discuss
You might talk about:
Anything they're proud of this week.
• Anything that felt easier or harder than expected.
· Anything else they'd like to talk through or that you're observing.

Tips:
• Sometimes it can help to break a problem into smaller pieces.
If you have a story of your own that relates to what they're going through, consider
sharing it.

Celebrate Progress
Step 6 of 7
Take a few moments to recognize how far you've come together. If you're still working
together toward a goal, that's something to be proud of.

Discuss
You might talk about:
• Something specific you saw them accomplish.
How they feel about the progress they made.
• Anything that surprised them along the way.

Tips:
Congratulate them for the steps they've taken toward their goal.
• Remind them that this is a journey, and if they've made some progress toward their
goal, they should feel a sense of accomplishment.

Look Ahead
Step 7 of 7
The structured mentorship program has ended. So where do things go from here?

Discuss
.
You might talk about:
The progress they've made.
Any tips for how to keep making progress.
Staying in touch

Tips:
• Encourage your partner to keep working toward their goals. Remind them of
resources that are out there to keep supporting them, including the group you're
both in.
If you'd be willing to stay in touch even though the official program has ended, let
them know.
