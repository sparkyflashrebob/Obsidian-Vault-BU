So, What is a Spiritual Awakening?

Some DEFINITIONS:
My favorite source WIKIPEDIA says that Enlightenment is the "full comprehension of a situation".  Various religions view enlightenment and mysticism as equivalent terms for religious or spiritual insight.  Hence the connection to awakening.  Another equivalent term is Illumination - where the process of human thought is aided by divine grace.  Romanticism describes it as insight into a timeless, transendent reality or transcendent truth.

...most of them labour under the old cliché that the goal of Buddhist psychological analysis is to reveal the hidden mysteries in the human mind and thereby facilitate the development of a transcendental state of consciousness beyond the reach of linguistic expression.

Some describe it in in terms of Experience, such as William James book on The Varieties of Religious Experience - based on a feeling of the infinite. 
There is some debate on this as Western cultures definitions of Experience introduces a false notion of duality between "experiencer" and "experienced" whereas buddhism and some forms of hinduism understand this essence as a realization of "Non-duality".  Pure Experience does not exist as all experience is mediated by intellectual and cognitive activity. Specific teachings may even determine what experience some has which means the this experience is not the proof of the teaching but a result of the teaching.  

In buddhism enlightment is known as bodhi, the knowledge or wisdom, or awakened intellect of a Buddha.  Buddha is the one who has awakened.  Enlightenment is western in definition.  It is used to denote insight, knowledge or the blowing out (nirvana) of disturbing emotions and desires and the subsequent freedom or release.  
The cause of the buddhas enlightenment is understood to be as a result of a combination of mindfulness and jhana/samadhi.    
    
Many sites I researching listed signs of a spiritual awakening.  I share this list frequently at meetings. 

TWELVE SIGNS OF A SPIRITUAL AWAKENING
1. An increased tendency to let things happen rather than make them happen.
2. Frequent attacks of smiling.
3. Feelings of being connected with others and nature.
4. Frequent overwhelming episodes of appreciation.
5. A tendency to think and act spontaneously rather than from fears based on past experience.
6. An unmistakable ability to enjoy each moment.
7. A loss of ability to worry.
8. A loss of interest in conflict.
9. A loss of interest in interpreting the actions of others.
10. A loss of interest in judging others.
11. A loss of interest in judging self.
12. Gaining the ability to love without expecting anything in return.

SO NOT TURNING TO THE PATH
So how do we get there.
First of all, I would like to remind you that it is not intellectual.  The Buddha has a quote saying 
“Whatever you think it will be, it will always be something different” (MN 113,21). 
It not something that you gain or something to get.  It is something to realize.
So what is this path?
In buddhism, there is this threefold training that is called the Noble Eightfold path. Some say that first we go thru this stage in a mundane way because it does not lead to Nibbana, it only sets up the conditions to get into the Noble Eightfold Path where we come to understand anicca, anatta and dukha. These are divided into the three baskets of Sila (Moral discipline) and for us non-monastics the 5 Precepts of not killing, not stealing, non-harming with our speech, not harming with our sexuality, and not using intoxicants***
By the way, this last one is not usually talked about....
If on a retreat three more important renunciant precepts are added.  I mention them here because I feel they are foundational to the practice.  They are not eating after noon, not indulging in entertainment, and noble silence.
These, I believe, are added so that we focus on the path itself.
Explain.
The second of the trainings is in mental discipline or samadhi.  This is our meditation practice. We look to the teachings on the Four Foundations of Mindfulness and we look to be mindful at all times as we go about our day. 
The last of these trainings is called panna or wisdom. 
The idea is that we come to a calm state where we see into the nature of the self.
The Noble go around involves deeper understanding into our own true nature so that eventually we break free from the bondage of self.

There are practical benefits along the way.
I mentioned the 12 Signs of a Spiritual Awakening.  These are not realized at the very end.
As we loosen the grips of fear we start to connect with our genuine being, to act with more understanding of our actions, to become less reactionary.
This all comes naturally in most cases.
But we cannot rest on our laurels.
