to understand what happens to the Mind and Body in meditation
we first have to understand the nature of the mind and body
there is no separate mind and body they are directly related.
 
so a psychological experience can trigger a physical response 
many of them were familiar with stress leading to high blood pressure,
faster heart rate, higher body temperature. 
but many things physically can happen as a result of psychological changes 
such as panic attacks and nervousness, twitching shortened breath and choppy
breaths , often holding our breaths.

and when we meditate, the mind, which tends to only see things in terms of instant gratification, it really only sees this moment and what it wants in this moment. 

and seeing the big picture, seeing how it will have consequences, this is where our higher mind, our sensing, our intuition and higher intelligence comes into play. 

And, because the mind wants instant gratification, meditation is incredibly threatening to the ego.

the thinking mind, that's been in control of Our Lives, because we've believed  it is our inner voice and we've believed that it is who we are. and so we listen to those thoughts. We Believe those thoughts.  

And, when someone has started meditating for the first time, and they have made a habit most of their life of stuffing down their feelings, suppressing them, often unconsciously, and sometimes consciously, by focusing on something else, instead of healing from trauma we may have experienced.

And so when we spend our whole lives avoiding and not resolving our trauma.
When we start to meditate,  all of our feelings that we've been suppressing,
through food, or shopping, or social media, or entertainment, all of the anger, sadness, anxiety, disturbing thoughts and feelings, now finally have some space where they can be healed and processed through. 

So it's not the meditation that is creating these physical and sometimes emotional and psychological responses to meditation.  It is that the meditation is allowing these feelings  that have been under the surface, to finally be processed and healed 

when we first meditate people describe all sorts of physical symptoms 
it could be their heads kind of shaking back and forth or their eyes engaging in rapid
eye movement or they feel a tightness in their chest
these are all very common
any kind of twitching
these are all ways that the body heals from trauma that we don't allow ourselves to heal from

many animals once they've been startled in the wild and they have a rush of adrenaline
have mechanisms to burn off that chemical of adrenaline that is in their body
and humans are supposed to have similar mechanisms
we run from prey or we sometimes uncontrollably shake and break down and
crying even laughter is one of those responses that engages our entire body
raises our temperature and is a coping mechanism for trauma

and so when we experience discomfort or disturbing feelings in meditation 
it's important not to resist them not to fear them
but to look and learn and in this way
we allow whatever arises to be 
even if it's panic and anxiety and stress 
because we can't fight it 
when we fight it we create more stress more tension more conflict within us
and we don't want to listen to it and go  along with it and believe
that we are these anxious people filled with anxiety 
and that's the way it'll always be
instead we just bring awareness to it we simply allow it to be
and by becoming aware of it, by allowing it, 
with no negative resistance, 
we create a space of peace and acceptance around it
and this is how any kind of negative feelings can be healed and processed through meditation
it doesn't mean we should stop 
it just means we should keep going
