
Nowadays, the Thanksgiving holiday may evoke thoughts of bargain-hunting more than its original purpose: giving generously and sharing gratitude. According to Bhikkhu Bodhi in his introduction in Dāna: The Practice of Giving the Buddha always emphasized the importance of cultivating generosity through giving, usually speaking at length on this topic:
 
... Only after the audience had begun to appreciate this virtue would he introduce other aspects of his teaching." Giving is seen as "the foundation and seed of spiritual development...
 
Dāna (generosity) is one of the ten pāramī (perfections) necessary to develop on the path to enlightenment.
 
Gratitude is one of the thirty-eight highest blessings mentioned in the Mahā Mangala Sutta: "Reverence, humility, contentment, gratitude, and the timely hearing of the Dhamma, the teaching of the Buddha — this is the highest blessing."

Kataññū and Kataññutā, the Pāli words for 'grateful' and 'gratitude' contain two parts:  'kata' and 'ññū'. 'Kata' means 'what is done'; 'ññū' means 'knowing', 'acknowledging what has been done'.
 
This suggests gratitude involves a element of knowledge; that we feel grateful because we know someone has done something for us or something has benefited us.
 
Vipassana Teacher S.N. Goenka mentioned gratitude to be an indicator of progress on the path of Dhamma. As one progresses on the path, one starts to appreciate the gift of Dhamma more and more, gaining awareness of the value of it and of the effort the Buddha and the Sangha made in order for us to receive it.
 
In the discourse on day 10 of a Vipassana course Goenkaji says: "… along with the experience of Dhamma there is bound to grow a feeling of gratitude to Gotama the Buddha for finding and teaching this technique, and gratitude as well to those who selflessly strove to maintain the teaching in its original purity through twenty-five centuries to the present day."
