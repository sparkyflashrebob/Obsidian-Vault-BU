A few weeks back in my talk I referred to the Instructions from the Satipattana Sutta, Four Foundation of Mindfulness.  
READ STATEMENT:
There is the case where a practioner remains focused on the body in & of itself — ardent, alert, & mindful — putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world. She remains focused on feelings... mind... mental qualities in & of themselves — ardent, alert, & mindful — putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world.

LET"S BEGIN BY MEDITATING TOGETHER:
We should hold a comfortable and dignified posture whether we are sitting in a chair or on a cushion.    
For our purposes, we focus bring attention to one object , usually to the body or to the breath....... if the body, we become aware of being supported by the earth, pressure on the feet and you butt, we can relax into our sit bones and know that mother earth is there to hold us. We relax - letting go of any obvious tension.
If the breath, we bring our attention to where it is most comfortable to bring awareness to.  That can be either at the top of the nose, or at the throat, at the diaphragm, or even whole body breathing - anywhere you can sense the breath, with ease.
And if, as we go through these meditations and trainings, you find your body
uncomfortable, you can notice that and work with it mindfully for a time and then feel free to change posture or move. Just do so mindfully.  

PAUSE FOR A SHORT TIME
Referring back to the 4 Foundations of Mindfulness we bring in a sense of ardency. Ardency is to have an interest - to be enthusiastic or passionate, vigilant, serious in our effort, zealous about our practice at this time, in this place, here and now.
We set the intention to be awake - alert to our experience.     
We get the intention to comprehend what is going on, knowing，understanding, conscious - Observant - the one who knows
We set the intention to be mindful, remembering the teachings and why we are here.

After a minute or two

How do we put aside grief and distress for the world? We do this by giving up the baggage of the past and future.
We let go of the past, what we're doing before we got here, our conversations, our opinions, our views, what we had for dinner, what the politician said today, our upbringing, our parents, 

Abandoning the past means not thinking about your work, your family, your commitments, your responsibilities, your good or bad times in childhood, and so on. You abandon all past experiences by showing no interest in them at all. During meditation, you become someone who has no history.


After a minute or two

If we abandon all that history, we are equal and free. We free ourselves of some of the concerns, perceptions, and thoughts that limit us, that stop us from developing the peace born of letting go. Every part of our history is finally released, even the memory of what happened just a moment ago. Whatever has happened no longer interests us, and we let it go. It no longer reverberates in our mind.

Do not linger on the past. Do not keep carrying around coffins full of dead moments. If you do, you weigh yourself down with heavy burdens that do not really belong to you. When you let go of the past, you will be free in the present moment. As for the future—the anticipations, fears, plans, and expectations—let that go too. The Buddha once said,“Whatever you think it will be, it will always be something different” (MN 113,21). This future is known by the wise as uncertain, unknown, and unpredictable.

Awaken to this moment.

1 minute before end

As we conclude this period of meditation let us open our mindfulness to the whole body,.... mindfulness of the whole room, to this city, to california, to the us of a, to the whole of earth, and beyond, bringing this mindfulness to each moment as we go about our lives....
