Archbishop Tutu who says in Africa when you ask someone "How are you?" the reply you
get is always in the plural even if you're speaking to one person. If you ask "How are you?" a
man would say, "We are well," or, "We are not well."


---
From Gemini:

Archbishop Desmond Tutu's statement highlights the concept of **Ubuntu** in African cultures. Ubuntu is a philosophy that emphasizes interconnectedness and humanity. Here's how it explains the response to "How are you?":

- **Individual wellbeing is tied to the community's wellbeing:** In Ubuntu, a person's well-being is seen as fundamentally linked to the well-being of the entire community. So, even when responding to an individual question, the answer reflects the state of the collective.
    
- **Sharing and communal spirit:** By saying "We are well" or "We are not well," the person acknowledges that their situation is intertwined with others'. It reflects a spirit of sharing and a sense of responsibility towards the community.
    
- **Not a literal reflection of everyone:** It's important to note that it doesn't necessarily mean everyone in the community is exactly the same. It's more about acknowledging the interconnectedness and expressing a shared experience.
    

So, Archbishop Tutu is using this example to illustrate the core principle of Ubuntu - **"A person is a person through other people"**.
