> "Again, a learned noble disciple contemplates in this way: “This world is empty, empty of a self, empty of what belongs to a self, empty of what is permanent, empty of what is everlasting, empty of existing continuously, and empty of being unchanging.”
> 
> Practicing and training in this way, developing [the mind] in this way in a broad and extensive manner, one easily attains purity of the mind in regard to that sphere. Having attained purity of the mind in regard to that sphere ... one will attain entry into the sphere of nothingness herein, or else employ wisdom for the sake of liberation. 

The Point Being
The Meditative Approach to nothingness is thru the contemplation that this world experience is empty of a self and of what belongs to a self.

This is a natural progression with the overall dynamics of the teaching
	with contemplation of infinite consciousness we come to the realization that
	there is nothing in the sense that there is nothing of a self and nothing that belongs to a self

But with this Contemplation of Not-self
	there is the possible to merely attaining sphere of nothingness 
	instead of full awakening - we can mistake this for the goal
	A profound experience of emptiness does not necessarily lead to awakening 
	even if having the insight that all this is empty of a self

So Sphere of nothingness falls short of liberation
	What is Important is the need to eradicate the defilements

All these insights are impermanent - dukkha - and subject to cessation
	and usually Reckoned with as one's notion of their identity

# NOT-SELF
1st Sermon by the Buddha that he gave was on the 
4 Noble Truths
2nd discourse to the same five former companions
was a teaching of not-self leading 

> Bodily form is not-self. If bodily form were the self, then it should not happen that disease and pain arise in relation to bodily form, and one should not get the wish for bodily form to be in this way and not in that way. Because bodily form is not-self, there is the arising of disease and pain in relation to bodily form and one gets the wish for bodily form to be in this way and not to be in that way. Feeling ... perception ... formations ... consciousness is also like this.

Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

The main points to this teaching was that there is:
1. A self that is not in control and cannot have everything it wishes
2. that the Body can get sick
3. that Feelings are sometimes painful
4. that perceptions not always enjoyable
5. that one decisions do not always lead to desired results
6. that consciousness does not experience only what one wishes
7. that we can influence things to a certain extent, but they are clearly beyond one's complete control.

The idea is 
A. Aggregates impermanent and subject to change
B. Dukkha in that they cannot provide lasting satisfaction
C. Since they are dukkha, they are not fit to be regarded as a self

It is important to note that what is being pointed out here 
We are defining the type of self in question:
	That there is Not a self that is <u>permanent</u> - 
	and as a result "Not a source of unalloyed happiness" (it is conditioned)(not  pure)

Because it is not self 
then it should be examined in that way.
When understood, the result is that one learns to not cling to anything in the world
Not clinging, one is not attached
Not being attached on realizes Nirvana

In the Samyutta Nikaya it is summarized in this way:
	When seeing the aggregates as they really are
	One becomes disenchanted
	Once disenchanted one Becomes dispassionate about what clung to
	Once dispassionate one Becomes liberated

> In short, not identifying with any aspect of experience—be this body, feeling, perception, formations, or consciousness—is the path to freedom.
> 
Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition.
