MMR#80 - Role of Effort and Self View in Practice
As I mentioned earlier, I had thought about the subject of practicing Mindfulness Internally -
Externally and both Internally and Externally - which is a refrain from the Satitpatthana that is
repeated over and over in the instructions on Mindfulness, and, yet, this talk is primarily NOT
about this but more in line with that of the “Role of Effort and Self View in Practice”. And as you
shall see, these teachings are connected. Our internal perspectives and actions have a huge
role in how we see ourselves in the external world.
Now many “good” buddhists would think that having a goal is something to be looked down
upon. Like it feels contrary to seeking a true spiritual reality. It can be thought that - to be really
wise and awake is to have no goal and no path. But the Buddha did talk about a path and the
goal. The Noble Eightfold Path. And the goal being freedom, release, enlightenment.
He said many times that “If this is developed... it would be of great fruit and of great benefit.”
The word “Bhavana” - development - which also a word for meditation.
So having a path is not going against the teachings for spiritual progress.
The problem is that we, as human beings, turn this path into something that is not. We place
value on Effort to get somewhere, on striving, on attainment. Many of us think about being
peaceful as being something we experience in the future. That we will get the conditions right at
some point in the future when it’s just right. Like being able to relax and at peace when the
weekend comes, or to relax when the meditation bell rings when we stop all of this striving.
When the task is completed. Then we can relax. Thinking we can be at ease after this work gets
done. Waiting for this future thing so there can be peace at that time. Like looking forward to
going on vacation or to the holidays.
Even the Activity of the mind can be seen as an intrusion to peace. I’ve got to stop thinking or
worrying, or striving to be at peace.
Right Effort is part of the path. The manner in which work can be done in accordance with
peace - it can be a way of peace. It’s not having a direction or the activity itself that’s a problem.
It is the attitude that the work is held in. Attitude is everything. Even we are alone with
nothing going on you can be stressed.
In a brief teaching from Dawn Scott, a local Insight Meditation Teacher and head of the Family
Program at Spirit Rock, she says”
“There’s no need to banish our longings or our aspirations because we can recognize the gold
and the beauty at the center of them. When we relate to them wisely, they can fuel our practice.
They can be onward-leading.
That said, if we forget to invite openness and kindness, our longings can fuel tension or
over-efforting. And then we start to shame and blame ourselves when our practice at the current
moment doesn’t match up with our aspirations.

That’s when the inner critic can come in and make our inner world feel so untenable and so
oppressive that we don’t even want to practice. So we want to protect our aspirations and the
gold within them, making sure that they’re not hijacked by the inner critic and over-efforting.”
This tension and over-efforting that she refers to -this is the 2nd Nobel Truth - the cause for
suffering - craving. Tanha. There are 3 kinds of craving that the Buddha emphasized.
1- Craving for sense-pleasures - searching for something interesting or exciting - for good food -
beautiful things to see, feel, taste touch and think about.
2- Craving for Becoming - to be something - to gain something - to get something
3- Craving for Non-becoming - the opposite - to not be - to get rid of - for annihilation.
This craving for sense-pleasures we know in terms of addiction and recovery is extremely
powerful. But the other two can be more difficult to be aware of. It is with these that we can
make a problem of Effort.
Doing can feel really stressful and we can’t wait for it to end. And when the heart gets tied up
with craving - this is the cause of our distress and dissatisfaction. And it can disguise itself in
meditation. There can arise a sense of self that says that “I have to get rid of my distracting
thoughts” or “I want to experience something else other than what is present.” This is the habit
of “I” making and “my” making. This desire to become or the desire to get rid of. This is
Self-view and conceit. “Me” trying to get somewhere so I can be that way when I do. The
problem is “I have to”.
But at the same time when those qualities are recognized and let go of - then effort can be
made & direction taken that is free of self-view. What then guides us in our choices or direction
is then coming from a place of being mindful and wise, rather than from wanting to be or not be
like that. Rather than trying to find peace at some ideal time in the future, with mindfulness and
wisdom in the present moment, even the act of doing - of making choices, it is itself is
peaceful...
So this is Right Effort. It is regarded to contain 4 elements.
1- Restraining the unwholesome
2- Recognizing and letting go of that that is unwholesome that has arisen
3- Conscious cultivation of the wholesome
4- Maintaining the wholesome. In other words, protecting what you got.
This is different than what it feels like when we are not mindful & wise when we are caught in
the attitude of “I’ve got to do this!!!” With this “I” making and “my” making more apparent with
mindfulness - we can be aware that a stressful tone is present. We can recognize this tensing.
Stress is known. This is the task of the 1st Noble Truth - to understand.
So with regard to the path and goals, it is okay to place to exert energy into that. With
mindfulness & wisdom, it is not stressful - there is no tension in the heart. We are not waiting for
it to be over in order to be peaceful.

Ajahn Chah would council many people who would complain about not having time to practice -
they had to take care of the kids, their jobs and business, etc... He would listen to them and
then ask them if they had time to go to the bathroom, time to eat, and to breath? And they’d say
of course. And he’s say “then you have time to practice.”
The practice is in all parts of our lives. As we go about living we can pay attention to what we
are doing - aware of our thoughts - feeling the moods as we go. There is no distinction between
formal meditation and daily life. This is where it ties into our being aware of the internal and the
external and both the internal & the external. Wherever we are at internally or externally we
bring awareness to that. We can bring awareness to our attitudes of liking and disliking.
Opening the field of practice. Keeping our attention focused in a deliberate way whether we are
feeling comfortable or in discomfort. Whatever the reality is not a problem then. We use our
environment to develop skills - open to a broader range of experience.
When we open the heart - liking and not liking - are of equal value - likewise with success or
failure. Whether something is appreciated or doesn’t have the same value. Whether in a worldly
way it is good or bad - conditioned or unconditioned.
When the field of practice is opened then we realize the possibility of wisdom and the capacity
to be awake. We are aware that suffering is ever-present, yet, even in that, we can notice how
this moment is being held.
This desire for peace and freedom is not to be found when something stops so we can get to it,
it’s is here in our attitude of how the heart experiences this moment.
With the conditioned world - this presents limitations. But the mind that is aware is limitless.
Intrinsically free. That that knows a person is not a person. This knowing is aware and awake. It
is not young or old, American or French, sickly or in good health, rich or poor, not a high school
drop-out or someone with a PhD. It doesn’t have those personal qualities. It does not have any
qualities other than knowing what is being know. We can be awake and attuned to the world but
not be limited by the world.
There is a saying in pali “vija chacarana sampano” which is worthy of contemplation for us. Vija
means “knowing or awareness or the awakened aware quality of the heart.” Which has it’s
opposite of “avija” - or ignorance - not knowing. And then there is “chacarana” which means
conduct or activity. They are a partnership - a combination.
So the Buddha’s transcendent wisdom and quality of awareness, which is boundless, limitless,
timeless, non-personal, and, its partner, activity is that kind of transcendent awareness that is
not disassociated or spaced out. But fully attuned to the world. So that when the mind is fully
awake, fully aware as that boundless quality that is realized, it is perfectly attuned to the time
and the place and the situation.

That was one of the qualities of the Buddha. He was extraordinarily attuned and observant with
the people who he was with at each moment. Attuned - sensitive to the time, the place, the
situation.
So this quality of chacarana or activity - it’s that perfect wisdom or awakened aware quality that
takes shape as being as a mindful, caring activity in the world. Harmless - honest - having a
generosity of spirit. The quality of complete nonattachment. A boundless heart that’s partnered
with a sensitivity and attunement to the moment. One can say something or not, be serious or
crack a joke, be warm and friendly or cool and remote. This is for One who has a heart that
can adapt and be free.

So that was part 1. I’d also like to briefly talk about another aspect of effort which is selfless -
the possibility of Stream Entry as a Goal. Now I should provide a disclaimer right now, that I
have not reached stream-entry. Many teachers would shy away or avoid speaking of any
attainments because, as I mentioned earlier, we can get caught in our world-building and
goal-oriented attitudes. But as part of the path, with Right Effort, we can set the Intention - set a
direction for one’s life that is in line with the teachings and the goal. Again, this is another area
that runs counter to conventional Buddhist wisdom. For there is this idea that “If you are a good
Buddhist then you have no ambitions.”
We can set a conscious intention to use this life to understand the true nature of things and to
live in a way that is leading towards the abandonment of Greed Hatred & Delusion and the
cultivation of virtue, mental focus, and wisdom. This is a beautiful thing. And, in the teachings,
the value of stream-entry is emphasized.
The Buddha once asked his followers, as he scratched the surface of the dirt with his fingernail,
if the amount of dirt under his fingernail would be more or less than the great earth itself. They
answered that the amount of dirt under his fingernail would be very small in comparison.
Similarly, the amount of suffering for one who has reached stream-entry that they can expect to
experience in the future is comparable to the amount of dirt under his fingernail. And the amount
of suffering for a person who has not reached stream-entry is comparable to the great earth.
This is an encouragement that we can take this seriously - it is in our world of possibility to
awaken. The conditions that are needed to develop this are not out of reach for those who hear
the dhamma.
So this is different than finding interesting things to latch onto to use to escape from or switching
off and not being connected. We can choose to follow the track of how to be awake, how to
recognize the things that obstruct the heart, how to let go of them, and how to free the heart
from its apparent limitation.
There are some teachings 4 qualities - that are supportive of stream-entry:

#1 is to draw close to good-hearted people - This means we might ask - who do you choose to
spend your time with? And what media do you fill your mind with?
The 2nd is listening to good teachings - what do you do with your time? Do we pay attention to
that which will lead to clarity and the unburdening of the heart -does what we listen to or watch -
does it lead to more complication - to greater self-centeredness, to self-inflation or to
self-criticism? We can choose to attend to things - to read things that are conductive of genuine
liberation - to awaken to things that ennoble the heart.
The 3rd quality is wise reflection - yoniso manusakara - an inner curiosity - looking - examining -
exploring - Curious as to why the mind feels excited or sleepy or depressed or sad - how it goes
about liking things or disliking things - seeing the processes of your own mind - looking at your
mind - your character - to cultivate a rounded intelligence - not just cruising along - but getting to
the root of things.
And the 4th quality is practicing dhamma in accordance with the dhamma - this is not self-view -
It’s not trying to get rid of my defilements or thinking I am better than some other - not the
worldly mentality - not wanting to be the most impressive meditator. This can take over the
efforts of our practice. It can appear that we are putting in Right Effort and we are sincere. But
this “I” making and “my” making can slide into the picture. Many dedicated people to this
practice find them caught into this trap that hinders our development. Even those with a
dedicated practice can be consumed and obstructed by conceit.
We will want to learn how to practice the dharma free of self-view - so what guides the effort and
the direction of practice is mindfulness and wisdom.
When there is the thought “i'm an unenlightened person who's got who's got to do something
now in order to become enlightened in the future.” That's wrong view!!! If you start from that - if
you start from ignorance you end up with dukkha. The thought at “I am an unenlightened
person” - being your wrong view. Notice that the mind is saying “I AM”. Instead we can BE
AWAKE NOW as our knowing. Not buying into self-view. Eventually, we can begin to see. Be
aware of buying into self-view - not seeing that as an absolute reality - and instead say - “being
awake now.”
That mind which knows the person isn't a person. That aware awake quality - knows the body -
knows the personality - and those feelings and perceptions arising and passing.
But that awareness isn't a person - it's the awake mind knowing the way things are here and
now.
So you change the paradigm from the person who has to do something now to be enlightened
in the future to simply that of seeing the dhamma - being awake to the way the things are here
and now.
Sincere effort can be made with their being me - the doer. But Effort with mindfulness and
wisdom that’s a different quality. Freeing that effort from self-view, from conceit - then this is
supportive of that realization of stream-entry. This is not beyond us.

Those 4 qualities are, if recollected and put into good use, that which creates the conditions -
creates the environment that is most supportive and helpful for the realization of the
breakthrough of seeing the deathless.
I close with this from Charlotte Joko Beck, founding teacher of the Zen Center of San Diego:
“In the realm of no liking or disliking, the world is absolutely perfect. We do each single thing
with no discrimination. Suppose you’re trying to work, you’re really busy, and the phone rings.
We resent the ringing, because we’re trying to work. But the problem isn’t the phone ringing. It’s
just ringing. It’s not good or bad.
When we become more able to notice and experience this phone ringing, life gets less rigid.
Because life is always changing, we become more able to move with it as opposed to being
stuck. Being in harmony means you’re experiencing yourself as an integral part of life instead of
as someone who is opposing a piece of life. When you experience harmony, you know it.”

I offer this for your consideration.
