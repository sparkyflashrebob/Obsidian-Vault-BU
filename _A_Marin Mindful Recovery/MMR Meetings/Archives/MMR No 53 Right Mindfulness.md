What is mindfulness?
● Buddhist word Sati
● Spacious Presence
● Focused attention
● Wakefulness to our experience
● It is the Recollecting & recognition of direct experience
● While Awareness is more sensory-based state of mind that is the mental concept that
allows us to be more present in the world around us, mindfulness is a state of mind that
involves more introspection and thought process adjustment.
● The most common metaphor for sati is that of a cowherd watching over his cows - if
there are fields that they should not enter because they would damage the crops the
cowherd would need to be extra vigilent to keep them out of them but if the crops have
been already farmed then the cowherd could relax.
● Mindfulness relates to our innate ability to be fully present in the moment
● It is a Non-judging Awareness of our current experience that knows

“If you are depressed you are living in the past – If you are anxious you are living in
the future – If you are at peace you are living in the present.” ~ Lao Tzu Taoist Master
Generally, Mindfulness allows us to be:
● Relaxed
● Aware
● Receptive
● & Inclusive
● However, as will explore some tonight, Andrew Olendzki is quoted saying that:
“True mindfulness is deeply and inextricably embedded in the notion of
wholesomeness. . . . Just as a tree removed from the forest is no longer a tree
but a piece of lumber, so also the caring attentiveness of mindfulness, extracted
from it’s matrix of wholesome co-arising factors, degenerates into mere
attention.” (Clear View Blog)
● To be right - Mindfulness - in this case it is taken within the context of the
Buddhism framework of understanding suffering, the cause, and it cessation.
Within the framework of the Noble 8fold path.
● Mindfulness is a common mental factor of mind
● Common (otherwise known as universal) mental factors include feeling, perception,
volition, contact, the desire to act, wisdom, attention, decision, mental concentration
otherwise known as one-pointedness, and of course mindfulness.
● While any mindfulness is a good thing - as it's one of the seven factors of enlightenment
and the one that acts as a catalyst of the others - the more mindfulness, the better.

How to be Mindful
“What are the four? A person dwells contemplating the body in the body, ardent,
clearly comprehending, and mindful, having subdued longing and dejection in regard
to the world. He or she dwells contemplating feelings in feelings, ardent, clearly
comprehending, mindful, having subdued longing and dejection in regard to the
world. He or she dwells contemplating mind in mind, ardent, clearly comprehending,
mindful, having subdued longing and dejection in regard Right Mindfulness to the
world. He or she dwells contemplating phenomena in phenomena, ardent, clearly
comprehending, mindful, having subdued longing and dejection in regard to the
world.” [In the Buddha’s Words., p. 281]

● Contemplating body in the body
○ Means being aware of immediate, experiential phenomena as it is without
conceptualization or commentary.
○ An example of this is awareness of sensations of the rising breath in the belly
● Feeling in Feelings
○ Sense of subtle feeling of a sound being mildly unpleasant, liking or not liking,
gravitating towards or away from what we sense

● Mind in Mind
○ Sense of our consciousness being contracted or spacious
○ Aware of a single thought emerging and then disappearing
○ Pure Awareness
● Phenomena in phenomena
○ Increasing absorbed by its objects with growing concentration
○ Vanishingly little difference between the observer and the observed
○ Spotlight illuminating the nature of mind and reality
○ Insight into the causes of suffering and into the causes leading to the end of
suffering
○ Remember that this is our primary task, the goal

Mindfulness is the counter to our habitual state of mind.
● Image of us in a lovely & peaceful meadow
● Train full of thoughts & feelings & desires rolling by in the distance
● Normally as the train approaches we hop aboard and get carried away
● Mindfulness allows us to see the train coming but have the presence of mind to stay in
the meadow

● If not, even if we get carried along, as soon as we become mindful we can immediately
return to the peaceful meadow

Where is Mindfulness to be Established?
● Four Frames of Reference
● Body as a body but also as a reference for subjective experience such as sensations,
sights, sound, smells, and tastes
● Feeling - not emotions but tones of pleasant, or unpleasant or neutral tones that come
with every experience
● Mind - Consciousness and states of consciousness
● Phenomena - all of the other contents of the mind such as thoughts, emotions, desires,
plans, view, etc.

Mindfulness in Meditation
● Primary opportunity for meditation is as an opportunity for practice and to cultivate
mindfulness. As we have noted, it is the primary catalyst for the other factors. When we
are not mindful it is as if we are run by our habitual tendencies.
● Progressive process - the mind is steadied internally, quieted and brought to singleness
and concentration, and leading to liberating insight

Continuity of Mindfulness
● Not reserved for meditation
● Intended to be cultivated and used as a tool throughout the day
● As mentioned in the Satipatthana - to be aware of the body, position of the body, the
movement & activities of the body, aware of our body internally and the body of others
externally, and both internally & externally in relation to others.

Results of Mindfulness
● Mentioned some of the benefits earlier, but...
● Ongoing mindfulness helps to become aware of larger and long-term patterns of cause &
effect so it is a means of gaining insight.
● It allows us to use daily, householder life for liberating practice and understanding.
Some of the Insights that we become aware of as a result of being mindful:
● Seeing the three Characteristics
○ Anicca - impermanence - change

○ Anatta - that everything is interdependent of everything else, nothing is inherent
and independent of nature or existence, including a self. This means that
everything is empty.
○ Dukkha - suffering
○ As a result these insights become liberating

TOTAL MINDFULNESS
The possibilities of total mindfulness "The . . . concentration . . . referred to in the eightfold path
as right concentration, or perfect concentration . . .[is] developed on a moment-to-moment basis
in insight meditation. Only moment-to-moment concentration following the path of mindfulness
leads to the destruction of defilements. This concentration is not developed by fixing the mind
motionless to one object, but by being mindful of the changing bodily sensations, feelings,
consciousness, and mind objects. When properly established in the inner body and mind,
moment-to-moment concentration leads to the destruction of the rounds of rebirth. Through this
concentration, we develop the ability to see clearly the five aggregates---form, feeling,
perception, volition, and consciousness---which make up what we conventionally call men and
women." (Living Dharma, pps. 260-261)

Right Mindfulness
● For it to be Right Mindfulness (samma sati) it must be contained withing the buddhist
framework, which means that it must include skillfulness in terms of sila and help us with
the understanding of and ending of suffering.
● the Buddha used the terms “ardent” (atapi) and thorough understanding or
“discrimination” (sampajanna).
● These are the aspects of mindfulness he described in the prefect to the Satipatthana
Sutta
● Mindfulness must include and be at the service of liberation
● Not just Philosophy
● Emotion skills
● It means that we work towards restraint overcoming the tendency towards greed, hatred
and delusion.
● Reduce suffering
● Greed hatred and delusion
● Greed endless discontent lacking and we have ideas to fill that lack
● The sources of craving & clinging - Sense desire, becoming, if only....
● Right mindfulness - relieve that sense of lack
● Sustained contentment
● Requires us to cultivate the other aspects of the path
