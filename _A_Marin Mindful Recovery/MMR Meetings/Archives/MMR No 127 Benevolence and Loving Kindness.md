MMR No 127 Benevolence and Loving Kindness

Continue with what are considered the divine abodes in Buddhism
Cultivating a mind imbued with gratitude, compassion, joy, kindness, patience and tolerance.
To counter act the forces of suffering so apparent in the world.
Compassion (or karuna) is actually the 2nd among 4 brahmaviharas
1st is lovingkindness (metta)
● Analyo prefers benevolence
● Comes from the root word - mitra in pali = "friend"
● basic sense of friendliness and an attitude of friendship
● Means to act for mutual benefit, for being of assistance or helping another
● In general, It is not the kind of love say a mother to their child
○ In pali there is another word for that (apekkha, pema or piya)
● However the metta sutta gives this impression
○ Just as a mother who has an only son would protect her own son with her life, so
one should cultivate a boundless mind towards all living beings.
○ Protection is the main theme here
● providing and receiving protection is a re-occuring aspect of metta in the early
discourses
● examples of metta can include being able to project one from snake bites, or against
non-human beings
● Metta-Sutta encourages cultivation of an attitude of protection towards others
○ So it is not of a form of love
○ Which is seen as a form of attachment
● Metta should be boundless and free from attachment
● It is also the most frequently mentioned brahmavihara
● It provides the Foundation for the cultivation of the other three, compassion, sympathetic
joy and equanimity
● This is given by example in as aSimile as a TREE OF COMPASSION
○ water is the metta that nourishes the tree
○ Illustrating how metta nourishes the root of the tree
● Metta can be Expressed thru bodily, verbal and mental activities
● One of six underlying principles that lead to cordiality in one's relationship to others
○ Reciprocity
○ Autonomy
○ Honesty
○ Equity
○ Loyalty
○ Integrity
● It is also the only brahmavihara explicitly highlighted in the early discourses
● And it is used as a testing ground for one's meditation practice
● As such is should permeate all aspects of one's behaviours and activities

● It also provides the fertile soil for compassion and other brahmaviharas to grow and
flourish
● A foundation of kindness should be prevalent in one's activities, one’s communications,
and one’s mental attitude
● It also shows up in the broader scope for expressing one's compassionate intent
● The idea for us is to cultivate the appropriate attitude to others and perhaps more
ordinary daily activities
● These can best when under the mode of metta

One of the major benefits is when FACING AGGRESSION:
● Metta can come to one's aid when having to face others who act aggressively
● Simile of the SAW
○ Many examples in response to a monk and bhikkhunis who were defending each
other for to intimate/friendly with each other and being called out for it.
○ Buddha encouraged them “to abandon what is unwholesome and devote yourself
to wholesome states”
○ Two similes
■ Kali the jealous maid and her housewife Vedehika.
● Tested the nerve of Vedehika
○ Habitually waking up late, not cleaning up properly
○ Getting Vedehika so mad that she hit her with a rollin-pin
● Types of speech you may experience
● One is extremely kind, gentle and peaceful as long as one does
experience disagreeable speech but let goes of that if so.

■ Speech
● Timely or untimely
● True or untrue
● Connected with good or harm
● Spoken with a mind of loving-kindness or with inner hate
● 'Our minds will remain unaffected, and we shall utter no evil
words; we shall abide compassionate for their welfare, with a mind
of loving-kindness, without inner hate. We shall abide pervading
that person with a mind imbued with loving kindness, and starting
with him, we shall abide pervading the all-encompassing world
with a mind imbued with loving kindness, abundant, exalted,
immeasurable, without hostility and without ill will.' That is how you
should train, bhikkhus.

MENTAL BEAUTY:
● when anger arises, even if within, metta is the antidote
● ill-will cannot arise in someone who cultivates liberation of mind by metta
● having overcome ill will, one achieves metta
○ metta directly opposes ill will
● deprive of its nurishment ans sustenence
○ Not feeding it
● metta is the way to face ill will, anger, and aggression whether arisen in oneself or
confronted by others under the influence
● if one has to reprimand another - it should not be done with an angry mind
○ Refer to right speech noted above
● when being criticized, justified or not, an attitude of metta is beneficial
● those who become angry have an ugly appearance (TRUE)
● We can verify this in our own experience
● A tendency to be angry leads to being reborn ugly (karma)
○ Not to judge those who are born unattractive
○ highlight repercussions of being carried away by anger and irritation
● The point is that cultivation of metta leads the mind to supreme beauty.
○ Physical and mental beauty
○ one's action and words
● one rooted in kindness and compassion
● An attitude of kindness and benevolence is recommended for any situation
● So metta is the forerunner on the path to true beauty
● Also brings into the light the beauty of the compassionate mind
● compliments the other two brahmaviharas
○ Sympathetic joy and equanimity
