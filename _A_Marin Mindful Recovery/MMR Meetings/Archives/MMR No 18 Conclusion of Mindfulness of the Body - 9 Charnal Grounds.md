We start not with the Satipatthana today but a story of the Four Heavenly Messengers.
The Buddha was born into royalty, a prince and led a sheltered experience and it is said that he
did not venture out of his palace for the first time until he was 29 years old.
And when he did he first ran into an old man - he was reminded that this happens to all beings
alike.
2ndly he ran into a sick person suffering from a disease - it was explained to him that all beings
are subject to disease and pain. This too troubled him.
The 3rd sight was of a dead body. This too was explained to him as being inevitable - this fate
befalls everyone. So he was awakened to the suffering that have to be endured in life.
It is said at this point that he ran into an ascetic who had devoted himself to find the cause of
human suffering. This gave him hope that he too might be released from the suffering of being
born and vowed to follow his example.

Luckily the Buddha shared with us this path of awakening. We are currently looking at what the
Buddha considered to be a key tool for us. To be Mindful.
Looking at the teachings on the 4 Foundations of MIndfulness.
This practice is effective if are Relaxed - interested in our own welfare and that of others -
interested in being awake and aware - not lost - not easily swayed - without greed or distress in
the world - this term we used from the Pali language - Samapajhana.
We conclude today the first of the 4 Satipatthanas - Mindful of the Body.
Basic awareness of breathing - knowing when we are breathing in or out, the quality of our
breathing, deep or shallow, fast or slow. Breathing
Training the mind to know breathing experiencing the whole body, calming the body.
Contemplation of this awareness of the body internally, and externally and both internally and
externally.
Contemplating the nature of arising and passing away. We breathe in and then we breathe out
and then there is another breath as we breathe in and we breath out.
Being mindful that there is a body extent for bare knowledge and mindfulness.
This quality of knowing there is a body.
Connecting with the one who knows..... the watcher... the observer....

Extending this awareness into all of our activities. Whether walking, standing, sitting or lying
down. Being fully aware as one acts when walking about, when looking at things one way or the
other, when flexing or extending our limbs, when wearing clothes or carrying things, when
eating, drinking and tasting, when going to the bathroom. In all activities.
Our awareness of the Body also includes contemplation of the body.A few weeks ago we took at
look at the parts of the body.
We look at ALL of the parts of the body, not just the heart or the brain but all aspects. The skin,
the hair, the teeth, the organs, fluids, the muscles and connecting tissue, and the waste matter.
One of the reasons we do this is so that we get a proper perspective of the body. One the one
hand, it is this miraculous and wonderful vessel in which we live our lives. And on the other, it
ages, and has complications. Some of us are lucky to perfectly functioning and beautiful bodies
for awhile... and to do so we have to maintain it constantly...... but that all changes.
We can also contemplate this body in terms of the elements. I read one estimate that when you
add up all of the elements contained within the typical body - it is worth just over $160
This same article said that if you sell the bone marrow, DNA, lungs, etc it could be worth up to
$45 million.
By the elements here we use the earth elements, the wind element, the water element and the
fire element. It is thought that the earth provides solidity, water element cohesion, fire element
heat or cold, and the air element expansion.
As we take a look at how these elements feel in the body. We can be aware of these aspects in
the body. One of the most skillful ways we can use this is by working with Mindfulness, being
mindful of the breath in the body. Different aspects and ways of breathing. Breath as energy.
Breathing as healing..... Thru the bones or into and out of pain.
In dealing with emotions, for instance anger, we can feel hot with emotion, being mindful of
cooling down can bring about a more peaceful state.
So then lastly in this section, we are asked to look at the Nine Charnel Ground Contemplations,
which is primarily teaching on impermanence.
The first as if we are seeing a corpse thrown aside in a charnal ground, which is similar to our
modern and sanitized cemeteries).
To see it one, two or three days dead, bloated, dark blue gray in color, and oozing. Comparing
our body with that one - "this body too is of the same nature, it will be like that, it is not exempt
from that fate".
Another as seen being eaten by scavengers, and worms... again reflecting that "this body too is
of the same nature, it will be like that, it is not exempt from that fate".
Continuing on with these contemplations, seeing the insides exposed - the bones and organs
and connecting tissue. Again reflected that "this body too is of the same nature, it will be like
that, it is not exempt from that fate".
And finally reflecting on the body being turned to a pile of bones and ash, blowing away in the
wind. "this body too is of the same nature, it will be like that, it is not exempt from that fate".

"These three are the marks of conditioned existence. What are the three? One knows whence it

arises, one knows that it will change, and one knows that it will cease and disappear.
How does one know whence it arises? It is birth, the growing up and maturing of these five
aggregates of clinging, the acquiring of all the elements and sense-spheres – this is whence it
arises.
What is its cessation? It is death, the passing away and discontinuity of life, its impermanence,
the breaking up of the aggregates, one’s being separated from one’s clan and family, the life
faculty being cut off – this is its cessation.
What is its change? It is loss of teeth, whiteness of hair, exhaustion of one’s energy and strength
with the waxing and waning of the years, the falling apart of the body – this is its nature of being
affected by change."
_______________
Modern Day charnel ground are morgues. In Thailand Monks and Nuns allowed to observe
Autopsies. One of my teachers Guy Armstrong describes his experience in a morgue and
seeing autopsies performed for the first time. After seeing this he had to travel back to the
monetary where he was staying, to take a bus, and he could not help but see the people around
him as walking corpses, in his words. As he began to think about it he realized the only
difference between those corpses in the morgue and these walking corpses was the illumination
of their life forces, which all animals have, which is the light of our being. Which is our true
essence... Which is our path to realize.
In this practice we are urged to reflect on our deaths again and again. Part of the Native
American death lodges that we practice as part of a vision quest indicates this practice,
especially in the last night where are also encouraged to stay us all night.
It is a path of gratitude and connection. The Buddha is said to have suggested that one who
practices mindfulness of death should be aware of the fact that even the next breath might not
take place. To not even take this current breath for granted. To bring our awareness of mortality
into the present moment.
There are a few versions of the Charnal Ground Contemplations in addition to this one in the
Satipatthana. From what at first seems a fairly gruesome practice - it can produce great joy if
undertaken properly because it leads to detachment. It can lead to the removable of
unwholesome thoughts and to being increasingly unaffected by worries and sorrow.***
In one, it is said that understanding this practice one "experiences joy in oneself". The joy
comes from the mental freedom of suffering from attachment to the body.
It is also a reflection on how we spend our time.
Old age, sickness and death are coming.
"Days and nights go by, what are we doing"
Young or old - we can use these reflections.
As we age.

As we lose others.
