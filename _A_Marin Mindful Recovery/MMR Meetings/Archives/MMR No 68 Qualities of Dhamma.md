MMR#68 Qualities of Dhamma
Taken from a talk by Ajahn Sumedho on “The Six Qualities of Dhamma”
It can helpful to think of each day as a timespan
Just like an inhalation and exhalation
In which to resolve to mindful - observant - alert - paying attention
Reflecting on the Buddha, Dhamma & Sangha - a way of composing ourselves
As a group - thinking of us together - composing our bodies together as a group mediation
Reminding ourselves about Buddha, Dhamma & Sangha - just to keep reminding ourselves of
what they really are.
When we describe the qualities of the Buddha - of the merits of sharing, ethical morality,
patience, renunciation, wisdom, diligence, truthfulness, determination, loving-kindness and
equanimity - these paramita’s, this is not about an entity outside, a historical event in time but
that very knowing, that alertness of being. Being aware of all that arises - passes away - and is
not self.
Meditation practice is the practice of being in the present moment. Not believing the thoughts,
memories, doubts and worries that cross the mind.
We are Aware of them - not annihilating them or rejecting but not being caught up into them as
my problem - as me and mine. This is the function of the Buddha - the knowing - just that
knowing is enough!
The Buddha is the personification of wisdom. We use the word the Buddha rather than saying
my wisdom as a way of dispersing the tendency of claiming wisdom as me and mine but in the
ultimate truth wisdom doesn’t belong to any being, person -- an individual.
Then in the reflections of the dhamma
Sanditthiko, Akaliko, Ehipassiko, Opanayiko. Paccattham veditabbo vinnuhi
This is chanted in Thai Forest Monestaries every morning.
The Dhama has no personality - is not personified in any way - you cannot say dhamma is male
or female.
The Dhamma was NOT born thru a woman. Wasn’t conceived in any particular time.
Dhammo is not a historical sage.
Dhamma has no form. The Dhamma remains the formless - the unformed - that we cannot
conceive it through the senses.
Personifications are done through the Buddha & Sangha but the Dhamma remains impersonal.

START DEFINITIONS
The descriptions of the dhamma are not used like saying it is red or blue, but in terms like
sanditthiko - here and now - imminent - in this moment. Not something that is in time or space.
Akaliko is timeless. Kala in pali is time. Akala is not bound by time - a time bound condition.
This is what they call a Kaliyuga in the Hindu tradition - Kali-yuk.
Yuca is a period of time (a world age). Kali - in the Hindu tradition- represents darkness - the
dark age.
Or the attachment to time - much of our society is attached to time - to clocks - tos schedules.
The Hindu goddess Kali - this voracious goddess - mother Kali - she gives birth and then she
consumes what she gives birth too. That’s time isn’t it? Time consumes what it gives birth to.
Anything born in time is destroyed by time.
But the Dhamma is akala - not that way - not time bound.
Ehipassiko - encouraging investigation - come and see for yourself - here and now - not waiting
for future - it is not something that you will find at some future time. Not thinking, well tomorrow I
turn toward the Dhamma. Today I don’t have the time. - there is a sense of urgency - that you
have to go to it - to make an effort of turning toward it rather than waiting for it to come and tap
you on the shoulder and say “Here I am”. You have to make that effort - to be alert.
Opanayiko - moving inward - inclining inward - not seeking Dhamma is something out there -
that dhamma is in outer space or up in the sky - something separate. But inclining inward -
attentive to the conditions of your own mind. Seeing that Nibbana is in the mind - it is not
something that you will find by looking outward - trying to find it as an object of sense or thought.
Paccattaμ veditabbo viññþhi - that which is known thru direct experience. No one else can give
you the dhamma - not even the Buddha, Ajahn Sumdho, or Andy McGuire. Any teacher, any
human being cannot say “Here is the dhamma” - and you say - “Yes, this is it”.
Means you have to put the effort in you own life - be alert and attentive and then you will know -
thru direct experience.
Just like the taste of honey - you only know its taste and flavor when you actually taste it
yourself. My tasting doesn’t really help you to understand what it is. You do not know the flavor
of it. I might be able to write a great poem about it or give you the formula for honey, but you
still don’t know the flavor of it until you taste it.
The same with the Dhamma - the tasting of the dhamma - like we are doing in meditation.

END DEFINITIONS

So if you noticed, when we chart these characteristics of Dhamma you really don’t know
anything about them - other than you have to be alert and mindful. You cannot conceive of
anything that is timeless, can you? It is not bound by time - it is timeless.
What do you mean by time? What can be outside of time? Try to think of something out of time.
Our thoughts, our conditions are time bound themselves. So obviously THOUGHT is not the
way to seek the Dhamma. Is it?
We could try to write poetry about the dhamma - write ecstatic poetry of the Dhamma. “Oh, the
joy, the bliss of the perfect peace. It is the supremist - the ultimatist” You can use the most
superlative forms of all adjectives.
In the Zen tradition sometimes they try to bring it down to just ordinariness - like gathering
firewood and drawing water from a well.
Language itself is not a vehicle for understanding the Dhamma. It is something that we have to
turn towards, in a direct way. An alertness, an attentiveness, a humility - giving up our own
personal interest in our personal interest and desires - our inclinations and habits. To be open -
to be open rather than to try to condition ourselves into beings becoming something else.
Sometimes people consider religious experience as kind of a conditioning process where you
make yourself into a saint - see yourself into a saint thru being very moral and thinking high
thoughts and doing good action. But even though we might do all these things - they're certainly
good to do that - if we do not really open and let go of our desires - our views and opinions -
and even to let go of goodness itself - saintliness itself then you will never know the Dhamma.
You might know about it. You have heard about it - obviously. But knowing is something is thru
direct experience - it is not knowing about. One can know about somebody. We have heard
about them but we don’t know them.
In the meditation, when we take refuge in Dhamma - it is an opening to the Ultimate Truth - not
trying to find it or think about it or conceive it or get Andy McGuire’s view of the dhamma. .
Or waiting for some powerful Sage to come around and zap you or take a drug like LSD or
something that that so you can have a real dharmic cosmic experience. So you say what is a
“cosmic dharmic experience”? It’s where you feel the absolute oneness with the ultimate
supreme..... You still don’t know what it is?
The words “cosmic dharmic experience” is just a silly expression describing ecstasy. In the
dhamma, we needn’t say what it is. It is something that we know only thru our direct experience
of it. That experience is not really an experience - it is an insightful way of letting go of self.
There is no longer anything to understand or know - an emptiness and freedom.
Now this puts the state of mind not in way of reaching out - of trying to find something. We have
to learn to be very, very patient and wait. Just wait. When we sit, walk, lie down - waiting
patiently - listening silently - nothing to do. You don’t have to go and look for it. If you start trying
to find something it would be like chasing shadows.

So In practice we take the attitude of just this gentle opening - silent listening - waiting patiently -
humbly - AND THAT IS ENOUGH.
That posture of silent waiting. Noting any hopes, aspirations. Noting but being caught up into
them, believing them. But say for today, this day, to just sit, walk, lay down, work -- waiting,
silently listening, watching, being alert from one moment to the next. Not thinking, well, first I
have to do this and then when the mind is quiet..... 😀
Not to try to make it some kind of exercise. “First I have to do my Yoga Practice, get my body
into proper condition, and then I’ll do pranayama and then I’d get all the chakras in alignment.
And then take on the Anapanasati to get calm and the letting go - and then the bare awareness
practice. Something simple and undemanding then becomes a whole system of mental,
physical exercises - views and opinions - a various assortment of clinging attachments - hopes -
expectations.
All kinds of mental conditions can arise and we aren’t even aware of it. We just believe.
So patient waiting and listening means that you shouldn’t even think that you shouldn’t think
that. But to be aware of the thinking process - the habitual tendencies of you mind - the
obsessions, fears, doubts and worries. Listen to them as the changing conditions.
In this practice we are not concerned with the quality of the conditions anymore. It is like
counting the sand - first we look at this grain of sand - we really like it - its color - its beauty - its
shape. And then we look at another grain of sand and we notice it is ugly, and disgusting. And
then we go to another grain of sand and we notice that it is all right - it’s ordinary - not really
beautiful. All that time lost. We can get lost in looking at each grain of sand and admiring it,
analysing it and reacting to its beauty or its ugliness. But all we need to know is that it is a grain
of sand. We don’t have to bother to count them all or analyze them. We are looking at that
which is common to all sand grains.
The same with conditions of the five khanhas of body and mind - these aggregates - they are
just conditions. Some are beautiful conditions, some ugly, some neither beautiful or ugly. They
are all just conditions. We are no longer trying to analyze each condition. Figure it out.
Entranced by its beauty or repelled by its ugliness. We are just looking at the condition as a
condition. The condition-ness of condition. The Sankharas in Pali. And know the characteristic
of all conditions is change. Anicca, anatta, dukkha. So we are not trying to figure out what
condition we should keep and what conditions we should throw away. But just patiently
listening and watching that any condition that arises passes away - is not self.
This is a different kind of looking at things in a different perspective than the kind of way you
have trained your minds before. When you become entranced by the quality of conditions or
fascinated or repelled or bored by the quality of thoughts you have or the memories that move
thru your mind. When you are caught up by the quality of conditions of mind and body of the
world around you. But this practice is no longer concentrating on quality but on the
characteristic of change. When I use this word condition I am talking about anything that arises
and passes away - has the characteristic of change.

These 5 khanadas or aggregates are impermanent. These categories include everything that
we perceive, conceive and know thru the senses, the body - in all the conditions of mind. We
reflect on the impermanent characteristics of these conditions. Likewise - the same with them
not being self - the body, not me not mine - feelings , not me not mine - memories- perceptions
of mind are not me - not mine, conceptions - thoughts are not me, not mine. Consciousness is
not me, not mine. All conditions are impermanent. All Dhamma is not self.
We can even let go of claiming that even the ultimate realization of personal attainment.
Practice is a constant, silent, listening - alertness & attentiveness in the moment and not
something you get an insight and suddenly, “oh, I’m enlightened”. Being you know, “I have
attained Nirvana”. That kind of thought itself is another condition - changing. Just the patient
knowing that even thoughts of that nature are impermanent - not self. Not to believe or grasp
any kind of insight or experience. The point is to not having a particular insight but living
insightfully in this moment and the next moment - being alert, attentive.
