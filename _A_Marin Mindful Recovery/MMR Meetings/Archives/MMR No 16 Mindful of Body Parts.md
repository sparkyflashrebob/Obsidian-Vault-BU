2nd Part:
The next 3 sections of contemplation of the body (contemplation of anatomical parts, of the
elements, and of a corpse in decay) are intended to cultivate insight into three dimensions of the
nature of the body. The first decontructs projections of beauty and sexual attractiveness onto the
body (be it our own or that of others).
The second reveals the empty nature of material existence as experience in the body (as weel
as outside of it).
The 3rd drives home the mortality of body (again of our own bodies and the bodies of others).
As suggested by Will's meditation, this week we will continue with mindfulness of the Body -
using the body as a strategy for calming the mind and developing liberating insight - looking just
at the first of these three areas - Contemplation of the body parts.
(4. Foulness— The Bodily Parts) 10. “Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu reviews this same body up
from the soles of the feet and down from the top of the hair, bounded by skin, as full of many
kinds of impurity thus: ‘In this body there are head-hairs, body-hairs, nails, teeth, skin, flesh,
sinews, bones, bone-marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, diaphragm, spleen, lungs, intestines,
mesentery, contents of the stomach, feces, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease,
spittle, snot, oil of the joints, and urine.’
Just as though there were a bag with an opening at both ends full of many sorts of grain, such
as hill rice, red rice, beans, peas, millet, and white rice, and a man with good eyes were to open
it and review it thus: ‘This is hill rice, this is red rice, these are beans, these are peas, this is
millet, this is white rice’; so too, a bhikkhu reviews this same body... as full of many kinds of
impurity thus: ‘In this body there are head-hairs... and urine.’ 11. “In this way he abides
contemplating the body as a body internally, externally, and both internally and externally... And
he abides independent, not clinging to anything in the world. That too is how a bhikkhu abides
contemplating the body as a body.

ADDITIONAL NOTES:
These body contemplations are meant as aids to cultivate ways of relating to the body without
clinging. We like to consider bodies as beautiful, clean, attractive and alluring. Based on a
particular view.
Seen as a whole, we are attracted to other bodies - it is in our instincts to replicate.
Young bodies are more cherished - more attractive -- while older bodies are not so cherished.
Appearance is important. More desireable and wanted.
Make them smell good and look good and feel good.
In relating to the body - we have to wash them, our clothes that touch the body, our bedding that
contacts the body.
Contemplation of the parts no so much. Hair may seem attractive on others but not so much in
your soup or salad.
Not so much as a problem of recognizing beauty - the problem lies in sensual desire. Connects
to teaching on craving and clinging as being stressful and the cause of suffering. Wanting and
not wanting.
Only a few minority of human beings fit into the tight mold of modern expectations of physical
beauty.
Most of us are too plump or too thin, too tall or too short, have this or that skin color, have one
aspect of our physical being or another that is not up to snuff.
Those who love us look past those aspects.
Sensual desire easily can get out of balance in respect to our instincts. Sensual desire cannot
be simply equated with love.
Dangerous form of mental illness. Keep mental balance. The more we indulge the stronger
senual desire will become.
Competition, jealousy, harming others.
Simile of a bird hold a piece of meat - pursued by other birds trying to get the meat. Unless the
bird lets go it risks being hurt of being killed by the other birds.
It is not lasting. It is incapable of lasting satisfaction. Likened to the taste of a bone to a dog.
Tied to our sense of identity - Males identifying with their sense of masculinity and finding joy in
it, likewise with a female which tends to identify with her sense of femininity and find joy in it.
Contemplation of the body intended to males and females transcending the narrow confines of
their sense of identity and open up the path of freedom.
Sensual Desire as one of the hindrances
Desire is not necessarily a hinderance - desire to develop ourselves and progress on the path is
praiseworthy.
The problem is the mistaken belief that true happiness can found by gratifying the senses.
With progress in practice it becomes increasingly clear that it is more worthwhile to cultivate the
mind in such a way that it gives rise to wholesome joy and happiness. We turn more towards the
goal -
This contemplation is used as a reminder - a reality check.

How traditionally practiced:
Steps for the practice:
Typicially grouped --
Group 1: head-hairs, body-hairs, nails, teeth, skin,
Group 2: flesh, sinews, bones, bone-marrow, kidneys,
Group 3: heart, liver, diaphragm, spleen, lungs,
Group 4: intestines, mesentery, contents of the stomach (gore), excrement, brain,
Group 5: bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat,
Group 6: tears, grease, saliva, snot, fluid of the joints (sonovial fluid), and urine.
Some folks promote Memorize in forward and reverse order.
Visualize/see each part in terms of color, shape, location, and surrounding matter.
Contemplate one group at a time, then combine groups until you can easily review the 32 parts
forward and backward.
Contemplate these parts as they occur within your body. Also contemplate them in other
people’s bodies.
See the world of bodies as collections of parts, not-self, not so personal, not so appealing,
nothing to become enthralled with or attached to.
CLOSING COMMENTS
--- Later on the brain was added to this list to form 32 parts.
-- In addition to sati (mindfulness) and samadhi (concentration), this form of meditation is
considered conducive to overcoming lust and desire.
-- It is also considered one of the four protective meditations, along with recollection of the
Buddha, matte (benevolence) and recollection of death.
-- Used to develop the four bases of power by which one is able to achieve liberation from
suffering - these four are concentration due to intention or purpose or desire or zeal,
concentration due to effort or energy or will, concentration due to consciousness or mind or
thoughts, and concentration due to investigation or discrimination.
-- This, and anapanasati (mindfulness of breathing) is one of the few body-directed meditations
suited for the development of samadhi/concentration.
-- There are some cautions -- contemplation on repulsiveness can be taken to an extreme.
One must balance this practice with loving-kindness and reflect upon how our body is the only
one we will ever have and that it is the vessel in which we live within on the path to freedom.
-- In some circles this practice is placed in context with the four elements, earth, air, water and
heat, which will be the subject of our discussion next week.
