[[4th Noble Truth]]
	NT of the way leading to the cessation of suffering
	this [[Noble Eightfold Path]] 
	with its [[three phases and twelves aspects]]
	And when he realized these truths
"Vision arose, insight arose, discernment arose, knowledge arose, illumination arose within him with regard to things never heard before 
	 (recited before each of the 12 permutations below) 
	 
This list of the three phases of each truth and twelve total aspects is how the
sutta was named. 
In ancient Indian philosophical and legal traditions this is know as a wheel.
	  Dhammacakkappattana meaning [[The Setting in Motion of the Wheel of Dhamma]]
These three phases and 12 aspects are:
1. This is the Noble Truth of Suffering
2. This noble truth of suffering is to be fully understood.
3. This noble truth of suffering has been fully understood.
4. This is noble truth of the origin of suffering.
5. This noble truth of suffering is to be abandoned.
6. This noble truth of suffering has been abandoned.
7. This is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering.
8. This noble truth of the cessation of suffering is to be realized.
9. This noble truth of the cessation of suffering has been realized.
10. This is the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering.
11. This noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering is to be developed.
12. This noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering has been developed.

The Buddha noted that:
> “So long, bhikkhus, as his knowledge and vision of these Four Noble Truths as they really are, in their three phases and twelve aspects, was not thoroughly purified in this way, I did not claim to have awakened to the unsurpassed perfect enlightenment in this world.

> But when his knowledge and vision of these Four Noble Truths as they really are, in their three phases and twelve aspects, was thoroughly purified in this way, then he claimed to have awakened to the unsurpassed perfect enlightenment in this world

When this was said is when the Wheel of the Dhamma is considered to have been set in motion.

### Hanging from a cliff - A Zen Tale from Japan

START HERE
There was once a man who was being chased by a tiger across a field. He ran and ran and ran to escape the ferocious tiger until suddenly...

… at the edge of the field, he bumped into a steep cliff. What could he do? There seemed nowhere to go from here but down. He had to make a hastily decision!

He noticed a vine hanging down from the cliff. In order to escape the jaws of the tiger, the man caught hold of the vine and swung himself over the edge. 

Dangling down, he saw, to his dismay, there were more tigers on the ground below him! And, furthermore, looking up again, he saw two little mice gnawing on the vine to which he clung. He knew that at any moment he would fall to certain death.

And that's when he noticed a wild strawberry growing on the cliff wall. It was bright, voluptuous and of the deepest red he had ever seen. Clutching the vine with one hand, he reached out for the wild berry and with the other hand and plucked it. He then put it in his mouth.


Ohhh… He never before realized how sweet a strawberry could taste!

And that is the end of the story...

### KNOWLEDGE AND VISION

In the Dhammacakkhapavattana Sutta the Buddha notes that when his knowledge and vision of these Four Noble Truths as they really are was thoroughly purified then he claimed to have awakened to the unsurpassed perfect enlightenment in this world. 

To me this points to this path of purification as being a process

[[Adyashanti]] notes that "
> The path to awakening is not about positive emotions. Enlightment may not be easy or positive at all.

> For those of us on a spiritual path, our whole identity may have been wrapped up in being the seeker. Life may have literally been defined by spiritual seeking, by the yearning for God or union or enlightenment.

[[Steven Boden]] says in "Wake Up Now":
> "Keep relaxing, letting go, and allowing listening to happen. If your awareness habitually focuses or jumps from sound to sound, just let it do what it does as you continue to relax in global awareness. You may find that you naturally include other sensations as well: the contact of your body against the chair, the air against your skin, the rumbling of your stomach, the beating of your heart. Just keep allowing the play of what is, without efforting to pay attention in any way. 
> Eventually, the sense of a separate experiencer may drop away, and only experiencing remains. No separation between subject and object—just this! As you continue allowing experiencing to happen, you may discover that it arises in a limitless stillness and silence that can’t be experienced in any way. 
> This is your very own Self, unconditional presence, consciousness without a second, the source of all experience. 
> You can never know it with the mind; you can only be it knowingly. Allow everything to be as it is as you relax into the silent presence that you’ve always been. "
> 
[[Salvadore Poe]] says:
> "What we come to believe, and I believed it too, in all of these incredible mystical experiences is that the experience is 'it'. That this experience is enlightenment, and if it lasts forever I will be enlightened. What we fail to see is that I am aware of the experience. No matter what the experience is, I am aware of it. So what we need to find out is who is this that is aware, because that is what doesn't come and go. What we come to know in these inquiries is what doesn't change. What never changes and is always the same. In coming to know that, it becomes possible for this mind to be liberated from seeking."

And I end with a quote from [[Unsorted Notes/Anthony DeMello]]:
> You’ve got the clouds and you’ve got the sky. Many of the Oriental masters will say that enlightenment, before they saw, meant they would identify themselves with the clouds. After enlightenment, they would identify themselves with the sky.
