First Sermon
	The [[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Suttas 1/Dhammacakkapavattana Sutta]]  is found 
	<u>dhamma - chaka - pavattana sutta</u> 
	In Chapter 12 of the Mahavagga 
	the Saccasamyutta
	The Connected Discourses of the Truths 

It begins with the Buddha addressing 
the [[bhikkhus of the group of five]]
with him stating 
that two extremes should not be followed 
by one who has gone into homelessness.
	specifically speaking to those who had given up the home life and gone into homelessness 
		not necessarily monks at the time 
		those who have not given up normal daily life for spiritual development
		we are called [[laypersons]] or [[householders]]
	Two extremes 
	1. [[pursuit]] of [[sensual happiness]] in sense pleasures ([[Bhikkhu Bodhi]]  translation)
	2. [[pursuit]] of [[self-mortification]]
By not veering toward these extremes, the Buddha awakened to the [[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Middle Way 1/middle way]] 
	gives rise to vision, and to knowledge
	leads to peace (calm), to direct knowledge and to [[nibbana]] 

Instead of the word "pursuit" Thanissaro Bhikkhu uses "devoted to" [[sensual pleasure]] or [[self-affliction]].

Nanamoli Thera uses the work ''devotion to [[indulgence]]'' and devotion to [[self-torment]]

Piyadassi Thera uses the phrase "[[Z_Subjects and Themes to Explore (WIP)/Addiction]] to indulgence" and "[[_My Research/Dharma Subjects and Themes/Subjects & Themes/Addiction|Addiction]] to [[self-mortification]] "

So we are talking about pursuing or devoting to or devotion to indulgence or addication to either sense pleasures or self-affliction, self-torment, or self-mortification.
	somewhere in between these two extremes

May switch between different interpretations 
	encourage to use whatever wording that makes sense for you.
	Will primarily use Bhikkhu Bodhi's translation in his translation of the [[Samyutta Nikaya]]

The middle way can be seen and have it effects on many of the Buddha's more abstract concepts

	when questioned whether or not a self exists   
		In this case if he answered  
		yes he would be seen as promoting [[eternalism]] 
		if no, 
		he would be seen as promoting the views of annihiliationism or nihilism 
[[Kalama Sutta]]
