Summary:
1. Satipatthana Sutta exploration of the Foundations of Mindfulness & Spiritual Practice
a. Can be viewed as the two wings of a bird
b. Wakefulness & Compassion
c. This wakefulness as being attentive to our direct experience in each moment
d. Mindfulness is one our primary tools we use to awaken
e. This mindfulness is defined as remembering
f. But also includes interest, persistence, and clearly knowing
g. So Mindfulness is used as a foundational tool of the buddhist spiritual path itself
2. Noble Eightfold path is the 4th of the 4 Noble Truths
a. This is the path that leads to the cessation of suffering
b. The Buddha presented the 4 Noble Truths in terms of a prescription
c. Presents us with the issue at hand, the prescription and the results we should
expect.
d. 1st Three Noble Truths
i. There is suffering, this suffering must be understood and that it has been
fully understood
ii. That craving is the cause of suffering, that it must be abandoned and it
being our duty to let go of craving
iii. There is a cessation of suffering. Our duty to realize this and to know
when cessation has been realized

e. 4th Noble Truth
i. This is the truth of the path to the cessation of suffering -
ii. The Eightfold Path
iii. It must be cultivate
iv. Knowledge that it has been cultivated.

7. Right View
a. Right View is the forerunner of the Noble Eightfold path as it leads us away from
Wrong View
b. Understanding that our actions have consequences
c. Understanding that death is not the end
d. Understanding that our actions & beliefs also have consequences after death
e. One of the 7 requisites of Right Samadhi
i. Others being the remainder of the Noble Eightfold Path
f. Purpose of Right View is to clear one’s path from confusion, misunderstanding &
deluded thinking
g. Gain right understanding of reality
h. Right View also considered to the understanding of non-view
i. Nothing can be expressed in fixed conceptual terms
ii. Rigid, dogmatic clinging to concepts is discarded
iii. Cause of suffering thru clinging & attachment
iv. Part of the wheel of samsara

8. Two divisions
a. Typical to teach Mundane Right View to followers
i. Knowledge of the fruits of good behavior
ii. Bring merit and support favorable rebirth
b. Supramundane right view to monastics
i. World-transcending view - karma & rebirth in terms of the 4 Noble Truths
leading to awakening and liberation from the realm of samsara

c. Karma
i. Every action of body, speech & mind has karmic results
ii. Influences future becoming & births that we experience
d. Right View is understanding of Three Marks of existence
i. Everything, whether physical or mental, is impermanent, a source of
suffering and lacks a self

e. 4 Noble Truths
i. Gain insight into dukkha - its causes - its cessation and the path

9. Karma
a. In order to fully understand karma it is also necessary to understand
i. The ethical distinction of kamma into what is unwholesome and what is
wholesome
ii. The roots from where the unwholesome & wholesome stem from
iii. Unwholesome is action that is morally blameworthy, detrimental to
spiritual development and conductive to suffering for oneself or others
iv. Wholesome action is morally commendable, helpful for spiritual growth &
benefits oneself and others

ii. Not taking what is not given
iii. Not undertaking wrong conduct in regards to sense pleasures
b. Verbal Action
i. Not engaging in False Speech
ii. Or Slanderous Speech
iii. Or Harsh Speech
iv. Or Idle Chatter
v. How our speech affects others
c. Mental Action
i. Free from Covetousness
ii. Free from Ill Will
iii. Not holding to Wrong View

13. Wrong View to Right View
a. Use right Effort to abandon wrong view & enter into Right View

d. Right Effort used to generate of wholesome states
e. Guarding the sense-doors
f. Restraint of the sense faculties
