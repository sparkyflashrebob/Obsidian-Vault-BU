Effort:
● Might equate with struggle, ambition, trying to get somewhere, heroic effort
● Here talking about Wise Effort
● Effort to be present
● Jack Kornfield equates it with an act of love..

● Invites us to show up with open awareness and an open heart
● The exertion involved in right effort is a wholesome form of energy,
● The energy in wholesome states of consciousness directed to liberation from suffering.
● This last qualifying phrase is especially important.
● For wholesome energy to become a contributor to the path it has to be guided by right
view and right intention, and to work in association with the other path factors.
● As the energy in ordinary wholesome states of mind, it merely engenders an
accumulation of merit that ripens within the round of birth and death; it does not issue in
liberation from the round.
● The Buddha does what he can by pointing out the path to liberation; the rest involves
putting the path into practice, a task that demands energy.
● This energy is to be applied to the cultivation of the mind, which forms the focus of the
entire path.
● The unwholesome side requires that the defilements lying dormant be prevented from
erupting and that the active defilements already present be expelled.
● The wholesome side requires that the undeveloped liberating factors first be brought into
being, then persistently developed to the point of full maturity.

The Four Exertions
● Another way we might practice right effort is by practice of the
● The 4 Right Exertions
● Abandon the unskillful, develop the skillful
The four exertions (cattārimāni padhānāni) are summarized as:

1. Restraint (saṃvara padhāna) of the senses. Also known as Guarding the Senses.
2. Abandonment (pahāna padhāna) of defilements.
3. Cultivation (bhāvanā padhāna) of Enlightenment Factors. Also Developing
4. Preservation (anurakkhaṇā padhāna) of concentration, for instance, using
charnel-ground contemplations. Also Maintaining.

“The Fourfold Right Diligence is nourished by joy and interest. If your practice does not bring
you joy, you are not practicing correctly.” – Thich Nhat Hahn
5 Factors of Exertion (that determine our level of effort)
1. Conviction - how much do we believe in what we are doing
2. Free from Illness & discomfort and in good health - fit for exertion
3. Honest - vs avoiding to look at
4. Persistent and steadfast
5. Discerning - thinking of the lute - aware of suffering

Crossing the Flood
§ 62. A deva: Tell me, dear sir, how you crossed over the flood.
The Buddha: I crossed over the flood without pushing forward, without staying in place.
The deva: But how did you cross over the flood without pushing forward, without staying in
place?
The Buddha: When I pushed forward, I was whirled about. When I stayed in place, I sank. And
so I crossed over the flood without pushing forward, without staying in place.
The deva:
At long last I see
a brahman, totally unbound,
who without pushing forward,
without staying in place,
has crossed over
the entanglements of the world.
— SN 1.1
