3. Early discourses do not offer a succinct definition of the term "compassion"
● Similes that the Buddha used are somewhat more helpful
● Describes a situation where one arouses feelings of compassion to illustrate the
attitude one should cultivate towards someone who immersed in
unwholesomeness
○ Person on an extended Journey on a long road
■ Become sick halfway there
■ Exhausted and suffering extremely
■ Alone and without companion
■ Nearest village is actually far away

○ And another person comes upon them and sees this and thinks
○ "If one were to get an attendant, emerge from being far away and reach a
town, were given medicine and be fed and nourished, be well cared for,
then in this way this person's sickness would certainly subside."
○ So that person has extremely compassionate, sympathetic, and kind
thoughts in the mind towards this sick person"

4. Concern for others to be relieved from suffering and affliction.
● The vision is for the sick person to be cared for.
● Mentally dwelling on the actual suffering would be contemplation of dukkha.
● Contemplation for the meditative cultivation of compassion.
● Contemplation itself in the wish for others to be free from dukkha

16. Compassion in Meditation:

● Kruna being the meditative cultivation of compassion
● compassionate responses in life will strengthen the compassionate disposition of
one's own mind
● cultivation of compassion and other brahmaviharas as a "liberation of the mind"
(temporary)
○ different types of liberation - some temporary
● compassion contributes to, but is not itself, final liberation
● ethical basis for overcoming the hindrances (which are impediments for
successful mental culture).
● Kasina used as object in concentration
○ spread everywhere
○ totality of one's experience
○ mind that was once narrow now become boundless

Objects of Meditation:
● lacks any reference to an object
● instead of mentally commiserating with instances of suffering and affliction, at the heart
of compassion stands the wish and aspiration for others to be free from suffering and
affliction.
● Such a wish can and should be free from sadness and grief.
● boundless radiation of compassion to the living beings in all directions
● later teachings point to taking actual persons as it object
○ for when the mind is defiled and unable to practice boundless radiation
■ gradually extending the object of one's practice until it becomes universal
○ Use of individuals as the object of meditation has a place, especially in the initial
stages of meditation practice
■ And it can also be helpful when one’s mind is in a defiled condition
○ It can be a skillful tool for building up the practice.
● But eventually the practice should take the form of boundless radiation to all being
