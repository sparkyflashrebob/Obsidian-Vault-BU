Today we will discuss another Sutta that sheds light on the 4 Noble Truths 
	 The [[Simsapa Sutta]]
	 where the Buddha compares the knowledge he gained in his Awakening to all the leaves in the forest, but what he taught as a mere handful of leaves. 
	 And why he didn't reveal the rest.

Very Short Sutta 
	5 paragraphs

Sutta named after the place he was staying
the Simsapa Forest
The Simsapa tree
	thought to be a indian rosewood tree or a similar tree in the same family that grow in India and southeast asia
	
![[dalbergia sisoo roxb - indian rosewood.png]]

While in this forest
Picked Up a Handful of Leaves and asked: 
""What do you think, monks: Which are more numerous, the few simsapa leaves in my hand or those overhead in the simsapa forest?""

And they  said "The leaves in his hand were fewer in number. Those overhead in the simsapa forest are more numerous."

"In the same way, those things that I have known with direct knowledge 
but have not taught are far more numerous [than what I have taught]. 
And why haven't I taught them? 
Because they are not connected with the goal, do not relate to the rudiments of the spiritual life, 
and do not lead to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to calm, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to Unbinding. 
That is why I have not taught them.

"And what have I taught? 
'This is stress... This is the origination of stress... This is the cessation of stress... This is the path of practice leading to the cessation of stress': 
This is what I have taught. 
And why have I taught these things? 
Because they are connected with the goal, relate to the rudiments of spiritual life, and lead to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to calm, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to Unbinding. 
This is why I have taught them.

"Therefore your duty is the contemplation, 'This is stress... This is the origination of stress... This is the cessation of stress.' Your duty is the contemplation, 'This is the path of practice leading to the cessation of stress.'"
Pointing back to the 3 phases of the 4 Noble Truth - the 12 aspects.

	such as

So here he is reaffirming this Handful of Leaves

TNH then goes on to tell the story of some quarreling monks. 
Pointing out the dangers of not staying on the path that been layed out.

Certain monks were tasked to remember the teachings of the Buddha, they were called sutra masters, and others, memorize precepts (called the [[vinaya]]). Vinaya Masters.
One day an argument broke out between a sutra master and a precept master over the sutra master not cleaning out a wash basin.
With the sutra master saying he did not intentionally leave the basin dirty so as to not to blame.
One side accused the other of slander and the other side accused their opponents of acting foolishly.
The situation quickly got out of hand.
When the Buddha found out he went to each of the monks and told them that they should not become too attached to our own viewpoint. That they should listen carefully in order to understand the other’s viewpoint. And that they should seek all means to prevent the community from breaking.”

And then turning to the group of monks as a whole; asking them to  cease their arguing because it only creates division in the community. 
Asking them to return to their practice. And if they truly follow their practice, they would  not become victims of pride and anger.”
 
But it fell on deaf ears.
So the Buddha decided to set out on his own.
 
At first he met a monk who was dwelling alone.
When the Buddha asked him about his practice he replied that he found great ease and joy in the practice, even though he was currently living all alone. 

The Buddha remarked, “Sometimes it is more pleasant to live alone than with many people.”

Next he met a group of three monks who were also getting along.
He asked them if they were content there together? How was there practice? Are they encountering any difficulties?

They said they were very content here. It was calm and peaceful. They were are able to share the Dharma. And there were all making progress in our practice.”

And when asked in they were living in harmony together they noted:
- they deeply cared for each other
- they were living in harmony like "[[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Engaged Buddhism 1/milk and water]]"
- they considered living with each other a blessing
- and they treasured their friendship

- they would ask themselves, before they do anything, whether the others are present or not, what the other reaction of the others would be.
- if their words or actions would disappoint the others in any way they would refrain from those words or actions.
- and that <u>although they were three persons, they were one</u>

The Buddha said that a sangha is only a true sangha when such harmony exists.
That they had experienced real awakening and that is why they had realized such harmony.

He stayed with them for a month, observing them, and before he left he noted:
> that the very nature of a community is harmony. He believed harmony could be be realized by following these principles: 
> [[Six Principles of Harmonious Living]]
> “1. Sharing a common space such as a forest or home. 
> “2. Sharing the essentials of daily life together. 
> “3. Observing the precepts together. 
> “4. Using only words that contribute to harmony, avoiding all words that can cause the community to break. 
> “5. Sharing insights and understanding together. 
> “6. Respecting others’ viewpoints and not forcing another to follow your own viewpoint. “
> That any community that follows these principles will have happiness and harmony.
