5. 3rd Method expansion of Conditionality
- Chapter 4: The Origination and Cessation of Suffering - otherwise known as Dependent Origination - or Conditionality - or the chain of causation 
-   But the Basic premise is that
  - Things arise due to conditions
  > - “When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises. When this does not exist, that does not come to be; with the cessation of this, that ceases.”
- referred to as "this-that conditionality"
The main way that this principle is applied in the Suttas
  - is as sequence of 12 factors
  - each dependent on its predecessor

  - It <u>reveals a more fundamental condition than craving for the cause of suffering - </u> which is [[avijja]] [[ignorance]]
  - This ignorance here being narrowly defined as "not knowing suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path"
  - and also a lack of awareness of the principles that shine the light on the true nature of phenomena (experience)
	  - 4 Nobel Truths
	  - 3 Characteristics (impermanence, not-self, dukkha)
  - Within the understanding of Dependent Origination and on conditionality
	  - Ignorance is what sustains the round of dukkha
	  - And when Ignorance comes to the end, the entire network of conditions also ends

TRADITIONAL EXPLANATION:
- the "many lives theory" - is the Middle Way between the opposing views of eternalist (in being reborn in heaven) and nilism where there is only this life and no rebirth
- The Process goes like this
	- because of [fundamental [[ignorance]]]]], one engages in [[volitional activities]]
		-  beliefs, views, perspectives, dispositions, biases
			- can be divided into what is [[wholesome & unwholesome]]
				- [[bodily, speech and mind]]
					- body (physical)
					- speech (talk to or about others)
					- mind (how we think about others & the world)
				- these actions generate [[Google Keep/Karma]] (which are like planting seeds)
					- plant wholesome or unwholesome seeds  
				- these seeds flower over time - and 
				- become potentials new existence (repercusions)
				- at death this karma propels consciousness into a new existence (result)
- new existence, this [[birth]], begins when consciousness arrives at a new embodiment
	- new assemblage of bodily & mental phenomena (name & form/aggregates)
- with [[name & form]] the [[six sense bases]] take shape and begin to function
- When the Six sense bases encounter objects there is [[contact]]
- with contact this gives rise to [[feeling]]
	- which as we know can be either pleasant, unpleasant/painful, & neutral
	- which trigger reactions/responses
	- with an untrained person, these feelings that arise cause [[craving]]
		- which is the desire for pleasant experiences & avoiding painful ones 
		- for wanting things that we do not have
		- so this comes with the belief that something outside of ourselves will make us happy or fulfilled.
	- Now there is the tendency when one gets what one wants for us to delight in that experience & for us to want to hold on to it tight
		- we can feel entitled to it - when nature does not make any guarantees
		- THIS IS [[clinging]] and attachment
			- which can be thought of as a more intense type of craving
		-  craving on the level of the senses and in terms of personality view this is what [[becoming]]* is
		- this is what addiction is 
			- wanting to feel a certain way (good) and to avoid feeling another way (bad)
- with this clinging, there is a fresh round of internal thought processes and activities that occur that create the potential for this new existence/of becoming
- this is how karma works

This is also conditionality 
A Chain of Events - that follow the natural law - the law of nature
- which is fixed, stable & invariable thru all time
- it is "[[real, not unreal, not otherwise]]"
- what the Buddha was able to discover was by "going back" thru sequence to the most fundamental condition he saw this fundamental [[ignorance]]
- and with [[discernment]] of each link, which came about thru application of [[thorough attention]], he had the "[[break-through by wisdom]]"
- seeing the Mutual Conditioning of consciousness & [[name-and-form]]
- and how each ceases with the ceasing of the other
- he discovered the path to enlightenment

1. Mindfulness and Spiritual Practice
- coming to these teachings that have been passed onto us 
- we can unwind all of this
- at a basic leve all we need to do is pay attention 
- to be mindful, to be aware
- at a root level what we are experiencing is not necessarily personal
- they are simply the sights, sounds, tastes, touches, & mental process of being a human being
- contact with our senses
- which generate a feeling
- that we like or do not like or not care about depending on our past and present karma
- based on our sensitivities
- Understanding this noticing what I experiencing
- but also what I adding to it.
- I do not need to react and can just allow.
- and even if I do react I can be aware of this so that I can gain in understanding the nature of things
- If not, if I am not aware and keep reacting, this is what keeps the wheel of dependent origination, this causality, this suffering going
- so my practice is to look at what I am experiencing and my reactivity
- in body, speech, and mind
- to note craving & clinging
- to note feeling
- to note if I am craving for sense pleasures or wanting to be otherwise in the moment 
- all this expands our ways of being aware
- of being mindful - moment to moment
- internal, externally, & both internally & externally
- this mindfulness does not take away the pleasure nor the pain & suffering that being alive brings
- but we can learn to appreciate life as best we can while it lasts
- and to learn to not compound our suffering or of others
- karma has a lot to do with bringing our intentions in line with our actions 
- these 4 noble truths and these practices are here to help us free ourselves from the cycle of suffering
