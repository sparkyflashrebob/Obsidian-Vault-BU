### [[Compassion]] Or #Karuna

	not clearly defined in early teachings
	- but there is a simile that describes it meaning 
		- imagine coming upon a person who has traveled a long way, is sick and tired of traveling, has a long way to go and is suffering.  The compassion the concern for others to be relieved from suffering and affliction.

	- compassion finds its being expressed in terms of the wish for others to be free from dukkha

### Mudita or [[Roam-Export-1674627649766/sympathetic joy]] #sympathetic_joy

	is closely related to #pamojja "delight" and #anumodana "rejoicing"
	 Rejoycing for others is direct antidote to having a competitive attitude and being jealous
	 Sympathetic joy is the genuine happiness for others' success, intelligence, & happiness, and the good works that they do.  
	 We appreciate these qualities in others
	 The Buddha is quoted as saying- “if we rejoice in the good deeds of others, you share in the merit of those deeds”
	 In this case, as a Brahmavihara, sympathetic joy, or Mudita, stands out on being cultivated as a boundless radiation of joy
	 So much so that it can lead to the temporary liberation of mind
	 In Buddhist practice, Sympathetic Joy considered one of the most difficult of the four brahmaviharas.
	 We cannot make ourselves feel joy
	 But we can set the intention
		 To Rejoice in our own good qualities
	 In relation to being with others, [[Sympathetic Joy]] means to dwell in harmony with others, and looking upon each other with kindly eyes 
	 
	 

### Upekkah - [[Google Keep/Equanimity]]

	The origins of Equanimity or equipoise, in the pali the word upek(k)hā, suggests a mental attitude of “looking upon” or “to look over”, and not an indifferent “looking away”. 
	The term thus conveys an awareness of whatever is happening combined with mental balance and the absence of favoring or opposing.
	It arises from the power of observation and the ability to see without being caught by what we see.
		And as I have mentioned - it can be hard to distinguish from how mindfulness is fined in this manner
	As a result, when well-developed, Equanimity's power gives rise to a great sense of peace.
	It can also refer to the ease that comes from seeing the big picture.
	In the Indian culture upekkha is sometimes used to mean ‘to see with patience”.
	We might see this as “seeing with understanding”
	Another take on equanimity is to “stand in the middle of all this”.  A balanced strength or stability in the face of whatever is happening.
	The strong presence of inner calm, well-being, confidence, vitality or integrity that can keep us upright, like the ballast that keeps a ship upright in strong winds.
	- rounds of the cultivation of compassion by freeing it from any trace of compulsion and obsession

## Radiating Compassion and Absorption

		- referred to as the mind "BECOMING GREAT & BOUNDLESS"

#### Compassion as a Liberation of Mind

	- Usually, when speaking about the divine abodes, they stand in place of the deeper meditations - talking about the jhana practices
	- In other places in the teachings though they stand side by side
	- When Meditating on other objects (other than brahmaviharas ) a Liberation of mind comes about when we reach these deep jhana states
	- With the brahmaviharas, a temporary liberation of mind is possible with a lesser degree of concentration simply due to their boundless nature
	- Practicing with them in this boundless way actually can offer a distinct advantage to meditation practice versus the pressures that can come up to enter deep meditation states thru the usual process of mediation alone.
		- this can be a distinct obstacle for some

## COMPASSION AND KARMA

	- Beside benefitting our ability to cultivate tranquility and concentration thru the boundless radiation of compassion and the other brahmaviharas, the teachings point to a relation between these divine abodes and fruition  of [[karma]] 
	- As we have discussed, karma in early Buddhist thought, is closely related to intention, since intention is what is responsible for deeds by body, speech and mind. 
		- What was done in the past will in some way come to fruition, but the way this happens depends on what one does now. 
	- As for the past, the suttas state that “Formerly my mind was narrow and not well developed; now my mind has become boundless and well developed.” 
	- When the mind of practitioner has in this way become boundless and well developed, if there bad karma was as a result of associating with friends who were not good influences, or if One formerly was neglectful and performed unwholesome deeds, those deeds cannot lead one along, cannot defile one, and will not come back to meet one in one who practices the brahmaviharas.
		- This also makes clear sense to me.
	- Another way this is thought of is if the same piece of salt were placed in a glass of water or into a fresh water lake. The amount of salt in a glass will taste stronger than if tasted directly from the lake. 
	- Likewise, having been changed by the practice of compassion and the other divine abodes, the present repercussions from the past will have a different outcome on us.  They will be minimalized.
	- One of the Buddhas disciples, Angulimala, who was said to have murdered many, when we became a monk at first he was attacked.  As the story goes, he had compassion for a woman in labor one day and help here with the birth. From then on, it was one way he was of service to others, helping women in labor. So at first taking lives and then helping to bring them in.  His karma had changed.  And eventually he became enlightened.
	- In practical terms when unwholesome thoughts arise and distract the mind, these can be delt with by just returning to the boundless condition of the mind that one develops with the brahmaviharas.
	- Unwholesome intensions require some about of mental narrowness to survive.

## COMPASSION AND Insight

	- With the maturing of compassion practice comes with it the potential for the cultivation of liberating insight.
	- In a couple of the teachings it is implied that with the maturing of ones' practice with the brahmaviharas, one will have a good chance to reach at least the 3rd of the 4 stages of enlightenment, which is that of a non-returner, if not the highest liberation itself.   #stages_of_awakening [[4 Stages of Enlightenment]]


#### Relationship Between Tranquility and Insight 
	- There were generally though of Four (4) different approaches to gaining Liberating Insight. Three of these involving tranquility and insight.  
		1. There is Insight that is preceded by tranquility- 
		2. tranquility preceded by [[Insight Dialog]] 
		3. and then there is tranquility and insight conjoined. 
	- So it would appear that there is no hard as fast rule the either tranquility or insight must be practices first. 
	- Some of us may be better off by placing the emphasis on insight (vipassana) while others will prefer to begin instead on calming down the mind and then turning to insight.
		-  This would depend on our own personal preference and disposition
		- I'd like to note here, that we are implying that our degree of tranquility is as a result of being associated with the cultivation of the brahmaviharas
	- There is no situation where either tranquility or insight would lead to liberation  
	- At some point they need to be combined. 
	- The important contribution made by tranquillity, aka compassion practice, is that it brings about a change in the attitudes of the One's mind, by providing it with a higher form of pleasure and inner satisfaction.
	- Tranquility though does not stand on its own.  In it clear in the teachings that the factor that eradicates ignorance and leads to liberation is actually having [[insight]]. 

##### Cultivating two dharmas
		- One way of looking at the practice of mediation is by means of cultivating two dhammas
			A. tranquility 
			B. Insight 
		- If one gains the calmness of tranquility they will be successful in having morals and not violating any of the precepts, and generating all virtues.
		- If one has gained insight, they will contemplate what is dukkha, knowing it as it really is, and, with contemplating it arising and cessation, the way out, knowing it as it really is.
		- Having contemplated in this way one attains liberation from what is called the [[influx]] of sensuality, the influx of becoming, and the influx of ignorance. 
		- The [[Asava - influx]] 
			- This is what is meant by gaining our freedom or another way of saying being  Enlightened 
		- Another way of saying this is that 
			- thru tranquility, the mind is developed & lust is abandoned; 
			- thru insight, wisdom is developed and ignorance is abandoned.
		- Insight takes as its vehicle the contemplation of the 4 Noble Truths - which leads to final liberation.
		- Tranquility adds to this by bringing about a change in attitude in the mind by providing it with a higher form of pleasure and inner satisfaction 
		- This mental attitude that the brahmaviharas engender is integral to the path to awakening
	- I've already noted that Tranquility itself does not stand on its own
		- However, the teaching reckons the four absorptions (deeper meditations) as instances of a practitioners' "happiness" and the the brahmaviharas as instances of a practitioners' "wealth"
	- In this way, the practice of compassion also has a positive effect on others.
		- They sense the harmony and well-wishing that accompany the words and actions of one whose mind is steeped in compassion. 
	- It is seen as a direct expression of harmlessness
		- one of the [[three modes of right intention]], the 2nd factor of the [[Noble Eightfold Path]] 
	- Engaging in compassion is also a means of implementing [[Right Effort]]
	- And as we have seen, successfully practicing compassion can come to fulfil the path factor of [[Right Concentration]] aka tranquillity
	- And finally, in terms of Compassion and Insight, cultivation of compassion could be used as one's main vehicle of practice.  
		- This is where we turn to the awakening factors, those qualities that need to be brought into being for awakening to take place.
