> Everything in life begins with your mindset, and then your actions follow.
> 
> What you do is shaped by what you believe and think.
> If you want real change, start by shifting your mind, then move, and let your actions align with it.
> @BuddhismPageFB

I saw this in a Facebook post image this morning and it triggered me to think about things a bit. Initially it suggests that I look at my mindset and then to follow up with action. However, my mind went to thinking about this in the reverse. Are my actions in alignment with my mindset? 

This immediately led me to a feeling of self-judgment. How have I been acting and how have I been thinking? There is a natural expectation and belief system imprinted on us - our conditioning. We are inclined to judge ourselves and  who we believe we should be.  And when that image doesn't meet our own expectations then there is a sense of shame.  When faced with others, whether in person or in our own mind, this can manifest in many ways of coping with this sense of ourselves.

We see it all the time, especially in our many politicians and religious leaders.  Totally denying the truth of their being and putting on a front to overcome it.  Pointing their finger outside instead of looking within.  We "Co" ourselves, accuse others of doing what we are doing, or point out our judgement of others so that the attention moves over to them. While we appear to look good  - at least on the outside.  

My primary moment to moment concern is how I feel and generally that is that I do not feel that great. It is also a great part of my spiritual practice too as a result. I experience a lot of feelings that don't feel good and can be considered painful at times. Pains appearing here and there in my stomach, my head, and in various parts of my body most of the time. Of having a general feeling of diss ease.

For me, this has really has nothing to do with my mindset - at least what I am aware of. I know and believe that I am a good person. I have learned to be resilient. I have proven this over and over again in my actions.  I can see it in how I treat people in general. Occasionally I can get triggered and my street smarts awareness takes over and I experience fear and anger. But generally, I am good-natured and try to treat people that way - to be kind, caring and friendly. It is the way I want us to treat each other. 

I'm on the verge of retiring and so there is this other mindset, this work mindset that I've been carrying for a very long time. We all do I believe.  My work mindset is one of an overwhelming sense of needing to be responsible and meeting others expectations. I believe this is being driven by subconscious fear. I don't want to feel that I am letting people down or that I am not doing good enough.  That I might be fired. So it is an issue twofold - one of self-worth and the other of innate fear.  Impostor Syndrome. 

There have been periods of time where I have spoken out when maybe I should not have.  And more succinctly to have spoken out in ways that I shouldn't have. I have come off to myself as being a little arrogant and self-righteous. It has always bothered me and yet I repeated this behavior for quite a long time.  Being mindful of it, I was able to overcome and turn the tide. I am grateful that the feedback that I have gotten basically validates that they see me as a kind and caring person. 

I don't completely fit the person that I believe that I want to be. But by being mindful and as present as possible, I trust that this will help me to move forward towards being that person.  But if I never look, if I avoid facing the truth, I do not think it can ever happen.  It's not like I'm going to magically turn into something that I'm not. 

My experience is that my defects actually worsen if neglected. This is a healthy use of fear so fear is not necessarily a bad thing.  Pain and fear are signposts. Nature is telling us to pay attention.  If I neglect to pay attention, I'm actually placing myself and others in great harm. And we can see how that affects the world that we live in.  

A friend of mine at one time shared a story him with a group of us and he talked about how he had to think about whether he wanted to be right or he wanted his marriage to flourish. And he chose the latter. We all have this same choice. It shows within ourselves and within our relationships and with how we want the world to be.
