ADD A PERSONAL STORY

There is this saying “Don’t push anyone, including yourself, out of your heart”.

Isn’t this the essence of true freedom? To have a heart free of the burdens of hate and disappointment-  free from resentment? To have a heart that is able to hold these with wisdom and compassion?   

“Darkness cannot drive out darkness: only light can do that. Hate cannot drive out hate: only love can do that.”
― Martin Luther King Jr., A Testament of Hope: The Essential Writings and Speeches

In order to love we need to let go of hate 
and in order to let go of hate we have to learn to let go 
and in order to let go of our hate and disappointment of ourselves and others 
we have to learn to forgive.

So forgiveness is essential to our learning to love, to not suffer, and to be free in our heart and mind.  What can be more important than this?
When speaking to the heart and coming from our true sense of self - nothing is.
But when we come from our small sense of self, our thinking minds only, our logical mind, our beliefs, our personal perspective, views, and opinions, then forgiveness is usually out of the question.

The poet Danna Faulds says, "Do you value your reasons for staying small more than the light shining through the open door? Forgive yourself. Perfection is not a prerequisite for anything but pain. Please, oh please, don't continue to believe in your stories of separation and failure. This is the day of your awakening." 

Evolutionally we have been instilled with the desire to identify and discipline wrongdoers. I guess it is part of our survival instinct.
The message is, "If someone wrongs you, retaliation reduces the likelihood that they will do it again."  
Additionally, if others see you retaliate, they will also be less likely to wrong you in the future. 
Direct punishment serves a clear signal, “Do not mess with me, or you will be sorry.” 

Our society is based on a sense what is "right", how things should be. 
An idealism of this. 
When are wronged
We want others to get punished, invariably so that they will not do it again
To somehow right the wrong, getting retribution, to make it fair  
To gain a sense of Justice. Some idea that when we have justice we can be at peace.

This justice is supposed to be predicated on right and wrong, and the rule of law.  But how often is justice really served fairly? How often does favoritism, bias, racism, sexism, money… You name it, get in the way of this system of justice?  What is fair punishment anyway?  An eye for an eye.   

It is in our nature when we are hurt we want to punish,, to get back get even, to never give in to those who wronged us, 
and probably the worst part of it, 
Is to never forget.  
When we vow to never forget, usually this means that we vow to never forgive.  

There is this misconception that forgiveness is weakness, if we forgive then we are ignoring what has happened. Somehow the wrong was justified in some way.   
Or somehow that we are lesser than others. 

Individually we remain caught in hurt and pain from our past.
And in society, because we cannot forgive 
We seem to never get beyond how we have been wronged. 

As a result, we have re-occurring wars and conflicts.  
We keep opening up old wounds.
- Addicted to the 2nd Arrow.
- Getting shot - taking arrow out and stabbing ourselves with it.
- See how you have harmed me!!!
- Get a rush on our hate.
This is what forgiveness is NOT.

- Probably the most important point is that Forgiveness is fundamentally for ourselves.
- Forgiveness enables us to be released from the sorrows of the past.
- We don't have to be loyal to your suffering.
- And Collectively humanity needs to learn forgiveness to end the cycles of retribution and violence.
- Extending and receiving forgiveness is essential for the redemption from our individual and collective past.
- Like compassion, forgiveness does not ignore the truth of our suffering.
- Forgiveness does not justify or condone harmful actions.
- In fact, we can and should resolve to prevent further harm where we can.
- Forgiveness does not mean you have to seek out or speak to those who have caused us harm. To be buddy buddy with them.
- Forgiveness is not weak.
- There is strength in forgiveness
- It demands a sense of courage and integrity, and a greater sense of honesty about what is going on and what has happened. 
- It is an act of the heart. 
- The Buddha once said FORGIVE OTHERS NOT BECAUSE THEY DESERVE FORGIVENESS. BUT BECAUSE YOU DESERVE PEACE.
- Let when we let go of the pain, resentment and outrage that we have carried as a burden for so long, it is an easing of the heart.
- Story of two prisoners:

		Two ex-prisoners of war meet after many years.

		When the first one asks, “Have you forgiven your captors yet?” the second man answers, “No, never.” 

		“Well then,” the first man replies, “they still have you in prison.”

- Forgiveness sees wisely
- It Acknowledges what is unjust, harmful and wrong
- It recognizes the suffering of the past
- It understands the conditions that brought them about
- When we forgive we also say "Never again will I allow these things to happen."

Extending forgiveness to ourselves is of upmost importance.

- Just as others have been caught in suffering so have we.
- There is also the recognition that we have all been betrayed and hurt others.
- Looking honestly in our life, we can see the sorrows and pains that have to lead to our own wrongdoing.
- In this, we can extend forgiveness to ourselves. 
- We can hold the despair that others have caused us and that we have caused in compassion.
- Without mercy, we will live our life in exile.
- But when we do forgive we can a Sense of space around whatever is here
- Can become Aware of the armor around our hearts

To me the act of forgiveness is extremely important to our own well-being. What others have done to us out of ignorance or hate or greed is not something we can control. Typically we suffer again and again as we relive or react to these wrongs in unskillful ways. To forgive is to suffer no more. We forgive so that we no longer suffer. Suffering no more we turn to true love and compassion. That which is not tainted by personal gain. The world is safer or us and those we come into contact with. The cycle of suffering is broken. - Sharon Salsberg


A couple of important points:

Forgiveness is a process, not a single action. It can take years. Ebb and flow of moods and emotions. It depends on our practice, on our intentions, on our values. 
It depends on having the courage to take an honest look at how we have been wronged and how others have wronged us. Notice conditions upon conditions from our past and that are present that have born our suffering 

We have to set your intention for forgiveness. What are our priorities?  To be right, to have justice - or is it to be at peace and to gain freedom from suffering.

It is important for us to learn that there are both inner and outer forms of forgiveness. Forgiving ourselves and forgiving others. Giving others the benefit of doubt.  Understand that we are doing the best we can.

So how do we do this. 
We can start with the easiest thing that can open your heart.
We can become willing to grieve and let go.
At some point there comes a Shift of identity in the heart. Towards healing, contentment, peace, and joy
A shift of Perspective.

Practice:
Usually start by asking for forgiveness by others.

ASKING FORGIVENESS OF OTHERS

Let yourself remember and visualize the ways you have hurt others. See and feel the pain you have caused them out of your own fear and confusion. Feel your own sorrow and regret. Sense that finally you can release this burden and ask for forgiveness.

And then to each person in your mind repeat:

To those whom I may have caused harm,
knowingly or unknowingly,
through my thoughts, words and actions
arising through greed, ill-will or delusion,
I ask your forgiveness.
I ask for your forgiveness.

OFFERING FORGIVENESS TO YOURSELF

Recite:

There are many ways that I have hurt and harmed myself. I have betrayed and abandoned myself many times through thought, word, or deed, knowingly and unknowingly.

Feel your own precious body and life. Let yourself see the ways you have hurt or harmed yourself. Picture them, remember them. Feel the sorrow you have carried from this and sense that you can release these burdens. Extend forgiveness for each of them, one by one.

Repeat to yourself:

For any harm I may have caused myself,
knowingly or unknowingly,
through my thoughts, words, and actions,
I offer my forgiveness.

OFFERING FORGIVENESS TO THOSE WHO HAVE HURT OR HARMED YOU

Recite:

There are many ways that we have been harmed by others, abused or abandoned, knowingly or unknowingly, in thought, word, or deed. Let yourself picture and remember these many ways. Feel the sorrow you have carried from this past and sense that you can release this burden of pain by extending forgiveness whenever your heart is ready.

Now say to yourself:

To those who may have caused me harm,
knowingly or unknowingly,
through their  thoughts, words and actions,
I offer my forgiveness,
as best I am able.

Let yourself gently repeat these three directions for forgiveness until you feel a release in your heart. For some great pains you may not feel a release but only the burden and the anguish or the anger you have held. Touch this softly. Be forgiving of yourself for not being ready to let go and move on.

Forgiveness cannot be forced; it cannot be artificial. Simply continue the practice and let the words and images work gradually in their own way. In time you can make the forgiveness meditation a regular part of your life, letting go of the past and opening your heart to each new moment with a wise loving-kindness.
