# Guided Meditation: 

The ancient teachings of the wise ones remind us about having the proper attitude and approach towards our meditation, that of being:
	- Ardent, Alert, Mindful, Clearly Knowing
		- taking interest in what we are doing, 
		- making our best effort, 
		- being present and wakeful
		- putting aside our worldly concerns temporarily so that we can develop this training of looking within 
		- giving ourselves this gift of knowing thy self thru our direct experience
- Today the focus of our session will be the awareness of the signs and with the awareness of the the signlessness
- The sign being the signs of experience itself
- And the signless being the changingness of that same experience

So I thought we'd start out with a guided meditation to explore this for a moment.
If you are comfortable with it, I'd ask that you try this meditation with your eyes closed
- but if not then you might simply lower & soften your gaze so you can bring your awareness inward

- We can start the meditation by bringing our awareness to the Body - 1st Foundation
	- noticing the signs of being in a body
	- noticing the 4 elements of earth, air, fire & water
		- the weight of the body
		-  the movement of the breath
		- the warmth & coolness as it touches the skin
		- and the cohesion of the body itself, its fluidity
		- things moving around in the body
	- You might initially notice if there is any tension present that you can easily let go of 
		- maybe scanning thru the body 
		- this highlights our awareness the feeling of sensations in the body
		- another sign
		- feelings of what is pleasant or unpleasant 
		- or not being aware of either sign
		- this awareness of feeling tone is the 2nd Foundation of mindfulness
	- notice how these sensations come and go
- (pause)
- And similarly with the Mind
- Noticing our State of Mind at this moment
	- not trying to figure it out
	- not judging
	- the simple knowing that the mind is like this at this moment
	- how do you know your state of mind?
- (pause)
 - Noticing the Movement of the mind
	 - why is the mind moving?
	- did something announces itself?
	- 	- tuning into what is coming in thru the senses
	- aware of sounds 
	- aware of sensations in the body
	- aware of the sensation of sight
		- even with the eyes closed 
		- we can experience images
		- colors coming and going
		- maybe images of something familiar to you
		- notice you cannot hold onto anything
		- that it has its own condition for being
		- notice how they are not fixed but changing
	- coming into being
	- persisting a bit
	- and then fading away
	- or simply disappearing instantly
		but constantly changing
	- different colors or patterns in sight
	- tingling, or pulsing sensations
	- sounds coming and going
	- continue to notice these signs of experience

- So now Bring your attention to the knowing of experience itself
	- as the signs come and go
	- the knowing does not flicker
	- (pause)
- Resting in this awareness of what is coming and going
	- allowing the signs to fade
	- not holding onto experience - not grasping
	- a relaxed attention 
	- being aware of the nature of thing
- This emptiness
(pause)
- The mind like a lamp
- Even when there are no signs
- Nothing to cling to
- and yet 
- there is this knowing that is present
- Relaxing into this experience of the knowing 
- Allow this knowing to be your refuge as things come and go
- as the conditions change
- as things come into being and then pass away
- not holding, not wanting, not not wanting
- resting in the awareness of your being
- and the peace that this can bring

# TEACHING
Welcoming back to the world of the illusion of constancy!!! 
... smiles ...
I hope you found the meditation inviting - peace - and nurturing 
It is inspired by a Book I am reading by Bhikkhu Analayo called, "The Signless and the Deathless"
- As I have been practicing with the same meditation I offered, I was reminded of  my own experience as a Teenager
	- one Saturday or Sunday morning
	- my eyes closed 
	- noticing the colors
	- changing colors
	- then not being able to see anything that I could label
	- this misty vision of nothing solid
	- ..... and how peaceful that felt
- It reminded me of a story about Buddha in his quest for enlightenment
	- where he was contemplating his practice 
	- and he remember sitting under a tree in meditation while his father was in the fields
	- how peaceful that felt
	- wondering if that the way toward enlightenment
 
And then Reflecting on My Own Practice Today
- a lot of the time Meditation can be like a battle field
- Sensations coming and going all over the place
- pain itself, or some throbbing, or tightness
- and then sometimes adding on to this 
- or even in spite of none of this physical difficulty
- distracted by my own thoughts
	- about my job
	- have to do something or about what is expected of me
	- or what I didn't do but I should have (which is rare)
	- or maybe just looking for something other that just being present with what is
-  All of this Minor suffering
- Lucky it is not usually more than that for me
- But frequently it can be more difficult
	- hence comparing it to a battlefield
- What can we do about it?
- Typically we react
	- we resist
	- we don't like it
	- we move
	- we distract ourselves
	The Buddha talked about generally two types of suffering
	- the 1st being the king of suffering that is inherent in life
		- such as old age, sickness & death
		- these pains and throbbing or pulsing I feel 
		- And then there is the 2nd
			- such as not being able to be with our loved ones
			- or having to be around people you do not like
			- or just generally not getting what one wants
				- (and we can easily add to this)
		- All of signs draw our attention
			- keeping up with the Jones', making a living or getting laid off, finding your love not reciprocated, worries about either things in the past or in the future 
			- and on the other end
			- wanting to experience pleasant experiences
			- good food, kind people, beautiful nature, fresh air
			- this is how we know we are alive
			- there is something that draws our attention
		- the conditions of the world
		- the things as they are
		- our experience of the world
		- what comes into the senses and then what we perceive
		- (what we see, hear, taste, touch, smell, and the thoughts that arise with them)
		- these are the signs we are aware of
	- So there is the suffering of being alive and there is the kind of which is what we add on to
		- what we personalize
		- there is life happening and then what we add to it
		- the things we do not want to experience and resist or we want to experience but can have it, or keep it
		- All things eventuly go away 
		- because that is the nature of all things
		- This adding on is often referred to as the 2nd arrow 
		- how we compound suffering
- The Unenlightened Person 
	- will just act in just this way
	- this will be their karma
	- resisting what they do not like
	- wanting what is not their to keep
	- stuggling
- The Wise One - the who knows 
- which is the purpose of our training as spiritual seekers
	- is the one that aware that things come and go of their own accord
	- is aware that these signs arise from the multitude of conditions that brought it into being
	- and knows that they pass away because the conditions have changed
	- This is someone who does not try to hold onto the things that do not belong to them
	- Who doesn't make a problem out of experience
	- Who looks within 
	- tending to the conditions in their own heart and mind 
	- that promotes well-being for themselves and others
	
- We have a choice.  
- Knowing that conditions change, and are mostly not in our control.
- Why am I giving attention to these signs?
- And what if I don't give attention to them?
- When we take a closer look at these signs we can be aware of the friction they emanate.
	- When we hold onto them they become the seeds of stress in our lives
Generally we are drawn to these signs
They are the content of our lives 
These are the the signs that draw our attention
Instinctly we are of these signs
Some call it a negative bias
	- these things we like and want, or do not and don't want, 
Most of us are so used to this we assume that is the way it has to be.
- But there also is the freedom found in letting things be what they are, not holding on to them, nor resisting
- In not giving them attention
- Allowing them to be without embellishing them
- It is here where we can experience stillness, 
- Can experience peace
- There is the arising and the passing away
- There is the silence within the noise
- We do not need to give attention to this noise
- We can rest in the silence, in the quietude
- There is the Conditioned 
- And there is also the unconditioned.
- We do not have to give all of our attention to what draws our attention
- It is not ignoring the world. 
- But giving this signlessness attention too, 
- And when we do we provide more space for clarity of mind, for creative expression, for skillful action in the face of the chaos that is also present in the world we live in. For attending to what needs to be attended to.
- Without creating suffering from it
- Finding refuge in this unconditioned space within our being, in this still place of signlessness. A freedom from all conditions.
