To today I would like to follow up on Will’s talk from last week and discuss
the signs of the times, pertaining to COVID-19 virus in particular. What I
would like to talk about is some additional facts about being human that
may be beneficial to you.

We all have our own individual way we are processing what is going on.
People are complex and we have different aptitudes and capabilities,
different needs, desires and concerns. We carry the unprocessed baggage
of our past, or shortcomings , and beliefs.
Today I was in the grocery store. They were only letting in a certain
number of people in at a time apparently under the guise of being better
able to keep a social distance from each other. Many people were wearing
masks.
Underneath that was this general unease. I could see the worry in most
peoples’ faces. It wasn’t as bad today, but you can feel the energy of this
unease.
In a sense it is the same unease that always is present for which people
comment - like making a general statement that the folks in cities are not as
friendly as in rural areas. We are generally focused on ourselves and
getting our own needs met.
Whole Foods has made it a rule that only one person be at the checkout.
At first I did not see this. A lady told me there was a line behind her. I saw
that another checkout stand had only one person so told the lady this and
then I got in that line. The lady who was on the checkout stand then told me
about the new rule. I realized that I had judged myself being a smarty
pants with my response to the first lady so I turned to her to apologize. She
didn’t really show me any response but kept to her task at hand.
At other times I have felt this energy of unease and took it on myself. At
one point I grabbed some pasta - even though I didn’t think I needed it -
just because it had been out of stock previously.
These situations can touch upon many of our fears, our impatience, our
greed, our frustration with not getting what we want when we want it, our
issues with never being satisfied. Our expectations not being in sync with
reality.
At times I seem to be trudging forward. I believe that it is helpful to find the
proper tools or have the proper view or outlook to allow ourselves to
skillfully get thru our daily lives

It is in our nature to want to push away the things we do not like.
We do not want to have to go thru this - we just want it to go away
Generally we hid from the truth and we want to avoid conflict. In our
society this shows up as not wanting to see homeless people in the streets,
our sick and elderly are tucked away from public view. Rarely do we see
death up close but it is happening all around us.
Yet there is unskillful fear and there is skillful fear.
In a strange and frightening forest, the Buddha found freedom from fear by
facing it down and recognizing it as a temporary mind state. ... We can't
know the future, and so allowing fear to hold us prisoner in the present is
ultimately unskillful - not helpful. Somehow we get temporary relief and the
issue comes back or even worsens.

The nature of trauma is that we are cut off from each other and ourselves,
our feelings, our truth.
So what is helpful? I hope to shine some light on this by sharing some
perspectives from other teachers and also some of my own.
So what do we do when all is seeming trouble, and we feel we cannot
seemingly face what is present?
There are a couple of things we can do.
One of the first suggestions when dealing with something really difficult -
that is beyond our control - is to gather ourselves and the resources we
have available. Getting support is important if we cannot do it alone. This
is called Resourcing -- It’s is what brings us strength, it provides us the
courage to move forward. It provides us with an anchor, a jumping off point.
One suggestion is that it is wise to gather some support from another
person. This can be a close friend, a sibling, our husbands or wives or
girlfriends or our boyfriends, our parents, a co-worker, a therapist, your
pastor or teacher.

It doesn’t need to be someone who is alive. It can be someone who has
passed on that was your benefactor, or an inspirational person such as
MLK or Gandi or Nelson Mandela.
In AA most of us lean on the support of our fellow travelers or our Sponsors
at some point.
This is the importance of Sangha of fellowship. It provides us with a sense
of being cared for, a strength - a support.
Resourcing can also be a place such as your church or the beach or the
mountains or the desert, or at the ocean. It can be with animals or trees or
with the wind or the air. Something that is inspiring and brings strength.
There is this aliveness of what is right here. -
What is here now? A sky with clouds - feeling the feet on the ground -
If we find ourselves with support we need we may just need a helpful
perspective. We can move towards reconnecting with what is here in a
larger sense. Some of these perspectives include:
1. The understanding that what has happened has already happened -
rational fear is fear about something that is happening, like being chased
by a tiger or being in the middle of a fire. This causes an immediate
response in us to either fight, run away or freeze. Irrational fear is fear of
the unknown -- of not-knowing. Our greatest desire is to know what is
going to happen. Yet what we are currently experiencing is or has already
happened. Evolution is about prediction. What we experience is a
prediction and not the true data. What we think we know is filtered and
based on our assumptions and biases. This past is not only our past but
our families past, our cultural history, and the history contained within our
DNA. Our fear is of an uncertain future. What appears to be happening has
already happened because our senses can relay the information only so
fast to us. Thinking about the truth of our consciousness and perceptions.
For instance, reflected light enters the eye. Our eyes are constantly and
rapidly adjusting the focal length to bring things into focus. Then the Brain
interprets the image. This takes about 13 milliseconds for an image to

register in the brain and more if it needs the image need to be retained. No
matter what the speed, it is clear that when we are aware of external
stimulus it has already happened. Our evolutionary brains require
prediction. When we can accept the present state of our perceptions we
realize everything is like this - this is the way it is.
2. The understanding that each morning I wake up. This is the recognition
that when we go to bed at night we expect to wake up the next morning.
There is some element of faith and trust that we will be here the next
morning. Or else how could we sleep at night.
3. Related to this is a reflection that despite our past difficulties we have
gotten thru them.
I am thinking of The Matrix when Morpheus is addressing the gathering of
everyone in Zion in the cave. He states he has faith in their survival
because, thru all the attempts of the machines to kill them, they were still
there and alive.
When I reflect on all my past difficulties, even my worst days, my bottoms -
at some moment after that I simply have chosen to surrender to the truth of
the way it is and to begin again, to carry on, or to start over. We are
resilient - so is all of nature.
4. And this reflection from a recent AA Facebook post: Everything that at
first seems to be unfortunate somehow becomes a blessing. Once we
accept our experience we start to move forward and find ways to see going
onward. When I look back from a certain perspective, with a certain
attitude - I have Gratitude.

ON SPIRITUAL LIFE
“...Ven. Ananda went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, having bowed
down to the Blessed One, sat to one side. As he was sitting there, Ven.
Ananda said to the Blessed One, ‘This is half of the holy life, lord:
admirable friendship, admirable companionship, admirable camaraderie.’
“‘Don’t say that, Ananda. Don’t say that. Admirable friendship, admirable
companionship, admirable camaraderie is actually the whole of the holy
life. When a monk has admirable people as friends, companions, &
comrades, he can be expected to develop & pursue the noble eightfold
path.” - Witnessing each other, connection.
6. We can also look at fear as fear itself. Isn’t that the real issue? What
would it look like if we had no fear. Reflecting on the Buddha’s words about
Facing fear I mentioned earlier- Fear is temporary. Fear is present and
then it is not, it comes and goes. This means that it is uncertain and cannot
be relied upon as truth. It is not ours. Are we currently in danger? When we
understand this, when we awaken we are free.
But our culture teaches us to lean on distraction. Is this better for us? At
times it might be best to take a back seat for a bit. To gather our energy for
the fight that is to come. But at some point, punting as I call it, can lead to
stagnation and paralyzation.
During the night of the Buddha's enlightenment and throughout his life, his
friend Mara, the God of Desire and Sensuality as well as death, frequently
visited him. When he recognized Mara he famously said, “Mara I see you”.
And seeing Mara he had no fear.
There is this Zen saying, called When Life is Tough. It is said that the world
is upside down - that the way the world looks from the ordinary or
conventional point of view is pretty much the opposite of the way the world
actually is.

7. When I reflect on the here and now - everything is usually okay.
PRESENCE is peaceful. It is when we, as Sakaya Ditti take over, we look
back from a certain perspective, we have regret or feel sorry for ourselves.
The same goes for prediction or projection into the future. It is from a
certain perspective. If we look at the here and now, it is peaceful. Unless
we take on the perspective of the personal - and we find we not like what
we are sensing, experiencing, perceiving or thinking about.
This is why we look at the details of our perception instead of the contents
--- for which we are constantly judging with a bias.
Story about the baby rats and the cat hair. Rats playing. Drop of cat hair.
Senses fill them with danger. They do not think anymore about playful
presense but become rigid in fear. When we were children at the beginning
we were childlike, free from cares and worries. As we started to
accumulate experience, we lose this childlike state and become more like
adults - we live with fear in our lives.

So what other Refugees do we have?
You can take these teachings as a refuge. We can make these real for us.
We look directly at what we are experiencing. In mediation and mediation
practice there is this growing sense of the eternal I. The I that is always
present and watchful. This growing awareness reveals itself as we move
along the path.
There is the awareness of what we previously discussed as the
aggregates: The pure and simple awareness of form, feelings, perceptions,
sense awareness, and the awareness of our moods and worldly concepts,
just as these things.
There is the awareness of our wanting things to be pleasant, not wanting
the unpleasant or neutral. Of our nature to get sick, to age and to pass
away.
There is the awareness or understanding, in Buddhism also, the four noble
truths. The truth of suffering, its causes, the truth that it can be overcome,
and the path to doing that. That is what we are doing here in the Marin
Mindful Recovery.

What is it that we want from our lives? How does this want me to be with it?

Do we want to live our lives with a sense of peacefulness, and calm and
goodwill regardless of the circumstances? The point of this discussion is
not to regate what is real for us today. It is as it is. We can be with that!
And, despite these worldly and personal concerns, we can also live in the
wisdom and acceptance of it all. Accepting this life - with its ups and
downs - not holding on - with skillful reflection that relieves our suffering
and that of others.
