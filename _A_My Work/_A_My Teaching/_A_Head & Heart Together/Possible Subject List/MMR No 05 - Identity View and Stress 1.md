SELF-CONCERN:

During my lifetime I cannot remember any time when we, as a society, as a planet of humans, have not lived with fear. Fear of Nuclear War, fear of war, fear of financial insecurity, fear of having a career path or lack of a career path, fear of not being loved, fear of being alone, fear of fitting in, fear of being accepted, fear of being hated and despised.

This fear is a natural and instinctive reaction to what was going on around us. There was The Cold War that started in the 50's and ended with the Berlin Wall coming down in 1991. The Vietnam War, the Civil Rights Movement, recessions, DotCom crash, Financial Crisis of 2008, 9/11, both Iraq Wars, the War on Terrorism, the rise of divisive politics (the Tea Party and what we are seeing today).

The real dangers to the Environment that existed even before Trump. Climate Change, destruction of the forests and pollution of the rivers, acidification of the oceans.

What is our normal pattern of viewing the world?

As we go about our lives we reflect on our experiences. During our childhoods we experienced the insecurity of growing up, having siblings or not, having nurturing parents or not, experiencing other people younger, our age or older as friends, acquaintances and the like and those relationships and experiences. There were struggles and joys.

As we start to grow up we continuously wake up to our navigating the world and become reactionary to it. If we are loved or not, had a lot of friends or not, lived in a safe environment or not. We started to have our own effect on the effects of what we were experiencing. When we misbehaved we were punished or we were allowed to misbehave. There were situations that took place and our reactions to those situations. We took all of this in as our lives and who we were. We became the judge of our and others actions. Our Ego's began to grow and solidify. As we became aware of this Self that we created we began to compensate for what we perceived as who we are. Perceiving our own gifts or defects as they manifest our perception of the outside world and our inner world hardened. We began to trust the world as it is as the way we perceived it.

Now let's add to this our physicalness and mental experience - were we generally healthy or did we have difficulties with our bodies? Were we good in school, had an aptitude for learning the way that we were being taught, were we generally outgoing and friendly or were we shy, introverted or difficult to deal with. Were we considered pretty, or handsome, desirable or not. Did we have a healthy self-view or did we view ourselves as damaged or defective?

We began to look at ourselves as having good luck or back luck, good or bad fortune, to have an ethnicity, identify with a religion, pride in our country, or neighborhood and city and in our sports teams. We formed and created a whole world of ourselves and of others.

It's funny - my perspective may not be your perspective, my view - not your view - my belief not your belief. We will not even see the same things the same way.

We took eye witness reports as facts. We believed what we read in the newspapers and what historians and scholars would write about in books. We believe in the things we see, feel, touch, taste, smell and think about.

  

Almost each and all of us see the world and our experience in some variation of this way.

  

But now we are beginning to realize that facts are not facts but are perspectives and views. Views with biases. This is a different take on what we have always known - now we are beginning to see how we fabricate our lives and our world.

We are starting to take a closer look. That is what the Mindfulness Movement is all about - WAKING UP. To ending our suffering. This is the path that the Buddha laid out for us - thru his own experience of awakening.

  

A few weeks back I talked about the fetters - the fetter or thicket of views that prevent us from freedom. The Sotapanna is someone who has reached the 1st Stage of the 4 Stages of Enlightenment in Buddhist Philosophy -one has overcome the 1st Fetter to be overcome is Personality or Identity View, and I believe any kind of view at all.

This is the ego centric view of judging our experience as being who we are -- instead of seeing experience as a manifestation of life itself.

When I feel something - say a pain in my knee, I say my knees hurt, when I have a pain in my head I say I have a headache. Same thing with my moods, emotions, and thoughts. I make them me.

But when I look at it. I am not in control of my body - only certain aspects like moving my arms and legs. But there are processes I do not control for sure.

  

This from Ajahn Chah

About this mind... In truth there is nothing really wrong with it. It is intrinsically pure. Within itself it's already peaceful. That the mind is not peaceful these days is because it follows moods. The real mind doesn't have anything to it, it is simply (an aspect of) Nature. It becomes peaceful or agitated because moods deceive it. The untrained mind is stupid. Sense impressions come and trick it into happiness, suffering, gladness and sorrow, but the mind's true nature is none of those things. That gladness or sadness is not the mind, but only a mood coming to deceive us. The untrained mind gets lost and follows these things, it forgets itself. Then we think that it is we who are upset or at ease or whatever.

  

But really this mind of ours is already unmoving and peaceful... really peaceful! Just like a leaf which is still as long as no wind blows. If a wind comes up the leaf flutters. The fluttering is due to the wind — the "fluttering" is due to those sense impressions; the mind follows them. If it doesn't follow them, it doesn't "flutter." If we know fully the true nature of sense impressions we will be unmoved.

  

Our practice is simply to see the Original Mind. So we must train the mind to know those sense impressions, and not get lost in them. To make it peaceful. Just this is the aim of all this difficult practice we put ourselves through.

I'd like to read part of The Great Way from the Third Chinese Patriarch:

  

The Great Way is not difficult

for those who have no preferences.

When not attached to love or hate,

all is clear and undisguised.

Separate by the smallest amount, however,

and you are as far from it as heaven is from earth.

  

If you wish to know the truth,

then hold to no opinions for or against anything.

To set up what you like against what you dislike

is the disease of the mind.

  

When the fundamental nature of things is not recognized

the mind’s essential peace is disturbed to no avail.

The Way is perfect as vast space is perfect,

where nothing is lacking and nothing is in excess.

  

Indeed, it is due to our grasping and rejecting

that we do not know the true nature of things.

Live neither in the entanglements of outer things,

nor in ideas or feelings of emptiness.

Be serene and at one with things

and erroneous views will disappear by themselves.

  

When you try to stop activity to achieve quietude,

your very effort fills you with activity.

As long as you remain attached to one extreme or another

you will never know Oneness.

Those who do not live in the Single Way

cannot be free in either activity or quietude, in assertion or denial.

  

Deny the reality of things

and you miss their reality;

assert the emptiness of things

and you miss their reality.

The more you talk and think about it

the further you wander from the truth.

So cease attachment to talking and thinking,

and there is nothing you will not be able to know.

  

To return to the root is to find the essence,

but to pursue appearances or “enlightenment” is to miss the source.

To awaken even for a moment

is to go beyond appearance and emptiness.

  

So we turn to practices that deepen our understanding of what it means to take on an identity. Mindful of our bodies, of breathing, of feelings, of perceptions, of moods, of thoughts, of concepts and ideas. We can choose our path and become our own teachers using everything that we can here in this very same mind and body.

We pay attention to what we call our selves - to our habits - our beliefs - and our stories. We see thru these things into our very nature and when we see ourselves we into the nature of others.
