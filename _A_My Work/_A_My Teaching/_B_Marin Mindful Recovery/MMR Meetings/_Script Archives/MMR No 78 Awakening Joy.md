MMR#75 - Awakening Joy
There is a well known saying in neuroscience that the mind is velcro for negative experiences
and teflon for positive experiences
We have a Negativity Bias which is tendency not only to register negative stimuli more
readily but also to dwell on these events. This negativity bias means that we feel the
sting of a rebuke more powerfully than we feel the joy of praise.
The negativity bias is the notion that, even when of equal intensity, things of a more negative nature
(e.g. unpleasant thoughts, emotions, or social interactions; harmful/traumatic events) have a greater
effect on our psychological state and processes than neutral or positive things. In other words,
something very positive will generally have less of an impact on a person's behavior and cognition
than something equally emotional but negative.

We can get caught in the struggle of living - get lost in the details and lose the greater perspective -
that we are alive and a part of the greater universal consciousness.
Many of us look for an escape - ways to not look at our experience - either thru distraction or having
a fixed view of how to look at the world. We can get in a spiritual bypass - using the spiritual
principles and teachings in a way to avoid looking at our direct experience.
The first noble truth calls for us to look to understand the stress and suffering that is inherent in being
human.
In learning to let go of unskillful ways of coping there are other alternatives. One of these ways we
can approach spiritual practice is by directly cultivating our well-being, joy, happiness, peace, &
contentment.
There are some mindful ways of doing this. For myself, the #1 Practice is to notice suffering and try
to meet it with compassion, to surrender to it - to let go - instead of feeling sorry myself or less than -
poor me - or as can happen pointing the fingers at other - blame external events - people, places &
things.
This looking directly looking at our suffering is something very accessible that we can turn to and
begin to learn how to apply it in our daily lives.

But that is not always the case. When I was going thru the panic episode the cultivating an
Awakening of Joy helped me to gain a footing into grounded well-being.
And, although I do not practice it, all the time, it has had a profound effect on my practice.
“Awakening Joy is based on a key principle: Our joy and happiness is up to us.”
Our suffering or well-being is not solely determined by what is happening in our present
circumstance but to a large degree by out relationship to what is happening.
Happiness can come and go as we get our way or not, but many believe that happiness is a choice,
and one that we can learn to make it a habit.
James Baraz is famous for saying “Joy is always present” - It is already in us - it is inherent in us -
we have this capacity - like learning a language or to love.
According to neuroscience, we have a changing happiness level that rises and falls over time.
In fact, there was a study about people who won the lottery and those who endured extreme
hardship - say have their legs amputated. In either case there would be great joy or deep
depression, but either way people would return to a default level of being.
Some people can become uncomfortable with the word “joy” - like it is a pollyanna attitude or living in
denial. James calls joy “a general feeling of aliveness and well-being that is characterized by
meeting the ups and downs of life with authenticity and perspective.” I like that definition very much.
You can choose your own definition that better resonates with you. - be it contentment - peace -
ease - aliveness - happiness.
Some may think that talking about joy is out of touch with reality, especially with all of the challenges
we face in the world today. Many in the world are in fear and not present for their lives. My message
to myself and to you is to set the intention to wake up and rise above. Be a light for the world.
Awakening our inner joy despite circumstances - to be our own light - would seem to be the skillful of
living life..
