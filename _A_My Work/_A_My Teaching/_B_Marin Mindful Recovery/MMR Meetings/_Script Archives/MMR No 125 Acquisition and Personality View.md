In terms of relating to karma:
According to Asanga (one of the most important spiritual figures of Mahayana Buddhism),
karma is a mental impulse. It is synonymous with the mental factor of an urge, which
accompanies every moment of our experience. It is the mental factor that brings us in the
direction of a particular experience, either simply to look at or to listen to something, or, in
this case, to do something with or to it, to say it, or to think it. Whether it is physical, verbal,
or mental karma, the karmic impulse is the mental factor of an urge to do, say, or think
something. It is like the impulse to hit someone, to tell the truth, or to think longing thoughts
about a loved one. It is also the mental urge to continue doing, saying, or thinking about
something, as well as the mental urge to stop engaging in them and to do, say, or think about
something else. Usually, we are not at all aware of these mental urges or impulses. In
Western terminology, we would say they are usually "unconscious."

Before we get to that discussion, I’d like to talk a bit about Personality View in Buddhist practice
As you know, this is one of my favorite terms in Buddhism is Sakkaya ditthi.
- Ditthi being defined as “View”.
Bhikkhu Bodhi, in his Introduction of the Samyutta Nikaya, or “The Connected Discourses of the
Buddha” describes “Sakkāya as a term for the five aggregates as a collective whole (III
159,10–13).
The word literally means “the existing body,” the assemblage of existent phenomena that serve
as the objective basis of clinging.
Most translators render it as “personality,”. Another prominent translator rendered it
“embodiment”). But since, under the influence of modern psychology, the word “personality” has
taken on connotations quite foreign to what is implied by sakkāya, he now translates it as
“identity”

Sakkāya-diṭṭhi accordingly becomes “identity view,” the view of a self-existing either behind or
among the five aggregates.
This is a means of we are how we define a person in Buddhism.
These are known as the Aggregates and the associated clinging
- Form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and sense consciousness
- Constitues a Person
