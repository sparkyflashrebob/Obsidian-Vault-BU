The objects of the sense organs. These are the things we get stuck on.

That’s desire. The desire to stick to something, to have something, to become something, to

Content with what we have - with good company - supportive people around us - with the

There is the contentment of just being at ease - without grasping or having to stick onto things.
We can become aware of the restlessness and compulsiveness of the human mind. When we
stop resisting and grasping then we will find true contentment.

I’ll end with two connected stories from my own experience. I may have told this story in the
past, and if you have heard it, then I apologize. But it is an example of how this has played out
in my life.
I noticed how there have been many times where I was caught up in my life - even when going
places that inspire me - like in the forest or to the beach. At the beach but all in my head. Barely
able to connect with nature.
That is one big story of my life.
And then there is the story of the little bird that I met at a concentration retreat at Spirit Rock.

I had been having some difficulty with concentration. I have this tendency to either put in too
little effort or two much. I believe this was simply the case of a mind that did not want to be
tamed. After sometime on the retreat, during our interviews that occur, it was suggested that I
use the phrase bud-ho - Bud with the in-breath and ho with the out-breath.
So I did.
Shortly thereafter I was doing walking meditation on a forest path out behind the retreat center
and I looked to the right and noticed a baby bird in a tree - practicing taking off and landing on a
small branch - over and over.
This scene so filled me with awe.
But what was even more intriguing was that I was completely contented. I wasn’t thinking about
any of my story - of who I was - I was empty - I was present. Right there with that little bird.
And then I reflected back to those times on the beach or in a forest or with loved ones, where I
wasn’t present. Not morbid reflection but an acknowledgment of the difference between the two.
My practice and nature as my teachers. I offer this for your consideration.
