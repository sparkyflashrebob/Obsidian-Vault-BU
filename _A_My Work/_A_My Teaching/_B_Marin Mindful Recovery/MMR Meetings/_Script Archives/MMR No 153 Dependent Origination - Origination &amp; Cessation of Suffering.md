Chapter 4: The Origination and Cessation of Suffering - otherwise known as Dependent Origination - or Conditionality - or the chain of causation 
- Terminology
  - typical for it to be also called "interdependent co-arising"
	  - BB notes that this is inaccurate - as some aspects may be mutually dependent
  - this means dependence on one factor upon another
  - but it does not mean simultaneous arising 
	  - it is true that some factors such as contact and feeling may arise simultaneously
	  - but others such as feeling and craving may be separate
  - and then there are others (birth, old-age, and death) have temporal (a time gap) for the conditions to manifest
  
  But the Basic premise is that
  - Things arise due to conditions
  > - “When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises. When this does not exist, that does not come to be; with the cessation of this, that ceases.”
- referred to as "this-that conditionality"
The main way that this principle is applied in the Suttas
  - is as sequence of 12 factors
  - each dependent on its predecessor

1. [[Ignorance]]
2. [[Volition]]
3. [[Consciousness]]** (see monkey branch to branch)
4. [[Name-and-form]]**
5. the [[Six Sense Bases]]
6. [[Contact]]
7. [[Feeling]]
8. [[Craving]]
9. [[Clinging]]
10. [[Existence]]**
11. [[Birth]]
12. [[Old-age & death]]

  - This list can be seen as an expansion of the 2nd and 3rd Noble Truths
	  - a finer detail of the origination suffering and it ending
  - It also <u>reveals a more fundamental condition than craving for the cause of suffering - </u> which is [[avijja]] [[ignorance]]
  - This ignorance here being narrowly defined as "not knowing suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path"
  - and also a lack of awareness of the principles that shine the light on the true nature of phenomena (experience)
	  - 4 Nobel Truths
	  - 3 Characteristics
  - Within the understanding of Dependent Origination
	  - Ignorance sustains the round of dukkha
	  - And when Ignorance comes to the end, the entire network of conditions also ends
  
  As we can see the Individual Factors are formally defined
  - But In the suttas they only state the definitions but not the how
  - As a result this has given rise to different competing interpretations of the formula
  - all versions show the sequence of causal factors over a series of lives

Modern Interpretation 
- the sequence is related to a single lifetime
- it is also explained in a least one sutta how DO operates at the level of individual mind-moments (see Tricycle Article on "How Long Is the Moment")
- this is my preferred way of thinking about causality - in ones life and in moment to moment awareness
- But in order to do this we will need to think a bit differently about existence or becoming, birth, old age, & death
- typically,though the twelve terms of the formula are spread out over multiple lives

TRADITIONAL EXPLANATION:
- because of [fundamental [[ignorance]]]]], one engages in [[volitional activities]]
- [[wholesome & unwholesome]]
	- [[bodily, speech and mind]]
		- body (physical)
		- speech (talk to or about others)
		- mind (how we think about others & the world)
	- these actions generate [[Google Keep/Karma]] (which are like planting seeds)
		- plant wholesome or unwholesome seeds  
	- these seeds flower over time - and 
	- become potentials new existence (repercusions)
	- at death this karma propels consciousness into a new existence (result)
	- this is why I like to look at it from the perspective of each moment and the next
- new existence, this [[birth]], begins when consciousness arrives at a new embodiment
	- new assemblage of bodily & mental phenomena (name & form/aggregates)
- with [[name & form]] the [[six sense bases]] take shape and begin to function
- When the Six sense bases encounter objects there is [[contact]]
- with contact this gives rise to [[feeling]]
	- which as we know can be either pleasant, unpleasant/painful, & neutral
	- which trigger reactions/responses
	- with an untrained person, these feelings that arise cause [[craving]]
		- which is the desire for pleasant experiences & avoiding painful ones 
		- for wanting things that we do not have
		- so this comes with the belief that something outside of ourselves will make us happy or fulfilled.
	- Now there is the tendency when one gets what one wants for us to delight in that experience & for us to want to hold on to it tight
		- because of the fear of loss or of separation
		- THIS IS [[clinging]] and attachment
			- which can be thought of as a more intense type of craving
		-  clinging, in fact, can be used to justify more craving for pleasure and [[becoming]]*
		- this is what addiction is 
			- wanting to feel a certain way (good) and to avoid feeling another way (bad)
- with this clinging, there is a fresh round of volitional activities that occur that create the potential for new existence/of becoming
- which result in birth > old-age-and-death > and other manifestations of dukkha
- this is how karma works

There is also what is called the
[[Three-life interpretation of dependent origination]]  or "THE COMMENTARIAL INVENTION"
- which looks at Dependent Origination in terms of the Past, Present & Future
- Specifically there is a Sutta where the Buddha illustrates the difference between a wise and unwise person. 
	- Unwise person does not eliminate ignorance and craving of the past
	- continually moves on to new embodied existence
	- while the Wise person does eliminate ignorance and craving and is freed from any future embodied existence
		- and as a result they are no longer bound to birth, old age & death

Now to be clear the 12 Factor Formula
- is not exclusively linear
- it serves as a simplified representation of a complex process
	-  of overlapping and intersecting lines of [[conditionality]]

This conditionality is a Chain of Events - that follow natural law
- referred to in Buddhims as "specific conditionality" [[idappaccayata]]
- that is fixed, stable & invariable thru all time
- it is seen as  "[[real, not unreal, not otherwise]]"

[[Unsorted Notes/Dependent Origination]] provides a dynamic perspective on [[non-self]]
- that complements analytical perspective of the [[the five aggregates]]
- where the process of rebirth & causation occur <u>without an underlying subject</u>, a substantial self, of one existence to the next.
- This taking it out of the personal is important

Buddhas Time Two Camps: Eternalists & Annihilationist's
- Eternalists - core of every person there is an immortal self - substantial & autonomous - resists thru rebirths and attains liberation - preserving its unchanging status
- Annhilationists - denied existence of a permanent self that survives after death - at breakup of the body, personal existence comes to an absolute end - at death the living being is annihilated.
- <u>Dependent Origination - "teaching by the middle" - avoids two extremes - </u>
	- avoids that "all exists" by showing how personal continuity is possible with a self that persists thru the process
	- avoids that "all does not exist" by showing that as long as conditions drive the process of [[becoming]] remain intact, the conditions will continue to operate

- that our actions matter
- that we need to pay attention (be mindful)
- that what we are experiencing is not necessarily personal
- I may be experiencing what I am experiencing but it is what I am adding to things that is what really matters
- this keeps the wheel of dependent origination/this suffering going
- to look at what I am experiencing and my reactivity
- in body, speech, and mind
- to note my craving & clinging
- to note feeling
- to note if I am craving for sense pleasures or being otherwise in the moment 
- all this expands on ways of being aware
- not take away the pleasure that being alive brings
- but to appreciate it as best we can while it lasts
- and to not compound our suffering or of others
