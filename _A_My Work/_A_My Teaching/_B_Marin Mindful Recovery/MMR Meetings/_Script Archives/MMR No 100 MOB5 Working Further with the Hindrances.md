MMR#88-0D MOB4 Recognizing and Relinquishing the Hindrances

1. This week we will Further add to our discussion of working with Vedena (feeling tone) and that
that hinders us on the path
2. When we talk of well-being and happiness in the mind this is from the relinquishing of the
hindrances
a. not just technique
b. Something we need to attend to
i. becoming aware of their being present or not
ii. when they are relinquishing - letting go

3. While in meditation , if any of the hindrances come up - any quality of clarity and peace is
disturbed immediately.
a. So we will want to direct our attention to the presence or absent of these hindrances
both in life and in our meditation
4. In mediation - Important to establish a base first
a. MOB
b. attention on the breath
c. settling and adjusting into the rhythm of the breathing
d. extending awareness thru the whole body
e. awakening the body and mind with alertness and attention
f. so that awareness pervades the body and the mind
g. A feeling of awakening and energizing
h. sort of an inherent characteristic of awareness/mindfulness
i. Stops the outflow of the proliferating mind
j. Steeping the body and the mind with this quality of alertness &amp; attention
k. Letting that awareness and the attentiveness to the physical sensation of breathing fill
the body and fill the mind
l. The breath is the base and the body is the base
5. When the Buddha talks about developing the jhanas he uses images that are grounded in the
body and bodily experience
a. One fills, drenches, steeps and pervades the body
b. https://www.accesstoinsight.org/ptf/dhamma/sacca/sacca4/samma-samadhi/jhana.html
6. 1st Jhana is considered the Bliss of solitude
7. 2nd Jhana - the bliss of concentration
8. Using the body as the basis for this filling, drenching, steeping and pervading so that the body
and the mind are working together
9. Starting from the very start of the breath - say at the tip of the nose - filling that field of
awareness
a. pervade with that sense of attention and presence
b. as the breath comes in - fills the lungs, the abdomen, the diaphragm moves, the
abdomen rises - filling the body

10. Literally filling the body with air so that it is able to survive
a. and then breathing out - allowing the air to expel and then regenerate itself

11. Likewise, we need to fill our minds, our hearts, with awareness and attention in order to survive
as spiritual beings
a. if we don’t do that - return to that foundation - that base of awareness - and quality of
knowing - knowing clearly we will lose our base - our foundation.

12. So using the breath, using the body as that vehicle, the means of extending awareness and
creating that presence - being present for the experience of the breath.
a. this is something that we have that is concrete to hang our awareness on.
13. It’s easy for the mind to drift - to wander
a. tendency to out - to sensual desire - something pleasurable, interesting.
b. sense of seeking an object
c. stimulation from that object
d. that hit of delight and pleasure
14. We find ourselves in that situation over and over again because that’s appears to be the more
apparent way out of suffering - out of boredom - a sense of lassitude (mental weariness/lack of
energy)
a. where we don’t want to put the energy out
b. looking to sensuality gets us out of that lack - to get us to feel alive
15. That’s where we want to bring the breath to mind.
a. so that we are seeking our justification for being - for existence in sensual objects.
b. this is a dead end - it doesn’t really ever satisfy
i. it can be gratifying, exciting &amp; pleasurable
ii. but satisfaction is a different thing
iii. For the mind to be satisfied, contented - is for it to not be overwhelmed by
suffering - even the subtlest shades of discontent

16. This is what the Buddha is pointing to - coming to a place of abiding in non-suffering
17. Choosing that option with each in-breath and each out-breath
a. challenging oneself
b. encouraging
c. giving oneself this opportunity
d. Can I pay attention to just this in-breath
e. Renewing the practice in each moment rathering waiting for the results to come
18. (SHIFT TO ILL-WILL) As meditators we will want to be attentive to recognizing when irritation or
ill-will sets in
a. the irritations and dislikes
b. where one one shrinks away
c. we will want to be quick to recognize when that kind of mental state starts
19. Theravada tend to calls these defilements - the Tibetians - afflictions - what are called the
“kilesa”
20. We are afflicting ourselves with pain
a. We feel afflicted when it is present
b. last week I spoke of how ill-will was seen as being physically ill
c. but it is equally true that we can see this as harming ourselves too
d. by harboring ill-will - feeding anger - this is an affliction and we are torturing ourselves
21. As meditators, when aversion rolls in - asking ourselves, “What does that do to the body?”

MMR Mindfulness of Breathing

a. This is one of the ways that it is helpful to use the body as an anchor
b. So as we are steeping, drenching, pervading the body with awareness and a feeling of
fullness of attention - this is relaxing on the body.
c. It’s actually difficult to keep ill-will going if your actually physically relaxed
22. With ill-will there is always some sort of tensing - tightening
a. this is a natural response to aversion
b. and when we do this we lock into the mental feeling of aversion and fail to recognize that
we are physically afflicted as well.

23. Returning to that quality of awareness - pervading the breath with this feeling of attention and
awareness
a. a natural relaxing and deepening of that attention so it pervades the body
b. it can be easy to dispel ill-will in that way - actually difficult to hold on to ill-will in that way
c. so we will want to use the mindfulness of breathing to work thru those qualities of
aversion that come up.

24. With Sloth and Torpor - it can be easy - as the mind starts to settle, things quiet down - if one
does not sustain the attention - one doesn’t apply effort - then it can quickly shift into dullness.
a. If the mind is taking interest and that quality of filling, steeping, &amp; pervading the body
with the awareness of the breath then this really helps to dispel that sense of lassitude
(mental weariness/lack of energy) that goes down into dullness - when the mind loses its
brightness

25. The suggestion is to not only take awareness - to hold it - but to allow that awareness to move
a. flowing into the body with the breath - the sensation of the breath - paying attention to
the subtleties of sensation - moving that awareness thru the body
b. so that the body is energized and as a result the mind is energized
c. when the mind and body are energized in that way it can become very stable
d. not slip into sloth and torpor

for your consideration and our discussion.
