[[_My Research/Dharma Subjects and Themes/Four Noble Truths/The Four Noble Truths|The Four Noble Truths]] are not just one Buddhist doctrine among the others
	but actually a framework for the whole of his [[teaching]]s

His chief disciple, Sariputta, underscored  the importance of this formula of the 4NT when he said: 
> “Just as the footprints of all other animals of the jungle can fit into the footprint of an elephant, which is declared chief with respect to size, so whatever wholesome teachings there are can all fit into the four noble truths” (MN 28, I 184).

The commentaries note that [[_My Research/Dharma Subjects and Themes/Four Noble Truths/The Four Noble Truths|The Four Noble Truths]] <u>can be extracted</u> from other teachings even when they are not explicitly mentioned 

The Four Noble Truths can also be seen as the blueprint of the [[Samyutta Nikaya]]
	the other sections can be seen as elaborations of the individual truths  
	- and so we will explore these a bit 
	
In addition to the reference to the [[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Suttas 1/Elephants Footprint]],  this is another commonly referred to Simile, [[Simsapa Sutta]], which is commonly known as the [[Handful of Leaves]] Sutta that I mention here.
	like the leaves in a forest which are  many, the leaves that I can hold in my hand are few
	Similarly the things that the Buddha knew were many
	but the things he expounded on were few
		- few things he focus on being suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path 
	It was not for him to disclose all of the things he had seen because they do not provide true benefit  
		He spoke about the [[4 Noble Truths 1]] because they were beneficial and lead "to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to peace, to direct knowledge, to enlightenment, to nibbana"

So it important to remember that the Buddha was not trying to explain the workings of the universe, to how it all began, the meaning of life, and our purpose in the world.
	he focused on the question of how to free ourselves. How we can be happy unconditionally.
	and that is by understanding these 4 noble truths and developing the noble path.

Dukkha 
	most translators render this word as "suffering" (so does Bhikky Bodhi)
		can lead to misunderstanding 
		- thinking Buddha holds all existence is continuously rife with pain & misery 
		- not true 
		- for this reason some translators prefer to leave it untranslated
		- in some contexts (especially in relation to feeling) dukkha does mean pain and suffering  - its original use 
		- Buddha drew this word out from its original meaning
		- used it to highlight the inescapably flawed, deficient, and unsatisfactory nature of sentient existence. 
		- Used this way, not only painful experience, but all experience within the conditioned world is inherently defective. 
		- Buddha understood that Life contains abundant opportunities for us to experience joy and [[Happiness]] 
			- [[Mangala Sutta]]  ([[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Paramis 1/The Highest Blessings]] )
				- conductive to blessings 
				- 38 blessings
				- regardless though they are unstable 
					- subject to change 
		Every factor of being 
		Every facet of experience 
		when examined
		turns out to be impermanent and therefore unreliable
		unable to provide lasting satisfaction
	pointing to the ultimate release from [[Google Keep/Dukkha]] 
		what lies beyond the sphere of transience and mortality.
		in [[nibbana]] 
			the unconditioned state free from birth, decay and death
