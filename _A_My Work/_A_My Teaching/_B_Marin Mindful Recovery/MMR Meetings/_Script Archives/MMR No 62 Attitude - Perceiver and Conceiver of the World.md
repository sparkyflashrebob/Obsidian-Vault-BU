2. Ajahn Amaro - who is the current Abbott of Amaravati - succeeding Ajahn Sumdho and
therefore the head of the western branch of the Thai Forest tradition.
3. He gave a talk here in California about his reflection of a buddhist teaching on being the
Perceiver and Conceiver of the World.
4. This ties together with teachings on the personality view, views in general, how we
create ourselves and the conceptualization or putting together of our experience that we
have been talking about in recent weeks.
5. Most of us have the idea that we are moving around in the world and we see the world
with an unbiased perspective. We feel like we're seeing the world - how we see the
people that we live with - the people around us, the landscape and the society that we're
in. Seeing it as it is. We feel that we are seeing that with a clear perspective.
6. So in this little teaching that the Buddha gives he says that we are both a perceiver of
the world and a conceive of the world.
7. How is it that one is a perceiver of the world and a conceiver of the world? All that we
know comes to us thru the eye, the ear, the nose, the tongue, the body, and the mind.
That is the means whereby one is a perceiver of the world and a conceived of the world.
8. As it did to me last week. It can feel a bit technical -or philosophical, but I feel it's a very
clear and simple expression of a more important principle which is that we don't see the
world, we see our minds' representation of the world. That's the world that we meet.
That's the world that we've all known throughout our whole lives.
9. So every single one of us, I can't read minds, but I can guarantee that every single one
of us throughout our lives ever since birth, everything that we have known about the
world has been known through our eyes, our ears, our nose, our tongue, our body, our
mind. So that's the only world any of us have ever known is the world that has been
filtered through our senses.
10. I would say that if we if we look, ever since we were a small child, a teenager and a
young adult, and now for many of us, our older years - all the time the world that we
have known has been the world built off of what we see hear, smell, taste, touch, think,
what we remember, what we imagined and so on and so forth.
11. So that's the world. This is the world as our mind presents to us. Is this reminding us of
last week - some? 😀😀😀
12. So you know “I think, yeah, but I'm seeing the real world. I mean, yeah, the skies blue,
the trees are green. That's the world.” But we are missing the fact that our world, on a
moment-by- moment basis, is pieced together through these different threads.
13. I feel that it's very significant, both in terms of the way that we look at our own life and
then we look at others, the people that we live with, the people we work with, the people
in the society in the and the world in general.
