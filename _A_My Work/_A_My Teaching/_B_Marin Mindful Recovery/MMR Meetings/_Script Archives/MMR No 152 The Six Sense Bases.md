- [[Six Sense Bases]] another approach based on our senses - really an expansion of the aggregare of consciousness - now it is called a base (which  is a foundation like the foundation of a house is the base of the base) - it is also called a sphere (like a gated fence) - an area of influence - like the [[sphere of awareness]] - they form the internal and external base for the objects they connect with - eye & [[forms]] & [[eye-consciousness]] - ear & sounds & sound consciousness - that same with the tongue & taste - nose & smell - body & touch - mind & mental objects - each are connected with the aggregate of consciousness - and also the other aggregates of feeling-perception-volition and the [[mind-base]] 
- [[mind-base]] - identified with consciousness specifically and all six classes of consciousness (as these are associated with their particular aspect of being) - the mind-base can exist on its own (as the base of mental objects) - so it can be associated with passive internal consciousness of other senses and it can have an  external base purely on mental objects (thoughts, introspection & reflection, imagination, meditative contemplation) - 
- [[Buddism Export/Senses/Dharma Subjects and Themes/Aggregates (skandha)/Senses]] based have close connection with craving - with their being the sense base - then their is contact with an object - which  generates a feeling - this is what conditionality is - with contact as a condition there arises feeling - with feeling there is craving - all things exist due to conditions
- as we know [[craving]] is the origin of dukkha - these cravings are nurtured by feelings that are originated thru sense contact - SO to eliminate craving we must change our perspective - our relationship to what we are experiencing - we need to control our reaction to sense input - in this practice, this training is called "[[restraint of the senses]]" - or [[GUARDING THE SENSES]] - this training is an important bridge between ethical behavior and formal meditation

Coming back to the subject of Craving (again)
- teaching of 4NT - human delusion of taking enjoyable feelings as permanent - and then grasping at change - which is dukkha - and with wisdom seeing feeling as impermanent  - likewise with forms, perceptions, volition and consciousness - when we see clearly ignorance is abandoned - if not, we keep holding onto them as ours and permanent
- Our sense bases are drawn to their objects - eye to forms - ear to sounds -  we are happy when we get the experiences we desire - but in doing so - set ourselves up for disappointment - for dukkha - dejection or anguish - agreeable senses experiences are like bait - sometimes called the six hooks for the misery of beings - for when we swallow the bait of Mara - we are under Mara's control.
- Our bondage is not in the senses themselves - this is an important distinction - it is not in the objects we experience - but in the craving that arises as a result of the contact  - the eye is not the fetter of forms - it is the desire and lust that arises in dependence on them - what the Buddha was able to do was eradicate craving - that is the purpose of our training
- A helpful analogy  is thinking of the senses as Six kinds of animals
  - a snake, a fish, a bird, a dog, a cow, a monkey
  - when bound together and released, each animal rushes to its familiar habitat
  - if we do not exercise restraint and self-control - each of the senses run toward their object
  - but if tied to a post (with mindfulness) - they will pull different directions but eventually become fatigued, settle down and become still
  - This is the mind in meditation being pulled about - but with mindfulness as our anchor - eventually the senses lay down and become quiet and peaceful
  - This is why [[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/Mindfulness/Mindfulness of the Body/Mindfulness of the body]] is emphasized as a method of training
