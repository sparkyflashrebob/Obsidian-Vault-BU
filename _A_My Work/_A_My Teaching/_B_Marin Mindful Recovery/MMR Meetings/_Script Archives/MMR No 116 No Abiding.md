Ajahn Chah
> "Making the postures even’ refers to the mind, to our awareness, giving rise to wisdom in the mind, to illumine the mind.
> This wisdom of ours must be present in all postures; we must know, or understand, constantly.
> Standing, walking, sitting or lying down, we know all mental states as impermanent,
> unsatisfactory, and not-self."

Ajahn Chah. The Collected Teachings of Ajahn Chah - Single Volume (Kindle Locations
745-747). Kindle Edition.

The point is:
● Whether we like or dislike what is in the mind - we are aware it.
● If we focus our attention on the mind constantly, then we have the gist of the practice.
● Even if we experience (identify) mental states as "good or bad" - we don't forget
ourselves.
● We do not get lost in the good or bad.
● We do not get high or low if we are praised or blamed
● We see the danger of this (because it is a cause for suffering).
● We want to be constant/persistent in our practice to the extent of knowing moods.
● Even if we cling to moods, if we are aware - this is better already.
● Even if there still isn't release, but we know if we let go, there will be peace.
● We want to be aware of the 8 worldly winds at all times.

● This is not the way of the world - usually, we get upset when we experience things we
are unhappy with or are happy when we get what we want.
● We will want to understand and know the consequences of clinging. (Knowing is seeing what we are experiencing in the mind).
● We will want to know the danger of clinging to anything.
● We will want to be sensitive to our moods. Sensitive meaning to be mindful of them. Aware. 
● Because clinging to them causes suffering
● Learn to see clinging itself as the cause of that suffering
● And then look for the way to let go.
● We can hold (touch our experience) and not cling - like picking up a flashlight. We can
hold it and we can put it down.
● Things do not have to stick to us.
● So our practice is 1st we have to hold (touch/be aware of) things - like wanting - set the intention.
● Desire or wanting is required to set our intentions. Like the desire to be free.
● Otherwise, we would not act.
● But when wanting arises we do not cling to it.
● We know and then let go.
● Learn to hold things with wisdom
● In this same way whatever we do - we ask for no return

● Buddha taught to work just in order to work and not ask for more than that.
● If we do something to get something in return that will clause suffering.
● Expectations are future regrets/resentments.
● We do, and then let go, and then let go some more.
● Practice to the point of not wanting any return.
● We practice in order to let go.
● If there is any wanting to get somewhere and we will never get there.
● You can see this in our meditation.
● We end up spinning out, or not being able to sustain our practice. Regressing.
● Allow the mystery to unfold. Allow things to develop thru presence.
● Whatever you get is a cause for suffering so we practice not getting anything.
● This is called 'making the mind empty'.
● It is Empty but still doing.
● This is not the emptiness of not having anything but the emptiness within the things that are there.
● This is not the same emptiness where we cannot see anything. As if nothing is there.
● If you believe this then that is wrong view.
● In Thailand, many people come to the monastery to get sprinkled with holy water.
○ Ajahn Chah would ask them "Why do you want this?"
○ They do this because they want to live happily and comfortablly and not get sick.
○ And they believe that this holy water will provide this to them.
○ The worldly way is to do things for a reason. To get some return.
○ In Buddhism we do things with the idea of gaining anything.
● The Buddha want us to
○ Go beyond cause and effect
○ go above happiness and beyond suffering
● "Think about it, there's nowhere to stay"
● We can't leave home and go to where there is no home.
● The reason is because we have always lived with becoming, with clinging.
● We cling because we do not know what to do. We grasp at whatever is present.
● We do not understand that what we are experiencing is not ours to possess.
● What we experience by the senses are things that are just passing thru.
● Thoughts - feelings - emotions - memories - all just passing thru.
● They do not last. They are impermanent.
● But we grasp them as if they are ours.
● When we talk about Nibanna - which is the cessation of suffering - this is something that
not gained.
● Most do not want to go to Nibanna because there is nothing there.
● We do not want to be free because we want to become something other than what is.
● We have some idea about it.
● To be free is to not be confined. Not bound.
● It is like space itself.
● In a home there is the roof - this is a place of abiding. Same with the ground floor.
● But in the space in between there is no abiding. Just emptiness.

● There are no qualities
● Nibbana is emptiness.
● Hearing this people back up.
● People attached to abiding - to being born again.
● We do not need to be afraid of letting go.
● When we let go we are not holding on.
● When we let go we are letting go of what we do not own anyway.
● We cannot let go of what we are.
● Our practice is to rest in the awareness of what is -without grasping.
● Rest in that isness of emptiness.
● To turn to what we are and not to hold on to what we are not.
● We can let go of everything and when we do we realize who we really are.
● This is our true refuge
● This is what the practice is all about.
