## Infinite Consciousness

Here we continue with refining our understanding on Emptiness
In this case:
The Instructions ask us to 
- now not give attention to infinite space but to infinite consciousness
- there is this non-emptiness: this unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness
- there is only only weariness because of this perception
- Whatever is not present, one sees as empty. Whatever else is present, one see as present. This is how one Dwells in emptiness without distortion

So here we are to go beyond what we intentionally perceived previously
but also to not lose what we have previously cultivated.
	We will want to know where one has been


So With this step we turn to the mind itself
	by earlier being absorbed with the notion of infinite space, consciousness itself has come to be at connected with the experience of infinity.
	We then turn back to the mind itself to enable the perception of infinite consciousness 
	consciousness itself becomes the object and content of our experience

This reveals the foundational role consciousness plays in our subjective experience
	infinite space involves the whole world without limit
	perception of infinite consciousness turns attention to that which has been aware of infinite space
		in a way then, the whole world is now seen to be in one's own mind.

Early Buddhist thought does not consider consciousness to be the source of the external world
	the external world exists as a conditioned and impermanent process independent of our act to cognize it.
	For with out our sense consciousness we would not know the world

In terms of "my" subjective experience, consciousness is its very foundation.
	our experience of the world is impossible without conscioiusness

In other words
	for phenomena in the world to exist for me, and be experienced by me, consciousness is indispensable.
	Consciousness is the source of my world
	It provides the ground that my world experience unfolds in

So once we get beyond "space", the subjective experience of infinite consciousness becomes the pervasive theme of meditative practice from here on out.

This step reveals the substantial contribution that minds bring to our experience of the world

## THE NATURE OF CONSCIOUSNESS

As we know, the Early Discourses frequently analyze the [[5 Aggregates]]
	Their main emphasis be used as a tool to reveal the empty nature of all the aspects of experience that one might identify with and cling to as "I" and "mine". 
	
Within the context of the 5 Aggregates, consciousness is only one aspect of mind
	It is something distinct from feeling, perception and mental formations.
	which stand for the affective, recognizing, and conative dimensions of the mind

In other passages, including this one, 
consciousness stands for the mind in its totality.
with this meditation training on the sphere of consciousness
our perceived experience converges on consciousness
	it has become all-embracing
	these other aspects of mind corresponding to the mental aggregates
		feeling, perception and mental fabrication
	have become subdued so much that they are barely noticable
	In this way, in actual meditation practice
	consciousness can come to represent the mind in its totality

To most of us, the quality of knowing and being aware is a constant given in mental experiences
	And this may lead us to believe that consciousness is permanent
	This is a common belief - even at the time of the Buddha.
	
	At the time of the Buddha, there was an exchange between a monk named Sati (:)) and the buddha
	who had come to the conclusion that this consciousness, which is permanent, is the same consciousness that is what is reborn. 
	(And as a note here:  you may want to think of this as the same consciousness as that might or might not go to heaven.)
	
> 	Being informed about this, the Buddha asked Sati to explain his understanding... 
> 	The Blessed One asked him: “Is it true that you speak in this way: ‘I understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One in this way: <u>it is this consciousness now which is going to be reborn, not another?’”</u> 
> 	The monk Sāti replied: “Blessed One, I truly understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One in this way: ‘it is this consciousness <u>now</u> which is going to be reborn, not another.’” 
> 	The Blessed One asked him: “What is this consciousness?” 
> 	The monk Sāti replied: “Blessed One, this consciousness is that which speaks, feels, acts, instructs, gives rise to, appears, it is that which does good or bad deeds and experiences their results.” 
> 	The Blessed One reprimanded him: “Sāti, how come you understand the Dharma taught by me in this way? From whose mouth have you heard that I give such a teaching? ”
	   
	   Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

Here clearly, Buddha did not teach this and we can come to understand this in our practice.  That consciousness is a permanent. How would it transmigrate from one life to the next if it is not permanent.  

### Another way of looking at consciousness

One way of looking at consciousness that might be helpful from the teachings is in the 
Simile of a monkey swinging from branch to branch in a forest
	similarly with consciousness, continually changing, consciousness taking one object after another, going branch to branch - grabbing what arises in consciousness
	sights, sounds, tastes, etc..
However stable it may appear, consciousness is a flux of conditionally arisen moments of being conscious
	so it might be better thought of as a string of consciousness
	or "consciousnessing"
		This is we can avoid the thought of consciousness being stable and substantial
	Consciousness being Just change itself
	
Think of this.
> 	If the cognizing part of the mind was beyond change then it would be forever frozen in the condition of knowing one single thing.
That is not how the mind works.

Knowing different things implies change. Something we know to be true.  That experience is the constant experience of change.

> "Conscioiusnessing" is a stream of conditionally arisen moments of knowing

## Dependent Arising

The Conditionality of experience is a theme that to some degree underlies the teaching on emptiness. And comes into our exploration of emptiness.

This Conditionality is also what underpins the reflection regarding "what weariness has been overcome and what weariness is still here".

Repeating the phrase so we are reminded of the context at this point:
> if ... one wishes to dwell much in emptiness ... one should not give attention to the perception of earth and not give attention to the perception of the sphere of infinite space, but should frequently give attention to the unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness. 
> In this way one knows that this is empty of the perception of earth and empty of the perception of the sphere of infinite space. Yet there is this non-emptiness: just the unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness. 
> [One knows]: “Whatever weariness because of the perception of earth is not present for me; whatever weariness because of the perception of the sphere of infinite space is also not present for me. There is only the weariness because of the unitary perception of the sphere of infinite consciousness.” 
> Whatever is not present, one therefore sees as empty; whatever else is present, one sees as truly present. Ānanda, this is called truly dwelling in emptiness, without distortion.

Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

So we are talking  about the Weariness that has been overcome and what weariness is still there.
%%Talking about stress%%
The experience of infinite consciousness is a conditionally arisen mental state
	reached by leaving behind the other conditions, the previous steps we are exploring.
	the perceptions of earth and infinite space
	Setting up the conditions for the present moments experience
	
"weariness" for the conditions left behind
	brings out an aspect shared by all that is conditioned
	at some point on should become weary of it
	this is an insight into the nature of things that we can experience
	
However sublime it is, it is a conditioned product of the mind and as such something wearisome.
Qualifying perception as a form of weariness
	combines this insight into conditionality with a direct appreciation and understanding of dukkha
And prepares us for the gradual entry into pure emptiness
	which is stepping out of depending on any experience at all
	Into the UNCONDITIONED

> One meditates in such a way that one <u>does not</u> cultivate meditation in dependence on the earth, one <u>does not</u> cultivate meditation in dependence on water, fire, wind, or [the sphere of boundless] space, or [the sphere of boundless] consciousness, or [the sphere of] nothingness, or [the sphere of] neither-perception-nor-non-perception, not in dependence on this world, not in dependence on that world, not [in dependence on] the sun or the moon, not [in dependence on] what is seen, or heard, experienced, and cognized, not [in dependence on] what one has attained, not [in dependence on] what one searches for, not [in dependence on] what conforms with one’s experience, not [in dependence on] what conforms with one’s contemplation; yet one cultivates in meditation.

Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 

This form of meditation can be accomplished once one is able and willing to let go of these perceptions. 
	And in order for this to happen 
	It requires for us to become disenchanted with them
	This is the purpose for the gradual entry into emptiness
	Progress in our meditation takes place by leaving behind this weariness

This is similar to what we covered when we talked about in the 16 Steps of Mindfulness of breathing.
Seeing things as being impermanent, we do not hold onto them.

Similarly, this practice and teaching on emptiness is designed to ensure that we see each stage as merely a stepping stone on the meditative path
Even the sublime experience of infinite consciousness is a type of weariness
	It is conditioned

#### Mahanidana Sutta

%% For a definition of name-and-form cf., e.g., SN 12.2 at SN II 3,34 (translated Bodhi 2000: 535) and its parallel EĀ 49.5 at T II 797b28, which similarly lists feeling, perception, attention, contact, and intention.%%

Another related sutta, the Buddha is talking with his attendent, Ananda, talking about dependent arising is.
Here referring to name-and-form (namarupa) and its relation to consciousness.
			name being (feeling, perception, intention, contact & attention)
			Form being the body.
	Asking the Question, if there is a condition for name-and-form.
			If consciousness were not to enter the mother's womb, would name-and-form manifest?
				It would not
			If consciousness were to depart, would it combine with anything else?
				It would not
			If consciousness for a baby be cut off at the onset, destroyed and made non-existent, would name-and-form come to growth?
				It would not
			With this the Buddha stated:
> 					for this reason it should be understood that this is the cause of name-and-form, the source of name-and-form, the origin of name-and-form, the condition for name-and-form, namely consciousness. Why is that? Because conditioned by consciousness there is name-and-form.
					
					Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 
					
He further asked Ananda if there a condition for consciousness?
> 				 ‘There is also a condition for consciousness.’ If someone asks: ‘What is the condition for consciousness?’, then one should answer in this way: ‘Name-and-form is the condition.’”
				 
				 Analayo. Compassion and Emptiness in Early Buddhist Meditation . Windhorse Publications Ltd. Kindle Edition. 
		
The idea that consciousness conditions [[name-and-form]] and name-and-form condition consciousness is pretty clear.  You cannot have one without the other in one who is alive. This has a profound meaning when we look at other forms that are alive.

The main point here is to emphases' this basic principal of conditionality.
	Referred to as This-That mentality
		when this is, that is,
		when this is not, that is not

These two lean on each other, like two bundles of reeds of grass.
	One cannot be without the other.
	Neither can claim independent existence

They form the matrix of existence. 
	conditioned experience arising from them
	and with that the possibility of attachment, defilements, craving & clinging.

This can be made to collapse when they no longer lean on each other. Going beyond the conditioned to that of the unconditioned.
So how might we go about unconditioning the mind?
