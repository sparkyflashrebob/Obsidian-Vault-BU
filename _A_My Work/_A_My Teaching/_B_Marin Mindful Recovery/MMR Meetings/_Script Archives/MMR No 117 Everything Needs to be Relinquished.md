This reflection is often translated as contemplating, but another translation is observing.
Ajahn Succito gives out the translation as witnessing.
I think this is helpful.
Say 'witnessing impermanence'
'witnessing change'
'witnessing uncertainty'
'breathing in - witnessing uncertainty'

'breathing in - witnessing impermanence'
In whatever way that the nature of impermanence stands out.
Attending to that sense of change
This is a foundational aspect of the insight that leads to relinquishment, that is in turn a part of
that foundational insight.
Noticing that things are constantly changing.
The 2nd aspect of the dhamma in the fourth tetrad
is this aspect of dispassion,
cooling
This insight comes about when we see and witness this impermanence, changeability,
uncertainty of things.
Getting the sense that nothing is really a sure thing.
There is arising and ceasing constantly taking place.
And as a result there is a cooling reflected in our being.
It's not that one doesn't feel anything.
We do. But there is a clarity to that to that settling and cooling.
We usually get excited in both ways.
We get excited about dreading something. Something that is going to take place.
And we get excited about how good something is going to be.
That excitement is warming, heating up.
Versus that cooling we are talking about here
To have a coolness of heart is to see really clearly.
It's grounded in the body.
Grounded in understanding
Ground in feeling, emotions, and mental formations.
Grounded in the understanding of the way the heart moves and shifts
It boosts the heart's potential for peace and stability

Seeing it in the light of truth of the way things are
So that seeing - observing - witnessing -
Is through the lens of anicca
impermanence,
changeability

This cooling and settling matures into that sense of niroda

Niroda is an interesting word because it is commonly translated as 'cessation"
But there is this tendency to approach cessation as like an ending or cutting off
Like we need to chase things down to make them end - to stop them. We don't need to do that.
That happens on its own.
You'd think that's the purpose of the practice
We can get really busy with cessation - if we view it as trying to stop things
And equally valid translation for niroda is non-arising.
We can pay attention to the non-arising -
say the non-arising of dukkha

the non-arising of clinging
