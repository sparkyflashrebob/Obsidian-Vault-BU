Theme: The Nutriments
________________________________
"All beings subsist on nutriment"
According to the Buddha, this one single fact about life deserves to be remembered,
contemplated, and understood.
If understood widely and deeply enough, this saying reveals a truth that leads to the root of all
existence and also its uprooting.
Nutriment
● Talking about both biological and mental life
● 4 kinds
○ edible food
○ sense-impressions
○ volitions
○ consciousness
● hunger is that which stands behind the entire process of nutrition
● The body craves ceaselessly for material food
● And the mind hungers as eagerly for its own nourishment
○ with sense-impression and ideas

This is the basis for Craving (tanha)
● Which is the principle condition for any "in-take" or "up-take" or getting
● it is the first factor common for all types of nutriment, be they physical or mental
The 2nd being the assimilation of food
● eating and digesting
● Where with digesting what was external becomes absorbed into the internal
● what was foreign matter becomes "one's own" and is identified with one's personality

Our mind feeds on "external" material
● sense impressions and experiences
● on the contents of the store-house of knowledge
● On our memories, when they become objects of mind themselves
● And what cannot be absorbed by the system is discarded
○ So there is this constant process of grasping and rejecting
○ assimilating and dissimilating
○ identifying with oneself and alienating
○ This is something more easily seen in terms of mind
● When we look closely at this process of nutrition, physical and mental, we shall notice
that it is not only the eater who consumes the food, but, in the course of assimilation,
also the food that devours the eater.
● There is thus mutual absorption between them.
● We know how much people can be changed (for better or worse) by ideas they have
absorbed and which finally have absorbed and consumed them.
○ This is something we can easily see in the world at large
● By thoughtfully observing this process we can see how illusory the concept of an abiding
self or substance is.
○ All the parts of our lives that have been building us into who we are at this
moment.

● This insight alone can lead us to an understanding of the Buddha's teaching on not-self.
● In any essay by Nyanaponika Thera, a prominent German monk, on "The Four
Nutriments of Life" he says:
○ "Individualized life is ' neither a metaphysical 'I' - an identity nor a mere physical
process, but a nutrimental process and as such it is neither something which is in
and by itself, nor something caused by another, but something that is maintaining
itself: and all these so-called higher faculties of thinking and feeling are different
forms of eating, of maintaining oneself"

● While supporting the Buddhist concept of not-self, it is also a vindication of the other two
characteristics of life - that of impermanence and suffering.
Impermanence and change are at the very root of the nutritive process
● This nourishment is that in need of constant replenishment
● It is a bottomless hole that needs to be filled again and again
● No different with our mental hole - with our ideas, and beliefs, views and opinions.
Urge to preserve life both on a physical level and a mental one
● eating and being hungry again
This is a suffering that is mostly hidden by the habituation of this elementary feature of routine
life

● The search for food and for acquisition
● in the animal kingdom devouring each other is the law of nature
○ And something we mainly look away from even though it is in plane site
● at some point, humanity will not be able to support itself
● This is at the heart of the 4 Noble Truths
○ The 4 Nutriments of life stand in for the 1st of these truths
■ Craving for the Nutriments is the 2nd Noble Truth
■ Stopping this craving process (its cessation) is the 3rd Noble Truth
■ and the way to cessation is the Noble Eightfold path

● This process of Nutrition demonstrates the conditioned nature of all existence
So what are these 4 Nutriments:
I will briefly describe them.
1. The 1st is the Nutriment of Food
a. Something we know all too well about.
b. Simile: A couple, foodless in the midst of a desert, eat their little child, to enable
them to reach their destination.
2. The 2nd is the Nutriment of Sense-impressions
a. The need for us to experience the senses.
i. The eye wants to see things
ii. The ear want to hear things
iii. The tongue wants to taste things
iv. The skin wants to touch things
v. The mind wants things to think about
b. Simile: A skinned cow, wherever she stands, will be ceaselessly attacked by the
insects and other creatures living in the vicinity.
c. Guided by phassa - touch - contact (both physical and mental) -
d. Nutriment is part of each and every state of mind, even occuring in dreams and
subliminal states of consciousness
i. It nourishes feelings and sensations
1. Which we know to be either pleasant, unpleasant or indifferent
a. This craving for sense-impressions
i. arises from unguarded feelings
ii. And as such, has an unlimited supply of foodstuff to
be digested
1. An unending stream of changing forms,
sounds, smells, flavors, bodily impacts, and
ideas which impinge on us as long as we
live

ii. Any kind of feeling bound to cause suffering and conflict in one who has
not freed themself from attachment
1. Painful feeling is suffering itself
2. pleasant feeling brings suffering thru its changing

3. indifferent feeling produces suffering thru the boredom and
dullness of it

iii. And even with this we Hunger for new experiences
3. The 3rd is the Nutriment of Volitional Thought - Our Intentions
a. chiefly Karma -
b. compared it to a person being dragged by others into a pit of glowing embers
c. good karma tinged with delusion and evil
i. life-afffirming volitions
1. plans and ambitions
ii. drag us in samsara
iii. nutriment for rebirth in the 3 rhelms of existence
1. desire realm
2. form realm
3. formless realm
d. urge to plan and to aspire
i. struggle and conquer
ii. build and destroy
iii. do and undo
iv. invent and discover
v. form and transform
vi. organize and create
vii. sent us to the depth of the oceans and the vastness of space
viii. makes us the vicious predatory animal
ix. also enables us to reach lofty heights
e. Restlessness is at the root
i. lust for activity and creative urge
ii. constant hunger for the 4 nutriments of life
iii. volitional thought has to go foraging to find the nutriment that we crave for
iv. it is incessent, short lived and constantly repetitive
v. world-building and world-destruction
vi. the bottomless depth which cannot be filled by whatever guise we
assume to take on

4. And the 4th Nutriment is that of Consciousness itself
a. The Buddha likened the Nutriment of Consciousness like the punishment of a
criminal who is 3 times daily pierced with 300 spears
b. desire for conscious awareness has the same character as that for sense
impressions
c. But it also has the element of being awareness of our punitive results of past
cravings and delusions -
i. Thinking back into our memory - suffering from our past deeds or from the
deeds of others.
