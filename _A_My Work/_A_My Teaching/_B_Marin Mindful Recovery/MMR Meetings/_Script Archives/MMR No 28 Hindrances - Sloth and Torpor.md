Review of the Hindrances:
Forces in the Mind can make it difficult for us to be mindful. Most of the time we take these
forces personally, maybe feeling that we are less than, maybe feeling that we are incapable of
being able to deal with life on life's terms, or maybe even “a bad person”. Buddhism shows us
that we can instead use them as an opportunity - for fuel even - for insight and spiritual growth.
The hindrances work on everyone and no one is immune from their influence.... without training
- without purposely looking at them, without learning how to recognize when they are present,
without knowing how they arise, without noticing when they are absent, or how they fade away,
or how to we might prevent them from arising in the future. Most of us become victims of the
worldly winds of life being thrashed about at feelings, moods, thoughts, personality traits -
usually both our own and also taking on these same mind states of others.
We can instead learn to recognize when they are present - understanding how desire can be
experienced as a leaning into, or as a pulling in, or maybe a tightening in the solar plexus. How
ill-will can express itself as jealousy, or spite, or anger. That feeling of boiling over internally,
uncapped, uncontrolled energy of not liking and wanting to push away. How sloth and torpor can
drain us of motivation, leaving us listless, sluggish, subdued and depressed. How anxiousness
and worry stir up our mind and body with unsettled agitated energy and thinking, and how doubt
can keep us in the dark, not knowing which way to turn.
This evening we turn towards the 3rd of these Hindrances, known as sloth and torpor-- or in the
pali language, thina-middha. They are treated as one Hindrance both are forces of the mind
but are expressed in different ways. Sloth manifests as a physical absence of vitality. It is
opposition to “striving”, to energy. There is a danger of avoiding oneself with the need to be
constantly - used as filler or a way to avoid our feelings. In the grips of Sloth, we may feel
lethargic, weak, or heavy - more of a physical feeling. Torpor, on the other hand, is a lack of
mental energy. The mind may be dull or foggy. It drifts easily into thought. Even though sloth
and torpor express themselves in the body and mind - they are not the same as being mental
and physical tiredness. Sloth and torpor are low energy states related to our attitude towards
ourselves and the world. When this hindrance is strong, our mindfulness is usually not even
present enough for us to notice.
There are a few ways that we can come under the influence of sloth and torpor but one of the
most common ways is when we become exhausted from the constant bouts of excessive desire
and ill will. When these first two hindrances are absent we can feel empty, like something is
missing. And something is. Instead of going back to wanting and not wanting, we can fall into
boredom and depression.

How is Sloth and Torpor Nourished?
There arises listlessness, lassitude, lazy stretching of the body, drowsiness after
meals, mental sluggishness; frequently giving unwise attention to it — this is the
nourishment for the arising of sloth and torpor that have not yet arisen and for the

increase and strengthening of sloth and torpor that have already arisen.

— SN 46:51

SN 46 2 Just as this body, sustained by nutriment, subsists in dependence on nutriment and
does not subsist without nutriment, so too the five hindrances, sustained by nutriment, subsist in
dependence on nutriment and do not subsist without nutriment.
And what is the nutriment for sloth and torpor? There are discontent, lethargy, lazy stretching,
drowsiness after meals, sluggishness of mind: frequently giving careless attention to them is the
nutriment for the arising of unarisen sloth and torpor and for the increase and expansion of
arisen sloth and torpor.

4. Staying in the open air; (being in nature)
5. Noble friendship; (interest)
6. Suitable conversation. (inspirational talk)

How does one stimulate the mind at a time when it needs stimulation? If
due to slowness in the application of wisdom or due to non-attainment of
the happiness of tranquillity, one's mind is dull, then one should rouse it
through reflecting on the eight stirring objects. These eight are: birth,

future rooted in the round of existence; the suffering of the present
rooted in the search for food.

And then there are additional contemplations that can be used to counteract sloth and torpor:

1. The five threatening dangers
If, monks, a monk perceives these five threatening dangers, it is enough
for him to live heedful, zealous, with a heart resolute to achieve the
unachieved, to attain the unattained, to realize the unrealized. Which are
these five dangers?
(1) Here, monks, a monk reflects thus: "I am now young, a youth, young
in age, black-haired, in the prime of youth, in the first phase of life. But a
time will come when this body will be in the grip of old age. But one who
is overpowered by old age cannot easily contemplate on the Teachings of
the Buddha; it is not easy for him to live in the wilderness or a forest or
jungle, or in secluded dwellings. Before this undesirable condition, so
unpleasant and disagreeable, approaches me, prior to that, let me
muster my energy for achieving the unachieved, for attaining the
unattained, for realizing the unrealized, so that, in the possession of that
state, I shall live happily even in old age."
(2) And further, monks, a monk reflects thus: "I am now free from
sickness, free from disease, my digestive power functions smoothly, my
constitution is not too cool and not too hot, it is balanced and fit for
making effort. But a time will come when this body will be in the grip of
sickness. And one who is sick cannot easily contemplate upon the
Teachings of the Buddha; it is not easy for him, to live in the wilderness
or a forest or jungle, or in secluded dwellings. Before this undesirable
condition, so unpleasant and disagreeable, approaches me, prior to that,
let me muster my energy for achieving the unachieved, for attaining the
unattained, for realizing the unrealized, so that, in the possession of that
state, I shall live happily even in sickness."
(3) And further, monks, a monk reflects thus: "Now there is an
abundance of food, good harvests, easily obtainable is a meal of alms, it
is easy to live on collected food and offerings. But a time will come when
there will be a famine, a bad harvest, difficult to obtain will be a meal of
alms, it will be difficult to live on collected food and offerings. And in a
famine people migrate to places where food is ample, and there
habitations will be thronged and crowded. But in habitations thronged
and crowded one cannot easily contemplate upon the Teachings of the
Buddha. Before this undesirable condition, so unpleasant and
disagreeable, approaches me, prior to that, let me muster my energy for
achieving the unachieved, for attaining the unattained, for realizing the
unrealized, so that, in the possession of that state, I shall live happily
even in a famine."
(4) And further, monks, a monk reflects thus: "Now people live in concord
and amity, in friendly fellowship as mingled milk and water and look at
each other with friendly eyes. But there will come a time of danger, of
unrest among the jungle tribes when the country people mount their
carts and drive away and fear-stricken people move to a place of safety,
and there habitations will be thronged and crowded. But in habitations
thronged and crowded one cannot easily contemplate upon the Teachings
of the Buddha. Before this undesirable condition, so unpleasant and

disagreeable, approaches me, prior to that, let me muster my energy for
achieving the unachieved, for attaining the unattained, for realizing the
unrealized, so that, in the possession of that state, I shall live happily
even in time of danger."
(5) And further, monks, a monk reflects thus: "Now the Congregation of
Monks lives in concord and amity, without quarrel, lives happily under
one teaching. But a time will come when there will be a split in the
Congregation. And when the Congregation is split, one cannot easily
contemplate upon the Teachings of the Buddha; it is not easy to live in
the wilderness or a forest or jungle, or in secluded dwellings. Before this
undesirable condition, so unpleasant and disagreeable, approaches me,
prior to that, let me muster my energy for achieving the unachieved, for
attaining the unattained, for realizing the unrealized, so that, in the
possession of that state, I shall live happily even when the Congregation
is split."[3]
— AN 5:78

These things, too, in terms of mediation are helpful in conquering sloth and torpor:
● Applied thought, of the factors of absorptions (jhananga);
● Energy, of the spiritual faculties (indriya);
● Investigation of reality, energy and rapture, of the factors of
enlightenment (bojjhanga).

When the mind is sluggish, it is not the proper time for cultivating the
following factors of enlightenment: tranquillity, concentration and
equanimity, because a sluggish mind can hardly be aroused by them.

When the mind is sluggish, it is the proper time for cultivating the following
factors of enlightenment: investigation of reality, energy and rapture,
because a sluggish mind can easily be aroused by them.

— SN 46:53
