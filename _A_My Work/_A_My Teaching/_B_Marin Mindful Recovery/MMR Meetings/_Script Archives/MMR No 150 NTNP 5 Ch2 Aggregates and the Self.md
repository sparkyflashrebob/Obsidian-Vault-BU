Why are these teachings on the aggregates so important to be aware of in spiritual practice
- Buddha casts the aggregates in a major role in his teaching because, <u>in the view of ordinary people</u>,  the aggregates serve as the primary basis for clinging.
- also know jokingly as the aggre-<u>gates</u> (as in aggravation)
- we do not explore or understand the deeper aspects of our being that make up our experience 
- under the pervue of science and spiritual practice 
- one of the wonderful things about buddhism is its connection to these two areas

How do we cling to the aggregates?
1st Way 
Via Appropriation:
- 5 aggregates comprise all of the things we most intimately take to be "mine"
	- we take the things we experience as ours
	- my thoughts, my feelings and emotions, my body
	- how they are a basis for appropriation
2nd Way 
- this appropriation is also a grounds for identification
	- gain a sense of our personal identity
		- last week discussed the various ways we can look at the aggregates
		- body (past, present, future)
			- I was better shape in the past or I did not treat myself well
			- I am ashamed of the way my body is or "I look marvelous"
			- I will never be the person I was when I was in shape - thinking in the future
			- comparing the body to others bodies
	- so as we can see we can see these aggregates act as objects that we attribute to this innate sense of "I" and reflectively define as our "self"

The Buddha designated the aggregates as "clinging-aggregates" because they serve as objects of clinging
- clinging to experience
- this clinging can occur in many ways
	- by attaching to the body, to feeling, to perception, to thinking, and to what we are conscious of 
- but the most insidious form of clinging is the attachment to the aggregates as being "mine", "I", & "my self"
- it is because we make these experiences as a form of suffering and stress and dissatisfaction
- most folks would not even think about themselves not being their body, their feelings, their perceptions, their thoughts, and their consciousness because we do believe this
- we spontaneously take the body and mind to be "I", "me" & "mine"

So in what way can one investigate these aggregates in terms of being self. 

with these aggregates we can have four views in terms of the sense of self
[[four views of self]] 
1. we can believe that we are identical to one or more of the aggregates or 
2. we believe to possess the aggregates, or 
3. we believe that we are contained within the aggregates, or 
4. we believe that the aggregates are contained within us.

Twenty possible formulations (5 times 4)
- one with each aggregate

This is a common way that Buddhism analyzes concepts or beliefs or views. 
Four perspectives or ways one can used to analyze any subject.

Once One has adopted a view of self,  identifies with it, we find ourselves revolving around the aggregates unceasingly, unable to find release.
Constantly seeking pleasure and avoiding pain, or finding oneself not caring or bored.
My teaching, Thanissaro Bhikkhu, calls this feeding on experience.

This self view is compounded even further because we see the aggregates as a source for pleasure and enjoyment, as a way of escape.
- it is enjoyment or bust
- if it is not enjoyable then something is wrong with it

So each aggregate can be seen from three perspectives
1. by way of the enjoyment it provides (by fulfilling our wants, needs and desires)
2. by the danger inherent in it (as a result of our reliance to them and its changing nature) which is the nature of everything
3. and by the way of one escapes and is released from it

Enjoyment is the
-  pleasure and joy that arises in dependence on the aggregates
- We cling to our bodies as essential instrument for finding happiness and pleasure
	- we crave pleasant feelings (things that are beautiful, that touch us and we like it to do so, physical touch, taste, smells, and thoughts)
	- we seek out agreeable objects of perception (these same objects)
- and we hold on to consciousness as a precondition for all experiences of pleasure

But there dangers that are Hidden - and out of site
These same objects are:
- impermanent 
	- our body is impermanent,
	- our feelings are impermanent
	- our perception is impermanent (we do not see as well, hear as well, we forget things for instance)
- and as a result unable to give enduring satisfaction
- they are subject to change themselves

So what we is being suggested here is that there is no permanent, unchanging self.

This is helpful when we consider them in terms of ending suffering
by INCORPORATION them INTO THE 4 NOBLE TRUTHS
When we 
- identify with them as "I" or to appropriate them as "mine" exposes us to suffering when the aggregates change and fail to meet our expectations
- And so to cling to them is to cling to dukkha (to stress and suffering)
	- how many of us cling to our suffering?
- And that is the escape. Once we recognize the suffering inherent in the five aggregates we can then seek to escape* or release* from them 
- this is won by "the removal and abandonment of desire-and-lust" in regard to each of the aggregates 
- the Buddha's achievement on the night of his enlightenment was to penetrate the true nature of the aggregates
- from then on out his teaching was to point them out, to make them known, analyze and explain them - which is what we are doing now.
- We may get a general understanding thru thinking and logic, but it really doesn't sink in until it is realized with direct experience
- In this practice, it is emphasised that those who follow the path of these teachings and practice become liberated by their own wisdom. No one can do that for you 
- Liberation also means we win release from the aggregates
 
so how do we BREAK thru the FALSE IDENTIFICATION WITH THE AGGREGATES
- the Teachings proclaim that these constituents of our being, which we cling to and identify with, are really non-self, not our true identity.
- It does not explicitly state "there is no self". 
- The path takes a pragmatic approach of examining the things assumed to be self and showing, thru reasoned arguement, that they fail to measure up to the criteria of true selfhood.
- It is not a blanket denial of self but a negation of the claims made about the things we take as self.
- These same 5 aggregates are not the self they are assumed to be.
- we can examine these for ourselves to reach this same conclusion.

There are VARIOUS APPROACHES that can be USED TO DEMONTRATE THE NOT-SELF NATURE OF THE AGGREGATES
The 
[[Anattalakkhanasutta]] Anatta_La _KHan_
- is the best know source
- this was the 2nd formal discourse after the [[_A_My Research/_Buddhism/_Dharma Subjects and Themes/Suttas 1/Dhammacakkapavattana Sutta]]
- It proposes two arguments for undermining the identification of the aggregates as a self
	-  and they strike at our innate conviction that there is an autonomous subject of experience residing at the core of our being
	- we should understand that even beneath the threshold of clear awareness, in our subconscious, we retain this sense that our life revolves around a solid, unchanging "I".
	- That there is a self at the center of thought and emotion, and a source of willful activity
	- that give us this concrete sense of "being somebody"
	- this is the target of the arguments regarding anatta (non-self)

ARGUMENTs
Basically the idea is that
- a true self should be invulnerable to pain and suffering
	- that the self should be autonomous
	- that the self should be able to exert control over itself and over things that come under its influence
	- and that it is intrinsically permanent
		- that everything might change or vanish but the self would persist
		- and as a result it would retain its intrinsic identity

So lets look at these individually:
1. INVULNERABILITY AND AUTONOMY (AFFLICTION)
- We can recognize that each aggregate is a source of affliction
	- body falls ill and decays
	- painful feelings harass us thru the day
	- disagreeable perceptions can assail us
	- our volitional/willful wishes fail to heed our wishes
	- consciousness flickers and falters
	- it throws at us thoughts, emotions & impulses that can cause conflict and distress
	- These refuse to conform to our desires 
	- which they would if they were truely ours
		- let my form be this way!!! (strong and beautiful)
		- let my form not be this way!!! (weak, or sick, or not beautiful)
		- same with other aggregates
- As a result of there not being invulnerable and not under our control they are not self

Secondly:
2.THE SELF BEING PERMANENT AND A SOURCE OF LASTING HAPINESS
- All of these aggregates are impermanent
	- each arises and passes away
		- is form permanent? everything is impermanent - what is our direct experience?  And what are our beliefs about this? 
		- are feelings permanent? (pleasant, unpleasant, neither?) but how do we treat them?
		- is perception permanent? (for me this is especially clear when I try to remember things.  But it is also clear that there is a flow of experience and not just one permanent perception.)
		- is our volition/will permanent (or emotions, thoughts, moods, etc...)
		- is consciousness permanent (what about when the brain is damage, or when we sleep, have a psychological or brain disorder, when we take drugs & alcohol that alter it?)
	- meditation practice allows us to see this clearer and clearer over time, and at subtler and subtler levels.
- So it becomes clear that the aggregates cannot be a source of happiness
- They are then dukkha, defective & unsatisfactory
- And as a result cannot be taken as "me" and "mine" or as "I"

And lastly regarding
3.IMPERMANENCE - DUKKHA - NON-SELF
- known are know as the [[three characteristics]] of experience
	- Can any aspect of experience be removed to being under the influence of these?
	- All things are impermanent
	- because even pleasure is impermanent and changes it to is a form of suffering when clung to
	- because all things are impermanent they cannot be called ours

- So these are sign-posts to be penetrated with insight - thru meditation and contemplation - thru insight
	- And when we understand the truth of this, it removes the delusion of permanence, pleasure & self
	- this insight progresses from impermanence to dukkha
	- from impermanence & dukkha to non-self
		- which  is subtlest of the three

- This leads to a 3 step progression
	- (2.1)from the understanding of impermanence to detachment & liberation (destruction of the defilements)
	- (2.2)from impermanence of conditions 
		-  we can enjoy pleasant experiences but understand that they are some something we posses, that they are changing.  We cannot hold onto them. So the perception of impermanence can eliminate sensual lust* 
	- Some teaching say we can directly contemplate the five aggregates as non-self
	- (2.15) As we have discussed in the past - when we explored emptiness the Aggregates can be viewed as a "[emptyness]"*
		- the body as a lump of foam 
		- feelings as bubbles on the surface of the water
		- perception as a mirage as a lake appearing in the heat
		- volitional aspects as plantain trunk (having no core)
		- magical illusion - as a trick of our senses
	- they appear solid but upon inspection they turn out to be nothing, hollow, and insubstantial

We would come to the same conclusion as other spiritually awakened persons have
- becoming disenchanted with the aggregate, losing one's fascination with them
- "being disenchanted, one becomes dispassionate.  Through dispassion one is liberated"
