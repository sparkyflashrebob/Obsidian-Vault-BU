The 4th Noble Truth - ‘...the way leading to the cessation of suffering.’
1. A path born out of suffering and looking for a way out
2. The 4 Noble Truths
a. 1st Noble Truth - “there is suffering”
i. Not quite right, unsatisfactoriness
ii. 3 Types of Dukka
1. Unavoidable Dukkha
a. Old age, sickness & death
b. Resultant sorrow, pain, grief & despair
c. Not obtaining our wishes - being with those we do not love
& not being with those we love - wishing for things that are
not possible
d. Identity with the Aggregates & the Senses

iii. Dukkha of change - impermanence
iv. Dukkha of conditioned experience - our expectations
v. Solving duhhka by avoiding
vi. Trying to change the way we feel - drugs & alcohol & other means
vii. Not disconnect from our experience
viii. Duty to understand
ix. The insight that we understand it
b. 2nd Noble Truth - The Origin of Suffering
i. Desire - Craving/Tanha & Clinking/Upadana
ii. Craving & Clinging - Thirst & Grasping
iii. Desire & Clinging for Sense Pleasures (looking for something pleasing
and avoiding those unpleasant)
iv. Craving & Clinking to Becoming (Personality, happy if something else,
putting on a different face)
v. Desire of not-becoming (related to 1 & 2)
vi. This/That Conditionality - Dependent Origination - Samsara
vii. Feeding - Volition - Fabrication -
viii. Body (breath), Verbal (directed thought and speaking to ourselves),
Mental (feeling (pleasant & unpleasant) and perception (labels))
ix. Ignorance - Volition - Sensual Consciousness (not organs) - Nama/Rupa
(name as aggregates minus form)(form as the elements) - Sense Bases
(organ of the senses) - Contact (coming together) - Feeling (sensation) -
Craving (Tanha/thirst as above) - Clinging (Upadana/grasping as above) -
Becoming (sense of identify-tied to volition) - Birth (the arising of a feeling
of a distinct self, tied to consciousness) - Aging & death and the entire
mass of suffering (the process of aging and breaking up of the
aggregates)
x. Duty to “Let go” - Understanding & Wisdom
xi. Knowing that they have been let go of - cessation
c. 3rd Noble Truth
i. There is the cessation of suffering

ii. Cessation must be realized
iii. Dut ta dah - suchness -see thing as they are
iv. Anicca - change - impermanence
v. Anatta - selfless nature of experience
vi. Dukkha - how dukkha is experienced
vii. Turn our attention to the pause between sounds, to the space, to
emptyness
viii. Cessation has been realized
d. The Fourth Noble Truth
i. The 3 knowledges
1. There is a path
2. The path must cultivated
3. Knowing that the path has been realized
4. Focus on the remaining time with this

vi. The Noble Eightfold Path
1. Right View or Understanding
a. Mundane Right View
b. Karma
i. “Beings are the owners of their actions, the heirs of
their actions; they spring from their actions, are
bound to their actions, and are supported by their
actions. Whatever deeds they do, good or bad, of
those they shall be heirs.”

c. Noble Right View
2. Right Intention or Resolve

a. Resolve to be on the path
b. Renunciation towards non-sensuality, non-ill-will (and
towards loving-kindness), or from cruelty (and towards
compassion)
c. Precepts
i. Not killing, stealing, improper use of speech,
improper use of sexuality, not using intoxicants
which dull the mind

3. Right Speech
a. No lying, rude speech, nor creation of discord, gossip
b. Idle chatter
4. Right Action or Conduct
a. Non-harming, not taking what is not given, misappropriate
sexual action and not feeding into greed

5. Right Livelihood
a. Arms & Poisons, killing
6. Right Effort
a. 4 right efforts
i. Prevent the arising of unwholesome states
ii. Generating wholesome states
iii. Guarding the sense doors - restraint of the sense
faculties
iv. Seven Factor of Enlightenment

7. Right Mindfulness
a. Mindful of the dhammas, bare attention, not being absent
minded, being cautious about what one is doing, 3 marks
of existence, impermanence, the aggregates, the
hindrances, Seven Factors of Awakening

8. Right Concentration or Samadhi
a. Develop meditation practice
b. 4 Jhanas
c. Perfection of Equanimity & Mindfulness
d. Vispassana - Insight
