4 Noble Truths
● understood as the essence of the Buddha’s awakening
● It comes from experience being devoid of any permanence, and as a result of it being
devoid of a self that can control or command phenomena. We are not gods ourselves.
● Dukkha itself can be be summarized as “a bumpy ride”
● But is also described as being Unsatisfying, unreliable, uneaseful and stressful to convey
the universal aspects of our experience.
● Bhikkhu Analayo makes an important point regarding these various translations.
● “suffering,” unlike “unsatisfactoriness,” is not inherent in the phenomena of the world,
only in the way in which the unawakened mind experiences them. This is indeed the
underlying theme of the four noble truths as a whole: the suffering caused by attachment
and craving can be overcome by awakening.
● Thus “suffering”, unlike “unsatisfactoriness”, is not inherent in the phenomena of the
world, only in the way in which the unawakened mind experiences them.
● All conditioned phenomena are dukkha - unsatisfactory, incapable of giving satisfaction
but, at the same time, conditioned phenomena is able to bring the suffering of our minds
to an end. There is a path.
● In many ways the Buddha was equated to that of a doctor. A Spiritual Doctor.
● The formula for these 4 Noble Truths includes 3 aspects - resulting in 12 Insights
● A statement, then the prescription and then the result.

This Law of Dependent Origination is also known as “this/that conditionality”
When there is this, that is.
With the arising of this, that arises.
When this is not, neither is that.
With the cessation of this, that ceases.
● There are twelve 12 Links
● There are two ways in which the 12 links are thought of and we will briefly explore both
of them
● The first is in terms of what is known as the 3-lives theory and the second which is
known as the Here-and-Now Theory.

3-LIVES THEORY
● In the 3-Lives Theory the first two links are described as having to do with previous
births, these are the links of ignorance and volitional activity.
● Because we don’t understand the truth, we are involved in all kinds of actions.
● Because we do not perceive things clearly, because we do not perceive the fact of
suffering and it cause and the way out, ..... that force of ignorance conditions the next
link in the chain.
● The 2nd link Sankharas - Volition - Fabrication: is the process of intentionally shaping
states of body and mind.
● These processes are of three sorts:
○ a. Bodily fabrication: the in-and-out breath
○ b. Verbal fabrication: directed thought and evaluation
○ c. Mental fabrication: feeling (feeling tones of pleasure, pain, or neither pleasure
nor pain) and perception (the mental labels applied to the objects of the senses
for the purpose of memory and recognition).

● The 3rd link - known as rebirth consciousness in this life or simple consciousness - is the
first moment of consciousness in this life. It is consciousness of the six sense media: the
eye, ear, nose, tongue, body and intellect.
● This is the conception that conditions the arising of mind-body phenomena. - Called
Name & Form.
○ Mental phenomena include:
○ Feeling
○ Perception
○ Intention
○ Contact
○ Attention
○ Physical phenomena include:
○ Earth (solidity)
○ Water (liquidity)
○ Wind (energy and motion)
○ Fire (warmth)
● Because of this name and form - what arises is all of the six spheres of the senses. It is
because of the six sense spheres (known as the all) which in turn conditions the arising
of contact.
● Contact involves the coming together of an object through its appropriate sense door
and the consciousness of either seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, or thinking.
● As a result of this contact there arises a feeling - pleasant, unpleasant or neither
pleasantness nor unpleasantness.
● Feeling is always present and is therefore called a common factor.
● Because of feeling craving arises. This is desiring, going after objects.
○ Craving for the objects of the six sense media. This craving can focus on any of
the six sense media, and can take any of three forms:

○ a. Sensuality-craving (craving for sensual plans and resolves).
○ b. Becoming-craving (craving to assume an identity in a world of experience)
○ c. Non-becoming-craving (craving for the end of an identity in a world of
experience).

● Desire for pleasant sights and sounds, tastes and smells, sensations and thoughts.
● Or we wish to avoid these. - referring to the 3 types of desire
● Desire conditions grasping. Because we desire the six senses we grasp or latch on, we
become attached. - Clinging
○ Clinging — passion and delight — focused on the five aggregates of form,
feeling, perception, fabrication, and consciousness. This clinging can take any of
four forms:
○ a. Sensuality-clinging
○ b. View-clinging
○ c. Habit-and-practice-clinging
○ d. Doctrine-of-self-clinging
● This grasping of consciousness is what conditions continual actions of becoming,
creating energy for rebirth consciousness in the next life.
○ Becoming on any of three levels:
○ a. The level of sensuality
○ b. The level of form
○ c. The level of formlessness
● Birth: the actual assumption of an identity on any of these three levels.
● The aging-and-death of that identity, with its attendant sorrow, lamentation, pain,
distress, and despair.
● This is cycle of conditioning.
● This process will only stop when once we have developed sufficient wisdom to commit
only karmically neutral actions which do not lead to another rebirth.
● Many folks find it doubtful that the Buddha intended Dependent Origination to cover
three lifetimes.
● There are a few reasons for this. The Buddha never mentions ‘re-birth’, 2) the teaching
of ‘non-self’ and ‘ rebirth’ do not fit together. Something needs to be identical to justify
the expression rebirth and it would violate the ‘non-self’ principle, or if there is not
something identical, then it is not re-birth but the birth of something new and different.
● Also consciousness and Mind & Body are mutually interdependent- one cannot exist
without the other. There is no consciousness without a body and no functioning body
without consciousness. In Theravada Buddhism, the understanding is that birth is
immediate.
● There is a lot of debate about how these factors fit together, especially since some of the
sub-factors appear in many places on the list and some of the factors seem to fit in
different contexts.
Leads us into what is known as the Here and Now Interpretation:
● In this version we have the same links and formula as the 3-Lives Version.

● Here though, the understanding is that Dukkha corresponds to the arising of the
“self”-concept in the human mind - both are happening many times each day in a mind
governed by ignorance.
● There are two main causes for Dukkha to arise with the proximate cause being craving
or unwholesome (ignorant) Desire - with the root cause being ignorance.
● However, as shown in the concept of Dependent Origination, a whole pattern of events
is involved, twelve elements which arise in a given sequence and in dependence on
each other.
● There generally is not a single cause but we may speak of a main or leading cause and
supporting factors like the see can be regarded as the leading cause for the growing of a
tree, but other supporting factor contribute to its growth.
● The Buddha said that “One who sees dependent origination sees the Dhamma; one who
sees the Dhamma sees dependent origination”.
● Those who do not see the Dhamma are ignorant of the Dhamma and therefore prone to
suffering.
● Ignorance is the starting point for the arising of Dukkha but not the first cause as
Ignorance is dependent on the taints (known as Asavas in Pali).
● The asavas being a propensity for sensual pleasures, craving for existence, and
ignorance which perpetuate samsara.
● We have a loop here, loop number 1. Ignorance is a condition for the taints, which
includes the taint of ignorance - which is in turn a condition for ignorance.
● There are four taints listed in the Abhidhamma, which an abstract and systemization of
Buddhist doctrine. 1)Concern for the gratification of desires of the five senses; 2)
attachments to views and beliefs; 3) desire for states of being; and 4) ignorance.
● The process, it should be stressed, with Dependent Origination is spiritual Dukkha not
the Dukkha that is as a result of being born in a body.
● It is the spiritual suffering like anger, hatred, envy, jealousy, worry or doubt, which can be
avoided or at least reduced.
● This ignorance is not a lack of intelligence but lack of understanding the 4 Noble Truths.
● It means not knowing about impermanence, the ever changing nature of life, and not
knowing about non-self.
● The second item on the list is Formations, Volitional Intentions or determinations - know
as the sankharas.
● Body Formation (in-breath and out-breath - determine necessary conditions for the
body); Speech Formation (thinking and pondering determine conditions for speech; Mind
Formations (perception and feeling determine conditions for the Mind).
Dependent Co-Arising or Dependent Origination:
● A few features stand out that we should reiterate.
● The descriptions involve All of the things that we directly experience in our body and
mind
● Just as suffering is something we experience directly are primarily hidden from us, deep
in our awareness, the causes also reside there. We can know this.

● There are many steps that come prior to contact with our senses. We prime ourselves
unconsciously by the way we think and breath - to suffer. Even from pleasant
experiences.
● As I mentioned ,the list includes many feedback loops, with such factors as feeling
appearing later at other steps.
● As a result these relationships are complex.
● The entire sequence depends on ignorance of the 4 Noble Truths.
● Not the knowing of the truths but not looking at how we experience life in terms of those
truths - when we look at our experience in terms of “me” and “mine” - from the personal
perspective.
● If the 4 Noble Truths are applied to any of the steps in the sequence then it turns that
step from a cause of suffering into a part of the path to the ending of suffering
● For example if knowledge is applied to the in-breath and out-breath under the step of
fabrication/volition, then the breath becomes part of the path.
● We can train ourselves to not suffer even from unpleasant input from the senses.
● When our knowledge becomes complete it develops into total dispassion for any of the
steps, and it allows that step to disband and cease.
● The effect of cessation ripples thru the various feedback loops allowing for the whole
sequence to cease.
● This is how knowledge of the four noble truths leads to the cessation of suffering.
● Just as suffering is caused from within, it can be ended from within.
In the 3rd Noble Truth this process is reversed. We shall explore this next week when we
discuss the truth of the cessation of suffering.
