# The 5 Qualities of a Teacher
*(Pali: AN 5.159 — Udāyī Sutta)*

> **Aṅguttara Nikāya 5.159:**
> *"Gautama Buddha’s teachings should be taught with the thought:*
> 1. *‘I will speak step-by-step’…*
> 2. *‘I will speak explaining the sequence and reasons’…*
> 3. *‘I will speak out of compassion’…*
> 4. *‘I will speak not for the purpose of material reward’…*
> 5. *‘I will speak without disparaging oneself or others.’"*

In the Sutta Piṭaka, the Buddha addressed Venerable Ānanda regarding the high standard of preparation and purity of intention required when sharing the Dhamma:

> *"It is not easy, Ānanda, to teach the Dhamma to others. When teaching the Dhamma to others, five qualities should be established within oneself."*

## Quick Reference Summary

| # | Classical Pali Quality | Core Principle | Practice in Teaching |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Progressive (*Anupubbī-kathā*)** | Step-by-step guidance | Teaching in logical sequence, meeting students where they are. |
| **2** | **Reasoned (*Pariyāya-dassāvī*)** | Cause & effect / rationale | Explaining the "why" and psychological causality, not dogmatic assertion. |
| **3** | **Compassionate (*Anuddayatā*)** | Sympathy & goodwill | Teaching out of care for the students' liberation and relief from suffering. |
| **4** | **Unselfish (*Na āmisantaro*)** | Free from material gain | Motivated by Dhamma, not by praise, gifts, money, or follower counts. |
| **5** | **Harmless (*Anupahacca*)** | Non-disparaging | Neither exalting oneself nor putting down others. |

## 1. Step-by-Step / Progressive (*Anupubbī-kathā*)
* **The Pali Principle**: *"I will speak on the Dhamma in a gradual, step-by-step manner."*
* **Core Insight**: The teacher does not jump prematurely into rarefied or disorienting concepts (such as non-self, emptiness, or advanced states) before foundational ground is stable.
* **Practice**: Traditionally starting with generosity (*dāna*), ethical integrity (*sīla*), the peaceful fruits of wholesome actions, the drawbacks of grasping, and only then introducing the deeper truths of liberation. A skilled teacher meets students at their current capacity.

## 2. Demonstrating Causes and Reasons (*Pariyāya-dassāvī*)
* **The Pali Principle**: *"I will speak showing the reasons and causes."*
* **Core Insight**: Explaining *why* things work through experiential cause-and-effect rather than authoritarian dogma.
* **Practice**: Rather than demanding blind belief, the teacher illustrates: *"When attention is directed this way, stress arises; when grasping is released, this peace follows."* It empowers students to investigate and verify (*ehipassiko*) for themselves.

## 3. Motivated by Compassion (*Anuddayataṁ paṭicca*)
* **The Pali Principle**: *"I will speak out of sympathy and compassion."*
* **Antidote To**: Intellectual vanity, lecturing, showing off mastery, or needing to be "the expert."
* **Practice**: The underlying intention of speaking is solely goodwill—a sincere wish for the listener to find freedom from suffering, stress, and confusion. Teaching with warmth, deep listening, and attunement to students' actual struggles.

## 4. Not for Material Gain or Personal Advantage (*Na āmisantaro*)
* **The Pali Principle**: *"I will speak not with an eye to material reward (food, gifts, status, or followers)."*
* **Antidote To**: Commercialization, status-seeking, and building an ego-brand.
* **Practice**: Offering the teaching as a pure gift (*dhammadāna*). The teacher does not tailor the message to curry favor, pander for approval, or build popularity. The integrity of the truth is never compromised for financial or social reward.

## 5. Harmless to Self and Others (*Attānañca parañca anupahacca*)
* **The Pali Principle**: *"I will speak neither hurting myself nor hurting others."*
* **Antidote To**: Spiritual arrogance, sectarian judgment, and subtle comparisons.
* **Practice**: 
  * *Not hurting oneself*: Maintaining one's own ethical integrity and avoiding careless, ungrounded speech.
  * *Not hurting others*: Never elevating oneself while belittling other teachers, traditions, or students. Pointing directly to universal principles rather than creating an "us vs. them" dynamic.

## Modern Mindfulness & Teaching Context (e.g. MMTCP)

In contemporary mindfulness and teacher certification frameworks, these five canonical qualities translate directly to:
1. **Pacing & Accessibility**: Scaffolded practices tailored to student developmental stages.
2. **Clarity & Inquiry**: Grounding instructions in direct somatic and mental experience.
3. **Empathic Presence**: Holding space with genuine warmth and trauma-sensitive care.
4. **Purity of Motivation**: Service-oriented teaching free from commercial manipulation.
5. **Humility & Inclusivity**: Speaking truth with courage while remaining free from superiority.
