Dear Spirit Rock Community,  
  
A past student in [**Living Dharma**](https://spiritrock.us5.list-manage.com/track/click?u=12ac17d74bfcd2c273165cf95&id=68dd8f325d&e=ed8b6548f8) shared something unexpectedly hopeful, followed by a real fear. She'd recently noticed an old pattern falling away — the habit of motivating herself through shame, pushing herself back to practice by feeling bad enough about slipping. She felt grateful it was loosening. But then came the worry: without that old pressure, would she still find her way back to the cushion at all? Or would she just let herself off the hook indefinitely?  
  
Her teacher didn't tell her to bring the guilt back. He told her what actually sustains a practice isn't shame or self-punishment — it's something closer to inspiration. Not "_I should_," but "_this matters to me, and here's why_." That kind of motivation doesn't come from cracking a whip. It comes from staying close to what drew you to this path in the first place, and letting that pull you back rather than force.  
  
This distinction matters more over eight months than it would over a single weekend. A short retreat can run on willpower — grit your teeth, get through it, go home. An eight-month container can't. Motivation will rise and fall over that stretch. The question isn't whether you'll have a harder month, you likely will, it's whether there's something in place to meet you there besides self-criticism.
