Artificial Intelligence: examples of ethical dilemmas by UNESCO

[**The Ethical Puzzles of AI: A Glimpse into the Future!**](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics/cases)

  

1. **The Gender-Biased Search Engine**:
	- Ever googled "greatest leaders"? Why's it mostly men? And why does "school girl" show provocative images, while "school boy" doesn't? AI's reflecting our societal biases, and it's time we address it!
2. **Robot Judges in Courtrooms?**:
	- Imagine a robot judge deciding your fate! Faster? Yes. Unbiased? Not always. As AI steps into our courtrooms, the line between machine justice and human ethics blurs.
3. **AI: The Next Picasso or Mozart?**:
	- A computer just recreated a Rembrandt painting! And another finished Schubert's symphony! But who gets the credit? The machine, the programmer, or the original artist? Dive into the art world's newest debate!
4. **Driverless Cars with Moral Dilemmas**:
	- Picture this: An autonomous car must choose between saving a child or an elderly person. Who should it pick? As cars get smarter, their decisions get trickier.

🌐 *UNESCO's diving deep into these AI dilemmas, setting global standards to ensure our tech future is ethical and just. Ready to hop on this futuristic ride?*

  

Here's a summarised overview of " [**Artificial Intelligence: examples of ethical dilemmas**](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics/cases) " from **UNESCO** discusses various ethical challenges posed by the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in different domains.

  

1. **Biased AI**:
	- Searching for "greatest leaders of all time" often yields results dominated by male figures.
	- An image search for "school girl" might show sexualised images of women and girls, whereas "school boy" typically shows regular young boys.
	- Such biases in AI arise from stereotypical representations in society.
	- AI systems can perpetuate and even amplify real-world biases.
	- UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence addresses gender bias in AI.
2. **AI in the Court of Law**:
	- AI is increasingly being used in judicial systems, potentially offering faster and more efficient justice.
	- AI can aid lawyers, judges, and public bodies in decision-making.
	- Ethical challenges include:
		- Lack of transparency in AI decisions.
		- Potential for biased or discriminatory outcomes.
		- Concerns about surveillance and privacy.
		- Risks to human rights and other fundamental values.
	- UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence provides guidance on this topic.
3. **AI Creates Art**:
	- In 2016, a computer designed a Rembrandt painting using deep learning algorithms, and a 3D printer created it.
	- Such AI-generated art raises questions about authorship and copyright.
	- AI's impact on human creativity needs careful consideration, especially regarding the rights and remuneration of artists.
	- UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence addresses these concerns.
4. **Autonomous Car**:
	- Autonomous cars operate without human intervention, relying on vast amounts of data from sensors.
	- They pose ethical dilemmas, such as deciding who to prioritise in a potential accident scenario.
	- UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence offers guidance on the ethical development of such technologies.

The webpage emphasises the importance of ethics in the development and application of AI technologies and highlights UNESCO's efforts in setting global standards on the subject.
