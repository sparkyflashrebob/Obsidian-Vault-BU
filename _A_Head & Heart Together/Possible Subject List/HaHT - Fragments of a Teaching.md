### Walking the Path of Buddha-Dhamma

GOING HOME
- those who want to go home are not those who merely sit and think of traveling
  - must travel step by step in the right direction
  - take wrong path they may run into difficulties, obstacles or get into difficult situations
  - those who reach home can relax and sleep comfortably
  - home is place of comfort for body and mind
  - if a traveler only passed by the front or walked around it they would not receive the benefit of having travelled

THE PATH
In the same way one walking the path to reach buddha-dhamma
- one must do it alone
- no one can do it for us
- must trave along the proper path of morality, concentration and wisdom to find the blessings of purity, radiance and peacefulness of mind
- these are the fruits of travelling the path

By only knowing the books and teachings one only knows the maps or plans for the journey
- One will waste their efforts and never get the real benefits of practice
- Teachers only point out the direction of the path
- They cannot take you there
- After listening to the teachers, whether or not we walk the path, by practicing ourselves, will we reap the fruits of practice.
	- It is normal for there to be an ebb and flow to our practice.
	- We can see this more clearly as time passes
	- But it is strictly up to us
	- There is no Genie with a magic wand to grant us our wishes

Another way we can look at this is to compare practice to a bottle of medicine.
- on the bottle there are written details for how to take the medicine
- but if one only reads the directions one will not get well
- the medicine is worthless if One doesn't follow the advice of their doctor and take the medicine as directed
- But if One does, then there is a stronger possibility that they will recover
- if very ill, it will be necessary to take a lot of medicine
- if only mildly ill, only a little medicine will be required

Teachings of the Buddha are prescribed to cure disease of the mind
- bring us back to a healthy state of mind
- So the Buddha can be seen as a doctor who prescribes cure for the ills of the mind
- Not a God or a Genie

Mental ills are found in each one of us, without exception
- it makes sense to look for the Dhamma for support
- With medicine we are looking to heal the body
- The Buddha-Dhamma is intended to heal the mind. 

Ajahn Chah mentions 3 levels of the practice.
1. Those who understand  that they must practice themselves. Who take the Buddha, Dhamma & Sangha as a refuge. Who resolve to practice diligently according to the teachings. Those who have discarded merely following customs and traditions.  And those use reason to examine for themselves the nature of the world. These are believers.
2. Then there are those who have practiced until they have faith in the teachings of the Triple Gem. Have penetrated the understanding of the true nature of all compounded formations. They gradually reduce clinging and attachment. And Do not hold onto things. Their Minds reach a deep understanding of the dhamma. And, depending on their degree of non-attachment and wisdom, they are progressively known as stream-enterers, once-returners, non-returners, or simply noble ones. These are those who have reached a level where they get to enjoy the fruits of their dedication to the practice.
3. And the Highest level is the group whose practice has led them to the body, speech & mind of a Buddha.  They are free of the world, free from all attachment and clinging. Known as arahants, or free ones, the highest of the noble ones.

But there is a level or two even before this.
- those who only have a rudimentary understanding of the teachings
- but who have the desire to awaken
- who have find it difficult to get a foothold due to conditions beyond their control
- or due to conditions that they have had a strong part in.
- But there is hope for all of us.  
- That is the path that the Buddha and other have shown us.

So this path includes morality or virture, concentration & wisdom
### So How Does One Purify One's Morality
- Morality is restraint and discipline of body and speech.
- Morality can get a bad rap based on the baggage that exists from elsewhere
- At least we can agree that having personal morals and being virtuous is better than being immoral. 
- In Buddhism, it is divided into two classes of precepts.  One for laypeople and another for monks & nuns.
- In general terms there is one basic characteristic to both - and that is of [[intention]].
- When we [[mindful]] or [[self-recollected]] we have [[right intention]]
- Practicing [[Readwise/Books/Mindfulness]] (sati) and [[self-recollection]] (sampajanna) will generate good morality.  This will come from within.
- When we put on dirty clothes and our bodies are dirty, our minds will feel [[uncomfortable]] and [[depressed]]
- But if we keep our bodies [[clean]] and wear clean clothes then it makes our [[minds light and cheerful]]
- When our morality is not kept clean our bodily actions and speech are dirty
- This is a cause for making the mind unhappy, distressed and heavy
- When are separated from [[right practice]] this prevents us from penetrating the essence of the dhamma in our minds. 
- Wholesome body and speech depends a mind, properly trained because the mind sets the intentions for our actions of body and speech
- So we must continually practice to purify our morality by training our minds.
- This wisdom is already within each of us.

So how do we train our minds.
That is where the practice of concentration comes in.
# THE PRACTICE OF CONCENTRATION
In Buddhism, another word for concentration is [[Samadhi]].
It is not like forcing the mind to concentrate.
This is a misconception. 
Samadhi
- makes the mind firm and steady
- It bring peacefulness of mind
- When the mind is untrained it is constantly moving and restless
	- making it hard to control and manage
	- mind follows sense distractions wildly
	- like water flowing this way and that 
	- comparing the mind to water is something Ajahn Chah is particularly known for 
	- pointing out how water is always seeking to get to the  lowest level
- But us humans know how to channel water
	- make more usable
	- water stored can be used as a source of power and light
- likewise with mind
	- a mind that has been controlled bring true happiness
- training it well has highest benefits
- similary to trained animals being useful for work
- a well trained mind help us in all professions
- helps us in all situations
- a disciplined mind helps keep our lives in balance
- it works easier
- it helps to develop and nurture reason and wisdom
- and to govern our actions
- Our happiness will increase as we follow the proper mind training

How do we train the mind:

MINDFULNESS AND BREATHING
- training the mind can be done in many ways
- the method most useful according to the teachings and Ajahn Chah is [[Readwise/Books/Mindfulness of Breathing]]
- This is a simple practice of developing mindfulness of [[in-breath and out-breath]]
- being aware of the breath here at [[tip of the nose]]
- Or using a method of noting, such as [[Bud-dho]]
- Or any another word you choose that is beneficial 
- or being simply aware of air moving in and out is fine.
- You can adjust the practice to suit yourself
- The essential factor is noting or awareness of the breath kept up in the present moment so that one is mindful of each in-breath and out-breath just as it occurs
- And with [[walking meditation]] there is the constant mindfulness of the sensation of the feet touching the ground. 
- The practice must be pursued as continuously as possible to bear fruit
- consistency is key
- To practice often
- To practice diligently
- To have continuous practice of mental training
- It can be most helpful to find a quiet place that is free from distractions
- but regardless, wherever we are we must practice
- And if our attention wanders, we gently bring it back
- Try to put away our thoughts and cares
- And if we are mindful of our thoughts as soon as they arise, and keep returning to our object, such as the breath, then the mind will become quieter and quieter
- Having brought the mind to a place where it is peaceful and concentrated - we release it from the object of concentration.  
- This is where we move from concentration to insight.
- This is where we examine the experience of this body and mind - [[the five khandas]]
- We watch these 5 khandas as they come and go
- You will clearly see they are impermanent
- And this makes them unsatisfactory and undesirable
- You will see that there is 'no-self' running things
- They happen on their own
- We are able to see nature moving according to cause and effect
- Seeing that all things are instable
- And seeing clearly in this way, our attachment and clinging to these khandas will be reduced
- This is the arising of wisdom through your own direct experience

THE ARISING OF WISDOM
- [[PANNA]]
- Wisdom comes from our ability is to see the truth of the various manifestions of body and mind
- These Five Khandas are impermanent - unsatisfactory and not-personal.
- And seeing all compounded things for what they are we do not cling or grasp them
- Whatever we receive, we receive mindfully
- We are not excessively happy
- When things break or disappear we are not unhapy and do not suffer painful feelings.
- We see the impermanent nature of things
- With illness or pain we are equanimous becase our minds are well trained
- Our true refuge is in a trained mind
- It is the wisdom that knows the true characteristics of things as they arise
- This wisdom arises from mindfulness and concentration
- Concentration arises from a base of morality or virtue
- Morality, concentration and wisdom are inter-related in a way that they cannot be separated. 
- Practice can be seen as first discipling the mind to be attentive of our breathing
	- This is the arising of morality
	- When mindfulness of breathing is practiced continuously then the mind becomes quiet
	- this is the arising of concentration
	- This examination shows the breath as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and not-self
	- then there is the non-attachment to it
	- This is the arising of wisdom
	- All three come together

When we practice in this way the benefits/the fruits will happen in three stages:

BENEFITS FROM PRACTICE
1st:
- For those as I mentioned who practice by faith
- there will be an increasing faith in the Buddha, Dhamma, & Sangha
- Finding in them a real inner support
- There will be the understanding of the cause-and-effect nature of all things
- They will understand wholesome actions = wholesome result
	- and unwholesome actions = unwholesome result
- They will find increasing happiness and mental peace
2nd:
- At the 2nd stage they will enter various levels of enlightenment
- starting at stream entry and ending with full awakening
- They will joyful and pulled towards Nibanna
Finally:
- These are the perfected ones
- Those whose happiness transcends all forms of suffering
- They are free from the conditions of the world
