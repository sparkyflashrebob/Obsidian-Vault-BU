"All beings subsist on nutriment"
According to the Buddha, this one single fact about life deserves to be remembered, contemplated, and understood.
If understood widely and deeply enough, this saying reveals a truth that leads to the root of all existence and also its uprooting.

Nutriment
- both biological and mental life
- 4 kinds
	- edible food
	- sense-impressions
	- volitions
	- consciousness
- hunger is that which stands behind the entire process of nutrition
- wielding its whip relentlessly
- The body craves ceaselessly for material food
- mind hungers as eagerly for its own nourishment
	- sense-impression and ideas

Craving (tanha)
- principle condition for any "in-take" or "up-take"
- it is the first factor common for all type of nutriment, be they physical or mental

Assimilation of food
- 2nd common factor
- eating and digesting
- what was external becomes absorbed into the internal
- what was foreign matter becomes "one's own" and is identified with one's personality

Our mind also feeds on "external" material
- sense impressions and experiences
- on the contents of the store-house of knowledge
- Our memories, when they become objects of mind, are as external to the present though-moment as the ideas in a book
- What cannot be absorbed byt the system is discarded
	- Constant process of grasping and rejecting
	- assimilating and dissimilating
	- identifying with oneself and alienating
- When we look closely at this process of nutrition, physical and mental, we shall notice that it is not only the eater who consumes the food, but, in the course of assimilation, also the food devours the eater. 
- There is thus mutual absorption between them. 
- We know how much people can be changed (for better or worse) by ideas they have absorbed and which finally have absorbed and consumed them.
- By thoughtfully observing this process we can see how illusory the concept of an abiding self or substance is.
	- parts to a horse-cart or a house
- This insight alone can lead us to an understanding of the Buddha's teaching on not-self.
- In any essay by Nyanaponika Thera, a prominent German monk, on "The Four Nutriments of Life" he says:  "Individualized life is,  as Paul Dahlke says. ' neither a metaphyusical 'I' - identity nor a mere physical process, but a nutrimental process and as such it is neither something which is in and by itself, nor something caused by another, but something that is maintaining itself: and all these so-called higher faculties of thinking and feeling are different forms of eating, of maintaining oneself"
- This is also an vindication of the other two characteristics of life - impermanence and suffering.

Impermanence and change at the very root of the nutritive process
- Constant replenishment
- bottomless hole to be filled again and again
- No different with our mental hole

Urge to preserve life
- eating and being hungry again

This suffering is mostly hidden by the habituation of this elementary feature of routine life
- search for food and acquisition
- in the animal kingdom devouring each other is the law of nature
	- something we mainly look away from
- at some point humanity will not be able to support itself
- This is at the heart of the 4 Noble Truths
	- The 4 Nutriments of life stand in for the 1st of these truths
		- Craving for the Nutriments is the 2nd Noble Truth
		- Stopping this craving process (its cessation) is the 3rd Noble Truth
		- and the way to cessation is the Noble Eightfold path
- This process of Nutrition demonstrates the conditioned nature of all existence
