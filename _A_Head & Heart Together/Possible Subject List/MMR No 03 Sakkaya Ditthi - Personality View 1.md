MMR#3- [[Sakkaya Ditthi]] - Personality View

Consider this statement, “This is just who I am. I have always been this way and I always seem to default back to the same old ways. I am me, I am who I am and there is nothing I can do about it.” 
How many of us feel this way? 
Most of us I imagine. “There is nothing I can do but suffer.”
But the great gift of Buddhism is that t gives us is a way to contemplate this way of thinking and offers us a path to freedom from from our own suffering. This path is of wakefulness and compassion.
 
From the Buddhist perspective, there are 10 beliefs or traits called Fetters that are chains or shackles that tie us to our cyclical existence. And it is the understanding - that by cutting through these chains that we are freed.

I like to come back to these because in my own life.  It is in this list that I can understand what it is transcendence is needed. 

These chains include:
1 - A belief in a self. 
2 - Skepticism or doubt or uncertainty, especially about the Buddha's enlightenment or about the teachings themselves, cause and effect, etc...
3 - An attachment to rites or rituals (the belief that some practice will free us)
4 - Sensual desire or lust in general
5 - Ill will
6 - Lust for a physical existence (passion for form)
7 - Lust for an immaterial existence (passion for what is formless) An existence after we die
8 - Then there is conceit - mana - pride - arrogance - inflated mind
9 - restlessness
10 - ignorance

Today I would like to talk about the first of these fetters, identity view, as it is so critical to so many aspects of our path to freedom, so critical to our suffering as addicts and alcoholics, depressives or you name it.

This first level of personality view - is caused by a conditioned sense of self -a self perspective - the ego as defined in our modern world - This sense of self - me and mine - is recognized in Buddhism as "sakkaya ditthi". Our transcence is not about getting rid of the ego - once we recognize sakkaya ditti we are no longer limited by the conditioned sense of self. -

What is the conditioned self? It is the voice inside of you that judges yourself and others, saying you aren't good enough, or you aren't capable, or you aren't deserving.
It is man-made by YOU, while you were growing up, in response to outside influences. It is the self that has a history, that has experiences.

Essentially you believe you are a condition. So as long as you believe you are a condition, if you are really committed to the sense of the body you have, what's going to happen to you? - You are going to get old and die - that's what bodies do - you are going to get sick - that is what bodies do.

What you identify with you become like that! - you take on that condition - you live within that limitation - and you experience this life from that limitation

that I am this physical body - young body - old body - male body - female body - whether you are black or white - whatever the identity is

Now consider this body.
The very fact that we can observe the body, just like we have been doing meditation - this awareness is not sakkaya ditti -

Do I sit properly? my body - my sitting posture - this is sakkaya ditthi.

If when we start our meditation or any activity - if we start from this position of sakkaya ditti then we will always be disappointed in our practice.
 
If we have the belief that I am here today to practice hard to become enlightened in the future or if I'm a mess now or I have all kinds of problems and I need to work hard on myself in order to become a healthy normal personality in the future - this is sakkaya ditthi.
 
If I believe that I am not enlightened, I'm this unaware person, neurotic with hangups, fears, and obsessions, which I shouldn't have, and I have to practice to get rid of them so in the future I will not have these obsessions and I will hopefully obtain some peace of mind or liberation in the future - This is Sakkaya Ditthi -
 
So if our practice is to start from this position WITHOUT investigation - we are going to get caught.
Observe this position....that of wanting to get something that could result in the future..... notice how it feels in the body....it is like this!
  
Listen to this word - hate. An inner listening - what does it conjure in you? That is sakkaya ditti.

How about this word - freedom. What does your awareness say about that word - freedom feels like this.
...be the listener - you are the Buddha who is the listener listening to the Dhamma. Awake - aware - The Observer of the nature of things.


Sakkaya ditthi is a "belief in an individual self" or, more simply, "self-view". It refers to a "belief that in one or other of the Buddhist work khandha or khandhas, otherwise known as the Aggregates, that there is a permanent entity. 

The Buddha describes "a fetter of views" (this cycle of beliefs that tie us to an ongoing suffering) in the following manner:

He said - Quote "This is how [a person of the wrong view] attends inappropriately: 'Was I in the past? ... Shall I be in the future? ... Am I? Am I not? What am I? ...'
"As he attends inappropriately in this way, one of six kinds of view arises in him: ...
'I have a self...'
'I have no self...'
'It is precisely by means of self that I perceive self...'
'It is precisely by means of self that I perceive not-self...'
'It is precisely by means of not-self that I perceive self...'
'This very self of mine ... is the self of mine that is constant...'
"This is called a thicket of views, a wilderness of views, a contortion of views, a writhing of views, a fetter of views. Bound by a fetter of views, the uninstructed ... is not freed, I tell you, from suffering & stress."

So what are these aggregates?
According to Buddhism, we cling to 5 physical/material and mental factors. We cling to and grasp onto them. We use them to create our sense of being, our personality. These are activities and not necessarily a description of what a person is. But they are referred to as aggregates, like the rock that is made of a bunch of other types of rocks.
It is the understanding that we tend to grasp at these aspects that we describe as part of our being.
As I list them here consider their implications. How do we relate to these things.
The 5 aggregates are:
1 - Form - material, the body, in Buddhism - these are the 4 elements of earth (solidity), water (cohesion), fire (heat or cold), and wind (motion).
2 - Feeling - sensations - pleasant, unpleasant or neutral
3 - Perception - recognition
4- Consciousness - visible form, sound, smell, taste, touch & mental consciousness
5 - Mental Formations or Fabrications (consciousness of conditioned mind states or moods, karmic imprints).****

***SIDENOTE ON FABRICATION: INTENTION, ATTENTION, EVALUATION, AND ALL THE ACTIVE MIND PROCESSES. SEE T.B. FIVE PILES OF BRICKS-THE KHANDAS AS BURDEN & PATH

All of these contain an element of intentional activity or volition and are conditioned - meaning they exist due to causes and conditions. As such, we do not necessarily own them. If we identify with them and take them on as me and mine - we create suffering for ourselves and others. This opens the possibility that suffering can be ended by changing our intentions. Is it one who understands this that gains freedom.

The teaching on Sakkaya Ditti is to explore what and we experience our lives. It is a reminder for us to not hold onto what is not really ours and that we can not hold on to, and instead to seek that which is always here and unchanging. To know the one we truly are.
It is by shedding the baggage of past memories and by not looking forward to an uncertain future that we fully become present in the here and now.
Mindfulness practice reminds us of these teachings, these understandings, it points us back to awakening, to compassion, to nurturing, to understanding --
to standing in our own awareness.
 
So what is freedom?
For now, it would appear that it is being in the middle of the pack.
It is being one of many, not special, not unique. Not sensing a problem, not being a problem, not demanding, not taking.
In the flow but not a part of it. Being free to act without being stuck to anything.
It is Unbinding
Unconditioned

Finally, planting a seed for future discussion, there is one of the most well know sutra's on emptiness, The Heart Sutra which contains many references to this idea of conditionality and Sakkaya Ditti. I'd like to read it here.

(Pause) The Bodhisattva of Compassion, meditating deeply on the Perfection of Wisdom, saw clearly that the five aspects of human existence are empty*, and so released himself from suffering. Answering a monk, he said this:

    Body is nothing more than emptiness, emptiness is nothing more than body. The body is exactly empty, and emptiness is exactly body.

    The other four aspects of human existence -- feeling, thought, will, and consciousness -- are likewise nothing more than emptiness, and emptiness nothing more than they.

    All things are empty:
    Nothing is born, nothing dies,
    nothing is pure, nothing is stained,
    nothing increases and nothing decreases.

    So, in emptiness, there is no body,
    no feeling, no thought,
    no will, no consciousness.
    There are no eyes, no ears,
    no nose, no tongue,
    no body, no mind.
    There is no seeing, no hearing,
    no smelling, no tasting,
    no touching, no imagining.
    There is nothing seen, nor heard,
    nor smelled, nor tasted,
    nor touched, nor imagined.

    There is no ignorance,
    and no end to ignorance.
    There is no old age and death,
    and no end to old age and death.
    There is no suffering, no cause of suffering,
    no end to suffering, no path to follow.
    There is no attainment of wisdom,
    and no wisdom to attain.

    The Bodhisattvas rely on the Perfection of Wisdom,
    and so with no delusions,
    they feel no fear,
    and have Nirvana here and now.
