# Somatic Centering Script: Putting Down the Hats

*A 5-Minute Guided Meditation to Open Your "Pain and Difficulty" Session*

---

### **Facilitator Guidance Notes:**
*   *Speak slowly, in a calm, grounded, and spacious tone.*
*   *Leave generous pauses (indicated by `[Pause - 10 seconds]`) to allow participants to actually feel and integrate your words somatically.*
*   *This centering practice helps participants put down their external "social hats" and meet whatever physical or emotional state is currently their "somatic priority" with spacious allowing.*

---

### **Guided Script:**

"Find a posture that feels upright, comfortable, and dignified... Whether you are sitting on a chair, a cushion, or a box. Let your eyes gently close, or simply soften your gaze toward the floor.

`[Pause - 10 seconds]`

As we begin, let’s consciously acknowledge the transition we are making. Throughout the day, we have all been wearing different **social hats**. Maybe you’ve been wearing the hat of the busy shopper, the focused driver in traffic, the professional, the partner, the parent, or the friend. 

Right now, in this space, you don't have to perform any of those roles. There is nothing you need to accomplish, and no one you need to please. You are invited to completely take off those social hats... and let them rest on the floor. 

Let yourself return to your the simple, unadorned state of being alone and present within your own mind and body. 

`[Pause - 15 seconds]`

As you step into this place of being present with yourself, take a slow, deep breath in... and let a long, soft exhale carry away any immediate tension in your shoulders, your jaw, and your hands. 

Now, bring your attention to your immediate physical reality. Let your awareness sweep through your body, and notice: **What is currently sitting at the very top of your attention?** 

Is it a tight sensation in your chest? Head pressure? A dull ache in your leg? Or perhaps a wave of mental restlessness? 

Whatever is most dominant right now in your experience is what naturally what draws your attention. For this next few minutes, our practice is not to try to adjust it, fix it, or change it. We are not trying to push it away to manufacture a false state of peace. Instead, we are practicing radical self-honesty by simply acknowledging: *'This is what is here. This is my body's priority in this moment.'*

`[Pause - a few mintues]`

If you notice your mind instantly beginning to spin stories about this sensation—wondering *why* you feel this way, complaining about it, or wishing it would stop—gently drop those stories mid-sentence. That is the 'second arrow' of resistance. 

Instead, use your breath to ride along with this experience. Imagine inhaling a sense of spaciousness, and as you exhale, invite a soft, conscious softening around the perimeter of the physical tension. 

You are not trying to force the tension to leave. You are simply choosing to stop fighting it. 

`[Pause - 20 seconds]`

If the sensation feels intense, think of it as a wave of the ocean. A wave does not stand solid; it rises, peaks, and naturally dissolves back into the deep water. Can you practice 'sitting in your house' and simply witnessing this wave of sensation, resting in the changingness of things? 

Step back and rest as the **Observer**. Letting go of that part of you that is wants to fix and control. 

Recognize that while your physical body experiences the sensation, there is a part of you—a silent, spacious **Knower**—that simply witnesses it all without being harmed or conditioned by it. 

`[Pause - 15 seconds]`

Rest here, in this wide, spacious awareness, allowing everything to be exactly as it is...

`[Pause - 30 seconds]`

When you are ready, gently take one more deep, grounding breath, feeling the pressure of your feet on the floor. And as you exhale, slowly open your eyes, bringing this upright presence and spaciousness into our circle."
