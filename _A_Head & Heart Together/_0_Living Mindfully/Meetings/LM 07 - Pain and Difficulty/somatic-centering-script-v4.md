# Somatic Centering Script: Putting Down the Hats and Resting on the Shore (v4)

*A 5-Minute Guided Meditation to Open Your "Pain and Difficulty" Session*

---

### **Facilitator Guidance Notes:**
*   *Speak slowly, in a calm, grounded, and spacious tone.*
*   *Leave generous pauses (indicated by `[Pause - 10 seconds]`) to allow participants to actually feel and integrate your words somatically.*
*   *This centering practice helps participants transition from their daily relational situations, acknowledge their current "somatic priority" with radical self-honesty, and step out of their "isolated box" onto the patient shoreline of the Knower.*

---

### **Guided Script:**

"Welcome, everyone. As we prepare to open our circle today, let’s take these first few minutes to arrive fully, transitioning from the busy-ness of our day into the somatic truth of this present moment. 

Find a posture that feels upright, comfortable, and dignified... Whether you are sitting on a chair, a cushion, or a box. Let your eyes gently close, or simply soften your gaze toward the floor.

`[Pause - 10 seconds]`

Before we do anything else, let’s consciously acknowledge the transition we are making. Throughout the day, we naturally move through different **social hats**. These hats are our healthy, natural relational situations—such as being a driver in traffic, a shopper at the store, a coworker, a partner, a parent, or a friend. Our personalities naturally shift and adapt to meet the exact experience and moment we are in. This fluid adaptation is a healthy and natural part of interacting with our world.

Right now, in this space, you don't have to perform any of those roles. There is nothing you need to accomplish, and no one you need to please. You are invited to completely put down those relational hats... let them rest on the floor. 

Let yourself return to your **default mode**—the simple, unadorned state of being alone with your own mind and body. 

`[Pause - 15 seconds]`

As you step into this default mode, take a slow, deep breath in... and let a long, soft exhale carry away any immediate tension in your shoulders, your jaw, and your hands. 

Now, bring your attention to your immediate physical reality. Let your awareness sweep through your body, and notice: **What is currently sitting at the very top of your attention?** 

Is it a tight sensation in your chest? Head pressure? A dull ache in your leg? Or perhaps a wave of emotional restlessness? 

Whatever is most dominant right now has hijacked your attention and become your **somatic priority**. Because whatever is our priority is the thing that most drives us. For this next minute, our practice is not to try to adjust this priority, fix it, or run away from it. Instead, we are practicing radical self-honesty by simply acknowledging: *'This is what is here. This is my body's priority in this moment.'*

`[Pause - 20 seconds]`

If this somatic priority makes you feel trapped, notice if you are **sitting in a box**—boxed in by your narrow perspectives, your worries, and your rigid views. It can feel like being shut inside a dark house, looking out of a single window. We often treat our minds as if we are completely alone and separated from the rest of existence inside this box, believing that the tiny, filtered sliver of reality we see through our window is the entire world.

But this separation is an illusion. You are not alone in your box, and you are not separated from the world. How we see the world inside our own heads is actually a very small part of the picture. 

`[Pause - 15 seconds]`

Let's gently open the door of that box and step out of the house. Step onto the warm, dry sand of Point Reyes North Beach. Imagine sitting on the shore, watching the ocean waves. 

The waves come in and go out, coming and going continuously on the shore. You aren't in the water fighting them. You are on the dry sand, watching them with deep, spacious **patience**. 

Can you bring this exact shoreline posture to your own internal waves of sensation, thoughts, or emotions? Just watch them come and go. 

`[Pause - 15 seconds]`

If a physical sensation or emotional wave feels intense, practice the art of **"No Doing."** Release the "doer"—the coordinator who demands to control and adjust your bodily conditions. Set the simple intention to wait out the first wave of intensity. 

If you can, offer a soft, compassionate whisper of welcome to the sensation: *'You belong. This too is part of this moment.'* 

Notice where your body is tensing to resist the pain—perhaps in your jaw, your shoulders, or your belly—and invite a gentle, conscious softening around the very edge of that sensation. Breathe space around it, and feel the space within it.

`[Pause - 20 seconds]`

Remember, as long as you do not step into the water to fight or **compound** these experiences with mental stories of isolation, and as long as you practice **patience**, these waves will naturally rise, peak, and recede back into the vast ocean of your awareness. 

Step back from the sensory storm of the experiencing body and rest as the **Observer**. 

Recognize that while your body experiences the passing waves, there is a part of you—a silent, spacious **Knower**—that simply witnesses it all, completely safe and inherently free.

`[Pause - 15 seconds]`

Rest here on the shore, in this wide, spacious awareness, allowing everything to be exactly as it is...

`[Pause - 30 seconds]`

When you are ready, gently take one more deep, grounding breath, feeling the pressure of your feet on the floor, connecting back to the earth beneath you. And as you exhale, slowly open your eyes, bringing this upright presence and spaciousness back into our circle."
