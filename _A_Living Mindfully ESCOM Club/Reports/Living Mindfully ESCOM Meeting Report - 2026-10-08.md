## 📌 Executive Summary & Core Dhamma Pillars

The Living Mindfully ESCOM Club gathered at Elizabeth Deedy Hall Rm 111 for its scheduled weekly session. Rather than succumbing to egoic disappointment or self-judgment (*sakkāya-diṭṭhi*), the session was dedicated to exploring the practice of **non-striving**, investigating sensory contact and sound, testing mindfulness as a physiological biofeedback mechanism, and honoring the spiritual discipline of showing up regardless of external validation.

## 📖 Contemplative Reading: Jon Kabat-Zinn on *Non-Striving*

Andy read and unpacked the chapter on *Non-Striving* from Jon Kabat-Zinn's classic work, *Wherever You Go, There You Are*:

> *"In the meditation practice, the best way to get somewhere is to let go of trying to get anywhere at all... Almost everything we do, we do for a purpose, to get something or get somewhere. But in meditation, this attitude can be a real handicap. Meditation is different from all other human activities. Although it takes a great deal of work and energy of a peculiar kind, ultimately meditation is a non-doing. It has no other goal than for you to be with yourself as you are. This requires letting go of all striving and doing and dwelling in the state of being."*

### Dhamma Reflection & Older Adult Conditioning
- **The Burden of Productivity**: Andy reflected on how societal conditioning pressures older adults and retirees to constantly justify their existence through measurable productivity, hobbies, and social activity.
- **Befriending the Present**: When mindfulness is turned into another self-improvement project or achievement metric, it generates subtle suffering. True practice is the radical willingness to pause, step out of striving, and befriend the feeling present right now—even if that feeling is fatigue, ache, or solitude.

## 🎙️ Spoken Dharma Discourse & Reflective Journal

### 1. Welcoming the Empty Room: Speech Wisdom & Egolessness
- **Transforming Expectation**: Setting up the laptop, webcam, and meditation room for a group that did not arrive provided a live laboratory for working with expectation and disappointment.
- **College Speech Wisdom**: Andy recalled an essential lesson from his college public speaking instructor: *"They don't know if you made a mistake unless you let them know."* When expectations collapse, we do not need to invent an inner crisis. We can simply adapt, smile, and carry on with clear seeing and compassion.
- **Pat’s Schurz, Nevada Story**: Andy recounted Pat's story of a Native American elder holding 12-step recovery meetings in an isolated portable building out in Schurz, Nevada. Week after week, month after month, the elder drove out, set up the chairs, made coffee, and sat in silence alone for nearly a year. Eventually, a single rough biker walked through the door. Showing up is not about headcounts; it is about tending our own side of the street and keeping the lamp lit.

### 2. 17-Minute Silent Meditation Sit: Sensory Contact & Sound
- **Postural Dignity**: Sitting in the quiet classroom, anchoring attention in the breath and bodily contact with the chair.
- **Sound as Bare Phenomenon**: Ambient sounds—footsteps in the hallway, air conditioning vents, distant traffic along Sir Francis Drake Boulevard—arose naturally.
- **Relinquishing Contention**: Drawing on early Buddhist sutta wisdom, Andy noted that sound is described as a thorn to deep absorption only when the mind fights it. By holding sounds as simple physical vibrations entering the ear-door, the sting is removed, leaving open space.

### 3. Somatic Biofeedback: Attention & Eye Tension
- **Direct Somatic Experimentation**: Following the sit, Andy observed a subtle sensation of tightness and twitching above his left eye.
- **Awareness as an Amplifier**: Deliberately directing intense, diagnostic attention toward the tension caused the muscles to knot up further; when awareness softened into spacious, gentle holding, the tension softened.
- **Biofeedback Insight**: Mindfulness is not a detached intellectual observation; it is an intimate somatic feedback loop where how we pay attention immediately influences physiological holding.

### 4. Stoic Inversion & Relational Boundaries
- **The Film and the Projector**: Reflecting on modern interpersonal friction, Andy connected Buddhist mindfulness with the Stoic inversion: when we find ourselves irritated by external words or events, we can pause and look at our own mental projector rather than cursing the screen.
- **Freedom in Equanimity**: Recognizing that we cannot control whether others attend, understand, or agree, but we have complete authority over whether we meet life with an open heart.
