To avoid both crossing into the 22% marginal tax bracket and triggering Medicare IRMAA surcharges in **2026**, you need to navigate two different definitions of income: **Taxable Income** and **Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI)**.

As a married couple filing jointly, here are your target thresholds:

### 1. The 12% Tax Bracket Ceiling

To stay within the 12% bracket, your **Taxable Income** (Adjusted Gross Income minus the Standard Deduction) must not exceed **$100,800**.

* **Standard Deduction (2026):** $32,200 (for Married Filing Jointly).
* **Total Gross Income Limit:** You can have a gross income of approximately **$133,000** and still remain in the 12% bracket after taking the standard deduction.

### 2. The IRMAA Surcharge Floor

Medicare IRMAA surcharges are based on your **MAGI** from two years prior. For your **2026** premiums, the SSA looks at your **2024** tax return. However, if you are planning for *future* IRMAA avoidance (i.e., making sure your 2026 income doesn't trigger surcharges in 2028), the thresholds are inflation-adjusted.

For **2026**, the threshold to avoid any surcharge is:

* **Married Filing Jointly:** **$218,000** or less.

### Comparison of Income Limits (2026)

Since the IRMAA threshold ($218,000) is significantly higher than the 12% tax bracket ceiling (~$133,000 gross), **the tax bracket is your stricter constraint.**

### Critical Strategy Notes

* **The "Cliff" Effect:** IRMAA is a "cliff." If you go $1 over the $218,000 limit, you and your wife will both be charged the full Tier 1 surcharge (roughly $2,300/year combined). Tax brackets, conversely, are progressive; only the dollars above $100,800 are taxed at 22%.
* **Life-Changing Event (Form SSA-44):** Since you mentioned retiring in April 2026, you may experience a "Work Stoppage." If your 2024 or 2025 income was high enough to trigger IRMAA, you can file Form SSA-44 to request a reduction in your 2026 premiums based on your new, lower retirement income.
* **MAGI Add-backs:** Remember that for IRMAA, MAGI includes **tax-exempt interest** (like municipal bonds), which is normally not included in your taxable income calculation.

If you stay under **$100,800 in taxable income**, you effectively clear both hurdles with a wide margin of safety for IRMAA.
