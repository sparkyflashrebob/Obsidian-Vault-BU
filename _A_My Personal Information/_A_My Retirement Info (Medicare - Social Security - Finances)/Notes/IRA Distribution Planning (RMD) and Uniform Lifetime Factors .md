Based on your birth year of **1960**, your RMD requirements fall under the newer rules established by the **SECURE 2.0 Act**.

### 1. Your RMD Starting Age

Because you were born in 1960, your "Required Beginning Age" is **75**.

* **First RMD Year:** 2035 (the year you turn 75).
* **Deadline:** You can delay your very first RMD until **April 1, 2036**. However, if you wait until April, you will have to take two RMDs in 2036 (one for 2035 and one for 2036), which could spike your tax bracket.

### 2. The Calculation Formula

Each year, your RMD is calculated using this simple formula:

$$\\text{RMD Amount} = \\frac{\\text{Account Balance on Dec 31 of Prior Year}}{\\text{IRS Life Expectancy Factor}}$$

* **The Balance:** Use the total value of your traditional IRAs, 401(k)s, and other tax-deferred accounts as of December 31 of the previous year.
* **The Factor:** You will look this up in the **IRS Uniform Lifetime Table** based on your age in the current year. For example, at age 75, the current factor is **24.6**.

### 4. Strategic "Still Working" Exception

Since you are retiring from AVDG on **April 24, 2026**, this exception won't apply when you hit age 75, but it's good to know: If you were still employed at age 75 and did not own more than 5% of the company, you could delay RMDs from *that specific* employer's 401(k) until you retired. However, your **Traditional IRAs** must always start RMDs at age 75 regardless of your work status.

### 5. Aggregation Rules

* **IRAs:** You must calculate the RMD for each IRA you own, but you can total those amounts and take the entire sum from **just one** of your IRAs.
* **401(k)s:** You must calculate and withdraw the RMD **separately** from each individual 401(k) account you still hold.

**Expert Tip:** Since you have about 9 years until your RMDs kick in, you are in the "Golden Window." This is the perfect time to consider **Roth Conversions** while your income is lower in early retirement. This reduces the balance of your traditional accounts, effectively "shrinking" your future RMDs and keeping you below those 12% tax and IRMAA limits we discussed earlier.
