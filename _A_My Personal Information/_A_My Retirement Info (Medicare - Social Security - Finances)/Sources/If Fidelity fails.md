In the unlikely event that a massive institution like Fidelity were to "fail," your 401(k) and IRA assets are protected by multiple layers of federal law and industry-standard insurance. Because your retirement assets are held in a custodial capacity (they belong to you, not Fidelity), they are not available to Fidelity's creditors in a bankruptcy.
Here is the breakdown of how your specific accounts are protected:

1. Asset Segregation (The Primary Defense)
   Under SEC and Department of Labor regulations, Fidelity is required to keep customer assets strictly separate from its own corporate funds.

* The "Wall": Your stocks, bonds, and mutual funds are held in your name (or for your benefit). If Fidelity goes bankrupt, those assets still belong to you.
* Creditor Protection: Fidelity’s creditors cannot seize your 401(k) or IRA to pay off the company's debts. In a typical failure, another healthy brokerage would simply be appointed to take over the management of your accounts.

2. SIPC Protection (The Safety Net)
   Fidelity is a member of the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC). If Fidelity failed and assets were found to be missing (due to fraud or record-keeping errors), SIPC steps in.

* Coverage Limits: Up to $500,000 per customer, which includes a $250,000 limit for uninvested cash.
* Function: SIPC doesn't protect you against market losses (if your stocks go down); it protects you against the brokerage losing your shares or cash.

3. "Excess of SIPC" Coverage
   Fidelity carries additional private insurance for accounts that exceed the $500,000 SIPC limit.

* The Limit: This "excess" coverage provides an aggregate loss limit of $1 billion, with a per-customer limit of $1.9 million specifically for cash.
* Scope: This is intended to ensure that even high-net-worth investors are made whole if the primary SIPC limits are exhausted.

4. Specific Account Protections
   | Account Type | Primary Protection | Key Regulatory Body |
   |---|---|---|
   | 401(k) | ERISA (Employee Retirement Income Security Act) | Department of Labor (EBSA) |
   | IRA | SIPC & SEC Customer Protection Rule | SEC / FINRA |
   | Cash / CDs | FDIC (if held in the "Bank Sweep" position) | FDIC |
   Important Distinction on 401(k)s:
   Your 401(k) is even more insulated because it is governed by ERISA. The assets are held in a Trust. Even if the company you work for and the recordkeeper (Fidelity) both went bankrupt simultaneously, the trust remains a separate legal entity dedicated solely to the plan participants.
