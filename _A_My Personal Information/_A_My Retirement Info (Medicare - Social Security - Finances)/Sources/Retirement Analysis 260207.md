In assessing asset mix, the Planning & Guidance Center (the “Tool”) sorts the holdings in your selected accounts among several major asset classes, including domestic stocks and foreign stocks (equities), bonds (fixed income), short-term securities (cash, cash equivalents, CDs, money market funds, etc.), other, and unknown. Underlying investments held in a mutual fund or other pooled investment vehicle are considered individually. A model asset mix is then identified based on the percentage of stock holdings in your selected accounts:
Short-Term: Stock holdings 0% Conservative: Stock holdings greater than 0% and less than 25% Moderate with Income: Stock holdings greater than or equal to 25% and less than 35% Moderate: Stock holdings greater than or equal to 35% and les than 45% Balanced: Stock holdings greater than or equal to 45% and less than 55% Growth with Income: Stock holdings greater than or equal to 55% and less than 65% Growth: Stock holdings greater than or equal to 65% and less than 80% Aggressive Growth: Stock holdings greater than or equal to 80% and less than or equal to 95% Most Aggressive: Stock holdings greater than 95%
If a large percentage is identified as "Unknown" or "Other," your asset mix assessment may not accurately reflect the current risks inherent in your selected accounts. The "Other" category includes non-asset class holdings (e.g., derivative securities, options, and precious metals) in some of your investments. The "Unknown" category represents holdings you may have, details of which are unknown or unavailable to Fidelity.
Value for Asset Mix is the sum of the account balances assigned to the goal. It is provided as a convenience for informational purposes only.
For manual accounts, you may either enter specific allocations or select from sample asset mixes. A representative model asset mix for the accounts assigned to your goal is then identified based on the percentage of stock holdings in your assigned accounts for comparison to a selected Target Asset Mix (TAM).

The Income Strategy Evaluator was designed for users planning for retirement income now or in the near future. If you do not require current income, the Tool may not be appropriate for you.
The Income Strategy Evaluator leverages financial information and preferences you provided when creating your retirement goal within the Planning & Guidance Center (the “Tool”)to generate a proposed income strategy.
Please visit the Tool to update any information that you think is incorrect or stale. The hypothetical income strategy utilizes either a hypothetical fixed income annuity or hypothetical deferred income annuity (collectively, “fixed income annuities”) to attempt to cover your estimated essential retirement expenses exceeding your estimated income not associated with withdrawals from your portfolio. Depending on the information you provide, the allocation to annuities in your hypothetical income strategies may not cover the identified essential income need. The hypothetical income strategy is a starting point for creating your income strategy. The Income Strategy Evaluator may not consider all information you previously provided to Fidelity or its representatives.
The Income Strategy Evaluator may also provide you with the ability to choose to view a hypothetical alternative income strategy, which utilizes a hypothetical fixed deferred annuity with a guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit (“GLWB”) to attempt to cover your estimated essential retirement expenses exceeding your estimated income not associated with withdrawals from your portfolio. Please note additional types of annuities providing guaranteed lifetime income exist that may be appropriate for you.
All annuity payments illustrated in the Tool are based on the best quotes available from among fixed income annuities and fixed deferred annuities with a GLWB distributed by Fidelity Insurance Agency, Inc. Rates may vary daily. These annuities are issued by third-party insurance companies, which are not affiliated with any Fidelity Investments company. Annuity guarantees are subject to the claims paying ability of the issuing insurance company. Before investing in any annuity, ensure it is appropriate for your personal financial situation.
Depending on where you live and the assets you use to purchase the annuity, you may not be taxed on the initial purchase--you'll pay income tax on the money you receive each month.
For taxable accounts, it is assumed that the tax basis for the account is the same as the currently identified account value. The actual adjusted tax basis of the securities in your taxable account often will be different from the fair market value of those securities on any given day. For workplace savings plan accounts and non-Roth individual retirement accounts (IRAs), it is assumed that the entire value of these accounts is subject to ordinary income taxation upon distribution.
Hypothetical annuities are assumed to be purchased for either a single life or joint lives (if planning with a partner). Hypothetical fixed income annuities are assumed to also be purchased with the following features: fixed payments with no cost of living adjustment (COLA); 100% survivor benefit (if planning with a partner); and beneficiary protection in the form of a cash refund.
In the event that your plan would end early enough any beneficiary payout will not be included in the analysis.

The Retirement savings strategy page may provide you the ability to analyze a hypothetical savings strategy. It leverages financial information you provided when creating your retirement goal within the Planning & Guidance Center (the “Tool”) along with cohort data to generate a modeled contribution strategy. Please visit the Tool to update any information that you think is incorrect or stale.
The savings strategy page is designed to provide educational insights on how you may be able to save money more effectively. It is intended as a general guide for illustrating how contributing money and utilizing tax-advantaged accounts can help improve your financial goals and does not imply that you deposit funds into a traditional “savings” account at a bank. Investments are not bank deposits and are generally not insured or guaranteed by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (“FDIC”) or any other governmental agency.
Keep in mind, additional monthly contribution amounts selected to model are assumed to be after-tax amounts. In order to maximize your hypothetical assets at the start of retirement, the modeled contribution strategy may shift some of your after-tax contributions to pre-tax contributions, meaning you'd be lowering your taxable income and paying less taxes now - deferring, or delaying, paying those taxes until you withdraw your savings in retirement. That means you'd have extra pre-tax money to save, since you'd not have to pay taxes on those contributions yet. We add this extra amount to your modeled contributions strategy calculation to help maximize the advantage of that hypothetical strategy.
Your modeled contribution strategy includes the additional savings amount selected from the contribution slide bar, if any, and shows how you might spread your contributions across the eligible account types assigned to your retirement goal, taking advantage of potential tax benefits to help maximize the assets you'd hypothetically have at the start of retirement. Contribution amounts are grown at the Tool's salary growth rate assumption across the entire contribution period and modeled contributions shown are assumed to be for the next full calendar year.
Your modeled contribution strategy does not consider any student loan repayments as it relates to retirement contributions. For some employer plans, student loan repayments are treated as contributions for the purpose of calculating employer 401(k) contributions. You should refer to your plan documents to evaluate if student loan repayments could be eligible for an employer matching contribution to your 401(k).
Your modeled contribution strategy assumes that contributions to Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) are saved for healthcare expenses in retirement, rather than spent on current healthcare expenses.
The contribution behavior that could potentially help you maximize your retirement assets may change year to year with your financial situation. As a result, you may want to visit the tool to update your financial information and run the savings strategy analysis periodically.
