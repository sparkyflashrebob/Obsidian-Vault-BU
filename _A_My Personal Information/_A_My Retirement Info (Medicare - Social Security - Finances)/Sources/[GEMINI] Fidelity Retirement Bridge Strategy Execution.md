Should I rollover to an IRA first?

---

Rolling over your 401(k) to a Rollover IRA at Fidelity before starting your "Bridge Strategy" is highly recommended for your specific situation. Since you are 65, the biggest hurdle (the 10% early withdrawal penalty) is already behind you, but an IRA offers several tactical advantages for the "Bucket" approach.
Here is why a rollover makes sense for your execution in 2026:
Superior Investment Selection (Buckets 2 & 3)
401(k) Limitation: Most 401(k) plans have a "menu" of 15–25 mutual funds. You likely won't find specific tax-efficient tools like the Fidelity Treasury-Only Money Market (FDLXX) or California-specific Municipal Bond funds inside a standard 401(k).
IRA Freedom: In an IRA, you have the "open architecture" of the entire market. This allows you to buy the specific Treasury funds and CD ladders required for Buckets 1 and 2 to minimize your California tax bill.
Flexible Distribution Control
Net vs. Gross: 401(k) plans often have rigid withdrawal rules (e.g., they might force you to take "pro-rata" distributions from all your funds).
Precision: In a Fidelity IRA, you can choose to sell only your cash/bond positions (Buckets 1 and 2) to fund your $1,900 monthly income, leaving your Growth Engine (Bucket 3) untouched during market downturns.
Tax Withholding Customization
The 20% Trap: Many 401(k) providers mandate a 20% federal tax withholding on distributions.
IRA Flexibility: With a Fidelity IRA, you can specify your own withholding percentage (e.g., the 15% Federal / 5% CA target mentioned in your strategy), allowing for better monthly cash flow.
Key Considerations for a 65-Year-Old in California
While the rollover is generally better, keep these two nuances in mind:
Feature 401(k) Rollover IRA
Creditor Protection Stronger: Protected under federal ERISA law from almost all lawsuits/judgments. Limited in CA: Generally protected in bankruptcy, but otherwise only protected to the extent "reasonably necessary for support."
NUA Opportunity Possible: If you hold highly appreciated company stock in your 401(k), a rollover could trigger immediate taxes on those gains. Lost: Rolling company stock into an IRA eliminates the Net Unrealized Appreciation (NUA) tax benefit.
