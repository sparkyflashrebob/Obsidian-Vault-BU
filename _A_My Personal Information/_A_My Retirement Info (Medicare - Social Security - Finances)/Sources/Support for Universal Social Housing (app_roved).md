RESOLUTION IN SUPPORT OF UNIVERSAL SOCIAL HOUSING
California Progressive Alliance Founding Convention
San Luis Obispo
March 30-31, 2019
Presented by Laksh Bhasin on behalf of the San Francisco Berniecrats Housing Committee, co-signed by
the San Francisco Green Party County Council.
The rental housing crisis in much of California stems from a lack of guarantees around housing. There is no
right to shelter, rents are set by a private for-profit market, affordability is not a guarantee, non-profit housing
is insufficient, and public housing has been systematically dismantled thanks to defunding and
Reaganomics. In San Francisco, where the authors organize, all of our public housing has been privatized
thanks to an Obama-era program, tech booms have brought massive gentrification and displacement, and
the upcoming IPOs of large tech companies threaten to aggravate displacement even further.
The authors of this resolution wrote and supported the SF Community Housing Act, a proposed 2018 SF
ballot initiative (now being reworked for the 2020 ballot) that would tax large corporations to fund the
acquisition and construction of publicly-owned, publicly-managed and unionized affordable housing,
sometimes known as "social housing."
Social security is a universal program, and so is Medicare for All, so why wouldn't we build a
universal constituency around public housing? That's why, in San Francisco, we are urging the creation
of universal social housing, where anyone who lives or works in SF may qualify. Rents are generally capped
at 25% of income, and an income distribution ensures the housing predominantly serves
low-to-moderate-income residents. In addition to the housing, we support amenities in close proximity such
as childcare centers, community meeting spaces, and rapid public transit. Tenants are allowed to form
associations called Community Councils, and are empowered to advocate for their interests.
Relation to California Progressive Alliance Mission
We believe this resolution is in line with the CPA's mission to elevate the poor, the working class, and
historically marginalized communities, reversing the crisis of displacement wrought by the private market. It
is also in line with the initial platform to create a public program for very low, low, and moderate-income
affordable housing, which would be the primary focus of universal social housing.
Resolution Body
WHEREAS, the housing unaffordability and gentrification crises in several of California's incorporated areas
has caused rent-burdening, significant displacement, and geographic exclusion on the basis of income,
thereby tearing apart the social fabric necessary to operate a functioning and just society.
WHEREAS, the National Apartment Association, a private real-estate industry group, has reported that
for-profit real-estate entities are making record profits off the backs of the working class in hot real-estate
markets, with net operating incomes as high as $\$18/sq$ ft in San Francisco in 2016.
WHEREAS, the right to affordable shelter is essential for every human being, yet is not recognized as such,
with real estate predominantly used as an investment vehicle for the wealthiest in society.
WHEREAS, a universally accessible program for public sector social housing can equitably provide for the
needs of society and create a broad-based political constituency to safeguard against privatization and
WHEREAS, a public sector program for social housing can leverage interagency cooperation to fund
amenities including improved public transit, childcare centers, and community meeting spaces.
WHEREAS, the model of Tenants Associations in the United States, and Resident Welfare Associations in
countries such as India, can provide a platform for ordinary working class tenants to improve housing
conditions and shape municipal politics.
WHEREAS, the racist and classist demonization of public housing at the federal level has led to the
defunding of the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) through neoliberal
presidencies including Richard Nixon, Ronald Reagan, and Bill Clinton, in turn further contributing to the
disrepair of public housing and providing political cover for privatization.
WHEREAS, HUD's budget authority shrank from $83 billion in 1978 to little more than $18 billion in 1983 (in
2004 dollars), with further cuts thereafter, thereby complicating the task of rehabilitating properties and
expanding federally-funded public housing stock.
WHEREAS, the Obama administration continued the trend of housing privatization under the Rental
Assistance Demonstration (RAD) program, under which public housing units have been transferred to
private real-estate entities, instead of funding a true public sector commitment to public housing.
WHEREAS, the Reagan-era Low-Income Housing Tax Credit system that funds most nonprofit affordable
housing invested only a paltry $300¹ per rent-burdened household in 2017, thus failing to meet the
necessary scale of the housing crisis in place of slashed HUD funding, while providing generous tax credits
for wealthy investors and corporations on the public dime.
WHEREAS, private models of nonprofit affordable housing do not in general guarantee community input,
deep affordability, public accountability, universality, and unionized good-paying jobs for property
management, nor do such programs function at the necessary scale to combat the affordability crisis.
WHEREAS, explicit and/or systematic housing discrimination on the basis of race, sexual orientation,
gender identity, current or former homelessness status, disability, and immigration status still persist today
in the private housing market.
WHEREAS, the classist 1950s-era Article XXXIV of the California Constitution requires a ballot referendum
to authorize the construction or acquisition of low-income housing, including public housing, in any
incorporated area of CA, thereby adding obstacles to tackling the housing crisis.

WHEREAS, the "Faircloth Amendment" of 1999 (42 U.S.C §1437g Subsection (g) (3) of the federal Housing
Act of 1937) states that HUD cannot fund the construction or operation of new public housing units with
Capital or Operating Funds if the construction of those units would result in a net increase in the number of
units owned, assisted, or operated by a Public Housing Authority.
THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the California Progressive Alliance (CPA) supports a significant
multibillion dollar annual investment for universally accessible public sector social housing at every level of
government, to acquire, construct, and rehabilitate millions of dignified public housing units, with the goal of
counteracting the present crises of unaffordability, rent burdening, gentrification, and displacement plaguing
California and the United States.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA stipulates that "social housing" as envisioned in this resolution have
the following characteristics at minimum: generally capping rental payments at an affordable percentage of
one's income (such as 25% in the SF Community Housing Act); primarily serving tenants of very-low to
moderate incomes, while remaining universally accessible; permanent public ownership and management;
financial self-sustainability, with excess rental revenues less operating and capital expenditures re-invested
into the social housing program; funding for public transit and amenities such as childcare centers and
community meeting spaces in close proximity to housing; community input in project planning and tenant
advocacy systems to ensure maintenance; and strict nondiscrimination clauses on the basis of protected
classes including race, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, immigration status, current or
former homelessness status, and other protected classes under the CA Fair Employment and Housing Act.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA supports social housing as a means to create a permanent unionized
public sector program for the acquisition, construction, and property management of affordable housing,
thereby revitalizing public sector unions after repeated right-wing attacks.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA supports a fair and progressive system of taxation, with a predominant
impact on the monied classes and wealthy corporations of present-day society, to fund the acquisition,
construction, and rehabilitation of social housing.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA supports interagency cooperation and a reorganization of the powers,
laws, and productive forces of government and society to rapidly effectuate universal social housing, with
good governing practices and regular performance audits to ensure accountability.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA supports housing as a human right, not a vehicle for investment.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA also supports other means to decommodify housing, including
democratically-owned and deed-restricted alternatives such as limited-equity cooperatives.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA supports the creation of Community Councils of tenants, by tenants,
and compensated for activities by public housing agencies, so that tenants of social housing may advocate
for their interests with government agencies managing their housing, elected representatives, and other
organizations, and with such Councils serving as platforms for a diverse working class to lead and
participate in municipal politics. Building on the model of tenants associations envisioned by SB 529 and the
SF Community Housing Act, such Community Councils should be given powers including overseeing
evictions for just cause, compelling meetings with public officials, and withholding rent in response to
grievances or complaints.
FURTHER RESOLVED that the CPA advocates for tenant protections during the process of acquiring
property into the public trust, including - in the case of necessary rehabilitation for existing tenants in
newly-acquired property - financial assistance for relocation, financial assistance for interim changes in
tenants' monthly rental payments, and a right of return.
Request for Action
To achieve the goals envisioned in this resolution, the authors of this resolution urge CPA members to:
• Join the nascent Coalition for Universal Social Housing proposed by the San Francisco Berniecrats
to organize around and further the goals envisioned in this resolution.
Support the repeal of Article XXXIV of the CA Constitution, through means including the enactment
of the proposed SCA 1.
Support state and local legislation to enact universal social housing as envisioned in this resolution,
following examples such as the SF Community Housing Act, and urge state and local legislators, as
well as housing justice advocacy groups, to produce such legislation.
Support the repeal of the federal Faircloth Amendment.
Support federal legislation to enact universal social housing as envisioned in this resolution, and
urge Congressional candidates and candidates for the 2020 presidential election to include such a
system in their platform.
