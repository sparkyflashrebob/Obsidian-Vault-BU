1. The Best All-Purpose Method (Bacteria & Dirt)

According to the FDA and CDC, the standard effective method for general cleaning is simple running water and friction. This removes dirt and 40–90% of surface bacteria.

* Wash your hands for 20 seconds with soap before handling produce.
* Rinse produce under cool, running tap water. Do not use soap or detergent (produce is porous and can absorb these chemicals, which may cause nausea).
* Rub the surface briskly with your hands while rinsing.
* Scrub firm produce (melons, cucumbers, potatoes) with a clean vegetable brush.
* Dry thoroughly with a clean cloth or paper towel. This friction step is crucial as it further reduces bacteria left on the surface.
* Peeling: Peel root vegetables and remove the outermost leaves of leafy greens (lettuce, cabbage) to reduce bacterial and pesticide load. [[6](https://extension.umn.edu/food-safety-basics/washing-fresh-fruits-and-vegetables#:~:text=How%20to%20wash%20produce%20*%20Wash%20hands,foods%2C%20including%20fresh%20produce.%20*%20Clean%20and), [9](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/7-tips-cleaning-fruits-vegetables#:~:text=If%20possible%2C%20FDA%20says%20to%20choose%20produce,bags%20of%20lettuce%20or%20watermelon%20slices%E2%80%94are%20either)]

2. For Pesticides (Baking Soda Soak)

While water removes loose residues, a baking soda solution is scientifically shown to degrade certain surface pesticides more effectively than water or bleach alone.

* The Method: Mix 1 teaspoon of baking soda per 2 cups of water (or 2 tsp per quart).
* Soak: Submerge produce for 12–15 minutes.
* Rinse: Rinse thoroughly with fresh water to remove the salty taste and residues.
* Limitations: This only works for surface pesticides. "Systemic" pesticides (those absorbed into the fruit's flesh via roots) cannot be washed off. 3. For Mold & Bacteria (Vinegar Soak)

A vinegar solution can help kill bacteria (reducing counts by up to 98% in some cases) and inhibit mold growth, potentially extending shelf life.

* The Method: Mix 1 part white vinegar to 3 parts water.
* Soak: Let the produce sit for roughly 10 minutes.
* Rinse: Rinse well under running water to remove the sour flavor.
* Note: This may cause delicate items like berries or lettuce to wilt or affect their texture/taste if soaked too long. 4. For PFAS ("Forever Chemicals")

There is no proven "wash" that chemically destroys or extracts PFAS from produce tissue. Prevention and physical removal are the only current strategies.

* Peeling: Since PFAS can be present in soil particles adhering to the skin, peeling root vegetables and thoroughly scrubbing dirt off all produce is recommended.
* Soil Removal: Wash or scrub all visible dirt, as PFAS often accumulate in soil particles attached to the produce.
* Water Filtration: Use filtered water (e.g., Reverse Osmosis) for washing if your local tap water is known to be contaminated with PFAS, to avoid adding more contaminants to the food.
