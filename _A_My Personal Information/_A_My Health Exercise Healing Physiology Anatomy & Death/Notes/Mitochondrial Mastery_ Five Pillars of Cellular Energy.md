The gist of this video by Dr. William Li is that you can combat fatigue and significantly boost your daily energy levels by improving the function of your mitochondria, which act as the "power plants" or batteries inside your cells [1].

He outlines five main science-backed strategies to help your body repair damaged mitochondria and generate new ones:

* **Consistent Exercise:** Exercise is the most powerful way to improve mitochondrial function [2]. He recommends a mix of high-intensity interval training (HIIT) to signal cells to create new mitochondria, "Zone 2" aerobic exercise (like a brisk walk) to train them to use oxygen efficiently, and resistance training to boost mitochondria in your muscles [2-4].
* **Quality Sleep:** During 7-8 hours of sleep, your body goes into a repair state where it clears out damaged mitochondria—a process called mitophagy—and repairs or replaces worn-out cellular proteins [5-7].
* **Morning Sunlight Exposure:** Stepping outside for 5 to 30 minutes of natural light within an hour of waking sets your brain's master clock (the suprachiasmatic nucleus) [8, 9]. This signals your mitochondria to shift out of nighttime repair mode and into daytime energy-production mode [8, 10].
* **Urolithin A:** This is a compound that helps activate mitophagy to remove failing mitochondria [11]. It is produced by healthy gut bacteria when you eat foods rich in ellagitannins, like pomegranates, walnuts, and berries [12]. Because less than half of the population can efficiently produce Urolithin A in their gut, Dr. Li notes that direct supplementation is also an option [10, 11, 13].
* **Fasting and Reducing Calories:** When you are constantly eating, especially ultra-processed foods or refined carbs, your cells stay in a "fed state" focused only on storing energy [13-15]. By practicing time-restricted eating (eating within an 8-10 hour window and fasting for 14-16 hours) or reducing daily calories, you force your body to drop insulin levels and shift back into a mitochondrial repair and cleanup mode [14, 16].
