### The 20 Highest Protein Foods Vegetarian Runners Can Eat

We've ranked the top veggies, legumes, and minimally processed meat alternatives.
by [Stephanie Eckelcamp For Eat Clean](https://www.runnersworld.com/author/216331/stephanie-eckelcamp-for-eat-clean/) Updated: Apr 25, 2026 10:57 PM EDT
Save Article
Share Article
Mitch Mandel
Maybe you're considering giving up meat to [lose weight](https://www.runnersworld.com/nutrition-weight-loss/a25646745/running-for-weight-loss/), trying to lighten your carbon footprint, or you've noticed a lot of record-setting endurance athletes are [vegan](https://www.runnersworld.com/uk/nutrition/a41830879/benefits-of-vegan-diet/). Whatever your reason, relying less on animal products can be a great first step in upping your intake of nutrient-rich whole foods. But how are you going to get enough [protein](https://www.runnersworld.com/nutrition-weight-loss/a70114399/what-runners-should-know-about-protein/)?
Don't sweat it—we did the math for you. Here, we've ranked 20 of the highest-protein veggies, legumes, and minimally processed meat alternatives, like tempeh, for your convenience.

1

#### Brussels Sprouts

Feifei Cui-Paoluzzo// Getty Images
**2 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2685575/nutrients)
These little green guys get a bad rap in the taste department—especially the frozen variety—but they're actually nutritional superstars. In addition to protein, brussels sprouts pack hefty doses of potassium and vitamin K.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
2

#### Broccoli

Helen Camacaro// Getty Images
**2 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/747447/nutrients) **(cooked)**
Broccoli's not only an awesome source of fiber, its protein content is surprising, too (for a veggie anyway). And you can't go wrong with a vegetable that's been [proven](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10313060/) to deliver cancer-fighting compounds like sulforaphane.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
3

#### Avocado

AleksandarGeorgiev// Getty Images
**2 grams of protein in** [½ avocado](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2710824/nutrients)
This fruit is creamy, dreamy, and super filling, thanks to its bend of monounsaturated fatty acids and a bit of protein. For a new spin on everyone's favorite guacamole ingredient, you can even make it into a smoothie.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
4

#### Corn

OsakaWayne Studios// Getty Images
**2.5 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2710826/nutrients)
Like potatoes, corn often gets put into the “plants with no redeeming qualities” category, but paired with protein-rich veggies and legumes, it can nicely round out a protein-packed plant-based dish. Pick organic or non-GMO fresh or frozen varieties, though, as most conventional corn has been genetically modified.

5

#### Spinach

Minh Hoang Cong / 500px// Getty Images
**3 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/1999633/nutrients) **(cooked)**
Sure, 3 grams may not sound like a lot, but for a green veggie it is. Still, don't just make a salad and call it a day. Cooking this green is the secret to upping its protein content.

6

#### Potatoes

wulingyun// Getty Images
**3 grams of protein in 1 medium white potato**
Another stealth source of protein! Despite having a reputation for being pretty much devoid of all nutrition, a medium-sized spud actually contains a good bit of protein, along with about 20 percent of the recommended daily intake of heart-healthy potassium, per [The Alliance for Potato Research & Education.](https://apre.org/)
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
7

#### Pumpkin Seeds

Javier Zayas Photography// Getty Images
**9 grams of protein in** [¼-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2515380/nutrients)
Pumpkin seeds aren't just a super convenient way to get a dose of satiating protein, they're total nutrient powerhouses. The snack [contains](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9182978/) about half the recommended daily intake of magnesium, along with immune-boosting zinc, plant-based omega-3s, and tryptophan—which can help ease you into a restful slumber.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
8

#### Cashews

HUIZENG HU// Getty Images
**5 grams of protein in** [¼-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2515374/nutrients)
In addition to a decent protein punch, cashews are a great way to increase your magnesium intake, according to [UCLA Health](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/article/are-you-getting-enough-magnesium). They're also high in vitamin K, so it's an ideal for those looking to support bone density.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
9

#### Steel-Cut Oats

James Ransom
**5 grams of protein in** [¼-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2346397/nutrients) **(dry)**
Steel-cut oats aren't just a solid source of protein; they also have a lower glycemic index than rolled oats. This means they don't spike blood sugar as much, so you're likely to be more satisfied and experience fewer cravings after eating them.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
10

#### Chia Seeds

HUIZENG HU// Getty Images
**5 grams of protein in** [2 tablespoons](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2710819/nutrients)
Chia packs a ton of protein in those pint-sized seeds, which are also a great source of alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a type of plant-based omega-3 fatty acid. Bonus: Omega-3s help stimulate the satiety hormone leptin, which signals your body to burn these fats instead of storing them.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
11

#### Almonds

Kentaroo Tryman// Getty Images
**7 grams of protein in** [¼-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2346393/nutrients)
Along with protein, almonds deliver some serious vitamin E, which is great for the health of your skin and hair. They also provide magnesium, which can help curb sugar cravings, soothe PMS-related cramps, boost bone health, and ease muscle soreness and spasms.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
12

#### Chickpeas

Javier Zayas Photography// Getty Images
**6 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2644282/nutrients)
Permission to eat all the hummus—well, maybe not all of it, but the combo of protein and fiber in chickpeas (a.k.a. garbanzo beans) make for one healthy dip. Try it slathered on sandwich bread in place of mayo, or serve up a tasty hummus recipe with veggie slices.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
13

#### Wild Rice

Whitney via Flickr and licensed under Creative Commons Attribution
**6.5 grams of protein in** [1-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2710821/nutrients) **(cooked)**
Move over, quinoa. Wild rice is the protein-rich grain you should be gravitating toward. With a nutty taste and slightly chewy texture, it's way more satisfying, too. For a comforting (but clean) meal, try something like a wild rice casserole.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
14

#### Peanuts and Peanut Butter

fcafotodigital// Getty Images
**7 grams of protein in** [¼-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2262072/nutrients) **(or 2 tablespoons of peanut butter)**
Not only are peanuts and peanut butter great for munching and whipping up classic childhood comfort food, they're also super versatile. They've also [been shown](https://www.denverhealthmedicalplan.org/blog/healthy-perks-peanut-butter) to help you eat less at lunch if you consume them at breakfast—aka the second-meal effect. PB and banana, anyone?
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
15

#### Lima Beans

Diana Miller// Getty Images
**6.6 grams of protein in ½-cup (cooked)**
In addition to filling protein, lima beans [contain](https://fjs.fudutsinma.edu.ng/index.php/fjs/article/view/757) the amino acid leucine, which may play a big role in healthy muscle synthesis among older adults. They're often used in Southern and Mediterranean dishes, like soups, salads, and bakes.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
16

#### Black Beans

Yurou Guan// Getty Images
**7.6 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/747444/nutrients) **(cooked)**
Black beans are also packed with heart-healthy fiber, potassium, folate, vitamin B6, and a range of phytonutrients. They also make a killer batch of black bean brownies!
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
17

#### Lentils

F.J. Jimenez// Getty Images
**9 grams of protein in** [½-cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2644283/nutrients)
Low-cal, high-fiber, and high-protein lentils can be morphed into a nutrient-dense side dish, veggie burger, or even whipped into a hummus-like dip. They've [been shown](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10857178/) to lower cholesterol and reduce risk of heart disease.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
18

#### Tofu

HUIZENG HU// Getty Images
**12 grams of protein in 3 ounces**
Ah, tofu, the classic vegetarian blank slate made from curdled soy milk. It's wonderful pan-fried, sautéed in a stir-fry, and even scrambled. Though it's not quite as protein-packed as other meat alternatives (see below), its taste may be more tolerable. Opt for organic varieties to avoid genetically modified soy and funky pesticides.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
19

#### Tempeh

Ar razzaq// Getty Images
**16 grams of protein in 3 ounces**
Tempeh is made by fermenting cooked soybeans and shaping it into a dense cake that can be sliced and pan-fried like tofu. It's nutty, chewy, and packs significantly more protein and fiber than tofu—and some find it easier to digest because it's fermented.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
20

#### Soybeans

Wang Yukun// Getty Images
**29 grams of protein in 1-cup (cooked)**
Talk about healthiest appetizer ever—just a cup's worth of edamame (or cooked soybeans) packs a huge protein punch. Be sure to pick an organic variety, though, as most soybeans in the US are genetically modified and heavily treated with pesticides.
