Hey everybody in this video I want to discuss
one of the most overlooked muscles used in human
walking, the psoas muscle. The psoas muscle
is critical to proper posture and walking
technique with low impact on your joints.
Many people do not engage the psoas muscle
properly when walking and it can lead to
a variety of different and common walking
problems that end up causing extra stress on
your joints and can ultimately lead to pain.
The psoas muscle begins attached to your top four
lumbar vertebrae and the bottom thoracic vertebrae
and expands outwards from the lumbar spine
into the pelvis attaching to the femur.
When we have one leg free if I activate the psoas
muscle on that side I am flexing the hip with it
pulling my upper leg up towards my spine. If
we have our weight on the legs and we activate
the psoas muscle what it's doing is pulling the
lumbar spine forward towards our legs. That helps
to maintain the lumbar posture and the lumbar
lordosis here in the lower back. If you're lying
down doing a sit-up if you activate the psoas
on both sides it helps pull your lumbar spine
towards the lower legs. One of the reasons I
believe the psoas has been ignored is because it's
one of our core muscles and the core is largely
ignored when people are doing EMG studies in favor
of imagining that all of our movement is coming
from our legs, but really the core is the central
mover or guider of all of our movement. And
if we ignore it we're really not going to be
moving correctly. So, when we are using our psoas
muscle correctly what's happening is this. As we
place our heel onto the ground we are using
our psoas muscle on the rear leg to pull
the leg forward by flexing it. So imagine
pulling my right knee forward as I shift
weight. Once I get to the flat foot position I'm
now going to use my psoas muscle on the front leg
to pull the lumbar spine forward. And at the same
time I'm using my lower abs which are also ignored
in research on walking to tuck the
pelvis the combination of those two
actions are going to allow me to pull my spine
forward while maintaining a vertical posture.
Let's see from the side view, when the heel
contacts I am pulling forward from the rear muscle
and then now once my foot hits the flat position
I'm going to pull forward from the forward leg
psoas and then I'm going to continue to cycle
through that also using the rotation of my upper
and lower waist muscles to coordinate with that
action. What happens when people don't use the
psoas correctly, a couple different things.
First, duck foot walking. You'll commonly
see people walking like this and it's a very large
group of people that do that. Duck foot walking is
very harmful to the joints people often think
it may be caused by the way they were born
the structure of their body the rotation of their
tibia but I believe it is a functional issue when
people walk duck footed and you can watch in these
images here. They will always be using the psoas
on the side of the swing leg to step forward not
using the psoas on the side of the standing leg.
If we keep our feet straight forward
we can't use the psoas on the swing leg
to step forward. You can practice this. Face your
feet forward and see if you can step forward with
the psoas. All I can do is temporarily lift
the leg up into a unstable position and then
place it back to get my balance. It doesn't
actually move my spine forward any further.
This leads some people to lean forward from the
upper waist to try to keep the body going. That's
when the feet are facing forward. Other people in
order to use the psoas muscle turn their feet out.
Now they can use the psoas muscle on the side of
the swing leg to step forward and that is a huge
problem with walking that's very common. Let
me give you a little exercise to help you
understand how to pull forward correctly using
the psoas muscle, keeping proper posture.
Place one foot out straight ahead and the
other foot angled out at about 30 degrees
and just practice shifting weight forward
and backwards. Shift to your rear leg.
When you do that face the forward leg and then
shift forward to your forward leg and you'll face
slightly at an angle and shift back and forth
without pushing. Don't try to push yourself
forward just shift yourself forward and back.
Maintain vertical posture all right and then
when you get to the forward leg you can see my
knee is lined up over my ankle. This is really
the equivalent of the position I would be in after
my forefoot hits the ground when I'm walking.
Now the first thing you're going to notice is
you can't push yourself forward using your glutes
on this side. If I try to push my leg down
it doesn't push me forward it's really going
to push me backwards. The only way to
get going forward from here is to pull
