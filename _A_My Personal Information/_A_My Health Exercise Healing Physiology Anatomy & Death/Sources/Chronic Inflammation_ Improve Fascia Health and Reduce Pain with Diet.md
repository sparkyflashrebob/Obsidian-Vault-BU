How an Anti-Inflammatory Diet Supports Fascia Health

Improve circulation and keep tissues hydrated

Support cellular energy production
Key Elements of an Anti-Inflammatory Diet

1. Omega-3 Fatty Acids
   Omega-3s help reduce inflammation and support overall cell health. Some of the best sources include:
   Fatty fish such as salmon, sardines, and mackerel
   Chia seeds and flaxseeds
   Algae-based supplements
2. Antioxidant-Rich Fruits and Vegetables
   Antioxidants help protect the body from inflammation-related damage. Eating a variety of colorful fruits and vegetables provides essential nutrients for healing. Consider adding:
   Leafy greens such as kale, spinach, and Swiss chard
   Berries including blueberries, strawberries, and raspberries
   Cruciferous vegetables like broccoli, cauliflower, and Brussels sprouts
   Tomatoes and bell peppers
3. Whole, Unprocessed Foods
   Minimally processed foods provide key nutrients that support tissue repair and energy production. Incorporate:
   Whole grains such as quinoa, brown rice, and oats
   Legumes including lentils, chickpeas, and black beans
   Nuts and seeds such as almonds, pumpkin seeds, and sunflower seeds
4. Hydration and Herbal Support
   Staying hydrated is essential for fascia flexibility and overall cellular function. Be sure to drink:
   Filtered water
   Herbal teas, including turmeric, ginger, and green tea
   Bone broth, which provides collagen for fascia support
   Foods to Avoid That Can Increase Inflammation
   Certain foods can trigger inflammation and negatively affect fascia health. Limiting or avoiding the following can support better movement and recovery:
   Processed and fried foods
   Refined sugars and artificial sweeteners
   Excessive alcohol and caffeine
   Hydrogenated oils and trans fats
