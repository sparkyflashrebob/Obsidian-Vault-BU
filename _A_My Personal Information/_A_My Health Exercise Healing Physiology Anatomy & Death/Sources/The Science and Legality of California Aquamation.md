Aguamation Info
Aquamation, scientifically known as alkaline hydrolysis, is an eco-friendly alternative to traditional flame cremation. It uses a mixture of water and potassium hydroxide, combined with heat and pressure, to gently accelerate the body’s natural decomposition process. [1, 2, 3, 4]

#### How It Works

The process places remains in a pressurized, stainless-steel vessel filled with 95% water and 5% alkali solution. Heated gently to about 300°F (150°C), the solution breaks down organic matter into amino acids, peptides, and sugars over 4 to 6 hours. [1, 3, 5, 6] The only solid remains left behind are the bones, which are perfectly sterile. These are dried and processed into a powdery, white ash returned to the family in an urn. [3, 5, 7]

#### Key Benefits

* Eco-Friendly: It uses a fraction of the energy required for flame cremation and produces no direct greenhouse gas emissions.
* Gentle Process: Because it relies on water rather than fire, it yields approximately 20% more remains for families to keep.
* Metal Recovery: Implants like pacemakers, artificial joints, and dental fillings are not destroyed and can be easily recovered intact. [3, 5, 6, 8]

  Yes, aquamation is legal and available for both humans and pets in California. [1, 2] The state officially legalized alkaline hydrolysis for human remains when Assembly Bill 967 took effect on July 1, 2020. Facilities providing this service are licensed and strictly regulated by the California Cemetery and Funeral Bureau. [3]

#### Availability and Costs

While fully legal, the specialized, expensive equipment means human aquamation is not yet universally available at every neighborhood funeral home. However, a growing number of dedicated regional providers serve families across : [3]

* Northern California & Bay Area: [Pacific Interment](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/1td25p42) provides direct aquamation starting around $3,750. In the Sacramento region, [Ethos](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11l6xmc6nx) offers full packages starting at $4,995. [1, 4, 5]
* Southern California: Providers like [Natural Grace](https://www.betterplaceforests.com/blog/aquamation-in-california/) (serving the broader Los Angeles region) offer services starting at $4,995. In the San Diego area, families utilize [Bravo Family Mortuary](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11rzfzdfp5) and [White Rose Aqua Cremation](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11q79kd09s) (packages from $3,750). [1, 3, 4]
* Statewide Options: Digital-forward providers like [Anubis Cremations](https://anubiscremations.com/eco-friendly-water-cremation-aquamation-and-resomation-services-across-california/) and [Aquaterra](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11w3gp3xlf) coordinate water cremation services across multiple regions. [6, 7, 8]
  On average, direct human aquamation in California typically ranges from $3,200 to $5,000. This makes it slightly more expensive than traditional flame cremation, but significantly less expensive than a formal casket burial. [6, 9]

#### Pet Aquamation

If you are asking on behalf of a pet, the rules are different. Pet aquamation is fully legal and widely accessible throughout all areas of California. Numerous specialized veterinary aftercare facilities and mobile pet mortuaries offer the service across the state.
