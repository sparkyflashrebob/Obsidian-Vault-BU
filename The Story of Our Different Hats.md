# The Story of Different Hats: Changing Our Minds to Change Our World

We often believe we are seeing the world exactly as it is. But neuroscience and psychology tell us a different story: we are seeing the world as *we* are. Our brain is a prediction machine, constantly running ahead of the present moment to anticipate what happens next. Before we even think a conscious thought, our brain has already reacted based on past conditioning, deep-seated biases, and our current emotional state.

I call this phenomenon **"The Story of Different Hats."**

Each "hat" represents a mindset—a specific neural filter through which we perceive reality. We don't just wear these hats; we become them. They dictate how we interact with our environment, our work, and the people we love.

Here are just a few examples of the endless number of hats we wear throughout the day if we are not mindful.

[cite_start]Instead, a familiar habitual and mostly unconscious script begins running immediately: *Must do, must rush, must worry*[cite: 6]. [cite_start]This is our default mode—a state where we are driven by the biological impulse for survival and efficiency, pulling us toward yesterday’s anxieties rather than today’s possibilities[cite: 6]. We aren't fully awake; we are simply re-enacting a habit.
