> 
> Allow your hands to rest comfortably in your lap or on your knees. Gently tuck the chin slightly to lengthen the back of the neck, and let the crown of your head lift toward the sky.
> 
> Allow your eyes to close, or rest with a soft, unfocused gaze downward.

> Take three slow, deep breaths:
> 
> - Inhaling deeply to fill the lungs... and a slow, complete out-breath, releasing the weight of your day.
> - Inhaling clarity and wakefulness... exhaling all tightness from the shoulders, face, and chest.
> - Inhaling presence... and exhaling into stillness.
> 
> Sense the solid contact of your body on the seat. Feeling rooted, grounded, and fully arrived in this present moment."

### 2. Establishing the Primary Anchor: The Breath (5–7 minutes)

> "Now, allow the breath to find its own natural rhythm. There is no need to control the breath, make it deeper, or change it in any way. Just let the body breathe itself.
> 
> Choose a place where you feel the movement of the breath most clearly:
> 
> - Perhaps the cool sensation at the tip of the nostrils or upper lip.
> - Perhaps the rising and falling of the chest.
> - Or the expansion and softening of the belly.
> 
> Let your attention rest right there, riding the natural waves of inhalation and exhalation.
> 
> Like a gentle friend sitting by a river, simply watching the water flow past: knowing the in-breath as an in-breath, knowing the out-breath as an out-breath."

---

### 3. Working with Hindrances, Wandering Mind, & Discomfort: Applying RAIN (10–20 minutes)

> "As you sit, it is completely natural for the mind to wander. You may encounter restlessness, drowsiness, obsessive thinking, emotional reactivity, or physical aches in the knees or back.
> 
> Whenever you notice that you have drifted away from your anchor, or when a strong sensation calls your attention, treat it not as an interruption, but as an invitation to practice **RAIN**."

                    ┌────────────────────────┐

                    │   R  ─  Recognize      │

                    │   A  ─  Allow          │

                    │   I  ─  Investigate    │

                    │   N  ─  Nurture/Return │

                    └────────────────────────┘

#### **R — Recognize (Notice What Has Arisen)**

> "The moment you realize your attention is no longer on the breath, pause.
> 
> Simply **Recognize** what has pulled you away. Acknowledge it with a light, friendly mental note:
> 
> - _'Planning...'_
> - _'Remembering...'_
> - _'Restlessness...'_
> - _'Sleepiness...'_
> - _'Ache in the lower back...'_
> 
> Just clearly seeing: _'Ah, this is what is present right now.'_"

#### **A — Allow (Drop the Struggle)**

> "Next, **Allow** this experience to be here without judging yourself or fighting it.
> 
> Avoid the impulse to scold the mind for wandering, and avoid tensing against physical discomfort.
> 
> Silently offer internal permission:
> 
> - _'It’s okay. This belongs.'_
> - _'Let it be here for a moment.'_
> 
> Release any tension around wanting the moment to be different than it is. Create spaciousness around the experience."

#### **I — Investigate (Shift from Story to Body)**

> "Now, gently **Investigate** what this experience actually feels like somatically.
> 
> Drop out of the mental storyline (the words, thoughts, and images) and feel the direct physical sensations in the body:
> 
> - If there is anxiety or impatience, where is it held? Is your stomach clenched? Is your chest tight? Is your breathing shallow?
> - If there is physical pain, what is its true texture? Is it burning, tingling, pulsing, throbbing, or sharp?
> - If there is sleepiness, notice the heaviness behind the eyes or the dullness in the mind.
> 
> Approach it with gentle, curious attention, not trying to get rid of it, but simply getting to know the changing nature of sensation."

#### **N — Nurture & Non-Identification (Release and Return)**

> "Finally, **Nurture** yourself with kindness and release identification.
> 
> Soften your heart toward this momentary distraction or discomfort. Remind yourself:
> 
> - _'This thought or feeling is not me, not mine. It is simply a passing weather pattern in awareness.'_
> 
> With a soft smile and an attitude of forgiveness, gently untangle your attention, and bring your awareness back home to the natural rhythm of the in-breath and the out-breath."

---

### 4. Silent Sitting & Sustained Practice (5–15 minutes)

_(Allow for periods of silence with brief periodic reminders...)_

> "Continuing in this way:
> 
> Resting with the anchor of the breath...
> 
> When the mind stays, enjoy the quiet presence.
> 
> When the mind drifts or an obstacle arises, meet it with **RAIN**:
> 
> - **R**ecognize it...
> - **A**llow it...
> - **I**nvestigate the physical feeling...
> - **N**urture with kindness, release the story, and gently return to the breath."

---

### 5. Resting in Open Awareness & Closing (3–4 minutes)

> "In the final moments of this sit, let go of the breath. Let go of the anchor and all techniques.
> 
> Rest as the open, silent space of awareness itself.
> 
> Notice that all thoughts, sensations, sounds, and feelings arise and pass away within this quiet, spacious knowing. You are the peaceful sky, and everything else is just passing clouds.
> 
> Feel gratitude for dedicating this time to nourish your heart, cultivate clarity, and develop compassion."

_(Sound of the bell...)_

> "As the sound of the bell fades, gently deepen your breath. Feel your fingers and toes, gently stretch the body as needed, and slowly open your eyes—bringing this awake, kind presence into the next activity of your day."
