Charles "Chuck" Johnson. Of Soul Beat TV. handout photo

Charles "Chuck" Johnson, a pioneering African American broadcaster who aired some of the first music videos on his Soul Beat cable television station in Oakland, has died of cancer. He was 66.

Mr. Johnson died July 27 at Alta Bates Summit Medical Center in Oakland.

"He was a person who would not let adversity stop him from pursuing his dreams," said Oakland City Councilman Larry Reid. "He dreamed of operating a communication vehicle for the African American community. His viewers at Soul Beat were his family."

Charles Edward Johnson was born in Tulsa, Okla., on Oct. 23, 1938, the first of 10 children to Roy and Castella Johnson. He was raised in Kansas City, Mo., where in 1959 he got his first chance as a guest disc jockey when the regularly scheduled morning host was injured in a car accident.

After serving in the Air Force, Mr. Johnson moved to Los Angeles, where in 1962 he became the first black manager of a Los Angeles radio station -- KTYM 103.9FM, which later became KACE. In 1964, he moved to San Diego, where he became the first black owner of an FM radio station and served as president of that city's NAACP branch.

He began working in cable TV in the late 1960s and later worked for Paramount Studios in the distribution and marketing of movies geared to black audiences.

Mr. Johnson moved to Oakland in 1977 and founded Soul Beat Television a year later. The cable network contained a variety of programming, from call-in talk shows to religion, often using volunteer talent. At its peak on Channel 37, Soul Beat reached an estimated 80,000 homes.

He pioneered the broadcasting of long blocks of music videos, including many funk, disco and soul groups such as Shalamar, Midnight Starr and Lakeside.

"We're a church, a friend," he once said. "People may laugh now, but they'll miss us when we're gone."

The station often faced financial problems. It was briefly taken off the air in 1977 when Mr. Johnson owed the Internal Revenue Service $37,000. But he was able to raise $40,000 from viewers during a special one-week telethon to pay the IRS and save his station.

Soul Beat was taken off the air in November by Comcast, which cited several hundred thousand dollars in unpaid bills as the reason. Mr. Johnson continued to broadcast over a low-frequency radio station and the Internet. The station's future remains unclear after his death, friends and family said.

Some of his programming was controversial, including a former show hosted by Yusuf Bey, a black Muslim leader who faced his own legal problems before he died of cancer last year.

Mr. Johnson saw himself as an activist who took pride that his station remained "100 percent black owned," and he refused assistance from potential investors over the years. He also unsuccessfully ran for Oakland mayor in 1985, receiving 685 votes.

He is survived by a son, Charles Johnson Jr. of Los Angeles; three brothers, Lee Johnson of Augusta, Ga., Lawrence Johnson of San Diego and William McDaniel of Temecula (Riverside County); five sisters, Patricia Webb of Oklahoma City, Jeanette Richardson of Missouri, Sandra Jones of Dallas, Rhonda McDaniel of Temecula and Paula Murdock of San Diego; and two grandchildren.

About 500 people attended services for Mr. Johnson on Monday at Union Baptist Church in Oakland, his family said.
