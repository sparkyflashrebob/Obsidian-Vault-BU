# Corte Madera Creek Historical Benchmark Floods & Watershed Chronology
**Location:** Larkspur, CA (`37.941° N, 122.535° W`) — Lower Corte Madera Creek Watershed  
**Apartment Entrance Landing:** `10.33 ft NAVD88`  
**Reference Gauges:** Golden Gate NWLON (`9414290`) & USGS Corte Madera Creek at Ross (`11460000`)  
**Date of Assessment:** October 11, 2026  

---

## 1. Overview & Recurrence Context

To understand when conditions last reached or threatened the **10.0 – 10.33 ft NAVD88** threshold at this property, the historical record of the Corte Madera Creek watershed reveals two distinct flood mechanisms:

1. **Pure Coastal Tidal Flooding (King Tides & Ocean Storm Surges)**:
   * Driven by astronomical perigean spring tides, coastal Kelvin waves, and low-pressure ocean surges entering the bay.
   * **Historical Ceiling:** Peaked at **8.46 ft NAVD88** (January 3, 2026) and **8.88 ft NAVD88** (January 27, 1983).
   * **Impact on Property:** Submerges street crowns and parking stalls, but leaves the apartment landing protected by a **+1.45 to +1.87 ft (17 to 22 inch)** dry buffer.

2. **Compound Freshwater Watershed Floods (Atmospheric Rivers + Tidal Backwater)**:
   * Driven by torrential rainfall over Mount Tamalpais (up to 16 inches in 24–36 hours) channeling down Ross Valley into lower Larkspur.
   * Only when catastrophic freshwater mountain runoff meets an incoming high tide can water back up into the **10.0 – 11.0 ft NAVD88** range.
   * **Historical Occurrence:** These conditions have occurred only **twice in the last 70 years** (1982 and 2005).

---

## 2. The Benchmark Historical Events

### 🏆 1. The All-Time Watershed Benchmark: January 3–5, 1982 *(44 Years Ago)*
* **Event Type**: Catastrophic Freshwater Atmospheric River / Mountain Deluge.
* **Rainfall**: Up to **16.0 inches of rain fell in 30 hours** across Mount Tamalpais and the Ross Valley watershed.
* **Ocean Tide at Golden Gate**: **6.97 ft NAVD88** (moderate high tide, not a King Tide).
* **Watershed Dynamics**: Corte Madera Creek reached its all-time record peak discharge. The volume was so intense that the concrete channel in Ross and Kentfield overtopped completely, washing cars down streets.
* **Larkspur Impact**: Near Magnolia Avenue, Lucky Drive, and Highway 101, floodwaters pooled **4 to 5 feet deep** in lower street dips. Local water surface elevations reached approximately **10.0 to 11.0 ft NAVD88**, the only event in modern history to reach the elevation of ground apartment thresholds in this zone.

---

### 🌊 2. The All-Time Ocean Tide Benchmark: January 27, 1983 *(43 Years Ago)*
* **Event Type**: Super El Niño Coastal Storm Surge + King Tide.
* **Ocean Tide at Golden Gate**: **8.88 ft NAVD88** (the highest verified ocean water level recorded at the Golden Gate since records began in 1854).
* **Rainfall**: Moderate coastal showers, not a torrential inland deluge.
* **Larkspur Impact**: Tidal water submerged all low-lying roads and entered parking lots throughout Larkspur, but without massive mountain runoff, the water line leveled off at the ocean boundary height (~8.9 ft NAVD88).
* **Landing Clearance**: The apartment entrance landing (10.33 ft) maintained a **+1.45 ft (17.4 inch)** dry buffer.

---

### ⚡ 3. The Last Major Compound Disaster: December 31, 2005 *(21 Years Ago)*
* **Event Type**: The "New Year's Eve Flood" (Compound King Tide + Atmospheric River).
* **Rainfall**: Over **8.0 inches of rain** fell across Mount Tamalpais in 24 hours.
* **Ocean Tide at Golden Gate**: **8.04 ft NAVD88** (high winter tide + storm surge).
* **The Compound Mechanism**: The peak pulse of mountain runoff met an 8.04 ft ocean tide entering Corte Madera Creek. The bay functioned as a hydraulic wall ("tidal backwater"), preventing creek drainage.
* **Larkspur Impact**: Over 1,000 structures were flooded across Ross Valley and downtown San Anselmo. Low-lying roads and parking lots across Larkspur and Corte Madera were deeply inundated to **9.5 – 10.0 ft NAVD88**.
* **Landing Clearance**: The apartment entrance landing (10.33 ft) maintained an estimated **+0.3 to +0.8 ft (4 to 10 inch)** clearance buffer.

## 3. Why 10.33 ft Has Remained 100% Dry for the Past 20+ Years (2006 – 2026)

Across every major storm and King Tide cycle over the last two decades, water levels in the lower lot have never approached 10.33 ft:

| Event Date | Character of Event | Peak Golden Gate Level | Highest Tier Breached | Distance to Apt Landing (10.33') |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| **Jan 3, 2026** | King Tide + Kelvin Surge | **8.46 ft NAVD88** | Tier 6 (Upper Walkway: 8.27') | **+1.87 ft (22.4 in) Dry** |
| **Dec 14, 2024** | Atmospheric River King Tide | **8.22 ft NAVD88** | Tier 5 (Prius Stall: 7.75') | **+2.11 ft (25.3 in) Dry** |
| **Jan 5, 2023** | Atmospheric River Bomb Cyclone | **7.77 ft NAVD88** | Tier 5 (Prius Stall: 7.75') | **+2.56 ft (30.7 in) Dry** |
| **Jan 11, 2017** | Atmospheric River Series | **7.74 ft NAVD88** | Tier 4 (Parking Aisle: 7.45') | **+2.59 ft (31.1 in) Dry** |
| **Dec 3, 2014** | Historic Winter Storm | **7.85 ft NAVD88** | Tier 5 (Prius Stall: 7.75') | **+2.48 ft (29.8 in) Dry** |
| **Oct 11, 2026** | *Today's Surge Event* | **7.63 ft NAVD88** | Tier 4 (Parking Aisle: 7.45') | **+2.70 ft (32.4 in) Dry** |

### The Two Protective Factors:
1. **Timing Asynchrony**: In all storms between 2006 and 2026, the peak hourly rainfall intensity on Mount Tamalpais missed the exact 60-minute window of peak astronomical high tide by several hours.
2. **Post-2005 Civil Infrastructure Upgrades**: Following the 2005 disaster, the Marin County Flood Control and Water Conservation District (Zone 9) implemented major flood-reduction capital projects:
   * **Corte Madera Creek Channel Dredging & Desilting**: Removing sediment buildup in the lower tidal reach to lower water surface profiles.
   * **Pump Station & Flap Gate Modernization**: Installing positive backflow prevention flap gates on storm drain outfalls along Larkspur and Corte Madera, preventing high bay tides from backing up into neighborhood street gutters.
   * **Upstream Flood Detention Basins**: Upstream storage designed to capture and attenuate peak flood waves along Ross Valley before they reach the tidal estuary.
