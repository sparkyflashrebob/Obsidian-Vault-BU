# Voice, Tone & Style Guidelines for Living Mindfully & Head and Heart Together (Andy)

1. **Non-Prescriptive, Gentle Tone (No Commands)**:
   - Never give orders or imperative commands to the audience or reader (avoid "Stop gaslighting yourself", "Catch yourself", "Do this", "Don't do that").
   - Use gentle, inviting, observational phrasing instead: "There is no need to gaslight yourself", "We can notice...", "We can remind ourselves...", "Holding...".
   - Use the inclusive human "we" rather than lecturing "you" ("we catch ourselves", "we experience", "we tend to add on").

2. **Authentic Natural Vocabulary & Phrasing**:
   - For physical tension in the stomach, say "our stomach is tight or knotted up" (do not use dramatic or cliché phrases like "clenched like a fist").
   - Use simple, direct everyday phrasing (say "have been feeling lately", not "feeling in our bones").
   - Frame difficulty as things "entering our senses" (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind) rather than "landing on physical resources".
   - Ground teachings in everyday life (the checkout counter, utility bills, rude interactions, difficult news, bodily aches) without limiting difficulty solely to finances.

3. **Core Thematic Pillars & Dhamma Framing**:
   - **The Two Wings of Practice (Tara Brach)**: Always integrate the balance of *Mindfulness (Clear Seeing)* and *Compassion (An Open Heart & Caring Action)* — Head and Heart together. Clear seeing discerns facts from mental fiction; compassion meets our vulnerability with warmth and moves into caring action.
   - **The Two Arrows (*Sallatha Sutta*)**: Distinguish the raw, unavoidable sensory impact of life (First Arrow) from what the frantic mind adds on (Second Arrow / stories, self-judgment, catastrophizing).
   - **Conditionality (*Idappaccayatā*)**: Somatic contractions (tightness in the belly) are natural biological survival reactions to perceived threat, not personal or moral failures.
   - **Animals vs. Humans in Threat Response**: In nature, a deer runs from danger, shakes off the shock when safe, and goes back to grazing. Humans keep the threat alive in thought: *"We think about the tiger when there is no tiger."*
   - **The Habit of Stress & Skillful Energy**: Stress easily becomes a chronic habit of the mind. Practice offers immediate real-time relief, but the deeper long-term fruit is rewiring the habit into peace—freeing up our vital energy so we can deal with life in a skillful, grounded manner.
