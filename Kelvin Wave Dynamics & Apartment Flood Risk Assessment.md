## 2. Physics & Mathematics of Coastal Kelvin Waves

When equatorial Kelvin waves generated in the central Pacific strike the Americas, they bifurcate and propagate poleward along the eastern boundary of the Pacific as **Coastally Trapped Waves (CTWs)**:

$$
\eta(x, y, t) = \eta_0 \, e^{-x / R_D} \, \cos(k y - \omega t)
$$

### Key Physical Parameters:
* **Offshore Trapping Scale ($R_D$)**: The wave amplitude decays exponentially offshore with distance $x$, governed by the internal Rossby deformation radius:
  $$R_D = \frac{\sqrt{g' H}}{f} \approx 20\text{ to }50\text{ km}$$
  *(The wave energy remains strictly confined against the California coastline and propagates directly into the Golden Gate entrance).*
* **Northward Phase Speed ($c$)**: Coastal internal/baroclinic Kelvin waves travel northward along the Central California shelf at:
  $$c \approx 1.5\text{ to }3.0\text{ m/s } (130\text{ to }260\text{ km/day})$$
* **Maximum Sea Surface Height Anomaly ($\eta_0$)**: Empirical satellite altimetry and tide gauge records show that even during the most severe "Super El Niño" episodes on record (1982–83, 1997–98, 2015–16), the sustained sea level anomaly caused strictly by coastal Kelvin waves peaks at:
  $$\Delta \eta_{\text{Kelvin}} \approx +0.8\text{ to }+1.2\text{ ft } (+25\text{ to }+35\text{ cm})$$
