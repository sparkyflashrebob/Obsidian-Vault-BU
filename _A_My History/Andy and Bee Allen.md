The band of trout eaters in the Northern Paiute nation is the ==**Agai-Dicutta**== (also spelled _Aga'idökadö_, _Agai Ticutta_, or _Agai Diccutta_). [[1](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/App/InterimCommittee/REL/Document/22628), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_Lake_Paiute_Tribe_Reservation)]

In the Northern Paiute language, _Agai-Dicutta_ literally translates to **"Trout Eaters"**. Historically, Northern Paiute bands named themselves after the most abundant food source or geographical feature in their territory. [[1](https://www.wrpt.org/environmental/), [2](https://www.celebratingnevadaindians.com/uploads/2/4/9/8/24985563/cni_13_n._paiute_student_booklet.pdf)]

Key Facts About the Agai-Dicutta:

- **The Name:** Their name originates from their reliance on the **Lahontan cutthroat trout**, a massive salmon-trout species that lived and spawned in their territory. [[1](https://www.celebratingnevadaindians.com/uploads/2/4/9/8/24985563/cni_13_n._paiute_student_booklet.pdf)]

- **Homeland:** Their traditional ancestral homelands center around **Walker Lake** (known natively as _Agai Pahnunadu_) and the **Walker River** in mid-western Nevada. [[1](https://www.facebook.com/groups/lasvegasnative/posts/10164822349694275/), [2](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/App/InterimCommittee/REL/Document/22628)]

- **Modern Recognition:** Today, they are federally recognized as the [Walker River Paiute Tribe](https://www.wrpt.org/), headquartered in **Schurz, Nevada**. [[1](https://www.wrpt.org/environmental/), [2](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/App/InterimCommittee/REL/Document/22628)]

Other Notable Fish-Eating Bands

Because the Northern Paiute people lived across the Great Basin's lake and wetland systems, they had other bands with similar fishing-based names: [[1](https://www.facebook.com/groups/artifactnation/posts/1081191802432249/), [2](https://www.celebratingnevadaindians.com/uploads/2/4/9/8/24985563/cni_13_n._paiute_student_booklet.pdf)]

- **Kuyui Dükadü (_Cui-ui Eaters_):** Located around Pyramid Lake, they were named after the _Cui-ui_, a large prehistoric sucker fish endemic to the lake. Because of a common translation quirk, some historical texts occasionally generalized them as "trout eaters" too, though "Cui-ui Eaters" is their specific name.

- **Agaipaninadökadö (_Summit Lake Fish Eaters_):** A band located around Summit Lake in northern Nevada.
