- Category: #articles
- Document Note: In the Cūḷasuññatasutta, a mendicant is taught to regard states of emptiness as genuine, undistorted, and pure, focusing on the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness, earth, infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, and neither perception nor non-perception. These states are understood to be empty of what is not there, and what remains is seen as present. In the end, the mendicant is freed from the defilements of sensuality, desire to be reborn, and ignorance, understanding that the six sense fields dependent on this body and conditioned by life are the only thing that is not emptiness.
