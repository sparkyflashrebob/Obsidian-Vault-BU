- IIT was first formulated by Giulio Tononi in 2004. It’s meant to quantify the consciousness that exists in any system, based on the total information that is brought together by its constituent parts. Other theories of consciousness tell more of a general story about how the brain generates consciousness; IIT gives it a mathematical expression. Controversially, IIT also suggests that there is consciousness in systems that we wouldn’t usually think of as being aware, including photodiodes and thermostats. Anil Seth, a neuroscientist who is not himself opposed to IIT, nonetheless described it to me as “kind of a bonkers theory.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hbhghgfx413j1h6fstdhtres))
- n 2020, a consortium of scientists and philosophers [secured $3.2 million](https://www.templetonworldcharity.org/projects-database/adversarial-collaboration-test-predictions-first-order-and-higher-order-theories) from one such donor, the Templeton World Charity Foundation, for a series of “adversarial” experiments. The idea was for proponents of different theories of consciousness to design experiments using neuroimaging technology, and to agree, in advance, which results would support which theory. In June of this year, they [announced](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.23.546249v2) the first results from a showdown that pitted IIT against the theory that consciousness arises from something called a “workspace” that brings together information from the senses and other cognitive processes into a coherent whole and broadcasts it to other neural regions. The outcome was largely unsatisfying. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hbhgjt44e698nmczvdgesn3t))
