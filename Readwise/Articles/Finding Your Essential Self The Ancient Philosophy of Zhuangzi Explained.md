- Document Note: Zhuangzi was a Chinese philosopher who lived around the 4th century BCE and is credited as the author of the ancient Taoist masterpiece, the Zhuangzi. He maintained that language was the foremost barrier in our search for our essential selves, and that we must be disorientated by language to discover a higher knowledge beyond words. He was a critic of the dominant and demanding ideals of his time, known as the Hundred Schools of Thought. He argued that we must resist committing to any one specific worldview by 'emptying the container of the Self', and that we must accept what is beyond our control with equanimity. His most celebrated parable is the 'Butterfly Dream', which encourages us to break free of the normal and human way of understanding the world. He believed that we can find our essential selves when we are open to our vast capacity for imagination and creativity.
