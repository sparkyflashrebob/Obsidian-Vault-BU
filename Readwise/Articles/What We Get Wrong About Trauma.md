- • Not everyone who experiences major trauma develops psychiatric conditions.
  • Low-grade, cumulative trauma can lead to worse outcomes than major trauma.
  • Tiny 't' trauma if often ignored, stigmatized, and invalidated.
  • By attending to the full spectrum of trauma we can help build resilience in an uncertain world. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x11yssvmr4q9hyfex15vr))
- Major trauma, what we call Big T Trauma, such as [childhood](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/child-development) [sexual abuse](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/sexual-abuse), leads to diagnosable mental health issues in approximately [30-50%](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3097048/) of people who experience these significant events. This means at least half of those who experience Big T Trauma do not have poor mental health outcomes. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x1pmrchhh5xyw64ztkzjs))
- Therefore, Big T Trauma cannot, in itself, explain the huge rise in increasing levels of [anxiety](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/anxiety) and [depression](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/depression), particularly in areas like the USA. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x1zwa3tcrs8byqq97dfnj))
- Yet issues such as [microaggressions](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/microaggression), challenging familial relationships, [toxic positivity](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/toxic-positivity), and medical [gaslighting](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/gaslighting) are so often ignored. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x311yacmmztf2j1zzm5cr))
- It's easy to forget over time that Post-[Traumatic](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/trauma) [Stress](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/stress) Disorder ([PTSD](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder)) was only entered into the [Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders in 1980](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5371751/), and its entry was very contentious indeed. Before this, those who experienced major psychological distress from Big T Trauma were very much ignored and excluded from the healthcare they so required. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x3f14bt3avcf2bkpf7903))
- Our knowledge and understanding of trauma and mental health is an evolution, and with the rise in psychological distress, we must evolve our practice and understanding of tiny t trauma and offer support to those experiencing the entire wide range of life's challenges. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x3v1wr7kf9bhae10513rt))
- Those who were less impacted by Big T Trauma often cite that they weathered other, perhaps "smaller" difficulties in life, and by experiencing these tiny t traumas they were able to build [resilience](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/resilience) and coping mechanisms. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x4t3wh8f1brrbvdrd4vrf))
- By attending to tiny t traumas, we can develop coping skills for challenges in the future. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x4zb292s4s107582xejjt))
- However, research shows that low-grade, cumulative trauma can result in [worse health outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14744522/) than acute, Big T Trauma. This is why it's important to understand tiny t trauma, and validate all lived experiences. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hk8x5g8ef4z4n4dntwmfg657))
