- A passage from a Buddhist text was read aloud, drawn from a chapter described as covering the human condition under the themes of old age, sickness, and death.

- The passage featured King Yama, identified as the lord of death, questioning a man who had lived with bad conduct in body, speech, and mind.

- The first divine messenger described in the text was the image of a person aged 80, 90, or 100 years old, frail, bent, and visibly deteriorated, presented as a sign that all people are subject to old age.

- King Yama's questioning established that the man had seen this messenger but had failed to reflect on his own mortality and act accordingly.

- Negligence was named as the core failure, with King Yama stating that the man's evil deeds were his own responsibility and that no other person, including family or friends, could bear the consequences on his behalf.

- The second divine messenger addressed the reality of illness, again asking whether the man had witnessed sickness in others and whether it had prompted him to reflect on his own vulnerability.

- The third divine messenger was the sight of a corpse one, two, or three days dead, described as swollen, discolored, and festered, used to confront the man with the inevitability of death.

- The reading concluded with the observation that all the evidence of impermanence is present in everyday life, yet people remain in a state of denial.
