### CALCHA Middle-Income Housing Program Failure in Larkspur

- The California Community Housing Authority, known as CALCHA, was formed as a mechanism for cities to issue tax-free bonds to purchase existing apartment complexes and convert them into middle-income affordable housing.

- Properties purchased under the program are exempt from property taxes, which was intended to reduce operating costs and enable below-market rents.

- In 2020, CALCHA and the city of Larkspur purchased a large complex called Serenity at Larkspur near the ferry terminal for more than $226 million, funded entirely through borrowed bond proceeds.

- The rental formula used by the program sometimes produced rents higher than prevailing market rates, resulting in only 15 to 16 percent of units being leased to middle-income tenants, far below the targets outlined in the bond documents.

- After five years, the debt exceeded revenues and the complex was sold to Pacific Multifamily Investors at a loss of more than $50 million, with the property subsequently rebranded as Hitch at Larkspur.

- The new owner is a market-rate landlord with no restrictions on rent levels or tenant selection, meaning the affordable housing mission was entirely abandoned.

- Larkspur Mayor Stephanie Andre acknowledged that the city took on more debt than it should have and that the structure of the program was poorly understood by the cities and counties that joined it.

- Key contractual changes were never brought back to the city for review or approval, leaving municipalities with little enforcement power or oversight.

- Andy connects the story to a broader point about rent increases of 30 percent and annual raises of 5 percent outpacing inflation, framing it as relevant context for housing advocacy communications.
