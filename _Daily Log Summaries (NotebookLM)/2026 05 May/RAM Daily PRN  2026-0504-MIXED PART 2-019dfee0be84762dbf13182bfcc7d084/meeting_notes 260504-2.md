### Trauma Seminar at the Noetic Sciences Center
- A week-long free trauma seminar hosted by the Institute of Noetic Sciences, an organization focused on the intersection of science and spirituality located in California, was completed during the first week of retirement.
- The seminar was led by British author Alex Howard, whose book "It's Not Your Fault: Why Childhood Trauma Shapes You and How to Break Free" formed the basis of the program.
- Trauma was framed through Howard's ECHO Model, which identifies events, their context, the resulting homeostatic shift in a person's being, and the long-term outcomes that then act as a filter for all future experience.
- Howard's REST Model was presented as a practical healing framework covering recognizing one's system state, examining how that state is being created, stopping unhelpful thought patterns, processing underlying emotions, and transforming one's relationship with self and body.
- A parallel was drawn between the REST Model and the mindfulness-based RAIN practice, noting the conceptual overlap between the two frameworks.
- Participant comments throughout the seminar revealed a recurring pattern of people holding onto the harmful behavior of others, particularly absent or addicted parents, without reaching awareness of how it continues to affect them.
- Awareness was identified as the essential first step in trauma healing, as no meaningful change is possible without first recognizing what is happening internally.
- Full notes and recordings from the seminar are available and were offered to be shared, along with a packaged summary document.

### Insight Timer App and the Concept of Triggering
- Frustration was expressed about the Insight Timer app's tendency to push users toward courses, challenges, and intention-setting features rather than providing straightforward access to the timer function.

- A nuanced view of triggering was shared, distinguishing between external events that may be intentionally provocative and the individual's responsibility for how they respond.
- The space between stimulus and response was identified as the domain of personal agency, regardless of whether the triggering event was accidental or deliberate.
