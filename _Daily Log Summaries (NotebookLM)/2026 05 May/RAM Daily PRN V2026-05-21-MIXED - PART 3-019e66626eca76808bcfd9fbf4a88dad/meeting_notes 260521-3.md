- The show is inspired by the poetry and spiritual transformation of the great Persian mystic Rumi and his encounter with Shams of Tabriz.

- Rather than following a conventional narrative structure, the experience unfolds through music, movement, and symbolic environments.

- Audiences are guided through a series of spiritual states including longing, encounter, loss, and illumination.

- Poetry is performed primarily in Farsi by master vocalist Ustad Shahram Nazeri, with original music composed by Lola Ramin Torquayon.

- Non-Persian speakers are not expected to understand every word, as the work is designed to be experienced through sound, rhythm, and feeling, similar to opera or sacred music.

- Subtitles are available for those who wish to follow along, though attendees are equally encouraged to relax and let the music carry them.

- A full breakdown of the narrative, along with translations and contextual notes for the poetry, is included in the program for those wishing to explore more deeply.

- After the experience, a lobby area offers special edition memorabilia including a collectible book, a vinyl record of the soundtrack, and apparel connected to the show.

- The opening spoken word piece references a lineage of people described as strung like graves fallen from a vine, invoking themes of memory, endurance, and collective identity.

- A recurring poetic line references breath, placelessness, and the idea of originating from beyond a fixed location, reinforcing the show's themes of spiritual transcendence.

- Sheldon reflects positively on the visual elements of the show, noting the beauty of rotating imagery and the way the art and music were synchronized in a trickling, layered fashion.
