### Tai Chi Breathing Techniques and Their Benefits

- Conscious breath awareness is maintained throughout all Tai Chi movements, with the instructor emphasizing slowing and deepening the breath even during less intensive motions.

- Breath is directed intentionally to specific areas of the body, with the pace of movement matched to the rhythm of breathing.

- Deeper and slower breathing is described as opening up circulation and enhancing the function of organs including the heart, lungs, and cognitive systems.

- The impact of breath on the mind, body, internal organs, and emotions is highlighted as a foundational principle of the practice.

- Post-movement breathing is emphasized as needing to be lower and deeper, particularly after more physically demanding sequences.

### Tai Chi Movement Instruction and Body Alignment

- Participants are guided through a series of movements involving weight shifts, knee alignment, heel stepping, and arm positioning with palms facing specific directions.

- Proper foot alignment is corrected during the session, with the instructor noting that toes pointing outward at a diagonal angle places the knee in a suboptimal position.

- Full benefit of the movements is described as being achieved only when the knees and feet are properly aligned during weight shifts.

- Movements are adapted to individual comfort levels, with modifications offered such as shifting the knee forward and back instead of lifting the heels.

- Techniques are named and sequenced, including a movement referred to as Moon Fast and Shine, involving arm raises, weight shifts, and finger positioning toward the ceiling.

- Centerline alignment is reinforced throughout, with participants reminded not to extend arms or shoulders beyond the body's central axis.

- The principle of applying high-level Tai Chi concepts to basic stepping and weight-shifting movements is explained as integral to the practice.

- Roundness and circularity are identified as key qualities to maintain, with the image of holding a large soft ball between the palms used to guide arm positioning.
