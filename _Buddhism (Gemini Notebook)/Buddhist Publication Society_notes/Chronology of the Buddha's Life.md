# Chronology of the Buddha's Life

Here is a timeline of the Buddha's life, drawing on the information in the sources provided:

Based on the Pali Canon, the life of the Buddha Gotama is the point where Indian history as a record begins, replacing archaeology and legend \[1\].

\*   \*\*Approximately 563 BCE:\*\* \*\*Birth of the Buddha\*\* \[2\]. The first event commemorated by Vesak is his birth \[3\]. He was born in the sixth century B.C. in what is now Nepal, the son of a king ruling over a small state in the Himalayan foothills \[4\]. His given name was Siddhattha and his family name Gotama (Sanskrit: Siddhārtha Gautama) \[4\].
\*   \*\*Approximately 534 BCE:\*\* \*\*The Renunciation of the House Life\*\* \[2\]. Accounts of his life describe him renouncing a life of luxury \[5\].
\*   \*\*From age 29 (at the time of Renunciation) for six years:\*\* The struggle for Enlightenment \[1\]. He undertook ascetic practices during this period \[6\].
\*   \*\*At age thirty-five:\*\* He attained his complete enlightenment at Uruvelā in the Ganges plain, known as the "Middle Country" \[1\].
\*   \*\*Approximately 528 BCE:\*\* \*\*The Enlightenment\*\* \[2\]. The struggle to attain enlightenment had lasted six years \[1\]. Enlightenment at the foot of the Bodhi-tree is a key event in his life \[6, 7\].
\*   \*\*After the Enlightenment for forty-five years:\*\* He wandered from place to place in central India, constantly explaining the Four Noble Truths that he had discovered \[1\]. This period covered his preaching ministry \[8\]. The Canon provides a chronological account of the period up to the Enlightenment and the last year \[9\]. The Commentaries of Ācariya Buddhaghosa place more canonical stories in order down as far as the twentieth year after the Enlightenment \[9\].
\*   \*\*At age 80:\*\* He entered Parinibbāna \[8\].
\*   \*\*Approximately 483 BCE:\*\* \*\*The final Parinibbāna (Death)\*\* \[1, 2\]. The Parinibbāna also occurred after his preaching ministry of 45 years \[8\].

The information presented here, particularly the principal dates, is drawn from sources that compiled material from the Pali Canon \[9-12\]. Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli's "The Life of the Buddha According to the Pali Canon" is composed entirely from texts from the Pali Canon and is cited as a source for life stories of the Buddha \[13-27\]. While later accounts may contain more miraculous happenings and embellishments, the earliest accounts in the Pali Canon are considered to be the historical core \[5, 28\]. The Mālāḷaṅkāravatthu, a late Burmese work, is mentioned as dating some canonical incidents but is noted as probably having no real historical authority \[9\].
