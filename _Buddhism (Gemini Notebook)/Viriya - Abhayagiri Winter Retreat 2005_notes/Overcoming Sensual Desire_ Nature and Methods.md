# Overcoming Sensual Desire: Nature and Methods

Based on the sources, sensual desire (also referred to as sensual lust, lust, desire, or craving for sensual pleasures) is identified as a significant obstacle and an unwholesome state that requires diligent effort to overcome.

Here's what the sources say about dealing with sensual desire:

\*\*Understanding the Nature and Consequences of Sensual Desire:\*\*

\*   Sensual desire is a \*\*hindrance\*\* \[1-3\] and a \*\*taint\*\* \[4\] or \*\*blemish\*\* \[5\]. It is also described as a \*\*shackle in the heart\*\* \[6\] and an \*\*imperfection of the mind\*\* \[7\].
\*   It is an \*\*unwholesome state\*\* that arises and, if not abandoned, can lead to harm \[8-10\].
\*   When one dwells with a mind obsessed and overwhelmed by sensual lust, one neither knows nor sees what is good for oneself, for others, or for both \[1, 2\]. It prevents hymns (presumably teachings) from recurring to mind \[1, 2\].
\*   Sensual pleasures provide \*\*little gratification, much suffering, and much despair\*\*, and there is \*\*great danger in them\*\* \[9, 11, 12\]. They are described as impermanent, hollow, false, deceptive, illusory, the prattle of fools, Mara's realm, Mara's domain, Mara's bait, and Mara's hunting ground \[13\].
\*   Unarisen taints of sensual desire can arise, and arisen taints can increase, when one attends to \*\*things unfit for attention\*\* \[4\]. Improper attention is a nutrient for lack of mindfulness and clear comprehension, which is a nutrient for lack of sense restraint, which is a nutrient for the five hindrances, including sensual desire \[3\].
\*   Sensual desire \*\*obstructs a noble disciple in training\*\* \[13\] and being not free from lust for sensual pleasures is a \*\*shackle\*\* that prevents the mind from inclining to ardor, devotion, perseverance, and striving \[6\].
\*   Someone "inflamed by lust for sensual pleasures" may miss the goal of asceticism \[14\].
\*   Clinging motivated by delight in form, feeling, perception, volitional formations, or consciousness (which can be sensual) leads to the entire mass of suffering: aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair \[15, 16\].
\*   Consciousness engaged with these elements \*\*with a sprinkling of delight\*\* comes to growth, increase, and expansion \[17\].
\*   Those strongly disposed to lust, hatred, and delusion may experience a \*\*painful mode of progress\*\* in practice \[10\].
\*   Lust for sensual pleasures is one of the \*\*five shackles in the heart\*\* that need to be severed \[6\].
\*   Desire comes from having \*\*preferences\*\* \[18\] and arises when people think of one thing as \*\*pleasant and another as unpleasant\*\* \[19\]. It also originates from \*\*perception\*\* \[20\].

\*\*Methods for Dealing with Sensual Desire:\*\*

The sources present a variety of approaches, from direct countermeasures to foundational practices:

1.  \*\*Abandoning Sensual Thoughts:\*\* When unwholesome thoughts connected with desire arise, one should \*\*abandon, remove, or do away with them\*\* \[20-22\]. This is part of right effort \[21, 22\]. Specific techniques for abandoning these thoughts include:
    \*   Giving attention to \*\*some other sign connected with what is wholesome\*\* \[23, 24\].
    \*   \*\*Examining the danger\*\* in those thoughts, recognizing they are unwholesome, reprehensible, and result in suffering \[24\].
    \*   Trying to \*\*forget\*\* those thoughts and \*\*not giving attention\*\* to them \[24, 25\].
    \*   Giving attention to \*\*stilling the thought formation\*\* of those thoughts, which can be accomplished by inquiring into their cause \[25\].
    \*   Using a forceful method: with teeth clenched and tongue pressed against the roof of the mouth, \*\*beating down, constraining, and crushing mind with mind\*\* \[26, 27\].

2.  \*\*Abandoning Lust/Desire Itself:\*\* The teaching emphasizes the \*\*removal of desire and lust\*\* for form, feeling, perception, volitional formations, and consciousness \[9, 12, 17, 28, 29\].
    \*   This abandonment is praised because dwelling amidst unwholesome states brings suffering in this life and a bad destination after death \[9\].
    \*   Abandoning lust for the five elements (form, feeling, perception, volitional formations, consciousness) \*\*cuts off the basis and support for the establishing of consciousness\*\*, leading to liberation \[17\].
    \*   Giving up desire and lust for someone is explicitly mentioned as a way to prevent the arising of sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair caused by seeing them with another person \[30\].

3.  \*\*Cultivating Opposite States and Wise Attention:\*\*
    \*   Cultivating \*\*non-ill will\*\* is part of right intention \[20\]. While primarily for ill will, this indicates the general approach of cultivating the opposite of unwholesome states.
    \*   Cultivating the \*\*Seven Factors of Enlightenment\*\* (mindfulness, investigation of dharma, energy, rapture, tranquility, concentration, equanimity) based on seclusion, dispassion, and cessation is key to abandoning the five hindrances \[21, 31\].
    \*   \*\*Wise attention\*\* is crucial \[3, 4\]. Attending to things \*\*fit for attention\*\* leads to the abandonment of arisen taints and prevents the arising of unarisen taints, whereas unwise attention causes them to arise or increase \[4\].

4.  \*\*Developing Mindfulness and Concentration:\*\*
    \*   Developing the four \*\*foundations of mindfulness\*\* \[22, 32\], particularly \*\*mindfulness of the body\*\*, is presented as a way to prevent Mara (representing defilements like sensual desire) from finding an opportunity or support \[33, 34\].
    \*   \*\*Clear comprehension\*\* is an aspect of right mindfulness \[22\] and its lack is a nutrient for improper attention which feeds hindrances \[3\].
    \*   Developing \*\*concentration\*\* is essential \[22\]. Secluded from sensual pleasures and unwholesome states, one enters and dwells in the first jhana, which is accompanied by rapture and happiness born of seclusion \[22, 35\]. This attainment is where unwholesome intentions, including sensual desire, cease without remainder \[20\].
    \*   Developing concentration allows the mind to become pliable, workable, lucid, and well concentrated for the destruction of taints \[36\].

5.  \*\*Understanding the Danger and Seeking Higher Pleasure:\*\*
    \*   Clearly seeing the danger in sensual pleasures with proper wisdom \[9, 11, 12\] helps in abandoning them.
    \*   Attaining the \*\*rapture and pleasure apart from sensual pleasures\*\*, apart from unwholesome states, provides a more excellent and sublime happiness \[11, 35\], making one no longer attracted to sensual pleasures \[11\]. This higher pleasure is associated with renunciation, peace, tranquility, and the cultivation of understanding \[37\].

6.  \*\*Withdrawal and Non-Engagement:\*\*
    \*   Living \*\*bodily and mentally withdrawn from sensual pleasures\*\* is necessary for the full abandonment and suppression of desire and the capability for knowledge, vision, and supreme enlightenment \[27, 38\].
    \*   Training oneself to perceive the attractive in the unattractive and the unattractive in the attractive, and to \*\*reject both and abide in equanimity\*\*, helps prevent lust (and hatred) from arising towards their respective objects \[23, 39\]. This is a method to change habitual emotional attitudes \[23\].

In summary, dealing with sensual desire involves recognizing its harmful nature and consequences \[1, 2, 4, 8, 9, 11-13\], understanding its origins \[18-20\], employing active strategies to abandon related thoughts and the desire itself \[9, 12, 17, 21, 23-30\], cultivating virtuous states and wise attention \[3, 4, 21\], developing mindfulness and concentration \[20, 22, 32-35\], seeing the danger in sensual pleasures while experiencing higher forms of happiness \[11, 35, 37\], and withdrawing from sensual engagements \[27, 38\] by modulating attention to prevent habitual responses \[23, 39\]. It requires consistent effort and application of different techniques depending on the situation \[23-26\].
