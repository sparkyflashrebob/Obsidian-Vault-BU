# Abandoning Ill Will and Grudges in Buddhism

Based on the sources, addressing issues such as \*\*ill will\*\* and \*\*grudges\*\* is an important aspect of Buddhist practice, as these are considered \*\*unwholesome states\*\* \[1-3\], \*\*defilements\*\* \[4, 5\], \*\*imperfections of the mind\*\* \[4, 6\], \*\*hindrances\*\* \[7-9\], \*\*taints\*\* \[5, 10, 11\], \*\*blemishes\*\* \[12, 13\], and \*\*shackles in the heart\*\* \[14, 15\].

These states are seen as leading to \*\*harm and suffering\*\* \[2, 16\], obstructing wisdom \[3\], causing difficulties, leading away from Nibbana \[3\], hindering concentration and insight \[7, 8, 17\], resulting in unhappy destinations \[4, 18\], and making the mind intractable .

The teachings provide various methods and advice for dealing with ill will and grudges, emphasizing the need for \*\*effort\*\* \[6, 19\] and \*\*skill\*\* \[5, 19\]:

\*   \*\*Specific Ways to Remove a Grudge:\*\* If a grudge arises towards any person, the sources detail five ways to remove it \[20\]:
    \*   Cultivate \*\*loving kindness\*\* towards him \[9, 20-22\].
    \*   Cultivate \*\*compassion\*\* towards him \[9, 20-22\].
    \*   Cultivate \*\*equanimity\*\* towards him \[9, 20-22\].
    \*   Pay \*\*no attention\*\* to him and give \*\*no thought\*\* to him \[20\].
    \*   Apply the fact of \*\*ownership of Kamma\*\* to that person \[20\]. This means understanding that the person is the owner of their actions, the heir of their actions, sprung from their actions, and that whatever they do, good or bad, they will be the heir to that \[20\].

\*   \*\*Abandoning Unwholesome Thoughts:\*\* When evil, unwholesome thoughts connected with hate (or ill will) arise \[23\], one should abandon, remove, or do away with them \[2, 3, 11, 23\]. This effort to abandon arisen unwholesome states is part of \*\*right effort\*\* \[19\]. Different approaches are suggested \[23\]:
    \*   Give attention to \*\*some other sign connected with what is wholesome\*\* \[2, 23\].
    \*   \*\*Examine the danger\*\* in those thoughts \[3, 23\].
    \*   Try to \*\*forget\*\* those thoughts and do \*\*not give attention\*\* to them \[20, 23\].
    \*   Give attention to \*\*stilling the thought formation\*\* of those thoughts \[23\].
    \*   With teeth clenched and tongue pressed against the roof of the mouth, \*\*beat down, constrain, and crush mind with mind\*\* \[23\].

\*   \*\*Cultivating Opposite Qualities:\*\* Rather than being overpowered by ill will or hatred \[16, 18\], one should cultivate \*\*non-ill will\*\* as part of \*\*right intention\*\* \[24\]. Abandoning ill will contributes to having a mind without ill will \[21, 25\]. The practice of cultivating \*\*loving kindness, compassion, altruistic joy, and equanimity\*\* (known as the Brahma Viharas) is described as developing liberation of mind \[9, 21, 22, 26\]. One dwells pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with these qualities \[9, 21, 22\], which are seen as vast, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, and without ill will \[21, 22\]. It is taught that \*\*hatred is never allayed by further acts of hate; it is allayed by non-hatred\*\* \[27\].

\*   \*\*Wise Attention:\*\* Dealing with taints, including ill will, is for one who knows and sees \[10\]. \*\*Wise attention\*\* (skillful reflection) is crucial; when one attends wisely, unarisen taints do not arise, and arisen taints are abandoned \[10\]. Unwise attention causes unarisen taints to arise and arisen taints to increase \[10\]. Understanding the need to apply wise attention is present even in methods like restraining and avoiding \[28\].

\*   \*\*Understanding Origin and Cessation:\*\* Unwholesome intentions, such as ill will, originate from \*\*perception\*\* \[29\]. The cessation of unwholesome intentions can occur when one is secluded from unwholesome states and enters upon and abides in the first jhana \[29\].

\*   \*\*Abandoning Blemishes:\*\* Anger and bitterness towards companions, arising from perceived slights regarding offenses or obtaining requisites, are described as a "blemish," a term for the spheres of evil, unwholesome wishes \[12, 13\]. Abandoning these wishes leads to the mind being undefiled \[12\].

\*   \*\*Training and Development:\*\* The holy life requires abandoning five wildernesses in the heart, including being angry and displeased with companions \[14, 15\]. Cultivating wholesome states and dealing skillfully with unwholesome ones requires putting forth effort \[19\]. Abandoning ill will and other unwholesome states is necessary to be capable of enlightenment and Nibbana \[6\]. The path to cessation of unwholesome states involves zeal for their non-arising and abandoning, and zeal for the arising and maintenance of wholesome states \[29\].

In summary, the sources present ill will and grudges as significant obstacles to spiritual progress, offering a combination of direct countermeasures like cultivating specific positive emotions and applying wise reflection on the consequences of thoughts and actions, alongside broader practices like developing concentration, mindfulness, and understanding the nature of phenomena \[3, 7-10, 17, 20-23, 29\].
