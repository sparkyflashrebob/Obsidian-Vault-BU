# AA Versus NA: Scope of Addiction

The biggest difference between Narcotics Anonymous (NA) and Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) lies in

the scope of the addiction they address

#### Here's a breakdown of this key distinction:

#### AA's Focus:

Alcoholics Anonymous is a program of recovery from alcoholism, specifically addressing addiction to alcohol \[4-8\].

#### NA's Broader Scope:

Narcotics Anonymous broadened the perspective of the Twelve Steps to be

all-inclusive with respect to any mood-changing, mind-altering substance

\[1\]. NA explicitly states that "Alcoholism is too limited a term for us; our problem is not a specific substance, it is a disease called addiction" \[1\]. This means that in NA, alcohol is considered a drug, and complete abstinence from all drugs is required for recovery \[3, 9\].

#### This fundamental difference in defining the problem leads to other aspects:

#### Identification and Inclusivity:

Many addicts were unable to identify with alcoholics in AA, finding that their identification was at the level of

apparent symptoms

rather than the deeper emotional or feelings of addiction \[10\]. NA was formed to address this, aiming to provide a fellowship where any addict, regardless of the drugs used, race, religious beliefs, sex, sexual preference, or financial condition, is welcome and can practice the NA way of life \[11, 12\].

#### Relapse Prevention:

The NA literature highlights that thinking of alcohol as different from other drugs has caused many addicts to relapse \[3\]. This reinforces NA's stance that the core issue is the disease of addiction itself, not just the specific substance.

While both fellowships share core principles like the Twelve Steps, mutual support, and spiritual growth \[8, 9, 13-16\], the foundational distinction of

what constitutes the "addiction"

is paramount in defining their respective communities and approaches to recovery.
