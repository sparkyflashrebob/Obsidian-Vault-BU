Compassion, gratitude, and abundance

The first precept—refraining from killing—extends beyond physical harm. It invites us to cultivate compassion toward all living beings.

Practising empathy and kindness counters anger and

https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/anger

, both powerful sources of stress. Compassion

The second precept—avoiding stealing—applies not just to possessions but also to time, trust, and intellectual property. Living honestly fosters integrity and strengthens relationships, reducing interpersonal tension. It also lessens the

Ethical relationships

The third precept—avoiding sexual misconduct—promotes

By acting mindfully in relationships, we cultivate healthier attachments,

emotional intelligence

The fourth precept—truthfulness—encourages honesty in thought and speech. Deceit and half-truths create

cognitive dissonance

Mindfulness and mental clarity

The fifth precept—avoiding intoxicants—protects our ability to think clearly and manage emotions.

Staying clear-headed supports

emotional regulation

Buddhism and modern therapy

The five precepts of Buddhism can complement contemporary therapies like cognitive behavioural

(CBT) and acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT).

Truthfulness supports CBT’s focus on challenging distortions. Compassion aligns with ACT’s emphasis on acceptance. Mindfulness and self-discipline reinforce healthier coping habits.
