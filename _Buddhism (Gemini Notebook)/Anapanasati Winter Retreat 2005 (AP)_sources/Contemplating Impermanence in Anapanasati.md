#### Significance and Results:

#### Seeing the conditioned nature of things:

Part of the insight into impermanence is recognizing

things are conditioned \[5, 8\].

#### Insight into Arising and Ceasing:

The insight that "whatever has a nature to arise has a nature to cease" is specifically mentioned as the characteristic insight of

entering the stream of Dharma

, shifting from the worldly realm to transcendence and liberation \[5\]. This insight into arising and ceasing also inherently includes the insight into non-self \[8\].

#### Freeing Quality:

Recognizing the arising and ceasing nature of things is described as

very freeing

, particularly in seeing through the feeling of an independent "me" trying to control things, which is the source of suffering \[8\].

#### Relationship to Earlier Stages:

The prior stages of Anapanasati (mindfulness of body, feelings, mind, concentrating the mind) lay the essential foundation of peace, mindfulness, and stability \[1, 2\]. This settled and clear state of mind is necessary to be able to discern things in their true nature, which is required for investigating impermanence \[23\]. Dealing skillfully with hindrances and cultivating positive feelings like rapture and happiness also contribute to the clarity and stability needed for insight practice \[24-29\].

In essence, contemplating impermanence in Anapanasati is a direct application of insight into the changing, uncertain, and conditioned nature of existence, using the breath and one's own experience as the immediate field of investigation. This practice naturally leads to a sense of dispassion, helps cultivate a state of non-desiring, reveals the lack of a stable self, and paves the way for the cessation of suffering and ultimate liberation.
