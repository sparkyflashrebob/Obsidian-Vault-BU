#### Anapanasati as the Framework and Vehicle:

Mindfulness of Breathing is the speaker's chosen "stick," the framework and anchor for practice \[3-6\]. It's a "vehicle," a "method," a "skillful means" to experience the mind more clearly \[7\].

The practice involves attending to the breath, starting with knowing the short and long breaths clearly (

) \[8, 9\]. It progresses through stages (tetrads) involving the body, feelings, mind, and mental objects (Dhammas) \[10-14\].

The simple act of breathing, when attended to and trained with, transforms into a meditation that provides an opportunity for growth \[15\]. The whole scope of Anapanasati involves applying attention using the body, feelings, mind, and Dhammas as foundations \[16\].

#### Mindfulness Functioning within Anapanasati:

Anapanasati is where we

cultivate this mindfulness

\[3, 5\]. The breath serves as the anchor or focal point for learning to develop, sustain, and cultivate this quality of attention \[5\].

Mindfulness provides the

base and firm foundation in attention

that is necessary for dealing with hindrances \[22\].

The deliberate use of the breath within Anapanasati, particularly attending to calming and settling the body/mind, is presented as a primary condition given by the Buddha for helping to

alleviate restlessness

, one of the hindrances \[23\]. This calming allows happiness to arise \[23\].

The awareness generated through Anapanasati, particularly by "filling, steeping, pervading" the body with awareness of the breath, helps to

dispel dullness and sloth and torpor

\[24\]. Moving awareness through the body energizes both body and mind, allowing for stable settling \[24\].

Mindfulness is needed to identify hindrances ("Yeah. This is the hindrance of sensual desire") and recognize when they are

there \[25, 26\]. This recognition gives a clear path forward \[26\].

Anapanasati, providing the anchoring ground of the breath, allows awareness to return when the mind gets caught up in disturbing thoughts like ill will, aversion, anger, desire, or attachment \[6, 16\].

As the mind settles in concentration using the breath, mindfulness is needed to shift attention from the sensation of the breath itself to the subtle feeling or presence of the breath, and eventually to the knower, the mind itself \[7\].

The practice of Anapanasati, when permeated with mindfulness, needs direction and a point \[1\]. This direction ultimately leads towards

relinquishment

, contemplating abandoning defilements and self \[1, 28\]. Mindfulness of breathing gives a "window into liberation" \[29\].

In essence, the sources depict Mindfulness (

) not merely as passive awareness, but as an active, energetic, guarding, and investigating faculty. Anapanasati serves as the primary vehicle and training ground for cultivating and applying this essential quality, providing the necessary stability and clarity to work with disturbances (hindrances) and progress towards deeper states of concentration and liberating insight, ultimately allowing one to recognize and relinquish defilements and suffering \[2, 6, 7, 16, 30\]. The breath is the anchor; mindfulness is the skill applied to that anchor and to the arising phenomena within the practice, enabling the realization of the Dharma \[15, 19, 31\].
