#### Relationship with Feelings and Perceptions:

The practice requires skillful engagement with both pleasant and unpleasant feelings and the perceptions associated with them \[5, 6\].

With painful or unpleasant feelings, the calming practice involves recognizing that the issue isn't necessarily the physical sensation itself, but the perception that adds suffering or agitation \[6, 7\]. One needs to discern the difference and use the experience as an opportunity to cultivate wholesome qualities or to return to a neutral sensation \[6\].

With pleasant feelings, there's a need for alertness to prevent the mind from drifting or losing its sharpness by associating the pleasantness with ease or lack of effort \[7\].
