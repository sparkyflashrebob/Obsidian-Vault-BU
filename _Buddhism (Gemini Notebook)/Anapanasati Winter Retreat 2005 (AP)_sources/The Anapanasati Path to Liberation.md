# The Anapanasati Path to Liberation

Mindfulness of Breathing, or Anapanasati, is presented in the sources as the

backbone of practice

\[1\]. It is described as an

essential, important, and necessary

practice for spiritual well-being and the well-being of the community \[2\]. The practice forms a

very stable and concentrated

foundation that allows one to develop calmness, focused attention, and the ability to contemplate and use discernment and wisdom to understand what holds us in suffering and how to free ourselves \[3\]. It is described as a

complete practice

that starts simply but keeps expanding and becoming more subtle and profound in its application \[4\].

The core instruction for Anapanasati is to

turn attention to the formal practice of meditation

, using a specific technique to develop and sustain attention \[2\]. The breath is the chosen object or anchor for this practice \[1, 2, 5, 6\]. The main thing is a sense of

knowing clearly (paññatti)

what the breath is doing, whether it is short or long, coming in or going out \[7\]. This clear knowing starts by being attentive to the sensation of the breath, for example, at the tip of the nose, noticing if it's cool, warm, restricted, or flowing freely \[7\].

While the tip of the nose is a common point of attention, the abdomen is also widely used \[5\].

tip of the nose

offers the opportunity to hone attention on subtle sensations and cultivate one-pointedness \[7, 8\].

provides a more clear object due to the physical movement of rising and falling \[5\]. It can be helpful for settling the mind, especially if the mind is restless and prone to thinking \[9\]. The abdomen is seen as a center of energy or balance, making attention here feel grounding and energetic \[9\].

Challenges in Anapanasati include the mind wandering, becoming dull, sleepy, drowsy, restless, or proliferating \[5, 8\]. Trying to make the meditation a particular way or trying to get results creates a frantic quality and hinders attending to the actual breath \[7, 39\]. The mind needs to be engaged; if not engaged, it becomes dull or restless \[5\].

Mindfulness of Breathing is also explicitly linked to

working with the Five Hindrances

: sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, and skeptical doubt \[40-42\].

These hindrances obstruct well-being, clarity, and peace \[40, 41, 43\].

The first step is to

recognize and identify

them clearly \[40\].

Mindfulness of breathing, by calming and settling the body and mind, is the

primary condition

given for alleviating restlessness \[44, 45\]. Using attention to pervade the body with awareness helps dispel dullness and latitude of mind \[46\]. Bringing energy to the body and posture helps cut through doubt \[47\].

Overcoming hindrances is said to bring a

sense of well-being and happiness

, likened to being freed from debt or safely reaching a destination after a dangerous journey \[40, 44, 48\].

The mind without hindrances is compared to

clear, still water

, allowing one to see clearly \[43\]. Hindrances are like dyes or waves or sediment in the water, obstructing clarity and stillness \[43, 45, 47\].

Ultimately, Mindfulness of Breathing is a skillful means or vehicle to give the opportunity to

experience the mind itself more clearly

\[6\]. The purpose of the whole practice, including Anapanasati, is to create

balance and clarity of mind

The fruit of Anapanasati is not just breathing, but learning how to attend, train, experience fully, and know things clearly, transforming the simple act into a path for growth \[55\]. It allows one to become familiar with peaceful places of the mind and the ability to stop and settle \[36\]. These principles of Anapanasati can be applied not just in sitting meditation, but also in walking meditation and daily life \[54-56\].
