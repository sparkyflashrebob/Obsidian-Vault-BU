# Awareness: Gateway to the Unconditioned

Based on the provided sources, \*\*Awareness, also known as Mindfulness (Sati) or Awakened Consciousness (Buddha/Buddho)\*\*, is presented by Ajahn Sumedho as the absolute core and essence of the Buddhist path, the direct gateway to realizing ultimate reality and freedom from suffering, transcending the realm of conditioned phenomena \[1-12\].

Here's a comprehensive discussion of what the sources say about Awareness/Mindfulness (Sati):

1.  \*\*Definition and Nature:\*\*
    \*   Sati is the Pali word for mindfulness or awareness \[1, 4, 13, 14\]. It is also closely linked with the word "Buddha" or "Buddho" which means "awakened" or "the knower" \[2, 4, 6, 8, 11, 14-21\].
    \*   It is the \*\*ability to awaken\*\*, to be \*\*attentive\*\*, to \*\*notice\*\*, \*\*observe\*\*, \*\*recognize\*\*, \*\*see\*\*, and \*\*reflect\*\* \[2, 4, 6-8, 13, 15, 18, 21-27\].
    \*   It is fundamentally about being \*\*here and now\*\*, paying attention to the present moment \[2, 7, 8, 10, 13, 15-19, 21, 22, 25, 27-36\]. It's about apprehending the point of intersection between the timeless and time \[10, 19\].
    \*   Awareness is \*\*non-intellectual\*\* and \*\*non-thinking\*\* \[2, 9, 14, 24, 25, 29, 37-40\]. It is what is \*behind\* thought or emotion \[14\]. It is recognizing pure consciousness \*before\* or \*while\* thinking occurs, seeing thought itself as an object \[36, 40\]. The thinking mind creates problems and questions but cannot solve them; awareness goes beyond thinking \[15, 24\].
    \*   It is a \*\*natural state\*\* and an \*\*innate ability\*\* that all human beings possess \[4, 11, 25, 28, 39, 41\]. It is not something difficult or complicated that takes years to acquire \[4, 7, 28, 42\].
    \*   Awareness is the \*\*knower\*\*, the \*\*observer\*\*, not the person trying to know \[6, 18, 43\]. It is \*\*impersonal\*\*; the knowing is not personal \[22, 27, 32, 44\]. It is not "my" consciousness \[20, 41\].
    \*   It is \*\*pure consciousness\*\*, an \*\*awakened conscious state of being\*\* \[2, 6, 8, 11, 12, 14, 16, 19-21, 36, 40, 41, 45\]. It is a continuous state with stability \[27, 45\].
    \*   It is characterized by \*\*non-striving\*\*, \*\*relaxed attention\*\*, \*\*openness\*\*, and \*\*receptivity\*\* \[8, 28, 30, 37, 38\]. It is \*\*non-judgmental\*\* and non-critical \[2, 4, 31\].

2.  \*\*The Gateway to the Unconditioned and Reality:\*\*
    \*   Awareness (Sati) is the \*\*path to the Deathless (Nibbana)\*\* \[5, 9\]. It is the \*\*gate to the Unconditioned reality\*\* \[2, 12, 24\]. It is the \*\*only opportunity to be free from suffering\*\* \[3, 7\].
    \*   It is the means to \*\*recognize reality\*\*, the truth, the Dhamma \[2, 8-12, 14, 17, 18, 23, 24, 26, 28, 30, 32, 33, 35, 36, 42, 46-48\]. The Dhamma is not to be defined or described, but recognized and realized through awareness \[14\].
    \*   Awareness allows us to \*\*see conditioned phenomena (Sankaras)\*\* – everything that arises and ceases – for what they are \[7, 11-13, 18, 23, 26, 27, 33, 46\]. This includes our bodies, feelings, thoughts, memories, views, emotions, etc. \[6, 7, 13, 18, 20, 23, 26, 27, 30, 31\].
    \*   It enables the direct perception of the \*\*characteristics of conditioned phenomena\*\*: impermanence (Anicca), unsatisfactoriness (Dukkha), and not-self (Anatta) \[23, 26, 27, 46\].
    \*   Awareness helps us to \*\*understand suffering (Dukkha)\*\* by observing it directly rather than identifying with it \[3, 13, 31, 39, 49\]. By seeing suffering, we gain insight into its causes \[1\].

3.  \*\*Seeing Through the Illusion of Self (Sakaya Ditti):\*\*
    \*   A primary function of awareness is to \*\*recognize the self-view or personality view (Sakaya Ditti)\*\*, which is the created sense of a separate self arising from identifying with conditioned phenomena \[18, 21, 22, 25, 31-33, 35, 41, 48, 50, 51\].
    \*   Awareness helps us see that the sense of "me" or "I am" or "my" is a \*\*creation\*\* from ignorance (Avijja) that is put into consciousness \[20, 35, 40, 41, 50\]. It is not the pure consciousness or awareness itself \[41, 50\].
    \*   The practice is not about destroying the self, but about \*\*recognizing it for what it is\*\* – a conditioned phenomenon \[50\]. By observing feelings, thoughts, views, etc., as they arise and cease, we see them as objects in consciousness, not as "me" or "mine" \[41\]. This observation loosens the attachment to identity \[18, 48, 51\].

4.  \*\*Awareness as Refuge and the Role of Practice:\*\*
    \*   Awareness itself is the \*\*refuge\*\* \[6, 10, 11, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 32, 33, 35, 36, 44\]. Taking refuge in "Buddha" means taking refuge in this awakened consciousness here and now \[6, 11, 14, 16, 19, 20, 35\].
    \*   The practice involves cultivating this \*\*attentiveness\*\* and \*\*trusting\*\* in awareness \[4, 5, 8, 14, 24, 28, 29, 33, 37, 48\].
    \*   We use various objects as anchors for awareness, such as the \*\*body\*\*, the \*\*breath (anapana)\*\*, and the \*\*four postures\*\* (sitting, standing, walking, lying down) \[4, 13, 17, 18, 25, 27, 28, 30, 31, 37, 46\]. These are simple, non-personal points of attention \[17\].
    \*   The practice is to \*\*observe everything\*\* that is present – physical sensations, feelings, thoughts, moods, emotions – with a non-judgmental attitude of "it is like this" \[6, 7, 13, 18, 20, 23, 26, 27, 30, 31\].
    \*   Buddhist teachings, concepts, and practices (like the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, the Ten Fetters, contemplation of the Khandas, Dependent Origination, chanting, taking refuge, Samatha, Vipassana, monastic life) are \*\*skillful means (Upaya)\*\* and \*\*tools\*\* \[10-12, 16, 18, 19, 23, 29, 31, 33-35, 45, 49, 51-53\]. They are not the reality itself, nor are they doctrines to be grasped or believed blindly \[10-12, 14, 15, 19, 24, 26, 29, 34, 35, 38, 43, 47, 53, 54\]. Their purpose is to \*\*point to awareness\*\*, to remind us to wake up and investigate reality directly \[6, 11, 12, 15-19, 23, 24, 34, 45, 51\].

In essence, Ajahn Sumedho's teaching, as drawn from these sources, centers on \*\*cultivating and trusting in awareness (Sati)\*\*. This is not an intellectual exercise but a direct, here-and-now recognition of the natural, impersonal, awakened consciousness itself \[2, 4, 14, 25, 27, 28, 37, 39-41\]. This awareness allows one to observe all conditioned phenomena – the entire realm of change and experience, including the illusion of self – without identifying with them \[13, 18, 41, 50, 51\]. By seeing conditions arise and cease through this lens of awareness, attachment and suffering are relinquished, leading to the realization of the Unconditioned, the Deathless reality that transcends all conditions \[2, 3, 5, 33, 55\]. Traditional Buddhist teachings serve as valuable maps and tools to guide this direct observation and realization \[11, 23, 34, 45\].
