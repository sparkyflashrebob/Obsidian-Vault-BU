# Ajahn Sumedho on Non-Attachment and Nibbana

Drawing on the provided sources and our previous discussion on attachment (Upadana), Non-Attachment (often referred to as Nirodha or cessation) in Ajahn Sumedho's teachings is presented as the \*\*profound solution to suffering and the ultimate reality to be realized\*\*.

Here's a breakdown of what the sources convey about Non-Attachment:

\*   \*\*Non-Attachment as Cessation and Liberation:\*\* Non-attachment is synonymous with the \*\*realization of cessation (Nirodha)\*\*, specifically the \*\*cessation of suffering (Dukkha Nirodha Satcha)\*\* \[1-4\]. It is described as \*\*liberation from suffering\*\* \[5\] and the \*\*total end of death and decay\*\* \[6\]. It is the \*\*third Noble Truth\*\*, which states that there is a cessation of suffering \[1-4, 7-11\].
\*   \*\*Nibbana as Non-Attachment:\*\* The ultimate reality, Nibbana, is defined as the \*\*reality or realization of non-attachment\*\* \[5, 12-14\]. It is described as the \*\*peace that comes from non-attachment\*\* \[13\], a state of \*\*true contentment\*\* \[15\], and \*\*peacefulness\*\* \[3, 15\]. It is not a mystical, high heavenly state, but the reality of non-attachment \[12\].
\*   \*\*The Unconditioned and Deathless:\*\* Non-attachment is connected to the \*\*unconditioned (Dhamma)\*\* \[2, 16-27\] and the \*\*deathless (Amata)\*\* \[6, 8, 18, 23, 24, 28-32\]. There \*is\* the unborn, uncreated, unformed, unconditioned, which means there is escape from the conditioned \[16, 24, 27\]. This unconditioned reality is not personal and is not "mine" \[22, 25\]. Awakening to this unconditioned reality is considered enlightenment \[27\].
\*   \*\*Realized, Not Attained:\*\* A key aspect is that non-attachment, Nibbana, and the deathless are \*\*realized\*\* through direct experience and understanding, they are \*\*not something to be attained or acquired\*\* as a personal possession or state \[7, 13, 18, 23-25, 32, 33\]. The path is described as being \*\*here and now\*\*, pointing always to the present moment, rather than a future goal achieved after much meditation or effort \[3, 7, 8, 10, 31, 34, 35\].
\*   \*\*Non-Attachment vs. Attachment:\*\* Non-attachment is the state \*\*after attachment has been understood and let go of\*\* \[1, 4, 9, 12, 13, 36, 37\]. Before one can realize non-attachment, one must \*\*know what attachment is\*\* \[12, 13, 36\]. Observing and understanding the suffering caused by attachment leads to a \*\*natural movement of letting go\*\* \[13\]. Non-attachment is contrasted with obsession, anxiety, anger, indignation, and jealousy which are signs of attachment \[38\].
\*   \*\*Letting Go is Key:\*\* The way to realize cessation and non-attachment is by \*\*letting go\*\* \[1, 4, 9, 12, 15, 36, 37, 39\]. Letting go is \*\*not destroying, suppressing, or getting rid of\*\* something out of aversion \[1, 4, 9\]. Trying to get rid of things creates more "fire" (suffering) \[1, 9\]. Letting go is described as \*\*allowing things to go\*\* \[9\], \*\*laying aside\*\* \[1\], or \*\*embracing or accepting suffering\*\* \[9, 11\]. This letting go is based on \*\*understanding\*\*, not aversion or fear \[36, 38\]. Letting go of desire, including the desire to get rid of desire (Vibhavatanha), is essential \[9, 36, 39\].
\*   \*\*The Role of Awareness and Knowing:\*\* Realization of non-attachment comes through \*\*awakened awareness (Sati)\*\* and \*\*knowing (Ñāṇa/Puru)\*\* \[3, 5, 8, 10, 11, 17, 18, 20, 21, 24, 25, 27, 29, 30, 35, 39-45\]. Awareness allows one to see conditions, including attachment and desire, as objects separate from oneself \[8, 39, 46\]. Trusting in this awareness, which is not a self, is crucial \[3, 30, 35, 45\]. This knowing is not merely intellectual but a \*\*profound knowing\*\* \[36\].
\*   \*\*Beyond Self-View (Sakaya Ditti) and Concepts:\*\* Non-attachment is deeply connected to the realization of \*\*non-self (Anatta)\*\* \[2, 3, 7, 14, 17, 22, 29, 32, 40, 42, 46\]. It involves \*\*letting go of the illusion of a permanent self or personality view (Sakaya Ditti)\*\* and no longer interpreting experience through the ego \[3, 5, 13, 17, 22, 31, 33, 40, 42, 43\]. Even identifying with spiritual attainments or being a "good Buddhist" is still Sakaya Ditti and an obstacle \[24, 33, 36, 43, 47\]. Furthermore, non-attachment requires letting go of grasping ideas, doctrines, views, and opinions, including those derived from Buddhist teachings or scriptures \[2, 4, 7, 14, 16, 18, 20, 26-29, 32-34, 38, 41, 44, 48-52\].
\*   \*\*Tools for Realization:\*\* The Four Noble Truths are highlighted as a primary tool for investigating suffering and its cessation \[2, 3, 5, 7-12, 16, 27, 31, 35, 36, 44, 47\]. Other tools mentioned include contemplating the Five Khandhas \[19, 22, 25, 32\], contemplating emptiness \[14\], practicing with the sound of silence as a point of surrender/non-attachment \[5, 8, 11, 18, 20, 25, 35, 46\], and taking refuge in Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha as internal realities (awakened awareness, reality itself, those who practice well) rather than external objects of attachment \[8, 10, 11, 21, 23, 24, 41-43, 49, 50, 53\]. These are \*\*skillful means\*\* to be \*used\* for awakening, not something to be attached to themselves \[26, 27, 42, 50, 52\].

In summary, Ajahn Sumedho's teachings, as reflected in these sources, point to \*\*non-attachment as the essential nature of Nibbana and the cessation of Dukkha\*\*. It is not a state to be achieved by forceful suppression but a \*\*reality realized through developing awakened awareness and wisdom\*\*, allowing one to fully understand the nature of attachment, the suffering it creates, and the illusion of a permanent self. This understanding naturally leads to the \*\*moment-to-moment letting go\*\* of grasping, revealing the peace and freedom of the unconditioned here and now.
