Timeline of Main Events

The excerpts primarily focus on Ajahn Sumedho's reflections and teachings on Buddhist practice, rather than a chronological sequence of external events. The timeline below highlights key periods or milestones mentioned in relation to his life and practice, as well as the historical context of the teachings.

c. 563-483 BCE:

The Buddha lives and attains enlightenment in India. He establishes the oldest surviving monastic order.

2550+ years Ago (from the time of the talks, which span 1978-2010):

The Buddha gives his first sermon after enlightenment, the Dhammacakka Sutta. This teaching, including the concept of the Four Noble Truths (dukkha, desire, cessation of desire, the path) and the characteristics of conditioned phenomena (anicca, dukkha, anatta), is a foundational element of the tradition Ajahn Sumedho represents.

#### Prior to becoming a monk (Approx. mid-20th Century):

Ajahn Sumedho is born and lives a lay life in the United States. He is conditioned by American culture, with a strong sense of self and emphasis on independence. He has a skeptical, critical mind and is aware of his weaknesses and inability to live up to his ideals. He has a different name before becoming a monk.

#### First Year as a Sāmaṇera (Novice Monk) (Likely 1966-1967):

Ajahn Sumedho begins to notice the "sound of silence" or ringing sound, a key element of his meditation practice, while being alone for long periods.

#### Early Years of Monastic Practice (Likely late 1960s - early 1970s):

Ajahn Sumedho experiences significant negative states of mind. He also begins to listen inwardly to his thoughts and feelings, including anger and self-criticism, as a form of meditation. He struggles with the ideal of being a "good monk" and the burden of guilt over negative emotions. He discovers the skillful means of consciously allowing and observing hatred. He finds total acceptance of difficult feelings, like apathy and depression experienced in Northeast Thailand, helps them cease. He reflects on the rules of vinaya and his pride in keeping them well. He spends time listening to his mind in Thailand.

#### Time in Thailand:

Ajahn Sumedho lives and practices in Thailand, encountering teachers like Ajahn Chah. He finds himself totally dependent on others as an alms mendicant, which is a shock compared to his upbringing emphasizing independence. He contemplates why the Buddha established such a monastic order. He is taught by Ajahn Chah to recollect his virtues, something he hadn't done before. He stays with a monk on Si Chang island.

#### Late 1970s - Early 1980s:

Ajahn Sumedho gives talks, including the "Asking yourself questions" talk in 1978 and talks on Paramitas and Opinions in 1981, and the Five Hindrances in 1983. He reflects on the importance of humility, determination, and reasonable resolutions in practice, referencing Ajahn Chah's advice against resolving to be a monk for life immediately.

1980s (Talks from 1986, 1989):

Ajahn Sumedho continues to teach and elaborate on the sound of silence as a constant, natural phenomenon and a sign of an open mind. He discusses listening inwardly to the ego and emotional states.

1990s (Talks from 1993, 1996, 1997, 1999):

Ajahn Sumedho continues to teach, referencing concepts like emptiness (anatta), the impermanence of the personality (sakayadi), and the value of inner listening. He discusses the utility of the sound of silence in stopping the thinking process. He teaches "Letting the world end" in 1999. He receives a calligraphic version of the Dhammacakka Sutta from Ajahn Sujito around 1999.

Early 2000s (Talks from 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010):

Ajahn Sumedho continues to teach extensively, covering topics like the nature of desire, dukha, anatta, the sound of silence, the limitations of thought, the practice of mindfulness, the role of monasteries, celibacy, and the discernment of conditioned phenomena. He refers to Buddha Dasabikku and his enigmatic Pali words. He discusses the conventional nature of titles and identities like "Ajahn Sumedho," "British," or "American." He contemplates the best offering he can give to the world as a monk. He discusses the monastic vassa tradition. He reflects on his age (71 in 2005) and having many memories. He talks about preparing to retire (2010).

Cast of Characters

#### Here are the principle people mentioned in the provided sources:

#### Ajahn Sumedho (Luang Por Sumedho):

The speaker and central figure in the excerpts. An American-born Theravada Buddhist monk in the Thai Forest Tradition. He is a teacher and abbot (at some point). He reflects extensively on his own experiences and uses them to illustrate core Buddhist concepts such as anicca (impermanence), dukkha (suffering/unsatisfactoriness), anatta (non-self), mindfulness, awareness, and the nature of the conditioned and unconditioned. He discusses his personal journey, struggles, insights, and his role as a monastic.

#### The Buddha (Gotama):

The historical founder of Buddhism, who lived in India over 2500 years ago. Ajahn Sumedho frequently refers to the Buddha's teachings, the monastic order he established, and the concept of "Buddha" not just as a historical figure but as awakened awareness or the knower of the world (Locu-vidū). He is also referred to as the Taki-dāda.

#### Ajahn Chah:

A highly respected meditation master in the Thai Forest Tradition and Ajahn Sumedho's primary teacher. Mentioned as insisting that Ajahn Sumedho recollect his virtues and advising monks against making lifetime resolutions of monasticism too soon.

#### Buddhadāsa Bhikkhu:

A prominent Thai Buddhist monk and scholar. Mentioned for his monastery (Suan Mokkh in Surat Thani province) and for presenting enigmatic Pali words for reflection, such as "atammayatā."

#### Ajahn Sujito (Venerable Sujito):

A Buddhist monk. Mentioned as giving Ajahn Sumedho a beautifully calligraphed and creative version of the Dhammacakka Sutta, highlighting his generosity and creativity. Ajahn Sumedho seems to refer to him as "Veito" in one instance.

#### Ajahn Panyasaro:

Another monk mentioned by Ajahn Sumedho when discussing the conventional nature of names and identities.

#### Ajahn Natico:

Another monk mentioned by Ajahn Sumedho when discussing the conventional nature of names and identities.

#### Mara (or Satan):

A figure representing temptation, hindrances, and the conditioned realm in Buddhism. Mentioned as relentlessly pursuing even the Buddha after enlightenment, serving as a test until total letting go is achieved.

#### Lay Communities:

Refers to non-monastic Buddhist practitioners who support the monastic order. Ajahn Sumedho discusses the relationship between monastics and lay communities and how the monastic life provides opportunities for generosity and connection.
