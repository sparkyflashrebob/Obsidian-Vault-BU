# The Gateway to the Deathless: Ajahn Sumedho's Teachings

In his talks on the "Gateway to the Deathless," Ajahn Sumedho emphasizes that this gateway is not an abstract concept or a destination to be reached in the future, but rather a direct experience available in the present moment through \*\*awakened awareness\*\* or \*\*mindfulness\*\* \[1-122\].

Here are the key points Ajahn Sumedho raises regarding the Gateway to the Deathless:

- \*\*The Nature of the Deathless (Unconditioned):\*\*
- It is a \*\*fact and reality\*\*, not an abstract philosophical concept, ideal, or vision created through thought \[1, 2, 18, 26, 33, 41, 42, 69, 70, 101, 102, 104, 121, 123\].
- It is described as the \*\*unborn, uncreated, unformed, and unoriginated\*\* \[2, 3, 16, 18, 22, 41, 55, 72, 77, 103, 104, 123\].
- It is \*\*here and now\*\*, an immediate reality, not something to wait for in the next lifetime \[2, 21, 22, 25, 31, 38-40, 42, 50, 51, 55, 69, 70, 73, 74, 76, 77, 88, 91, 92, 101, 102, 121-127\].
- It is \*\*timeless\*\*, transcending the conditioned realm of time, birth, and death \[13, 22, 51, 68, 75, 77, 86, 88, 92, 119, 125, 128-131\].
- It represents \*\*pure consciousness\*\* or \*\*awakened awareness\*\*, which is not personal or owned by anyone \[5, 22, 23, 32-35, 41, 46, 47, 50, 53, 58, 60, 69-71, 75-77, 80-85, 87-93, 95, 99-105, 108-113, 116-119, 121-123, 126, 132-140\].
- It is \*\*beyond definition, description, or conceptualization\*\*; attempting to intellectualize it leads to confusion \[2, 3, 5, 18, 22, 32, 33, 54, 83, 86, 100, 104, 117, 141, 142\].
- It is the \*\*constancy of the supernal mind that needs not move\*\* \[136, 143-145\].
- It signifies \*\*non-attachment\*\* and the \*\*cessation of suffering\*\*, leading to peace and calm \[12, 25, 39, 45, 57, 66, 70, 98, 111, 129, 143, 144, 146-154\].
- It is \*\*not annihilation\*\* or a nihilistic view; rather, it is liberation from the conditioned realm \[16, 27, 52, 68, 85, 90, 104, 136, 143, 148, 155-158\].
- \*\*How to Access/Realize the Deathless (The Gateway Itself):\*\*
- \*\*Mindfulness (Sati)\*\* is the \*\*gate, path, or door\*\* to the deathless, emphasizing direct attention in the present moment \[1, 5-7, 9-11, 13-17, 19, 22, 23, 32-36, 40-42, 45, 46, 50, 51, 58, 63, 65, 69, 71, 72, 74-77, 80-83, 87-93, 95, 99-105, 108-113, 116-119, 121, 122, 155, 159, 160\].
- It requires \*\*letting go of the thinking process\*\* and attachment to thoughts, allowing the mind to stop its endless movement \[1, 2, 4, 22, 50, 77, 80, 83, 86, 92, 100, 101, 104, 116, 123, 125, 161-167\].
- It involves \*\*trusting in one's own awareness\*\* and direct experience, rather than relying on external authority, beliefs, or speculative ideas \[2, 15, 18, 22, 23, 25, 28, 29, 32-35, 37, 38, 40, 42, 44, 47, 51, 53, 58-60, 69-71, 74, 76, 77, 81, 85, 88-90, 92, 93, 95-99, 103, 105, 108, 110-115, 117-122, 124-126, 138-140, 145, 155, 168-175\].
- The practice is \*\*non-judgmental, open, and receptive\*\* to whatever arises in the present moment, without trying to suppress or indulge \[5, 11, 19, 24, 30, 39, 45, 49, 53, 58, 62, 70-72, 83, 87, 89, 92, 96, 97, 99-101, 105, 112, 113, 116, 118, 119, 127, 174, 176\].
- It necessitates \*\*understanding suffering (Dukkha)\*\*, its causes, and its cessation, rather than trying to escape or deny it \[1, 31, 54, 57, 61, 62, 78, 97, 108, 115, 151, 159, 172, 176-179\]. The Buddha's first sermon, the Dhammacakka Sutta, is a perfect teaching containing all one needs to know for enlightenment, focusing on this common human experience \[54, 109, 180, 181\].
- \*\*Death\*\* and the contemplation of impermanence serve as a \*\*gateway to the deathless\*\* \[14, 20, 26, 46, 47, 65-68, 91, 95, 150, 157, 159, 160, 182-184\]. This includes the "death" or cessation of thoughts, emotions, and attachments within the mind \[70, 91, 150, 184, 185\].
- Transcending the \*\*ego, self-view (Sakayadi), and personal identity\*\* is crucial. The self is seen as a created mental object, not a fundamental reality \[17, 20, 23, 32-35, 41, 43, 50, 53, 55, 58, 68, 69, 72, 73, 75-77, 79, 80, 87-89, 92-96, 99-102, 105, 107, 109-113, 116, 120, 121, 132, 134, 137, 138, 142, 145, 146, 157, 161, 167, 169, 171, 172, 174, 176, 186-195\].
- The \*\*Four Noble Truths\*\* are presented as a brilliant, skillful tool for investigation and recognition of reality here and now \[1, 3, 4, 12, 21, 24, 29, 31, 34, 36, 39-45, 47, 54, 56, 57, 60-62, 65, 69, 73, 76, 78, 79, 82, 85, 86, 92, 93, 96-98, 103-105, 108, 110, 111, 115-118, 120, 122, 129, 137, 141, 144, 145, 147, 149, 151, 155, 156, 158, 161, 164, 170-172, 175-181, 183, 185, 195-205\].
- The \*\*Eightfold Path\*\* is the way of non-suffering, developed through right understanding, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration \[40, 43-45, 54, 56, 57, 60, 63, 71, 76, 81, 92, 103, 107, 110, 111, 114, 116, 117, 137, 144-147, 151, 152, 167, 170, 179, 185, 196, 199, 200, 203-207\].
- Ajahn Sumedho often refers to the "sound of silence" as a direct, natural, and continuous reference point for recognizing awareness \[4, 5, 7, 20, 25, 39, 80, 89, 91, 101, 133, 161, 208, 209\].
- \*\*What the Gateway/Deathless is NOT:\*\*
- It is \*\*not a specific physical place\*\* like heaven, nor is it about constructing a perfect external environment \[10, 82, 100, 117, 126, 136, 138, 142, 156, 172\].
- It is \*\*not about destroying conditions\*\* or forcing them away, but rather about understanding and letting go of \*\*attachment\*\* to them \[1, 20, 24, 34, 40, 45, 49, 50, 57, 58, 61-63, 68, 72, 83, 84, 91, 92, 96-98, 100, 108, 111, 118, 119, 143, 147, 151, 152, 158, 174, 176, 191, 210-213\].
- It is \*\*not dependent on belief in God or an eternal soul\*\*; the Buddha deliberately refrained from such metaphysical discussions \[26, 33, 84, 86, 119, 120, 141, 164, 202\].
- It is \*\*not about worldly success, status, or personal achievements\*\*; these are still forms of suffering if grasped \[2, 35, 50, 51, 53, 78, 79, 87, 107, 108, 142, 167, 172\].
- It is portrayed as \*\*not complicated or difficult\*\*, but rather as an ordinary and simple reality that is often overlooked due to being caught in conditioned perceptions \[2, 8, 10, 22, 35, 39, 40, 48, 50, 55, 56, 60, 70, 71, 76, 82, 84, 85, 101, 102, 104, 127, 170, 205, 214\].
