# Echoes of the Path: Narrative Journeys in Recovery

Based on the transcripts, while the teacher (Andy) does the majority of the speaking, participants frequently offer stories about their addiction, trauma, recovery attempts, and meditation experiences.

\*\*Stories of Addiction and Relapse\*\*
\*   \*\*The "Meth Addict Mind":\*\* One participant shared a story about the deceptive nature of the "meth addict mind," describing a woman who managed to hide her addiction from her husband for 18 years, going so far as to hide drugs in the washing machine because she knew how to cook and hide things \[1\].
\*   \*\*The COVID Relapse:\*\* A participant described having 22 months of sobriety and attending five meetings a week. However, when COVID hit, they lost their routine and eventually started using again \[2\], \[3\].
\*   \*\*Playing with Death:\*\* One participant recounted the severe physical toll of their addiction, listing four seizures, two concussions, a subdural hematoma, and liver cirrhosis, describing their behavior as "playing with death" \[4\].
\*   \*\*The Pill Bottle:\*\* A participant shared a moment of temptation when a relative asked them to dispose of a bottle of pills. They opened the bottle and thought about taking one, realizing that doing so would mean losing everything, before stopping themselves \[5\], \[6\].
\*   \*\*The "Fixer" Pattern:\*\* A participant explained a pattern of finding "lost souls" or boyfriends with issues to "save" or fix, realizing they did this to feel better about themselves \[7\].

\*\*Stories of Trauma and Hardship\*\*
\*   \*\*The Car Accident:\*\* A participant shared a traumatic timeline: they got sober, but a month later their father died. A month after that, they rolled their car three times in a horrible accident, leaving them with scars \[8\].
\*   \*\*Childhood Abandonment:\*\* One participant shared a story about their mother telling them their father "stepped out with the Navy" and instructing the child to "save himself" and leave the house when old enough \[9\].
\*   \*\*Medical Shame:\*\* A participant described thinking they were clear of detox, only to wake up having wet the bed due to a UTI. They described the shame and anger of the situation, sitting alone on a patio with their shirt rolled up \[10\], \[11\].
\*   \*\*The Doctor's Look:\*\* A participant recalled a specific moment where a doctor gave them a look that made them feel "less than," causing them to "melt" into fear \[12\].
\*   \*\*Crushed Dreams:\*\* A participant shared a story about wanting to be a nurse, but a counselor shut down the dream because of C-minus grades. The participant gave up for a time but eventually returned to school to get a bachelor's degree in health science \[13\], \[14\].

\*\*Meditation and Visualization Experiences\*\*
\*   \*\*The Samurai:\*\* During a meditation, a participant visualized their "spiritual warrior" as a Samurai protecting a boundary or fortress \[15\].
\*   \*\*Nature Visualizations:\*\* Another participant described a random sequence of visuals during meditation: a ladybug flying, a flying squirrel, and a monkey swinging from branch to branch \[16\].
\*   \*\*"Why Silence":\*\* A participant shared an epiphany they had in the parking lot about writing a book titled \*Why Silence\*, using the acronym "STFU" \[17\].
\*   \*\*Submarine Visualization:\*\* One participant imagined themselves inside a submarine, looking out at their own body functions \[18\].
\*   \*\*The Nature Walk:\*\* A participant mentioned a "nature lady" who took the group on a walk and identified every fern and plant they could eat \[19\].

\*\*Recovery and Life Situations\*\*
\*   \*\*Infatuation:\*\* A participant confessed to becoming infatuated with another client named "Sarah," noting she was sweet and beautiful—different from the "strong, willful women" they usually dated—and realizing this was a distraction from their recovery \[20\], \[21\].
\*   \*\*Family Boundaries:\*\* One participant discussed a difficult family session involving a daughter they described as an abuser, considering uninviting her from future sessions \[22\].
\*   \*\*Writing the Autobiography:\*\* Several participants mentioned the stress of writing their autobiographies for "phase work," with one participant noting they wrote seven pages and had to edit it down to two \[23\], \[24\].
