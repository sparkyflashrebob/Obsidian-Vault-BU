In early Buddhist thought (specifically the famous instruction to Bāhiya in the Bāhiya Sutta, Ud 1.10), "in the seen, there is only the seen" does not bypass perception entirely, nor does it destroy memory. Instead, it stops the automatic escalation from bare perception into conceptual proliferation (papañca), identity, and reactive judgment.

Perception, memory, and judgment can be separated phenomenologically by understanding how experience arises in sequential layers.

Summary of the Cognitive Process

- Bare Contact & Consciousness (Viññāṇa): The eye meets light; visual awareness registers colors and shapes.

- Bare Perception (Saññā): The mind recognizes and labels features (e.g., "red," "moving," "car") by comparing them with past memory traces (saṅkhāra). This happens automatically and is essential for functioning.

- Feeling Tone (Vedanā): The impression registers as pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral.

- Where Judgment Starts (Papañca & Taṇhā): Craving, aversion, or defense mechanisms latch onto the feeling tone, weaving a personal narrative: "I like this," "This shouldn't be here," "They are judging me."

- The "Bāhiya Shift": Stopping at "in the seen, only the seen" cuts the process right after basic perception/feeling, preventing the creation of a "seer" behind the seeing or a narrative of self-reference.

How Separation Works in Direct Experience

- Perception Remains Intact:
	You cannot navigate a room or comprehend language without perception and memory. When you hear "in the heard, only the heard," the auditory sense organ and auditory consciousness still register sounds, and memory still decodes words. What vanishes is the psychological friction and the impulse to project a solid, enduring self into the stream of sound.

- Where You "Get Away" From Judgment:
	Judgment is not embedded in the sensory object; it is an added mental reaction (cetasika). You do not eliminate judgment by suppressing thoughts, but by seeing the judgment itself as just another "heard" or "sensed" mental event arising and passing. The moment a judging thought is observed simply as "thinking occurring" rather than "the absolute truth of who I am," its compelling grip dissolves.

- The Illusion of the Subject/Object Split:
	When attention rests fully at the sensory boundary, there is simply the act of knowing (the seeing, the hearing, the sensing). There is no separate "me" in here receiving a sound from out there. Without a rigid self-center to protect or enhance, judgment loses its functional fuel.

Traditional Frameworks & Perspectives

- Theravāda (Madhupiṇḍika Sutta & Bāhiya Sutta):
	The Buddha describes how dependent on eye and forms, eye-consciousness arises. The meeting of the three is contact. With contact as condition, there is feeling. What one feels, one perceives. What one perceives, one thinks about. What one thinks about, one mentally proliferates (papañceti). The instruction to Bāhiya cuts this chain before proliferation takes root.

- Advaita Vedanta & Non-Dual Awareness:
	Perceptions and memories occur as transient appearances within pure awareness (Sākkṣī or Witness). The error is not the arising of a thought or memory, but the mistaken identification (adhyāsa) with the thought.

- Indigenous & Earth-Based Ways of Knowing:
	Sensory awareness in earth-centered traditions often involves open, direct relational presence—listening to the wind, water, or land without human-centric conceptual filtering. The landscape is experienced directly as living presence before the analytical mind begins categorizing and measuring it for utility.

**Summary**

  

To experience the gap between bare sensation and narrative judgment, practice shifting attention from the _story_ about an experience to the _raw sensory data_ itself. By isolating the immediate physical impression before the mind adds commentary, judgment naturally loses its grip.

**1. The Sound-Listening Practice (The Auditory Doorway)**
	Sound is often the easiest doorway for separating raw perception from mental proliferation. 
	- **Rest in Open Hearing:** Close your eyes and let sound wash over you without seeking it out.
    - **Notice the Raw Tone:** Experience the raw vibration, volume, texture, and rhythm before the mind attaches a label.
    - **Catch the Label Arising:** Notice how quickly the mind steps in with: _"That’s a car," "That’s a noisy neighbor," "I wish it were quiet."_
    - **Drop Back to Vibration:** The moment the mental label or judgment appears, gently drop back into the actual auditory sensation. Treat the mental label itself as just another fleeting "sound" inside awareness.

**2. Tracing the _Vedanā_ (Feeling Tone) Pivot**
	Judgment almost always enters through the narrow doorway of feeling tone (_vedanā_). 
	- **Identify the Contact:** Notice a physical sensation in the body—an ache in the knee, the warmth of the sun, or tension in the shoulders.
    - **Spot the Micro-Second of Feeling Tone:** Is the initial impression pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral?
    - **Watch the Reflex:**
	     - If **unpleasant**, watch how the mind instinctively manufactures resistance, blame, or impatience: _"Why does my back always hurt?"_
         - If **pleasant**, watch how the mind clutches and wants to hold on: _"I hope this peace lasts."_
	- **Rest with the Sensation:** Relax the mental tightening. Let the sensation be a raw physical pattern (pressure, temperature, tingling) without needing to fix, judge, or narrate it.
    
**3. "In the Cognized, Only the Cognized" (Handling Judgment Directly)**
	When judgments do arise, you do not need to fight or suppress them. Doing so simply creates another layer of judgment. 
	- **Treat Thought as a Sixth Sense:** In early contemplative frameworks, the mind (_mano_) is simply a sense organ, and thoughts are sense objects (_dhammā_).
    - **Observe the Judging Thought:** When a thought arises—_"I'm doing this wrong"_ or _"I don't like this person"_—treat it like a bird flying across the sky or a car driving past your window.
    - **Apply the Formula:** In the thought, let there be only the thought. It is simply a temporary event made of language and image, completely devoid of an absolute, permanent self.

**4. The Earth-Based Relational Shift**
	In many indigenous and earth-based ways of seeing, direct observation begins by allowing the senses to be receptive rather than extractive.
	- **Soft Gaze / Wide Peripheral Awareness:** Expand your visual field to the edges of your vision without fixating on a single object.
    - **Sense Before Defining:** Receive the environment as color, light, shadow, and movement first. Notice how this wide-angle, embodied awareness naturally quiets internal conceptual chatter and fosters direct kinship with your surroundings.

 oh, you're saying that feeling tone is inseparable from judgment?

No, feeling tone (vedanā) and judgment are not inseparable—they are distinct events in the mind.

Feeling tone is simply the immediate, automatic reflex of an experience being pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. Judgment is the secondary mental story, reaction, or defense mechanism layered on top of it.

Summary of the Difference

- Feeling Tone (Vedanā): The raw, involuntary flavor of contact (e.g., a sharp pinch feels unpleasant; a cool breeze on a hot day feels pleasant). It is non-conceptual and immediate.
- Judgment (Papañca / Saṅkhāra): The conceptual narrative that evaluates, blames, resists, or identifies with that feeling (e.g., "This pain is terrible," "Why does this always happen to me?", "I must get rid of this").
- The Key Point: Feeling tone happens naturally as long as the nervous system is alive. Judgment is where suffering (dukkha) starts, but that reaction is optional and can be uncoupled.

How They Become Entangled

- The Speed of Habit: Because the mind has spent a lifetime reacting to discomfort with aversion and to pleasure with attachment, the transition from unpleasant feeling to judgmental reaction happens in a fraction of a second. It feels instantaneous, creating the illusion that they are the same thing.
- The "Second Arrow" Dynamic: In the Sallatha Sutta (The Arrow), the Buddha explicitly separates the two. The first arrow is the physical sensation and its raw feeling tone (pleasant/unpleasant). The second arrow is the mental reaction—the judgment, self-pity, blame, or worry. The first arrow is unavoidable; the second is entirely optional.
- The Crucial Pivot Point: Vedanā is the pivot in the chain of Dependent Origination (Paṭiccasamuppāda). The link is: Vedanā (feeling tone) $\rightarrow$ Taṇhā (craving/aversion) $\rightarrow$ Upādāna (clinging/story). If mindfulness is present at the level of vedanā, the automatic progression to judgment and craving stops right there.

Perspectives Across Traditions
- Theravāda Buddhism: An enlightened being (arahant) still experiences physical pain and unpleasant feeling tones, but experiences zero mental proliferation, aversion, or judgment about them. The vedanā is felt completely, yet remains purely "felt."
- Advaita Vedanta: The body and subtle mind register sensations and their basic qualities (pleasant/unpleasant), while the underlying witnessing awareness (Sākkṣī) remains untouched. Judgment only arises when awareness mistakenly identifies as the personal "owner" of the pain.
- Stoicism: Stoic philosophy draws the exact same boundary. An initial impression or physiological reflex (propatheia—such as a sudden startle or the raw ache of cold) is completely natural and neutral. The judgment (dogma or assent) is the decision to label that sensation as an "evil" or a personal injury.
- Indigenous / Earth-Based Knowing: A sudden cold rain or biting wind carries an immediate sensory charge and physical discomfort. In an earth-relational view, this is received as raw communication from the natural world to be adapted to—not an affront or personal failure to be judged.

This process maps directly onto the core teachings of early Buddhism, specifically **Dependent Origination (_Paṭiccasamuppāda_)** and the cognitive breakdown given in the _Madhupiṇḍika Sutta_ (MN 18) and _Bāhiya Sutta_ (Ud 1.10).

The movement from raw physical contact to mental judgment is not a single instantaneous event, but a micro-chain of conditioned stages. Tracing this sequence somatically reveals exactly where unconscious reactivity hooks in—and where freedom (_nibbāna_ / unbinding) is accessible in real time.

### 1. The Step-by-Step Cognitive & Somatic Chain

![[Pasted image 20260826163746.png]]

- **Stage 1: Raw Sense Base & Object (_Rūpa_ / _Āyatana_)**
    
    - **The Mechanism:** An external vibration, wavelength of light, or physical pressure impinges upon an intact sense organ (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, or mind-organ).
        
    - **Somatic Experience:** Pure pre-conceptual physics. In the body, this is raw vibration, pulsation, heat, cold, or mechanical pressure with zero meaning or owner attached.
        
- **Stage 2: Bare Consciousness (_Viññāṇa_) & Contact (_Phassa_)**
    
    - **The Mechanism:** The meeting of sense base, sense object, and respective consciousness (e.g., eye + visual form + eye-consciousness = visual contact).
        
    - **Somatic Experience:** The raw "knowing" that contact has occurred. It is simple registration without categorization.
        
- **Stage 3: Feeling Tone (_Vedanā_)**
    
    - **The Mechanism:** The immediate, involuntary affective charge born of contact: _sukha_ (pleasant), _dukkha_ (unpleasant), or _adukkhamasukha_ (neutral/neither-pleasant-nor-unpleasant).
        
    - **Somatic Experience:** A micro-shift in the nervous system. Pleasant feeling manifests as an energetic opening/ease; unpleasant manifests as a subtle tightening, contraction, or recoil; neutral feels flat or easily ignored.
        
- **Stage 4: Perception & Recognition (_Saññā_)**
    
    - **The Mechanism:** The mind scans its memory bank (_saṅkhāra_ impressions) to identify features, match patterns, and apply a label (e.g., "siren," "knee joint," "critical tone of voice").
        
    - **Somatic Experience:** The perceptual "overlay." The raw sound pattern becomes a recognizable object in awareness.
        
- **Stage 5: Mental Formations & Volition (_Saṅkhāra_ / _Cetanā_)**
    
    - **The Mechanism:** Craving (_taṇhā_) or aversion reacts to the feeling tone. The mind decides what to do with the experience (fight, flee, hold on, ignore).
        
    - **Somatic Experience:** Muscular tension, bracing, shallow breath, or an internal reach/pull.
        
- **Stage 6: Proliferation & Narrative Judgment (_Papañca_)**
    
    - **The Mechanism:** As stated in MN 18: _"What one perceives, one thinks about; what one thinks about, one mentally proliferates."_ The mind spins stories: _"Why does this always happen to me?", "They don't respect me," "I must fix this immediately."_
        
    - **Somatic Experience:** Full-body stress response, clenched jaw, tight chest, mental rumination, and a solid sense of an "I" who is suffering.
        

### 2. Alignment with Dependent Origination (_Paṭiccasamuppāda_)

The breakdown is the exact middle arc of the 12 Links of Dependent Origination:

$$\text{Six Sense Bases} \rightarrow \text{Contact} \rightarrow \text{Feeling Tone} \rightarrow \text{Craving} \rightarrow \text{Clinging} \rightarrow \text{Becoming / Suffering}$$

$$\text{(Saḷāyatana} \rightarrow \text{Phassa} \rightarrow \text{Vedanā} \rightarrow \text{Taṇhā} \rightarrow \text{Upādāna} \rightarrow \text{Bhava / Dukkha)}$$

- **The Inevitable Links:** Sense bases, Contact, and Feeling Tone (_Vedanā_) are karmic results (_vipāka_). As long as you have an alive body and nervous system, they will arise.
    
- **The Breakable Link:** The pivot occurs between **Feeling Tone (_Vedanā_)** and **Craving (_Taṇhā_)**.
    
    - Unmindful processing: Unpleasant $\rightarrow$ Aversion/Judgment $\rightarrow$ Clinging to identity $\rightarrow$ Suffering.
        
    - Mindful processing: Unpleasant $\rightarrow$ Pure observation (_"in the felt, only the felt"_) $\rightarrow$ Craving does not ignite $\rightarrow$ Proliferation ceases.
        

### 3. A Somatic Method for Real-Time Deconstruction

When physical or emotional tension arises, use this sequential inquiry to trace backward through the chain:

```
[ Story / Judgment ]  -->  "This pain is ruining my day; I can't stand it."
         │
         ▼
[ Label / Perception ] -->  "Pain in the lower back."
         │
         ▼
[ Feeling Tone ]      -->  Unpleasant (instinctive micro-contraction).
         │
         ▼
[ Raw Data ]          -->  Heat, throbbing, pressure, shifting tingling.
```

- **Step 1: Notice the Story:** Recognize the narrative commentary and gently name it: _"Story arising, judging arising."_
    
- **Step 2: Drop into the Body (Somatic Grounding):** Bypass the mental story and locate the actual physical sensations in the body.
    
- **Step 3: Isolate the Feeling Tone (_Vedanā_):** Feel the raw unpleasantness without calling it an enemy. Notice it simply as a biological reflex of protection.
    
- **Step 4: Deconstruct into Raw Sensation:** Look closely at the "pain" or "tension." Is it a solid block, or is it made of dynamic micro-sensations—pulsing, warmth, density, movement?
    
- **Step 5: Rest in Bare Knowing:** Allow the raw sensation to rise, shift, and pass within open awareness, leaving no "owner" behind to be burdened by it.
    

### 4. Cross-Tradition Parallels

- **Advaita Vedanta:**
    
    The 5 Sheaths (_Pañcakośa_) describe the same layering: from the physical sheath (_Annamaya_) through energy (_Prāṇamaya_) and lower mind/perception (_Manomaya_), up to intellect/ego (_Vijñānamaya_). The error is _Adhyāsa_ (superimposition)—the Witness (_Sākkṣī_) falsely identifies with the contents of the mental sheath.
    
- **Classical Stoicism:**
    
    Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius distinguish between the raw initial impression (_phantasia_) and the subsequent value judgment (_dogma_ / assent). Physical discomfort is indifferent (_adiaphoron_); distress only begins when the mind assents to the belief that the sensation is an intrinsic evil.
    
- **Indigenous & Earth-Based Perspectives:**
    
    Relational listening treats raw sensory contact with the elements (weather, terrain, animal life) as a direct conversation before the conceptual intellect sections nature into objects for human use. Discomfort is not judged as a flaw in reality, but respected as an instructive physical signpost of the living landscape.

**Summary**

  

This guided contemplation is designed to help you catch the precise, fleeting moment of **feeling tone (_vedanā_)** before it triggers the habit of mental judgment, craving, or aversion.

  

By grounding attention in the body and softening around the primary doorways of experience—breath, sound, and physical sensation—you will practice isolating the raw affective charge (_pleasant, unpleasant, neutral_) and allowing it to simply be felt.

  

### Guided Contemplation Script: Isolating Vedanā

**1. Settling into the Posture and Earth Grounding**

  

Take a moment to establish your seat. Whether you are on a cushion or a chair, feel the direct physical contact between your body and the ground.

  

Let the spine be upright yet relaxed. Soften your jaw, release the muscles around the eyes, and let the shoulders drop away from the ears.

  

Take two or three deep, conscious breaths. With each exhalation, invite your awareness to descend out of the thinking mind and down into the direct, felt reality of the body. Feel the gravity holding you here—steady, supported, and grounded.

  

**2. Meeting the Raw Breath and Sensation**

  

Allow the breath to return to its natural, unforced rhythm.

  

Notice where you feel the breath most clearly—perhaps the rise and fall of the belly, the expansion of the chest, or the cool air at the rims of the nostrils.

  

Rest your attention on the raw physical sensations:

  

- Notice the warmth, the coolness, the tingling, or the gentle movement.
    
      
    
- Don't worry about measuring the breath or changing it. Just let the raw sensation register.
    
      
    

**3. Introducing the Lens of Feeling Tone (_Vedanā_)**

  

Now, bring a quiet curiosity to the immediate _flavor_ of whatever arises.

  

As each breath comes in and goes out, notice the subtle, involuntary feeling tone that arrives with it:

  

- Is this in-breath subtly **pleasant**—offering a sense of nourishment, relief, or energy?
    
      
    
- Is there an **unpleasant** tone—a feeling of constriction, shortness of breath, or fatigue?
    
      
    
- Or is it **neutral**—neither clearly pleasant nor unpleasant, just a quiet, ordinary baseline?
    
      
    

Do not search for high-intensity emotions. Simply catch that immediate micro-taste of the experience.

  

**4. Expanding to Sound and Bodily Sensations**

  

Allow your field of awareness to widen to include ambient sounds and general sensations in the body.

  

- **Working with Sound:** When a sound occurs—a bird outside, a distant motor, a clock ticking—notice the sound wave first, and then notice the immediate tone. Does the nervous system lean in with pleasure, recoil with annoyance, or remain indifferent?
    
      
    
- **Working with Physical Discomfort:** If an ache, itch, or tightness arises in the body, pause before shifting or reacting. Look closely:
    
      
    - Feel the raw physical data: heat, pressure, pulsing, sharpness.
        
          
        
    - Notice the **unpleasant feeling tone**: the biological reflex that says, _"Ouch"_ or _"Discomfort."_
        
          
        
    - Notice that this feeling tone is purely physical information. It is not an enemy, and it is not a personal failure.
        
          
        

**5. Watching the Gap Before Judgment Arises**

  

As you stay with the feeling tone, watch what your mind tries to build on top of it:

  

- When a tone is **pleasant**, notice if the mind instantly starts trying to grasp it: _"I want this quiet to stay."_
    
      
    
- When a tone is **unpleasant**, notice if the mind reaches for the second arrow: _"Why is this ache here? I can't concentrate."_
    
      
    
- When a tone is **neutral**, notice if the mind gets bored and wanders off into thought.
    
      
    

The moment you see the story or judgment forming, gently uncouple it. Say softly in the mind:

  

> _"Feeling tone is here... just pleasant, just unpleasant, just neutral."_
> 
>   
> 
> _"In the felt, only the felt."_
> 
>   

Let the feeling tone be felt completely in the body without needing to agree with it, fight it, or make an identity out of it.

  

**6. Resting in Open, Unreactive Awareness**

  

For the final moments of this practice, simply rest in the open space of knowing.

  

Sensations arise, feeling tones flash and fade, sounds come and go. You do not have to manage any of it.

  

Rest as the spacious, grounded awareness in which all feeling tones are allowed to arise and pass away naturally, leaving no residue and causing no friction.

  

Take one more deep, nourishing breath, feel your connection to the earth beneath you, and when you are ready, gently open your eyes.

**Summary**

  

During a busy workday, the pause between feeling tone (_vedanā_) and habitual reaction doesn't require stopping your work or closing your eyes for ten minutes. It is a **micro-practice of somatic anchoring**—learning to catch the physical tension or surge of energy the moment an unpleasant, demanding, or overwhelming stimulus hits, and resetting the nervous system before the typing, speaking, or reacting begins.

  

**1. The 3-Second "Airbag" (The Physical Pivot)**

  

Whenever an urgent email arrives, a meeting runs off course, or a software glitch happens, your nervous system registers an **unpleasant feeling tone (_dukkha vedanā_)** within milliseconds.

  

- **Catch the Somatic Reflex:** Notice where the body tightens first—jaw clenching, shoulders rising to the ears, holding the breath, or gripping the mouse/keyboard harder.
    
      
    
- **Drop into the Feet/Seat:** Instead of instantly firing off a reply or letting mental frustration spiral, drop 100% of your attention to the contact of your feet on the floor or the weight of your body in the chair.
    
      
    
- **Exhale Before Action:** Take one slow, deliberate exhalation with soft lips or a relaxed throat. That single out-breath acts as a biological "airbag," breaking the automatic jump from unpleasant feeling tone to reactive judgment.
    
      
    

**2. Natural Threshold Triggers (Building Pauses into the Routine)**

  

Rather than trying to remember to be mindful all day, attach the pause to concrete, everyday actions you already perform.

  

- **The Link / Click Boundary:** Before clicking "Send" on a sensitive message or opening a new task, pause for one natural breath. Note: _"Is there an unpleasant rush to get rid of this, or an urge to prove something?"_
    
      
    
- **The Doorway / Transition Reset:** Walking from your desk to the kitchen, stepping outside for air, or switching between browser tabs—use that 5-second transition to check the baseline: _Pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral?_
    
      
    
- **The Meeting Mute Buffer:** Before unmuting to speak or interjecting in a conversation, feel the physical sensation in the chest or throat. Let the raw energy settle so your words come from clarity rather than defensiveness.
    
      
    

**3. Mental Framing: "Signpost, Not Emergency"**

  

The mind often interprets an unpleasant feeling tone during a workday as a crisis that demands immediate defensive action.

  

- **Reframe the Contraction:** Treat the uncomfortable sensation (frustration, time pressure, irritation) as a **signpost**, not a mandate to react.
    
      
    
- **Label the Tone Silently:** Simply note inwardly: _"Unpleasant tone present"_ or _"Pressure felt."_
    
      
    
- **Separate Task from Story:** The task itself (drafting a response, solving a problem, fixing a schedule) is just practical physics and language. The narrative (_"This is ridiculous," "Why can't people be clearer?"_) is the optional second arrow. By feeling the unpleasant tone fully, you free up cognitive bandwidth to handle the task efficiently without emotional residue.
    
      
    

**4. The "Micro-Rest" with Neutral Experiences**

  

Workdays are full of **neutral feeling tones (_adukkhamasukha_)** that we ignore, which causes mental fatigue and dullness.

  

- **Spot the Neutral:** The feeling of holding a warm mug, the steady hum of a quiet room, or the act of walking across the floor.
    
      
    
- **Savor for 5 Seconds:** Consciously bring gentle appreciation to these moments of baseline ease. This resets the nervous system’s threat-detection bias and prevents cumulative burnout throughout the afternoon.
