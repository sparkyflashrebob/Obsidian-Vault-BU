### **[[The Speech of the Mind]]:** 

Ultimately, **"thinking is the speech of our mind"**. How we talk to ourselves internally is deeply shaped by how we have heard others talk, and these external habits of communication gradually colonize our internal dialogue. <font color="#ffff00">IS THIS TRUE?</font> Where does this come from and where is it supported? 

Yes, this is **entirely true and deeply supported** by both classical Buddhist scriptures and contemporary forest tradition teachings within your sources.

### 1. "Thinking is the speech of our mind"

This exact phrasing is a direct quote from **Thich Nhat Hanh**, who writes in _The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching_: **"Thinking is the speech of our mind."** He explains that our thoughts are our inner conversation expressing itself silently, and when we speak aloud, we are essentially giving "earphones" to others to listen to the audiotape running in our heads.

This modern insight rests on a foundational scriptural framework. In the _Cūḷavedalla Sutta_ (Majjhima Nikāya 44), the enlightened bhikkhunī Sister Dhammadinnā defines **"verbal fabrication" (_vacī-saṅkhāra_)**—the active process that constructs our thoughts and speech. She states:

> **"Having first directed one's thoughts and made an evaluation, one then breaks out into speech. That’s why directed thought and evaluation (_vitakka_ and _vicāra_) are called verbal fabrications."**

Commentarial texts explain that these two mental factors are the "mainsprings" of vocal activity because a mind without directed thought (focusing on a topic) and evaluation (examining or commenting on it) is completely unable to make a verbal sound. Thus, your thought process is canonically defined as the direct internal precursor and blueprint of speech.

---

### 2. How Our Inner Conversation is "Colonized"

The assertion that our internal monologue is deeply shaped and colonized by how we have heard others talk is explicitly detailed by contemporary Dhamma teachers:

- **The "Recordings" in the Mind:** In his transcripts on the Eightfold Path, **Thanissaro Bhikkhu** directly states:
    
    > **"The way we talk to ourselves has a lot to do with the way we’ve heard other people talk, and the way we’ve been talking to others."**
    
    He explains that we are constantly exposed to massive amounts of unskillful, negative speech in the world. These external inputs are stored as "recordings" in our memory. When we sit down to meditate, we find ourselves dealing with this very same negativity because those unskillful, habituated voices continue chatter inside our heads.
    
- **The Osmosis of Collective Consciousness:** Thich Nhat Hanh describes this in _Peace Is Every Breath_ as a consumption of the "collective consciousness". He notes that our personal concepts of happiness, manners, and ethics are largely absorbed through osmosis from the majority around us. Even if we have cultivated beautiful ideals, if we spend time in environments dominated by negative communication, we gradually become accustomed to those habits until we are "following the crowd without even realizing it".
    

---

### 3. Healing the Dialogue: The Outer-to-Inner Loop

The sources state that we cannot reprogram our minds simply by forcing ourselves into a vow of silence; the internal chatter does not become skillful just by stopping your mouth. Instead, we must use the **outer-to-inner loop** of Right Speech:

- **External Habits Shape Internal Paths:** Thanissaro Bhikkhu notes that **"your external habits of speaking become part of your internal dialogue"**. By practicing Right Speech externally—abstaining from lying, harsh words, divisive speech, and idle chatter—you actively build the mental clarity, honesty, and restraint required for deep meditation.
- **Maturing Your Thoughts:** When you carefully choose your physical words, timing, and tone with other people, it naturally forces your mind to become more skillful in how it thinks. Utilizing **good examples of outer conversation** serves as the primary tool to help your inner speech mature, transitioning your mental chatter into Right Thinking.
