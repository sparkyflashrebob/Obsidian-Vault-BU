# [[Non-Dual Teachings]]

## Definition 


![[Pasted image 20240921141745.png]]

Non-dual refers to the belief or philosophy that there is no separation or division between the self and the universe. It is the belief that everything is [[interconnected]] and part of a single, unified whole. Non-dual teachings often emphasize the importance of [[transcending dualistic thinking]] and experiencing a sense of [[Readwise/Books/Oneness]] with all of existence. This perspective is commonly found in Eastern philosophies such as [[Advaita Vedanta]] and certain forms of [[Buddhism]].

## [[Advaita Vedanta]]

<p dir="auto">In Advaita Vedanta, non-dual teachings are emphasized through the concept of "advaita," which means "[[not two]]" or "non-dual." This philosophical school of Hinduism, founded by Adi Shankara in the 8th century, posits that there is only one ultimate reality, Brahman, which is the source and essence of all existence.</p>
<p dir="auto">Advaita Vedanta teaches that the individual self, or Atman, is ultimately not separate from Brahman, and that the perception of duality and separation is an [[illusion]]. This realization of the non-dual nature of reality is considered the key to spiritual liberation, or moksha.</p>
<p dir="auto">Practices such as self-inquiry ([[atma vichara]]), meditation, and study of scriptures like the Upanishads are used in Advaita Vedanta to help individuals transcend their limited sense of self and realize their true nature as one with Brahman. The teachings of Advaita Vedanta emphasize that ultimate reality is beyond all dualities, including good and bad, right and wrong, and self and other, and that true understanding comes from transcending these dualities to experience the oneness of all existence.</p>
