Several other foods and nutrients are recognized by nutritionists and researchers for their ability to naturally stimulate GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) secretion:
### High-Fiber Legumes and Pulses
Legumes are among the most effective GLP-1 triggers because they combine high protein with fermentable fibers.
 * **Lentils and Chickpeas:** These are rich in soluble fiber. When your gut bacteria ferment this fiber, they produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) like butyrate, which directly signal the L-cells in your gut to release GLP-1.
 * **Black Beans:** These contain resistant starch (similar to the cooled potatoes mentioned in your previous video), which bypasses the small intestine and triggers satiety hormones in the colon.
### Specific Healthy Fats
Certain fats are more "hormonally active" than others when it comes to appetite suppression.
 * **Extra Virgin Olive Oil:** High in oleic acid, olive oil has been shown in clinical studies to stimulate GLP-1 more effectively than other fat sources.
 * **Avocados:** They provide a unique combination of monounsaturated fats and fiber, both of which contribute to the release of satiety peptides.
 * **Pistachios and Walnuts:** These nuts contain healthy fats and polyphenols that help slow down digestion and trigger the GLP-1 response.
### Lean Proteins
Protein is generally the most satiating macronutrient, and specific types are particularly good at signaling the "fullness" hormone.
 * **Whey Protein:** Often used in shakes, whey contains specific amino acids and bioactive peptides that have a potent effect on GLP-1 secretion shortly after consumption.
 * **Eggs:** Starting the day with eggs has been shown to result in higher GLP-1 levels compared to a carbohydrate-heavy breakfast.
### Polyphenol-Rich Foods
Recent research suggests that certain plant compounds can inhibit the enzyme (DPP-4) that normally breaks down GLP-1, essentially keeping the "fullness signal" active in your blood for longer.
 * **Green Tea:** Contains EGCG, which may help maintain higher levels of circulating GLP-1.
 * **Berries:** Blueberries and raspberries are high in anthocyanins, which improve insulin sensitivity and support the gut-hormone axis.
 * **Curcumin (Turmeric):** Some studies indicate that curcumin can stimulate the L-cells in the gut to increase GLP-1 production.
### Fermented Foods
 * **Yogurt and Kefir:** The probiotics in fermented dairy can improve the health of the gut lining and the population of bacteria responsible for triggering GLP-1 release. Some studies suggest that the fermentation process itself creates metabolites that stimulate these receptors.

### ​2. Cooled Potatoes

- ​**Benefits:** When cooked and then cooled overnight, potatoes develop **resistant starch** [[04:15](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=255)]. This starch acts as a prebiotic, feeding gut bacteria that produce short-chain fatty acids, which in turn trigger GLP-1 secretion [[05:01](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=301)]. It significantly lowers the insulin response by up to 25% compared to hot potatoes [[05:26](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=326)].
- ​**Preparation:** Boil or bake, refrigerate overnight, and eat cold (e.g., in a salad) or gently reheated below 130°F [[05:50](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=350)].

### ​3. Barley

- ​**Benefits:** The richest dietary source of **beta-glucan fiber**, which forms a thick gel in the gut [[06:55](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=415)]. This gel slows glucose absorption and promotes the fermentation of fatty acids that trigger GLP-1, keeping you full significantly longer than white rice [[07:04](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=424)].
- ​**Preparation:** Use pearl barley as a substitute for rice, or add it to soups, stews, and grain bowls [[07:35](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=455)].

### ​4. Apple Cider Vinegar (ACV)

- ​**Benefits:** Contains **acetic acid**, which slows gastric emptying—the rate at which food leaves the stomach [[08:18](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=498)]. This mimics the core mechanism of Ozempic by extending feelings of fullness and stabilizing blood sugar [[08:32](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=512)].
- ​**Preparation:** Dilute 1–2 tablespoons in a large glass of water and drink before your two largest meals [[08:52](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2BMFY0TleU&t=532)].
