Here are the six movements that Japanese elders practice daily, as presented in the "Senior Wellness" source, which are recommended for individuals over 50 to maintain strength, balance, and confidence:


1. **Shikodachi (四股立ち)**: This term is correct. It is a fundamental sumo stance and is widely used to describe this specific posture.
    
2. **Neko-ashi Dachi (猫足立ち)**: The term provided was "Nekawashi Dachi," which is incorrect. The proper term is **Neko-ashi Dachi** (猫足立ち), which literally translates to "cat leg stance."
    
3. **Seiza (正座)**: This term is correct. It literally means "proper sitting" and is a formal way of kneeling in Japan.
    
4. **Zazen (座禅)**: This term is correct. It means "seated meditation" and is a practice central to Zen Buddhism.
    
5. **Kiba-dachi (騎馬立ち)**: The term "Kibodachi" is a common misspelling. The correct term is **Kiba-dachi** (騎馬立ち), which translates to "horse riding stance" or "horse stance."
    
6. **Kabe-sukatto (壁スクワット)**: The term "Wall squat" is a direct English term. While this exercise is used in modern Japanese physiotherapy, its name is a direct Japanese transliteration of the English term. The correct Japanese term is **Kabe-sukatto** (壁スクワット), which combines _Kabe_ (壁), meaning "wall," with the katakana transliteration of "squat" (_sukatto_).
