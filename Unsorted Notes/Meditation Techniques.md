The seven factors of awakening, also known as bojjhaṅga, are mental capacities that are considered part of Buddhist practice and are valued so much that they are called "inner wealth": 
Mindfulness (sati): The first factor of awakening, mindfulness is a non-judgmental awareness of the moment that is developed by contemplating the body, feelings, mind, and mind objects. It is considered foundational for cultivating the other six factors. 
Investigation (dhamma-vicaya): This factor involves investigating the Dhamma as taught in the Satipatthana and Anapanasati Suttas, and other texts. 
Energy (vīriya): Another factor of awakening. 
Rapture (pīti): Also known as joy, this is another factor of awakening. 
Tranquility (passaddhi): This is another factor of awakening. 
Concentration (samādhi): This is another factor of awakening. 
Equanimity (upekkhā): The final factor of awakening, equanimity is a balanced stillness of mind that can accommodate whatever naturally arises.
