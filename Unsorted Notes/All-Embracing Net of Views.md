[[3 Kinds of Wrong View]]
- wrong views with fixes consequences ([[niyatamicchādiṭṭhi)]])
- speculative views ([[diṭṭhigata]])
- personality view ([[sakkāyadiṭṭhi]])

Niyatamicchādiṭṭhi
 - undermine basic principles of morality
 - deny the framework which give meaning and validity to ethical notions
 - ethical nihilism
	 - reject law of karma
- fixed becasue firm adherence to these views is unwholesome karma
	- obstruction to higher rebirths and liberation
- can be rabid and dogmatic
- karma generated sufficient to bring fall from human world to the planes of misery

Diṭṭhigata
-  speculative views
- all metaphysical theories
- religious creeds
- philosophies tenets that lie beyond verification
- not obstacles to higher worlds but impediment to liberation
- Arise out of Personality View
	- fundamental belief in a self or ego-entity that is reckoned separate

WHY DID Buddha Discourage the Inclination to Speculation?
thought to be because:
- Views proceed ignorance and blindness rather than from knowledge
	- subjective distortions
	- proclaim part of the truth to be the whole
	- see Blind persons impression of an elephant
	- lead to conceit
		- extolling oneself and disparagin others who hold different views
	- dogmatic clinging
	- quarrels and disputes
		- thinkers - nations - groups
- maintains movement of the round of becoming
- come from both reasoning and meditative experience
	- great majority from meditation
	- spring from a source more deeply grounded in the human mind
	- source of clinging
		- the fundamental need to establish and maintain some basis of selfhood, personality
		- [[sakkāyadiṭṭhi]]
			- affirming presence of an abiding self
			- 20 ways
				- 5 aggregates
	- crystalize in the [[62 Views]]

> if one begins a practice with a view of self, and persists without changing  
> this view, then whatever develops in the course of practice will go to  
> confirm the initial thesis. The attainments will not themselves alter the  
> view, while the deeper states of consciousness that unfold will be  
> misconstrued in terms of the erroneous notion. Taking the idea of self  
> at its face value, as indicating a real entity, the theorist will proceed to  
> weave around it a web of speculations apparently confirmed by his  
> attainments: as to whether the self is eternal or non-eternal, everlasting  
> or perishable, finite or infinite, universal or individual, etc.  
> 
> What is essential, therefore, from the Buddhist standpoint, is not  
> simply to practice rather than to theorize, but to practice on the basis of  
> right understanding.  
> - Bodhi, Bhikkhu, BP209s - All-Embracing-Net of Views p. 6
>
