In Buddhism, the twelve links of [[dependent arising]] (paticca-samuppada) explain how all phenomena arise in dependence on other phenomena. According to this teaching, name-and-form arises out of ignorance (avijja).

<u>Ignorance is the root cause of all suffering. It is the mistaken belief that there is a self that is separate from the world.</u> This belief leads to attachment and craving, which in turn lead to suffering.

When we are ignorant, we do not see the world as it really is. We see it through the lens of our own desires and expectations. We see what we want to see, and we miss what is really there.

This ignorance gives rise to the five aggregates (khandha), which are the building blocks of our experience. These aggregates are:

- Form (rupa)
- Feeling (vedana)
- Perception (sanna)
- Mental formations (samkhara)
- Consciousness (vinnana)

The five aggregates are impermanent, unstable, and unsatisfactory. They are constantly changing, and they can never give us lasting satisfaction.

When we see the five aggregates as they really are, we see that they are empty of self. There is no permanent, unchanging self that exists behind the five aggregates. The self is simply a collection of impermanent and changing phenomena.
