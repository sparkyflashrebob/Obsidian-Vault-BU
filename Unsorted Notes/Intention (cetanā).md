In Buddhism, **[[cetanā]]** (Pali) or **cetana** (Sanskrit) is a mental factor that is often translated as "intention" or "will." <u>It is the mental factor that gives rise to actions, both physical and mental.</u>

Volition, on the other hand, is a more general term that refers to the faculty of willing or choosing. It is the ability to make decisions and to act on those decisions.

The difference between cetana and volition can be seen in the following way:

- **[[Cetana]]** is a specific mental factor that is involved in the process of making decisions and taking actions.
- **Volition** is a more general term that refers to the ability to make decisions and to act on those decisions. It can be seen as a faculty of the mind, while cetana is a specific mental factor.

In the Buddhist tradition, cetana is seen as an important factor in the process of karma. <u>The Buddha taught that we can control our cetana by cultivating mindfulness and concentration.</u> When we are mindful, we are aware of our thoughts and feelings, and we can see how they lead to actions. When we are concentrated, we are able to focus our attention on the present moment, and we can avoid being carried away by our thoughts and desires.
