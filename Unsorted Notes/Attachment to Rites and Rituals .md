Attachment to rites and rituals (sīlabbata-parāmāso)

_[Śīla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9A%C4%ABla "Śīla")_ refers to "moral conduct", _vata_ (or _bata_) to "religious duty, observance, rite, practice, custom,"[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetter_(Buddhism)#cite_note-26) and _parāmāsa_ to "being attached to" or "a contagion" and has the connotation of "mishandling" the Dhamma.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetter_(Buddhism)#cite_note-27) Altogether, _sīlabbata-parāmāso_ has been translated as "the contagion of mere rule and ritual, the infatuation of good works, the delusion that they suffice"[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetter_(Buddhism)#cite_note-28) or, more simply, "fall[ing] back on attachment to precepts and rules."[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetter_(Buddhism)#cite_note-29)

  

While the fetter of doubt can be seen as pertaining to the teachings of competing _[samana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shramana "Shramana")_ during the times of the Buddha, this fetter regarding rites and rituals likely refers to some practices of contemporary brahmanic authorities.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetter_(Buddhism)#cite_note-30)
