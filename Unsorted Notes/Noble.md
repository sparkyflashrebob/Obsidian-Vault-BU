In Buddhism, the term "noble" (Pali: ariya) refers to those who have attained enlightenment or who are on the path to enlightenment. The Four Noble Truths are called "noble" because they lead to the end of suffering, which is the ultimate goal of Buddhism. The Noble Eightfold Path is also called "noble" because it is the path to enlightenment.

The term "noble" is also used to refer to certain qualities that are associated with enlightenment, such as compassion, wisdom, and non-attachment. These qualities are considered to be noble because they lead to the end of suffering and the attainment of happiness.

In general, the term "noble" in Buddhism refers to anything that is conducive to the end of suffering and the attainment of happiness.
