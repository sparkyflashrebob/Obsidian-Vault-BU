### Highlights & Notes

> The spiritual warrior is not an enlightened one who knows everything, nor a hermit who has left society to seek truths in solitude. It is someone who, living in the chaos of the world, consciously chooses to build a life based on deep values, self-knowledge, and connection to something bigger. Their fight is not with swords or weapons, but with tools such as discipline, introspection and love.

> In each of us there is a calling. It is that feeling that tells us that there is something beyond daily routines. Some perceive it as an emptiness, a desire for change, or a need to find meaning in their lives. This call is the first sign that the spiritual warrior is awakening. However, responding to that call is not easy. The path of the spiritual warrior is full of challenges: facing your own shadows, questioning what you think you know about yourself and the world, and building a life aligned with your deepest values. It requires courage, because it involves leaving behind the comfort of the known and venturing into uncharted territory.

> self-knowledge is the basis for any real transformation,

> The Mirror of Consciousness

> Imagine that your mind is like a mirror that reflects reality. When the mirror is clean, you can see things clearly. But when it's clouded by fear, guilt, anger, or limiting beliefs, that clarity disappears. You see yourself and the world in a distorted way. The spiritual warrior's task is to clean that mirror. This means learning to recognize the "fogging" in your mind and confront them. These foggings can manifest as: ​&bull;​Limiting beliefs: Ideas that we have adopted as absolute truths, even if they are not real. For example, "I'm not good enough" or "I don't deserve to be happy." ​&bull;​Emotional wounds: Past traumas that we have not healed and that affect how we relate to ourselves and others. ​&bull;​Negative automatic thoughts: Thought patterns that are constantly repeated and generate suffering.

> Practice: Conscious observation ​1.​Find a moment of calm: Find a quiet place where you can be with yourself, without interruptions. ​2.​Close your eyes and breathe: Take a few minutes to breathe deeply, focusing on the air that moves in and out of your body. ​3.​Observe your thoughts: Without trying to stop or judge them, pay attention to the thoughts that arise in your mind. Ask: ​&bull;​What am I thinking now? ​&bull;​Does this thought serve me or limit me? ​4.​Record your discoveries: Open a journal and write down what you observed. This record is a valuable tool for identifying patterns over time.

> Exercise: The Encounter with the Shadow ​1.​Identify an uncomfortable emotion: Think of an emotion that you frequently avoid or repress (e.g., anger or sadness). ​2.​Write about it: Answer these questions: ​&bull;​When do I feel this emotion most strongly? ​&bull;​What do I try to avoid when I feel it? ​3.​Accept the emotion: Say out loud, "I accept that this emotion is part of me. It doesn't define who I am, but I can learn from it." ​4.​Look for its message: Reflect on what this emotion may be trying to teach you. For example, anger could signal violated boundaries or injustices that you need to address.

> Practice: Taking off masks ​1.​Make a list of your roles: Write down all the roles you play in your life (child, mother, worker, friend, etc.). ​2.​Reflect on each role: Ask yourself: ​&bull;​Does this role define me or is it just a part of me? ​&bull;​What parts of myself am I sacrificing to fulfill this role? ​3.​Write a statement of authenticity: For example: "I am more than the roles I play. I am a unique and complete being." Read this statement every day to remind yourself of who you are.

> The spiritual warrior does not seek external confrontations, but internal ones. His greatest battle is not with other people, or even with the world, but with his own mind: the beliefs that limit him, the fears that paralyze him, and the ego that disconnects him from his true essence.
