# ‘Compassionate Listening’ Is a Buddhist Tenet: What It Is and Why It Matters

![rw-book-cover](https://images.theconversation.com/files/510571/original/file-20230216-16-fpe4im.jpg?ixlib=rb-1.1.0&rect=54%2C401%2C6099%2C3049&q=45&auto=format&w=1356&h=668&fit=crop)

## Metadata
- Author: [[Yang-Yang Cheng]]
- Full Title: ‘Compassionate Listening’ Is a Buddhist Tenet: What It Is and Why It Matters
- Category: #articles
- Document Note: [[Compassionate listening]] is a Buddhist tenet which encourages a [[shift in focus from talking to listening]], in order to [[overcome egocentricity]] and [[understand the perspectives of others]]. It is informed by Buddhist philosophy and practice, such as Thích Nhất Hạnh's concept of “deep listening”, and involves [[understanding the other person without judging or reacting]], abstaining from false and harsh speech, and [[cultivating humility]] to accept our limited ability to understand. Compassionate listening has wide implications for interpersonal and political communication, as it helps us to better understand problems and address them collectively.
