## Highlights
- The ***Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*** (Pali; Sanskrit: *Dharmacakrapravartana Sūtra*; English: *The Setting in Motion of the [Wheel of the Dhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmachakra) Sutta* or *Promulgation of the Law Sutta*) is a [Buddhist scripture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_texts) that is considered by Buddhists to be a record of the first sermon given by [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buddha), the **Sermon in the Deer Park** at [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath). The main topic of this sutta is the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths), which refer to and express the basic orientation of [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGethin199859-1) in a formulaic expression.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTENorman2003-2) This sutta also refers to the Buddhist concepts of the [Middle Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Way), [impermanence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impermanence), and [dependent origination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prat%C4%ABtyasamutp%C4%81da).
  [![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Sermon_in_the_Deer_Park_depicted_at_Wat_Chedi_Liem-KayEss-1.jpeg/220px-Sermon_in_the_Deer_Park_depicted_at_Wat_Chedi_Liem-KayEss-1.jpeg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sermon_in_the_Deer_Park_depicted_at_Wat_Chedi_Liem-KayEss-1.jpeg)
  [](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sermon_in_the_Deer_Park_depicted_at_Wat_Chedi_Liem-KayEss-1.jpeg)
  Modern Thai depiction of the *Sermon in the Deer Park* at [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath)
  According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha delivered this discourse on the day of [Asalha Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asalha_Puja), in the month of [Ashadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashadha), in a deer sanctuary in [Isipatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath). This was seven weeks after he attained [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism). His audience consisted of five ascetics who had been his former companions: [Kondañña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaundinya), [Assaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assaji), [Bhaddiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bhaddiya(arhat)&action=edit&redlink=1), [Vappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Vaspa_(arhat)&action=edit&redlink=1), and Mahānāma. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzry6mw0jah2hr0a946npfe9))
- *Dhamma* (Pāli) or *dharma* (Sanskrit) can mean a variety of things depending on its context;[[note 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-3) in this context, it refers to the Buddha's teachings or his "truth" that leads to one's liberation from suffering. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzry8yxcg3d3xmhjey1f43gm))
- *akka* (Pāli) or *cakra* (Sanskrit) can be translated as "wheel." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzry970py49wrrepvm8a3hpb))
- The *[dhammacakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmacakra)*, which can be translated as "Dhamma-Wheel," is a Buddhist symbol referring to Buddha's teaching of the [path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path) to enlightenment. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzry9kmt7q6038jhrh2779h7))
- *Pavattana* (Pāli) can be translated as "turning" or "rolling" or "setting in motion."[[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-6) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzry9rvz6h7qdzpyt2g317aw))
- The sutra contains the following topics:[[web 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-accesstoinsight_DS-7)
  • The two extremes to be avoided (sensual indulgence and self-mortification)
  • The [Middle Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Way)
  • The [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths)
  • The [Noble Eightfold Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path)
  • The Twelve Insights of the Four Noble Truths
  • Proclamation of release from the [cycle of rebirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%E1%B9%83s%C4%81ra) (commonly referred to as *[nibbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nibbana)*)
  • The Opening of the Dhamma Eye (the attainment of [right view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/View_(Buddhism)))
  • Proclamation of the [devas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_(Buddhism)) upon the setting of the [Wheel of Dhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmachakra) in motion by the Buddha
  • Response of the Buddha to [Aññā Kondañña's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaundinya) comprehension of his teachings ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzry7zkb1t4e20vehkrxbnvs))
- According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha attained enlightenment and liberation while [meditating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhy%C4%81na_in_Buddhism) under the [Bodhi Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi_Tree) by the Nerañjarā river in [Bodh Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodh_Gaya). Afterwards, he remained silent for forty-nine days. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzrycsrjn65jw6bvxpv2dfjr))
- According to MN 26 and MĀ 204, after deciding to teach, the Buddha initially intended to visit his former teachers, [Āḷāra Kālāma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80%E1%B8%B7%C4%81ra_K%C4%81l%C4%81ma) and [Uddaka Rāmaputta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uddaka_R%C4%81maputta), to teach them his insights, but they had already died and born in a place where it is not apt to preach or they were deaf, so he decided to visit his five former companions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzryd4percdgdga08ysgz0c5))
- On his way, he encountered a spiritual seeker named Upaka. The Buddha proclaimed that he had achieved full awakening, but Upaka was not convinced and "took a different path". ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzryc3szje3n9b1m2pn0krxb))
- he Buddha then journeyed from Bodhgaya to Sarnath, a small town near the sacred city of [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) in central India. There he met his five former companions, the ascetics with whom he had shared six years of hardship. His former companions were at first suspicious of the Buddha, thinking he had given up his search for the truth when he renounced their ascetic ways. But upon seeing the radiance of the Buddha, they requested him to teach what he had learned. Thereupon the Buddha gave the teaching that was later recorded as the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*, which introduces fundamental concepts of Buddhist thought, such as the [Middle Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Way) and the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths).[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTESucitto201010–12-8)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDhamma199722–24-9)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeshe_Tashi_Tsering2005Kindle_Locations_163-169-10)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGethin199825-11)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThich_Nhat_Hanh1991Kindle_Locations_1822-1884-12)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhammacakkappavattana_Sutta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThich_Nhat_Hanh19996–8-13) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gzrye0jnpqw7ya1p8y71qytn))
