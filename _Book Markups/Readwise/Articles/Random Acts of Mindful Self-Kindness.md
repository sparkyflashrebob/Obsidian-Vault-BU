- **Ways you can be kind to your body**
  Treat yourself with compassion, and practice positive [self-talk](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/self-talk) by focusing on your strengths and achievements rather than your shortcomings.
  • Prioritize sleep.
  • Wear comfortable clothes.
  • Speak nicely to yourself.
  • Nourish your body with foods that provide the nutrients it needs to function properly and give you energy.
  • Make time for mindful movement that you enjoy, such as walking, jogging, dancing, or swimming.
  • Take breaks throughout the day to stretch, move your body, and get some fresh air.
  • Practice [mindfulness](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/mindfulness) or deep breathing exercises to reduce stress and promote relaxation.
  • Surround yourself with positive people who support and encourage you.
  • Set realistic and achievable [goals](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/motivation), and celebrate your accomplishments along the way.
  • Listen to your body's signals of hunger and fullness, and eat mindfully by savoring each bite and paying [attention](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/attention) to your body's needs. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h26z60s98dz9gy7g6a7py626))
- **Ways you can be unkind to your body**
  • Pushing yourself when exhausted.
  • Dressing in uncomfortable clothing.
  • Ignoring pain and not seeking medical attention when needed.
  • Not allowing yourself to rest.
  • Comparing your body to other people's.
  • Putting your body in [stressful](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/stress) or potentially harmful situations.
  • Ignoring hunger and fullness cues.
  • Talking negatively about yourself or your body. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h26z66bgp5axdwqpwfevhprs))
