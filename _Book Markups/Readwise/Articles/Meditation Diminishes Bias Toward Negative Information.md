## Highlights
- Meditation Diminishes Bias Toward Negative Information ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21qzwz8n3c2r741reyf1xt8))
- A new study reveals that daily mindfulness meditation can help reduce individuals’ tendency to avoid negative information. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r0cvgbaxxcqdtt7haa1s5))
- The practice of regular mindfulness meditation allows people to better process uncomfortable emotions, thus enabling them to handle negative information more objectively. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r0k56h8dpxfsx3etzrq6v))
- The study concluded that meditation could foster better decision-making since meditators are more likely to consider information, even if it could elicit a negative response. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r0tykxggpatkke94926n9))
- This counteracts the cognitive bias that typically leads individuals to disregard potentially unsettling information. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r0zmmxg08k250r0dhq5f3))
- **Key Facts:**
  1. The study found that daily mindfulness meditation reduces the tendency to avoid potentially negative information, a common cognitive bias.
  2. Regular meditation not only aids in dealing with negative emotions but also facilitates a more objective processing of unfavorable information.
  3. This study suggests that daily meditation could enhance decision-making by encouraging the consideration of a wider spectrum of information, including potentially negative inputs. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r1cfzcbv0ax64j9ghscp1))
- Smart decision makers gather all the pertinent information, and weigh the pros and cons dispassionately. This also includes gathering information that could prove unsettling or unpleasant. At least that’s the theory. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r1yzg7dd75atkcd0an9aa))
- However, the reality is often quite different. Due to what experts call cognitive bias, people tend to ignore potentially negative information—even when such information is available to them. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r23t4my45fpf01q38j4eq))
- For example, they don’t want to find out whether an investment is no longer worthwhile, a medical test has confirmed an illness, or a friend has betrayed their trust. The reason is that even just thinking about negative information triggers fear and worry. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r2jc4z3zy6aftv06v1d73))
- The researchers define the practice of mindfulness meditation as sitting still with eyes closed, observing—but not responding to—breathing, physical sensations, thoughts, and emotions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r302eaaz0ncgsez8eg9ma))
- Meditating for just 15 minutes a day helps people relieve stress, increases their ability to concentrate, cuts their risk of depression, and enhances their productivity. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r39773tfn3fswn85g70ky))
- Like in other studies, Ash and his co-authors were able to show that daily meditation boosts people’s ability to contend with negative emotions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r3f80rwdgf70nfa1yprgs))
- “The study participants who meditated every day for two weeks were better equipped to simply observe their negative emotions and accept them calmly,” Ash says. This positive development was not observed in members of the control group. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r3q987gwdpdd82s171ssb))
- The study’s authors conclude that mindfulness meditation makes people more resilient to uncomfortable emotions, which in turn allows them to process negative information more objectively. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r40tjjz79rkf5xxy9b9wm))
- “Someone who copes well with negative emotions will also want to know what could go wrong as a result of a particular decision,” Ash says. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r46dah533kpt4yt3v74zq))
- In other words, meditation training could help people make better decisions. Since they are more likely to also consult information to which they might react negatively, they are more comprehensively informed. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r4byejh3ksgk0vza6wpb5))
- 261 participants ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r4npdycjxknnd9ssrkf6r))
- One group [meditated every day](https://neurosciencenews.com/web-stories/meditation-psychology-negativity-151) for 15 minutes, while the other spent that time listening to relaxing music. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h21r4y0qtj77x8b82p1x0tpq))
