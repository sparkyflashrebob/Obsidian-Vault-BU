- to withdraw the mind from the automatic responses to sense-impressions, "burn up" the [defilements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleshas_(Buddhism)), and leading to a "state of perfect [equanimity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equanimity) and awareness (*[upekkhā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upek%E1%B9%A3%C4%81)-[sati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sati_(Buddhism))-[parisuddhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purity_in_Buddhism)*)."[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhyana_in_Buddhism#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVetter19885-1) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h4h6qr9wm80yp2r2y7pen40q))
- *Dhyāna* may have been the core practice of [pre-sectarian Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-sectarian_Buddhism), in combination with several related practices which together lead to perfected mindfulness and detachment.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhyana_in_Buddhism#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVetter1988-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhyana_in_Buddhism#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronkhorst1993-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhyana_in_Buddhism#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGethin1992-4) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h4h6qv81gj1x95qqmgbk183e))
- *dhyāna* is equated with "concentration", a state of one-pointed absorption in which there is a diminished awareness of the surroundings. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h4h6r552nxckcf7aj2x253q4))
- In the contemporary Theravāda-based [Vipassana movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vipassana_movement), this absorbed state of mind is regarded as unnecessary and even non-beneficial for the [first stage of awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_stages_of_awakening), which has to be reached by mindfulness of the body and *Vipassanā* (insight into impermanence) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01h4h6rrkc24225pcxcpgbj2wc))
