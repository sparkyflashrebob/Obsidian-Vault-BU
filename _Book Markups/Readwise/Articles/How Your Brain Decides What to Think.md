## Highlights
- How your brain decides what to think ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjnred5fsk94w8v5jbecxr))
- “[default mode network](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.98.2.676)” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjp1gp51cr812snp5h6zsc))
- It’s engaged when we are [daydreaming](https://theconversation.com/explainer-why-daydreaming-is-good-for-you-50227), thinking about ourselves or others, [recalling memories, or imagining future events](https://theconversation.com/daydream-believer-why-your-brain-is-wired-to-wander-18881). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjpcmbtq67babyxtdvrmd7))
- The default mode network becomes engaged when people appear to be doing “nothing” (hence the term “default”). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjpk6c0bm63dmps0ge0mqh))
- This is usually when we are in a relaxed state and not focusing on a task or goal ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjpzhpm1pw5az67m2qh459))
- When the default mode network is engaged, other networks in the brain are down-regulated or become less active, such as the executive control network and other brain regions involved in attention, working memory, and decision-making. This is what allows the brain to wander. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjqdyqg93dfm8zb11kbhvb))
- Some memories are more likely to be spontaneously recalled, such as those that are more recent, highly emotional, highly detailed, frequently repeated, or central to our identity. They capture our attention – and for good reason. These types of memories were likely pivotal for engaging with our physical and social environments at the time, and so helped to contribute to our survival. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjr3j3yr0ex36ty1qys65b))
- memories may be associated with each other through different sensory, emotional and contextual details ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjrpsg8egtxy2yd2p5af3v))
- each of these bits of information can serve as a cue to trigger another memory ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjrwh670hep0bpe7ps6qay))
- Such as when we encounter a smell, sound or image – even if we sometimes don’t consciously know what the trigger was. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjs4mtde8f6hj7exca9tc5))
- much of our cognitive processing happens [without conscious awareness](https://theconversation.com/is-it-rational-to-trust-your-gut-feelings-a-neuroscientist-explains-95086). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjshdza1qakzw2ncphkqkp))
- The brain [holistically and unconsciously](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.4159/9780674045217/html?lang=en) deals with all kinds of sensory information that comes in all at once. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjsqa7emjt3dcs4mn6jzy4))
- As a result, it may feel like we are not in control of our thoughts, but much of this perceived control may be an illusion anyway. It might be that our consciousness is not in control of very much at all, but rather tries to explain and rationalise the [unconscious cognitive processing](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.3.231) of our brains after the fact. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjtb6ghkvxerbh13pb606c))
- In other words, the brain is constantly processing information and making connections between different pieces of knowledge. This means that it’s normal for thoughts and associations to come to mind when our conscious control mechanisms are switched off. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjtvg5gcn23gk9hzs9mqgn))
- the default mode network is what supports [imagination](https://theconversation.com/the-secret-to-creativity-according-to-science-89592) and [creativity](https://theconversation.com/new-study-reveals-why-some-people-are-more-creative-than-others-90065). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjvm2kwf2s17jd3nm659e6))
- The brain was allowed to rest and wander, so it was able to make associations between different bits in memories that our conscious working memory was not able to reach and bring together. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjvyvk1eqyrnd40ty5pcwb))
- Spontaneous thoughts are not always good, however ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjw5yzc0y4ww2tcvtxf0tg))
- [Intrusive memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10087639/) are unwanted memories, that are often vivid and disturbing or at least strongly emotionally charged and can take the form of [flashbacks](https://theconversation.com/the-possible-cause-of-flashbacks-discovered-59105) or [ruminations](https://theconversation.com/why-do-i-dwell-on-the-past-121630). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjwdp6zqk0qxdbfwz7yga6))
- Not only can they bring with them feelings of anxiety, fear and shame, but they can sometimes also consist of disturbing content that the person does not want to remember or think about. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjwsnrta258htj0vn80ys7))
- For example, in [postpartum anxiety and depression](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/mental/child/infant-related-intrusive-thoughts-of-harm/), new mothers can start having intrusive thoughts of harming their infant, without actually wanting to follow through with them ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjx5c3h8x3xhbwf599pv53))
- This is understandably a highly disturbing experience and if it happens to you, please rest assured that such thoughts are unfortunately common. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjxe26hjmt9ckb6ssgs56h))
- [Cognitive behavioural therapy](https://www.ocduk.org/overcoming-ocd/cognitive-behavioural-therapy/) (CBT) can help with techniques to deal with unwanted thoughts. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjxmkngtgaqhrqwtkrz857))
- For all of us though, it’s worth remembering that many thoughts enter our minds seemingly spontaneously and that this is a normal part of human memory and thought processes. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjy3mawqh9p1dedc5yzdm2))
- But by allowing ourselves and our brains to take a rest, we allow it to generate creative thoughts and solutions to problems. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjy962rgq328sj3cyqygfd))
- And when unwanted thoughts pop up, it might be best to take a mindful approach: observe the thought and let it go, like clouds in a passing storm. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01gsvjygf7kq0qqde5kes5bgmr))
    - Note: Mindfulness, clouds of a passing storm
