- What Is the Worst Habit for Inflammation?
  According to Dr. Grayver, poor stress management is the worst habit for inflammation. Why? In part because stress causes the release of the stress hormone known as cortisol. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfay2m7xt9qcyk2bxj0k56y))
- "Cortisol is one of those things that goes and disrupts the inner layer of our vasculature and creates unstable plaque," Dr. Grayver says. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfay8a44s0rtzhdtrv2wysz))
- Wait—isn't plaque a dental problem? Yes, but it's also a cardiovascular issue. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfayatqf7tsvzv36qyzcbz7))
- "It's covered under a little type of hat that sits on top of it that keeps it at bay," Dr. Grayver says. "When there’s inflammation, cortisol is released, it seeps out into our vasculature and it destroys that nice, contained fibrous cap sitting on top of the stable plaque and turns it into unstable plaque." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfaz6ccf6krv2vn015gbd2q))
- "Some stress is normal and allows us to achieve greater goals and create certain things," ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfaznmay2sk3hja4vsf87pz))
- "Severe, chronic stress is what I want to hone in on. That can have a significant impact on heart health in multiple ways.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfazt0zgt21zcvnb4ran3hv))
- "Normal stress has more goal orientation," ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfb01v2baj62tncbrxc34ba))
- "It's not the kind of stress keeping you up at night, leading you to make unhealthy choices or have horrible chronic fatigue." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfb06p7xydzhtmtw86p2b01))
- [Smoking is a big no-no](https://parade.com/health/worst-habit-for-heart-health-according-to-cardiologists) for various reasons, including inflammation related to heart health. "It’s one of the unhealthiest habits," Dr. Grayver says. "The chemicals in the tobacco [are terrible for you]." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfb0h9qbw7q0rrhc59g4t5j))
- Diet can also increase inflammation and the risk for heart disease risk, but Dr. Grayver says figuring out the best one for you can be a challenge. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfb0zbatjgfhqfepgkrs5y1))
- Diets Dr. Grayver recommends include vegan, [DASH](https://parade.com/976803/marysauer/what-is-dash-diet/) and the [Mediterranean Diet](https://parade.com/962840/marysauer/mediterranean-diet-plan/). Each is a bit different—vegan means no animal products, whereas Mediterranean and DASH are less rigid, for example. But the common ties include low sodium and an emphasis on fruits, veggies, nuts and lean—preferably plant-based—proteins. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfb1e4d0102ze5rp72v67np))
- It’s then easy to fall back on unhealthy coping mechanisms, like poor diet choices, alcohol and smoking," she says. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01grfb1sk0k6ewkjqyjx9n6pmm))
- Dr. Grayver says exercise does the mind and body good, in part, by reducing stress. "I’m someone who has never been good at [meditation](https://parade.com/969668/ericasweeney/benefits-of-meditation/), but when I exercise and do breathing during my run, that to me is meditating," Dr. Grayver says.
