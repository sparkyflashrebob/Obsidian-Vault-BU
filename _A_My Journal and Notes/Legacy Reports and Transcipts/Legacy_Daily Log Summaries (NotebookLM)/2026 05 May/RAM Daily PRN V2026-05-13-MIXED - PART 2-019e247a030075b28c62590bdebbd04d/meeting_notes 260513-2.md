- New Consensus has produced detailed policy papers, with one of the most developed proposals being the revival of the Reconstruction Finance Corporation, a state-owned institution that existed during the New Deal and World War II era
- Functioning similarly to a public bank but with a more proactive market role, the RFC would identify gaps in the market that private capital cannot or will not fill and intervene directly
- Specific applications would include directly financing affordable and social housing, spinning off a public developer when no nonprofit partner is available, and stockpiling building materials to control construction cost inflation
- The concept is not novel, as virtually every modern developed country maintains some form of public financing institution of this type

### Bird Survival Behavior During Storms
- Birds detect approaching storms through a specialized inner ear structure that functions as a biological barometer, capable of sensing microscopic drops in atmospheric pressure well before any visible weather changes occur
- Small backyard birds such as sparrows cannot outfly large storm systems and instead shelter in microhabitats such as the dense interior of evergreens, thick hedges, or hollow tree cavities, positioning themselves on the leeward side of structures to avoid direct wind exposure
- A passive tendon locking mechanism in birds' feet causes their grip to automatically clench tight around a branch when crouching, requiring no muscular effort and allowing them to remain attached even if the tree itself is damaged
- Waterproofing is achieved by activating the uropygial gland near the tail, which secretes an oil spread across the feathers using the beak, while simultaneously puffing feathers outward to trap warm air and create a personal thermal layer
- Large coastal birds such as gulls, terns, and pelicans fly directly into the eye of hurricanes, where conditions are calm, and are carried hundreds of miles inside the storm before being deposited in unfamiliar territory when the system dissipates
- Hummingbirds, whose hearts beat over a thousand times per minute and who require constant nectar intake, enter a state of torpor during multi-day storms, hanging upside down, dropping their heart rate to under fifty beats per minute, and lowering body temperature to near ambient levels in a near-death suspended state until conditions improve
