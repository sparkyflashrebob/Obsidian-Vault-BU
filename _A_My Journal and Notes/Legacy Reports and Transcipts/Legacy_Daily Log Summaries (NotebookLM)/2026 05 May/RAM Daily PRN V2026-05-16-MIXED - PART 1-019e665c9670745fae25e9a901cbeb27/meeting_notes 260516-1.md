- Fraud prevention was argued to be achievable by ensuring ballots are only issued to verified eligible voters.
- Voting machine vulnerabilities were raised, including concerns about firmware manipulation at the local level by technically skilled individuals.
- Paper ballots with extensive oversight were proposed as a more trustworthy alternative to electronic machines.
- Digital voting using encryption was proposed as a potentially superior solution, allowing votes to be scrambled and secured.
- A dedicated government-controlled voting app or platform was envisioned, modeled on secure systems like banking or the DMV.
- Voting centers were suggested as a way to assist those unable to use digital devices, such as elderly voters.
- Driver's license data and verified identity information were proposed as the basis for authenticating voters in a digital system.
- Remote voting, such as from abroad, was considered feasible under a properly secured digital identity system.
- Blockchain technology was identified as a relevant encryption method, drawing a parallel to how banks and cryptocurrency secure data.
- Quantum computing was flagged as a future threat capable of breaking current encryption, though the cost of such an attack would be prohibitively high.
- The risk of wealthy or powerful actors spending large sums to manipulate digital elections was acknowledged as a concern requiring preventive measures.
