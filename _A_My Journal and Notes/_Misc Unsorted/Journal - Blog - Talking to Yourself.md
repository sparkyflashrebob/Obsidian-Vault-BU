So this is a response mainly from an Abhayagiri reflection called "Talking to Yourself" by Thanissaro Bhikkhu. 
I received this email on August 7th and actually Andrea mentioned it to me also. 

The theme that he presents is that we largely suffer because of the way we talk to ourselves. 
And his contention is that we don't suffer from what we experience through our senses but because of our commentary about these things. This is really something that we can take a look at in our lives as we experience the world. 
I notice my eyes constantly moving towards things that are attractive and unattractive. And I largely ignore things that are not.  I'm not sure if I'm in control of this part of it. But as part of my mindfulness practice I'm trying to pay attention to this exact process, because, if not this is where I can actually get caught in ways that may not be helpful for me. 
There's nothing wrong with being attracted to things that we like. A lot of it is instinctual and a lot of it also isn't necessarily good or bad. I have heard of people getting old and losing some of their faculties and then their defects of character coming out because at this point in their live their basic being is unfiltered. This can be very hurtful for loved ones who experience us doing this. But really at this point it's out of our control. 
And I'm not very sure, even if I'm very mindful, if this will not be the case for me down the road. I don't want to hurt anybody but I may not have much of a choice. There may be a day where I don't have the faculties to control myself in certain ways. But the reality is being hurt emotionally is mostly as a result of the person taking things personally. I would just be being me at that point. 

Hopefully I can develop things inside of me, to grow spiritually and have my faculties about me, so that I won't hurt anybody.  But I don't know. What if I get dementia and I forget who you are? 

A lot of people take not remembering people's names or details about things very personally. Now this is the other person remembering names and details about you. And if you can remember people's birthdays, their names, and specific facts about people, I think that's great. Not find faulting in that. But I can't help for being how I am.  And even then, I wouldn't accept people faulting me for the way that I am. When I judge I try to personally reflect inward on how I might be so that I can become better and try not to place that judgment on a weakness or defect of others. I have to be my own judge and jury, and I have to look at my own intentions.  

It's so easy in this world to project outward and to criticize the outside world and never look inside ourselves.  To open ourselves to the world is to invite criticism and we need to understand that. But it still doesn't make it right for us to criticize all the time. Yeah if we are having an impact on others then that would be fair. But are we so sensitive that we can't be tolerant of how other people are being themselves? Not saying that we should accept people being racist and sexist and harming others. I'm not saying that at all. But do I have to constantly criticize other people for their other opinions if they're not controversial in these ways?

The gist of his article was really more about looking at how we talk to ourselves, our ongoing commentary. He also notes that the whole of the practice is about learning to talk to yourself in new ways.  If you are looking at what is going on inside of you then that's great. You might get some of what he's talking about.  But I don't thing we look at ourselves as all.  If we do, and  we don't like what we find then we have many defenses against that. It's easy to point the fingers at others and hard to look inward. 

I find that looking at my own habits and defects of character has to be looked at constantly. At the same time I have to forgive myself when I run into them and negatively criticize myself.  I also need to see what mood comes up and other emotions.  I try to keep moving forward and to purify what I can. But the reality is we are not really tested until we are tested. I can say that I'm going to act and behave in a certain way but until that comes up I don't really know. It's just me thinking about it.  It's very easy to come up with things I think that's the way it is. It's another to see, to face things and to see what really comes up.

Something he points out that I think is important is that the Dhamma, which is basically the truth about the nature of things, is not something that's "domesticated". By "domesticated" I believe he is referring to being experience being conditioned to the point that it's rigid.  Like for instance when someone says "jump" and you just jump as high as you can. 

Society has a very fixed way of looking at things. Right and wrong, good and bad.  But it's really about opinions, views and perspectives about things. As a result when we get down to the nitty-gritty, it's really easy to criticize. It's all coming from our own perspective - in the way we think the things should be and should be for everybody.  Our morals and values.  Like we are right and therefore they are wrong and we know better. 

The truth of the matter is that they're really not even looking at the right things -  we are focused on the minutiae - on getting our way and on winning.  We're not looking at the more important things.
How are we relating and treating each other? Are we caring and forgiving and tolerant and helpful? Or are we only self-serving? Are we fair and honest and open-minded? Or do we even care about that? 

Important things are figuring ways to come together so that we can solve society's big needs. There are people who are dying of hunger, of war, and disease and we can't fix any of these things because that is not our priority. It is about getting our own way. 

Let's change the point and look inwardly.  Do we even listen to ourselves? In traditional Buddhist teachings there is what is known as the three defects of a listener.  These defects are: not really paying attention due to our mind being on other things, the second is one when listening not retaining what is said (I'd like to come back to that myself). and lastly, is when our listening is poisoned - when it is biased and where we really don't want to listen in the first place. I believe this internal process of listening to ourselves deep first is of the greatest importance if we care about being better people. Otherwise the greater defects of being entitled, uncaring. and selfish rule us. 

Everything points towards this inner listening because if we can't purify it then everything else outside of us is just contrived and conditioned. And therefore should not be counted upon.  I believe we have a higher calling than this.  I am listening.
