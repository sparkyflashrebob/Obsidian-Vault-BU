**The San Diego Buddhist Center Experience**
One participant shares a detailed story about their past attempt to practice meditation while living in San Diego. They attended a Buddhist temple that hosted open meditation on Tuesdays and applied for a life program there, despite still actively drinking,. The participant describes a specific incident where, after doing laundry, they experienced severe road rage when a neighbor blocked their parking spot. This rage escalated until they went into their apartment and punched a stainless steel refrigerator, swelling their hand,.

The following morning, hungover and injured, they attended a Zen session at the center where the group was assigned yard work. The participant was left with "rusty weed cutters," which they felt was what they "deserved" at that moment. After being told by the leader that they shouldn't be working with their injured hand, and failing to receive comfort from others during a walking meditation, the participant "kind of gave up" and called their girlfriend to pick them up.

**The Delivery to Spirit Rock**
Another participant recounts a specific disappointment regarding expectations of spiritual teachers. While working as a delivery person, they dropped off lumber at Spirit Rock, a well-known meditation center. The participant encountered Jack Kornfield, a famous teacher, and expected him to be "radiating" or "like a Buddha," showing gratitude for the delivery,. Instead, Kornfield did not make eye contact and acted "just like a dude," which shattered the participant's idealized image of him.

**Meditation Breakthroughs and Frights**
Participants also share immediate experiences from the meditation sessions themselves:
*   **The "Soul Pop In":** One participant describes a moment where their mind went cool and blank, and they noticed their "soul pop in". They asked if there was a "higher conscious part" of themselves, after which poetry words came to them "crystal clear". Following this experience, the participant noted they felt clearer and felt "no pressure" when raising their hand to speak.
*   **The Scary Void:** Conversely, another participant shares a frightening experience where, toward the end of a session, everything in their mind "started moving faster and faster" until it "just went black". This startled the participant, making them wonder if it was "dark stuff" rooting inside them coming up.
*   **The Cello:** A participant describes using the sound of a cello (likely from the music played during the session) to manage their "internal dialogue" that was going "a mile a minute". By focusing intently on the cello, they were able to take a break from their constant mental energy.

**Daily Struggles and Resentments**
*   **The Text Message:** A participant admits to "simmering" in resentment for a week regarding a text message they had sent, realizing later that they were suffering because of their own reaction,.
*   **Drugs in the Hallway:** Another individual shares a story about finding a drug on the ground in their hallway. Although they did not use it, they remained angry that the situation occurred and that they had to encounter it.
*   **Incarceration vs. Recovery:** A participant compares meditating while incarcerated versus at the Salvation Army, noting they did more reflecting and thinking while at the Salvation Army because they felt safer and had clothes, whereas in prison, the focus was purely on survival.
