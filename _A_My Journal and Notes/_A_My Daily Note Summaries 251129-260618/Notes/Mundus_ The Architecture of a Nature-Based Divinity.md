Andy uses "Mundus" as his primary philosophical anchor to replace the concept of a personal, micromanaging deity found in traditional religions [1, 2]. **He defines "Mundus" as the totality of nature, the universe, or "the all"—the self-creating processes from which everything emerges and to which everything ultimately returns** [1, 3, 4].

By shifting his spiritual architecture toward a nature-based theology, **Andy deliberately rejects traditional religious structures, specifically Abrahamic and animist belief systems, which he views as carrying the "friction of dogmatic imagery" and "deity-based confusion"** [5, 6]. Instead of believing in a paternalistic God who actively manages outcomes, loves, or hates, he views divinity simply as natural law and an undeniable force [2].

This reframing allows Andy to use "Mundus" to replace traditional beliefs in several practical ways:

* **A New Framework for Surrender:** In his recovery and spiritual practice, turning his will over to "Mundus" means aligning himself with the "right, good, and orderly direction" of nature's laws, providing a tangible framework for spiritual health without relying on religious dogma [5].
* **Shifting Identity:** By viewing God as the universe, Andy breaks free from the conditioning of being a subject to a "micromanager" and instead becomes an active participant in a broader ecological system [2].
* **Acceptance of Mortality:** He embraces his physical existence as a "temporary arrangement of atoms" governed by the laws of physics, viewing his eventual death as a comforting "return to Mundus" [7, 8].

Ultimately, **Andy treats nature as his only religion and his Higher Power, asserting that "God is nature. I am home"** [7, 8]. This allows him to construct a highly personalized spiritual hierarchy—placing Theravada Buddhism first, Stoic practice second, and Mundus as the theological bedrock—that provides profound internal stability against the chaos of everyday life [6].
