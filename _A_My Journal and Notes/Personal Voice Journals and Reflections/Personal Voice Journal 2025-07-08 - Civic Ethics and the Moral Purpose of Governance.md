[00:00] **Andy McGuire:** Why do we need government? Amid rhetoric advocating for smaller government and deregulation, critical principles are frequently overlooked. While fiscal responsibility is essential, that fiscal discipline should not come at the expense of basic social services and structural protections that human welfare requires. Consider what happens when clear rules and guidance are absent.

[00:56] Recently at a museum planetarium, the queuing line wasn't clearly marked. People began lining up where they thought the queue was, and it quickly branched into conflicting directions. When staff arrived to admit people and clarified the line's actual path, individuals began crowding and cutting in because their sense of fairness was violated—they believed they were ahead of others. When rules are ambiguous, humans do not effortlessly self-organize; without clear structure, self-interest takes over. On an open two-lane highway, the dividing line designates when passing is safe; if drivers perceive that breaking the rule carries no consequence, safety breaks down.

[02:27] Consider what would happen without food safety regulations. Commercial producers would naturally be incentivized to use the cheapest available ingredients rather than the safest ones to maximize profit margins, or cut corners during transportation. While liability risk offers some restraint, without regulatory oversight, public health is severely compromised and people get sick or die. A recent example is severe weather alerting: when budget cutbacks diminish early warning infrastructure, vulnerable communities are left exposed to disaster.

[03:58] There is a profound civic principle in the 'bliss of blamelessness' (*anavajja-sukha*): when a society has done everything within its power to protect the public from foreseeable harm, that is honorable governance. Nature will do what nature does, but our collective duty is to protect vulnerable beings and ensure basic welfare. That is the fundamental role of government.
