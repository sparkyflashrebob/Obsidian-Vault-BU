- **Core Observation**: When people feel unwell or emotionally triggered (as observed in interactions with Holly and acquaintances), there is a strong unconscious temptation to make one's inner distress everyone else's crisis (`5:35 PM`).
- **Dhamma / Contemplative Framing**: In Buddhist ethics, compassion begins with self-containment (*ahiṃsā* and *sīla*). When we lack the capacity to hold our own uncomfortable feelings, we dump our agitation onto our loved ones, forcing them to walk on eggshells.
- **Practical Integration**: We can gently ask ourselves throughout the day: *"Am I making myself a problem right now? Can I hold this tight sensation in my stomach with warmth rather than demanding that someone else change their behavior to soothe me?"*

### The Simplicity of the Bāhiya Sutta vs. Intellectual Proliferation
- **Core Observation**: Practitioners often read profound spiritual texts yet remain caught in anxiety because they treat teachings as intellectual puzzles to solve rather than direct practices (`10:42 AM`).
- **Dhamma / Contemplative Framing**: The Buddha identified *papañca* (conceptual proliferation) as the engine of suffering. In the *Bāhiya Sutta*, he cut through all conceptual architecture in a single sentence: rest in pure sensory contact without manufacturing an observer standing behind it.
- **Practical Integration**: When the mind begins to spin elaborate theories or worries, we can return to the senses: seeing the tree outside as just seeing, hearing the car pass as just hearing, and letting the heart rest in bare knowing.
