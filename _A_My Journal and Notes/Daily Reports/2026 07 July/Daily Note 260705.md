### Andy's Dharma: Banging Your Head Against the Wall vs. Skilful Surrender
- **Core Observation**: When faced with creative blocks or cognitive fatigue, conditioned ambition demands that we push harder, leading to strain and exhaustion (`10:00 AM`).
- **Dhamma / Contemplative Framing**: Right Effort (*Sammā Vāyāma*) is balanced effort—neither slack nor strained, like the tuned strings of a lute in the Sona Sutta. When the wall appears, mindfulness clearly sees the obstacle without resentment. Dropping the brute-force hammer is not failure; it is honoring conditionality (*idappaccayatā*).
- **Practical Integration**: When feeling mental fog or a tight knot in the stomach, stop pushing. Take three deep breaths, step away from the desk, and allow clarity to return organically.

### Discerning the Edge: Restful Self-Compassion vs. Habitual Punting
- **Core Observation**: Retreating from difficulty can easily become a chronic pattern of procrastination and avoidance that generates underlying guilt (`10:22 AM`).
- **Dhamma / Contemplative Framing**: The Two Wings of Practice work together here. Compassion grants us permission to rest when biological reserves are depleted; Mindfulness honestly notices when aversion (*dosa*) is disguising itself as tiredness to avoid necessary growth.
- **Practical Integration**: Ask gently: *"Does my body need quiet healing right now, or is my mind resisting an uncomfortable task?"* Respond with warmth and gentle resolve.

### Finding Equanimity in Sudden Darkness
- **Core Observation**: An unexpected power outage disrupts evening plans and exposes human vulnerability to modern electrical infrastructure (`9:45 PM`).
- **Dhamma / Contemplative Framing**: Equanimity (*upekkhā*) meets the unexpected without panic. When the lights go out, we can notice the mind wanting to fix or complain, and instead welcome the quiet stillness of candlelight as an unrequested retreat from screens.
- **Practical Integration**: In any sudden breakdown, pause before reacting. Feel your feet on the floor, light a candle, and enjoy the rare gift of undisturbed quiet.
