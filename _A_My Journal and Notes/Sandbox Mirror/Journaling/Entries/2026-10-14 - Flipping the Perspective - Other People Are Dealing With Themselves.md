# Flipping the Perspective: Other People Are Dealing With Themselves

## Authentic Entry
> "We take actions from other people—how we think about other people, and we equate that with their behavior in terms of their mental behavior, but we may not have the whole picture of them. By mental behavior, I mean their thinking.
> 
> While we don't know what they're going through otherwise—for instance, what their moods are, what they are feeling in their body (they may be constipated, they may have some illness, they may have some mental illness, etc. that is affecting them)—it's not straight out that they've made a decision to affect you or me personally specifically because of the way they're thinking about us.
> 
> What I mean by this is flipping the perspective: instead of 'they're talking about me,' they are really dealing with themselves, and I am aware of them simply because they're somehow involved in my life."

---

## Thematic Categorization & Notes
- **Category**: Interpersonal Insights & Mindfulness / Conditionality (*Idappaccayatā*)
- **Core Observation**: Shifting away from personalizing others' words or actions. Recognizing that what appears directed at us is almost always a reflection of their internal condition—their physical states, bodily discomforts, moods, and mental health.
- **Related Principles**:
  - *Conditionality*: Behavior arises out of internal causes and conditions (somatic states, bodily discomfort, stress), not a calculated personal agenda against us.
  - *The Second Arrow*: Freeing ourselves from the added mental story of "this is about me."
