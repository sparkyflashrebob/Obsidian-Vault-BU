#### The Core Concept: "The Resilient Mind"

To appeal to both a diverse student body and university administrators, the pitch must strip away esoteric terminology and emphasize **practical, cross-cultural tools for living** . You are essentially offering a secular synthesis of East and West: the *applied attention* of Eastern contemplative traditions and the *applied perspective* of Western philosophy.
Here is how to translate these profound principles into compelling, modern campus concepts:

* **From Theravada Buddhism:** Translate *Sati* (mindfulness) and *Vipassana* (insight) as **Attention Training** and **Meta-Awareness** . Instead of discussing the dissolution of the ego, frame it as "learning to observe thoughts and emotions without being overwhelmed by them."
* **From Stoicism:** Translate the *Dichotomy of Control* and *Amor Fati* as **Cognitive Reframing** and **Radical Acceptance** . Frame it as "discerning between what is in your power and what isn't, reducing anxiety over external outcomes."
* **From Earth-Based Cultures:** Translate the deep connection to nature as **Ecology of Mind** or **Somatic Grounding** , emphasizing a felt sense of interdependence with our immediate environment to counter digital isolation.
* **The Scientific Bridge:** Ground the entire practice in **neuroplasticity and nervous system regulation** , showing how ancient practices actively alter the brain's stress response networks (the amygdala and prefrontal cortex).

#### Suggested Framework for a 50-Minute Session

To show administrators that the group is structured, safe, and actionable, you can provide a sample layout of how a typical meeting flows:

1. **The Arrival & Grounding (5 Mins):** A brief somatic drop-in (focusing on the breath or physical senses) to transition from academic rushing to presence.
2. **The Core Insight (10 Mins):** Introducing a single, universal principle. *Example:* Pairing the Buddhist concept of the "Second Arrow" (the suffering we add to a clean pain) with the Stoic view that "we are disturbed not by things, but by the views we take of things."
3. **The Practice (20 Mins):** A guided, secular meditation practicing the integration of that insight (e.g., open monitoring of thoughts, or a focus on grounding anchors).
4. **Group Council / Inquiry (15 Mins):** Mindful, non-judgmental dialogue where students share how this principle applies to their current lived experience (exams, relationships, future anxiety).

#### How to Pitch the 90-Minute Format

When administrators see "90 minutes," they might worry about student attention spans. You can proactively reframe this in your pitch as a major selling point:

* **The "Decompression Buffer":** College students arrive with highly activated nervous systems—racing from classes, staring at screens, and consumed by anxiety. A 90-minute container allows for an intentional, unhurried ramp-down period so they can actually settle into a deep meditative state.
* **Relational Wellness and Community:** The extra time is specifically dedicated to interactive inquiry and deep listening. This directly combats the campus epidemic of loneliness and digital isolation by fostering genuine peer-to-peer connection.
* **The "Lab" Concept:** Frame it not as a long lecture, but as an interactive, multi-phase workshop or "laboratory" where the format shifts every 20 minutes to keep engagement high.
