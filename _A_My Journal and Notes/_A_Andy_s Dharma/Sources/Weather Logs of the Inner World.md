From my insight Timer Journal entries tilDec 24, 2025

The Insight Timer Journal consists of a collection of personal meditation logs dating from late 2013 through late 2025. These entries record the start time and duration of various sessions, often distinguishing between "Journal only" entries and timed meditations ranging from a few minutes to nearly two hours.

The sources reveal several key aspects of the practitioner's journey:

Meditation Techniques and Focus

The practitioner utilizes a wide variety of meditative disciplines. The most frequent focus is on mindfulness of the body (MOB) and the breath, particularly observing the breath at the nostrils or nostrils and torso. Other methods mentioned include:

• Vipassana and Concentration (samadhi) practice.

• Choice-less awareness and "resting in awareness".

• Metta (loving-kindness) and contemplation.

• Walking meditation and observing the "screen of experience".

• The appearance of Nimita (meditative signs/lights) during deep states of focus.

Physical Challenges and Sensations

A significant portion of the journal is dedicated to documenting physical discomfort and its impact on practice. Recurring issues include:

• Sinus and Respiratory Issues: Frequent mentions of sinus congestion, pressure, and throat issues that often forced the practitioner to breathe through their mouth.

• Chronic Pain: Persistent reports of back, neck, and shoulder pain, as well as nerve issues in the legs and "sit bones".

• "The Claw": A specific term used by the practitioner to describe a recurring sensation of intense pressure.

• Other Sensations: Documentation of itching, tingling, "head pressures," and even struggles with constipation during sits.

Mental and Emotional States

The journal tracks the ebb and flow of the practitioner's mental state. While many entries describe feelings of being "peaceful," "deep," or "clear-headed," others record:

• Papancha and Rumination: The mind "going elsewhere," thinking about teaching, or ruminating on past regrets and "negative Karma".

• Distractions: Challenges such as phone notifications, construction noise, and "anger at coworkers".

• Hindrances: Experiences of "sloth and torpor" (sleepiness), as well as "overwhelming" sexual thoughts and fantasies.

Context and Environment

The entries provide context for where and with whom the meditations occur. The practitioner often meditates during retreats (e.g., Spirit Rock, Abhayagiri, or Rockefeller Forest) and frequently sits with others, specifically naming individuals like Audel, Andrea, Dennis, and "Wild Bill". The journal also notes sessions held in unconventional circumstances, such as while making coffee, after a car accident, or while dealing with a migraine.

Reflections and Insights

Beyond simple logging, the journal records deep philosophical reflections. The practitioner contemplates impermanence, "not-self" (the inability to control what arises), and the Dalai Lama's teachings on emptiness. They often explore the relationship between the "thinker," the "talker," and the one "experiencing" the meditation.

---

To understand the Insight Timer Journal through these sources is to see it as a weather log for the inner world; just as a meteorologist records temperature, pressure, and storms to understand the climate, the practitioner uses these entries to track the shifting winds of thought and the persistent pressures of the body to better navigate their path toward peace.
