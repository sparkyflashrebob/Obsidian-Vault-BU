#### Highlights & Notes

> relaxing the tightness caused by craving

> This &ldquo;kingly&rdquo; generosity manifests as giving through kind thoughts, kind speech, and kind actions. A person who is miserly has a tendency to have a selfish, tight, and limited mind.

> Another form of generosity is the giving of time and energy to help those who are having problems, i.e. to become real friends.

> The actual content of arising thoughts or sensations is not important!

> Seeing and understanding HOW mind&rsquo;s attention moves from one thing to another is the reason to meditate.

> &ldquo;keep a full awareness of what mind&rsquo;s attention is doing,&rdquo;

> This meditation is a very natural "un-forced" observation process leading to understanding.

> Mind is a prey for arising stimuli, which are its own personal attachments, and emotional reactions.

> Most people would describe this as concentration, but this is not as accurate or precise a description as it could be. Mind&rsquo;s attention is not absorbed into, fixed on, or glued to the object of meditation. This is because there is no information in that object of meditation. Instead, mind&rsquo;s attention is very still, relaxed, collected, and composed, naturally staying on the breath or mettā very well with almost no effort at all. At this time a strong feeling of &ldquo;joy&rdquo; arises and the body becomes very light and feels as if it is floating.

> If the meditator begins to think or internally verbalize about how nice this state is, and how much they like it, they will lose that state and sleepiness very often comes into mind.

> desire makes mind&rsquo;s attention try too hard

> Trying too hard makes mind&rsquo;s attention lean out of the present moment and then restlessness and suffering arises.

> &ldquo;Then why are you trying to make something happen in a particular way, if you don&rsquo;t know what will happen in the future? Why are you pushing to have everything happen in the way you want it to be?&rdquo;

> craving&ndash;clinging&ndash;habitual tendency cycle!

> &lsquo;wanting for things to be in a particular way&rsquo; makes all the spiritual development fade away.

> Even while in jhāna [a meditation stage of understanding] the meditator has no real control over the joy arising, because joy arises when the conditions are right for it to come up. At the same time, the meditator simply cannot force joy to stay, because it will fade away when the conditions are right. This causes more un-satisfactoriness to arise, because joy is such a nice feeling! Thus, one is able to see the characteristics of existence very clearly, i.e. anicca (impermanence), dukkha (suffering), and not-self (anattā). This is the way to develop wisdom, which gradually leads the meditator to the seeing of all of the links of Dependent Origination both arising and passing away (that is, seeing and fully realizing The Four Noble Truths).

> An interesting observation is that one can see the three characteristics of existence without ever seeing Dependent Origination, but they can never see Dependent Origination without seeing the three characteristics of existence (i.e., impermanence, suffering and not-self/impersonal nature) at the same time.
